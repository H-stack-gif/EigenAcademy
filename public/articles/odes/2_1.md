# ODE Topic 2.1: Homogeneous Equations with Constant Coefficients

A mass on a spring. A swinging pendulum. An oscillating electrical circuit. These seemingly different systems all obey the same mathematical equation: a **second-order linear differential equation with constant coefficients**. The acceleration (second derivative) depends on position and velocity (first derivative), with constant proportionality. For a spring-mass system, Newton's law gives $ m\frac{d^2x}{dt^2} = -kx - \gamma\frac{dx}{dt}$, which rearranges to $mx'' + \gamma x' + kx = 0$.

Second-order equations are fundamentally different from first-order. The general solution contains **two** arbitrary constants (not one), requiring two initial conditions to determine a particular solution. We typically specify both position and velocity at $t = 0$. The form of solutions depends on the roots of a quadratic equation—the **characteristic equation**—which can be real and distinct, repeated, or complex conjugates. Each case produces qualitatively different behavior.

This article develops the theory of homogeneous second-order linear equations with constant coefficients. We'll learn the characteristic equation method, understand why solutions have the forms they do, and explore the crucial concept of linear independence. The Wronskian will help us verify that we have a complete set of solutions. These techniques form the foundation for all second-order equation work, whether in physics, engineering, or beyond.

## Standard Form and Terminology

**Second-order linear ODE**: $ a\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} + b\frac{dy}{dx} + cy = g(x)$

**Standard form** (divide by $a$): $ y'' + p(x)y' + q(x)y = f(x)$

**Homogeneous**: Right side is zero ($f(x) = 0$)

**Constant coefficients**: $p$ and $q$ are constants

This article focuses on: $ay'' + by' + cy = 0$ where $a, b, c$ are constants.

### Example 1: Identifying Equation Types

Classify each equation:

(a) $y'' + 3y' - 4y = 0$

(b) $y'' + xy' + y = x$

(c) $y'' + 4y = \sin x$

(d) $t^2y'' + ty' + y = 0$

**Solution:**

(a) Second-order, linear, homogeneous, constant coefficients ✓

(b) Second-order, linear, homogeneous, **variable** coefficients (has $xy'$)

(c) Second-order, linear, **nonhomogeneous**, constant coefficients (right side $\sin x \neq 0$)

(d) Second-order, linear, homogeneous, variable coefficients (has $t^2y''$, $ty'$)

**Answer:** Only (a) matches all criteria for this article.

## The Characteristic Equation Method

For $ay'' + by' + cy = 0$, try solution $y = e^{rx}$.

Then $y' = re^{rx}$ and $y'' = r^2e^{rx}$.

Substituting:
$$ar^2e^{rx} + bre^{rx} + ce^{rx} = 0$$

$$e^{rx}(ar^2 + br + c) = 0$$

Since $e^{rx} \neq 0$, we need:
$$ar^2 + br + c = 0$$

This is the **characteristic equation** (quadratic in $r$).

### Example 2: Finding Characteristic Equation

Find the characteristic equation for $y'' - 5y' + 6y = 0$.

**Solution:**

Try $y = e^{rx}$: substitute into equation.

Characteristic equation: $r^2 - 5r + 6 = 0$

Factor: $(r - 2)(r - 3) = 0$

Roots: $r_1 = 2$, $r_2 = 3$

**Answer:** Characteristic equation is $r^2 - 5r + 6 = 0$ with roots $r = 2, 3$.

### Example 3: General Solution - Distinct Real Roots

Solve $y'' - 5y' + 6y = 0$.

**Solution:**

From Example 2: roots are $r_1 = 2$, $r_2 = 3$.

Since roots are distinct and real:
$$y_1 = e^{2x}, \quad y_2 = e^{3x}$$

**General solution**:
$$y = c_1e^{2x} + c_2e^{3x}$$

where $c_1, c_2$ are arbitrary constants.

**Answer:** $y = c_1e^{2x} + c_2e^{3x}$

## Case 1: Distinct Real Roots

If characteristic equation $ar^2 + br + c = 0$ has distinct real roots $r_1, r_2$:

**General solution**: $y = c_1e^{r_1 x} + c_2e^{r_2 x}$

**Why?** Both $e^{r_1 x}$ and $e^{r_2 x}$ satisfy the differential equation, and they're linearly independent (we'll verify this).

### Example 4: Initial Value Problem

Solve $y'' + y' - 2y = 0$, $y(0) = 1$, $y'(0) = 0$.

**Solution:**

Characteristic equation: $r^2 + r - 2 = 0$

Factor: $(r + 2)(r - 1) = 0$

Roots: $r_1 = -2$, $r_2 = 1$

General solution: $y = c_1e^{-2x} + c_2e^x$

Apply $y(0) = 1$:
$$c_1e^0 + c_2e^0 = c_1 + c_2 = 1$$

Find $y'$: $y' = -2c_1e^{-2x} + c_2e^x$

Apply $y'(0) = 0$:
$$-2c_1 + c_2 = 0$$

So $c_2 = 2c_1$.

Substitute into first equation: $c_1 + 2c_1 = 1$, thus $c_1 =  \frac{1}{3}$

Then $c_2 =  \frac{2}{3}$

**Answer:** $ y = \frac{1}{3}e^{-2x} + \frac{2}{3}e^x$

### Example 5: Behavior Analysis

For $y'' - 4y = 0$, describe solution behavior as $x \to \infty$.

**Solution:**

Characteristic equation: $r^2 - 4 = 0$

$r^2 = 4$, so $r = \pm 2$

General solution: $y = c_1e^{2x} + c_2e^{-2x}$

As $x \to \infty$:
- $e^{2x} \to \infty$ (exponentially growing)
- $e^{-2x} \to 0$ (exponentially decaying)

Unless $c_1 = 0$, the $e^{2x}$ term dominates, and $y \to \pm\infty$.

**Answer:** Solutions grow exponentially unless $c_1 = 0$ (then decay exponentially).

## Case 2: Repeated Real Root

If characteristic equation has **repeated root** $r$ (double root):

One solution: $y_1 = e^{rx}$

Second solution: $y_2 = xe^{rx}$

**General solution**: $y = c_1e^{rx} + c_2xe^{rx} = (c_1 + c_2x)e^{rx}$

**Why $xe^{rx}$?** When roots repeat, we need a second independent solution. The method of reduction of order shows $xe^{rx}$ works.

### Example 6: Repeated Root

Solve $y'' - 6y' + 9y = 0$.

**Solution:**

Characteristic equation: $r^2 - 6r + 9 = 0$

Factor: $(r - 3)^2 = 0$

Repeated root: $r = 3$ (multiplicity 2)

**General solution**: $y = c_1e^{3x} + c_2xe^{3x}$

Or: $y = (c_1 + c_2x)e^{3x}$

**Answer:** $y = (c_1 + c_2x)e^{3x}$

### Example 7: Repeated Root IVP

Solve $y'' + 4y' + 4y = 0$, $y(0) = 2$, $y'(0) = -3$.

**Solution:**

Characteristic equation: $r^2 + 4r + 4 = 0$

$(r + 2)^2 = 0$, so $r = -2$ (repeated)

General solution: $y = (c_1 + c_2x)e^{-2x}$

Apply $y(0) = 2$:
$$c_1e^0 = c_1 = 2$$

Find $y'$: $y' = c_2e^{-2x} + (c_1 + c_2x)(-2)e^{-2x}$
$$= c_2e^{-2x} - 2(c_1 + c_2x)e^{-2x}$$
$$= [c_2 - 2c_1 - 2c_2x]e^{-2x}$$

Apply $y'(0) = -3$:
$$c_2 - 2c_1 = -3$$
$$c_2 - 4 = -3$$
$$c_2 = 1$$

**Answer:** $y = (2 + x)e^{-2x}$

## Case 3: Complex Conjugate Roots

If roots are complex: $r = \alpha \pm i\beta$ (where $i = \sqrt{-1}$)

Then solutions involve:
$$y = e^{\alpha x}(c_1\cos\beta x + c_2\sin\beta x)$$

**Why?** Euler's formula: $e^{i\theta} = \cos\theta + i\sin\theta$

Complex exponentials $e^{(\alpha + i\beta)x}$ and $e^{(\alpha - i\beta)x}$ combine to give real solutions.

### Example 8: Complex Roots

Solve $y'' + 4y = 0$.

**Solution:**

Characteristic equation: $r^2 + 4 = 0$

$r^2 = -4$, so $r = \pm 2i$

Here $\alpha = 0$, $\beta = 2$

**General solution**: $y = e^{0 \cdot x}(c_1\cos 2x + c_2\sin 2x)$

$$y = c_1\cos 2x + c_2\sin 2x$$

**Answer:** $y = c_1\cos 2x + c_2\sin 2x$ (oscillatory solutions, period $\pi$)

### Example 9: Complex Roots with Real Part

Solve $y'' - 2y' + 5y = 0$.

**Solution:**

Characteristic equation: $r^2 - 2r + 5 = 0$

Quadratic formula:
$$r = \frac{2 \pm \sqrt{4 - 20}}{2} = \frac{2 \pm \sqrt{-16}}{2} = \frac{2 \pm 4i}{2} = 1 \pm 2i$$

Here $\alpha = 1$, $\beta = 2$

**General solution**: $y = e^x(c_1\cos 2x + c_2\sin 2x)$

**Answer:** $y = e^x(c_1\cos 2x + c_2\sin 2x)$ (oscillations with exponential growth)

### Example 10: Damped Oscillation

For $y'' + 2y' + 5y = 0$, describe solution behavior.

**Solution:**

Characteristic: $r^2 + 2r + 5 = 0$

$$r = \frac{-2 \pm \sqrt{4 - 20}}{2} = \frac{-2 \pm 4i}{2} = -1 \pm 2i$$

$\alpha = -1 < 0$, $\beta = 2$

Solution: $y = e^{-x}(c_1\cos 2x + c_2\sin 2x)$

**Behavior**:
- $\cos 2x$, $\sin 2x$: oscillate with period $\pi$
- $e^{-x}$: decays exponentially

**Answer:** Damped oscillations (oscillations with decreasing amplitude).

## Linear Independence and the Wronskian

Two functions $y_1, y_2$ are **linearly independent** if:
$$c_1y_1 + c_2y_2 = 0 \text{ for all } x \implies c_1 = c_2 = 0$$

The **Wronskian** is:
$$W(y_1, y_2) = \begin{vmatrix} y_1 & y_2 \\ y_1' & y_2' \end{vmatrix} = y_1y_2' - y_1'y_2$$

**Theorem**: If $W(y_1, y_2) \neq 0$ at some point, then $y_1, y_2$ are linearly independent.

### Example 11: Computing Wronskian

Verify that $y_1 = e^{2x}$ and $y_2 = e^{3x}$ are linearly independent.

**Solution:**

$y_1' = 2e^{2x}$, $y_2' = 3e^{3x}$

$$W = \begin{vmatrix} e^{2x} & e^{3x} \\ 2e^{2x} & 3e^{3x} \end{vmatrix} = e^{2x} \cdot 3e^{3x} - e^{3x} \cdot 2e^{2x}$$

$$= 3e^{5x} - 2e^{5x} = e^{5x} \neq 0$$

Since $W \neq 0$, functions are linearly independent ✓

**Answer:** Yes, linearly independent (Wronskian $= e^{5x} \neq 0$).

### Example 12: Wronskian for Repeated Root

Verify that $y_1 = e^{3x}$ and $y_2 = xe^{3x}$ are linearly independent.

**Solution:**

$y_1' = 3e^{3x}$

$y_2' = e^{3x} + 3xe^{3x}$ (product rule)

$$W = \begin{vmatrix} e^{3x} & xe^{3x} \\ 3e^{3x} & e^{3x} + 3xe^{3x} \end{vmatrix}$$

$$= e^{3x}(e^{3x} + 3xe^{3x}) - xe^{3x}(3e^{3x})$$

$$= e^{6x} + 3xe^{6x} - 3xe^{6x} = e^{6x} \neq 0$$

**Answer:** Linearly independent (Wronskian $= e^{6x}$).

### Example 13: General Solution Structure

For $y'' + py' + qy = 0$, if $\{y_1, y_2\}$ is a fundamental set (linearly independent solutions), then every solution has form $y = c_1y_1 + c_2y_2$.

Why do we need exactly two solutions?

**Solution:**

Second-order equation requires two initial conditions: $y(x_0)$ and $y'(x_0)$.

General solution must have two arbitrary constants to satisfy both conditions.

Linear combination $c_1y_1 + c_2y_2$ provides two degrees of freedom.

**Theorem**: If $W(y_1, y_2) \neq 0$, then $\{y_1, y_2\}$ forms a **fundamental set** and general solution is $y = c_1y_1 + c_2y_2$.

**Answer:** Second-order ⟹ need 2 independent solutions ⟹ 2 constants in general solution.

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: B**
For $y'' - 9y = 0$, try $y = e^{rx}$.
Substituting: $r^2e^{rx} - 9e^{rx} = 0$
Characteristic equation: $r^2 - 9 = 0$
Roots: $r = \pm 3$

**Problem 2: B**
Complex roots $r = \alpha \pm i\beta$ with $\alpha = 3$, $\beta = 4$.
General solution: $y = e^{\alpha x}(c_1\cos\beta x + c_2\sin\beta x)$
$= e^{3x}(c_1\cos 4x + c_2\sin 4x)$
Exponential growth factor $e^{3x}$ times oscillations.

**Problem 3: C**
Repeated root $r$ gives: $y_1 = e^{rx}$, $y_2 = xe^{rx}$
For $r = -2$: $y = c_1e^{-2x} + c_2xe^{-2x} = (c_1 + c_2x)e^{-2x}$
Need both terms for two-parameter family.

**Problem 4: C**
$W = \begin{vmatrix} e^x & e^{-x} \\ e^x & -e^{-x} \end{vmatrix} = e^x(-e^{-x}) - e^{-x}(e^x)$
$= -e^0 - e^0 = -1 - 1 = -2$
Constant Wronskian $= -2$ (never zero, so linearly independent).

**Problem 5: B**
$r^2 + 4r + 4 = (r+2)^2 = 0$ gives $r = -2$ (repeated)
$y = (c_1 + c_2x)e^{-2x}$
$y(0) = c_1 = 0$
$y' = c_2e^{-2x} - 2(c_1 + c_2x)e^{-2x} = [c_2 - 2c_1 - 2c_2x]e^{-2x}$
$y'(0) = c_2 - 2c_1 = c_2 = 1$
So $y = xe^{-2x}$.
