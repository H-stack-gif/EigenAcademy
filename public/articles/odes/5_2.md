# ODE Topic 5.2: Frobenius Method

What happens when the power series method fails? Consider Bessel's equation: $x^2y'' + xy' + (x^2-n^2)y = 0$. At $x = 0$, we have a **singular point**—dividing through gives $P(x) = 1/x$ and $Q(x) = (x^2-n^2)/x^2$, both undefined at the origin. Yet physically meaningful solutions exist there. Bessel functions describe vibrating drumheads, heat flow in cylinders, electromagnetic waves in circular waveguides—all centered at the origin.

The **Frobenius method** extends power series to handle **regular singular points**. Instead of assuming $y = \sum a_nx^n$, we assume $y = x^r\sum a_nx^n$ where $r$ might not be an integer. This extra factor $x^r$ captures singular behavior. The **indicial equation** determines possible values of $r$, and the nature of solutions depends dramatically on whether these roots differ by an integer.

This article develops the Frobenius method systematically. We'll learn to recognize regular singular points, derive the indicial equation, and construct solutions for all cases (distinct roots not differing by integer, repeated root, roots differing by integer). These techniques unlock Bessel functions, modified Bessel functions, and other special functions essential in physics and engineering. The method reveals why some equations have logarithmic solutions—structure invisible to ordinary power series.

## Regular Singular Points

For $a_2(x)y'' + a_1(x)y' + a_0(x)y = 0$, point $x_0$ is **regular singular** if:

1. At least one of $P = a_1/a_2$ or $Q = a_0/a_2$ is singular at $x_0$
2. $(x-x_0)P(x)$ is analytic at $x_0$
3. $(x-x_0)^2Q(x)$ is analytic at $x_0$

If conditions 2-3 fail, it's an **irregular singular point**.

### Example 1: Classifying Singular Points

Classify $x = 0$ for $x^2y'' + xy' + (x^2-1)y = 0$.

**Solution:**

Divide by $x^2$:
$$y'' + \frac{1}{x}y' + \frac{x^2-1}{x^2}y = 0$$

$P(x) =  \frac{1}{x}$, $Q(x) = \frac{x^2-1}{x^2} = 1 - \frac{1}{x^2}$

Check conditions:

$xP(x) = x \cdot  \frac{1}{x} = 1$ (analytic at $x = 0$) ✓

$x^2Q(x) = x^2\left(1 -  \frac{1}{x^2}\right) = x^2 - 1$ (analytic at $x = 0$) ✓

**Answer:** $x = 0$ is a **regular singular point**.

### Example 2: Irregular Singular Point

Classify $x = 0$ for $x^3y'' + 2y' + xy = 0$.

**Solution:**

Divide by $x^3$:
$$y'' + \frac{2}{x^3}y' + \frac{1}{x^2}y = 0$$

$P(x) =  \frac{2}{x^3}$, $Q(x) = \frac{1}{x^2}$

Check:

$xP(x) =  \frac{2}{x^2}$ (NOT analytic at $x = 0$—still has singularity) ✗

**Answer:** $x = 0$ is an **irregular singular point** (Frobenius method won't work).

## Frobenius Series

Near regular singular point $x = 0$, assume:
$$y = x^r\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} a_nx^n = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} a_nx^{n+r}$$

where $r$ is to be determined and $a_0 \neq 0$.

**Derivatives**:
$$y' = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} (n+r)a_nx^{n+r-1}$$

$$y'' = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} (n+r)(n+r-1)a_nx^{n+r-2}$$

### Example 3: Setting Up Frobenius

For $2xy'' + y' - y = 0$, write the Frobenius series substitution.

**Solution:**

Assume $ y = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} a_nx^{n+r}$

$$y' = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} (n+r)a_nx^{n+r-1}$$

$$y'' = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} (n+r)(n+r-1)a_nx^{n+r-2}$$

Substitute:
$$2x\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} (n+r)(n+r-1)a_nx^{n+r-2} + \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} (n+r)a_nx^{n+r-1} - \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} a_nx^{n+r} = 0$$

$$\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} 2(n+r)(n+r-1)a_nx^{n+r-1} + \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} (n+r)a_nx^{n+r-1} - \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} a_nx^{n+r} = 0$$

**Answer:** Combine terms with matching powers of $x$.

## The Indicial Equation

The **indicial equation** comes from coefficient of lowest power of $x$ (usually $x^{r-1}$ or $x^r$).

Setting $a_0 \neq 0$ and equating this coefficient to zero gives equation for $r$.

### Example 4: Finding Indicial Equation

For $2xy'' + y' - y = 0$, find indicial equation.

**Solution:**

From Example 3, combining like powers:

Lowest power is $x^{r-1}$:
$$2r(r-1)a_0 + ra_0 = 0$$

Factor:
$$a_0[2r(r-1) + r] = 0$$

Since $a_0 \neq 0$:
$$2r^2 - 2r + r = 0$$

$$2r^2 - r = 0$$

$$r(2r-1) = 0$$

**Indicial equation**: $r(2r-1) = 0$

**Roots**: $r_1 =  \frac{1}{2}$, $r_2 = 0$

**Answer:** Indicial roots are $r = 0,  \frac{1}{2}$ (differ by $\frac{1}{2}$, not integer).

### Example 5: Complete Solution

Continue Example 4 to find recurrence relation.

**Solution:**

For $x^r$ terms (after indicial equation):
$$2(n+r)(n+r-1)a_n + (n+r)a_n - a_{n-1} = 0$$

$$a_n[2(n+r)(n+r-1) + (n+r)] = a_{n-1}$$

$$a_n[(n+r)(2n+2r-2+1)] = a_{n-1}$$

$$a_n = \frac{a_{n-1}}{(n+r)(2n+2r-1)}$$

**For** $r =  \frac{1}{2}$:
$$a_n = \frac{a_{n-1}}{(n+1/2)(2n)}$$

**For** $r = 0$:
$$a_n = \frac{a_{n-1}}{n(2n-1)}$$

**Answer:** Two series solutions with different exponents.

## Three Cases for Indicial Roots

Let $r_1, r_2$ be indicial roots with $r_1 \geq r_2$.

**Case 1**: $r_1 - r_2$ is not an integer
- Two independent Frobenius series: $y_1 = x^{r_1}\sum a_nx^n$, $y_2 = x^{r_2}\sum b_nx^n$

**Case 2**: $r_1 = r_2$ (repeated root)
- One series: $y_1 = x^r\sum a_nx^n$
- Second involves logarithm: $y_2 = y_1\ln x + x^r\sum c_nx^n$

**Case 3**: $r_1 - r_2$ is positive integer
- Larger root gives: $y_1 = x^{r_1}\sum a_nx^n$
- Second may involve logarithm: $y_2 = Cy_1\ln x + x^{r_2}\sum b_nx^n$
- Constant $C$ might be zero (no logarithm) or nonzero

### Example 6: Case 1 Example

For $2xy'' + (1+x)y' + y = 0$, solve using Frobenius.

**Solution:**

Check $x = 0$: $P = (1+x)/(2x)$, $Q = 1/(2x)$

$xP = (1+x)/2$ analytic ✓

$x^2Q = x/2$ analytic ✓

Regular singular point at $x = 0$.

Indicial equation (from lowest power):
$$2r(r-1) + r = 0$$
$$2r^2 - r = 0$$
$$r(2r-1) = 0$$

Roots: $r_1 =  \frac{1}{2}$, $r_2 = 0$ (differ by $\frac{1}{2}$, **Case 1**)

Two independent series solutions exist.

**Answer:** Case 1—two Frobenius series with exponents $r = 0, \frac{1}{2}$.

### Example 7: Case 2 Example (Repeated Root)

For $x^2y'' + xy' + y = 0$, find indicial equation.

**Solution:**

$x^2y'' + 3xy' + y = 0$

Indicial: $r(r-1) + 3r + 1 = 0$

$r^2 + 2r + 1 = (r+1)^2 = 0$

**Repeated root**: $r = -1$

**Answer:** **Case 2**—one series solution, second involves $\ln x$.

### Example 8: Case 3 Example

For $xy'' + y = 0$, find indicial roots.

**Solution:**

Standard form: $ y'' + \frac{1}{x}y = 0$

Indicial equation:
$$r(r-1) = 0$$

**Roots**: $r_1 = 1$, $r_2 = 0$ (differ by 1, **Case 3**)

**Answer:** Case 3—may need logarithmic term in second solution.

## Bessel's Equation of Order Zero

**Bessel's equation**: $x^2y'' + xy' + (x^2-n^2)y = 0$

For $n = 0$: $x^2y'' + xy' + x^2y = 0$

### Example 9: Indicial Equation for Bessel

Find indicial equation for Bessel's equation of order 0.

**Solution:**

$$x^2y'' + xy' + x^2y = 0$$

Frobenius:

$$y = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} a_nx^{n+r}$$

$$x^2y'' = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} (n+r)(n+r-1)a_nx^{n+r}$$

$$xy' = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} (n+r)a_nx^{n+r}$$

$$x^2y = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} a_nx^{n+r+2}$$

Lowest power from first two terms: $x^r$

Coefficient: $r(r-1)a_0 + ra_0 = 0$

Indicial: $r(r-1) + r = r^2 = 0$

**Roots**: $r = 0$ (repeated!)

**Answer:** Double root $r = 0$, so **Case 2** applies.

### Example 10: Bessel Functions

The solution to $x^2y'' + xy' + x^2y = 0$ involves $J_0(x)$, the Bessel function of first kind, order 0.

$$J_0(x) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n x^{2n}}{2^{2n}(n!)^2}$$

$$= 1 - \frac{x^2}{4} + \frac{x^4}{64} - \frac{x^6}{2304} + \cdots$$

Second independent solution: $Y_0(x)$ (Bessel function of second kind), involves $\ln x$.

**Answer:** General solution: $y = c_1J_0(x) + c_2Y_0(x)$

## Modified Bessel Equation

**Modified Bessel equation**: $x^2y'' + xy' - (x^2+n^2)y = 0$

(Sign change from Bessel)

### Example 11: Modified Bessel Solutions

The modified Bessel equation has solutions $I_n(x)$ and $K_n(x)$.

For $n = 0$:
$$I_0(x) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{x^{2n}}{2^{2n}(n!)^2} = 1 + \frac{x^2}{4} + \frac{x^4}{64} + \cdots$$

(All positive—exponentially growing)

$I_0(x)$ is related to $J_0(ix)$ where $i = \sqrt{-1}$.

**Answer:** Solutions grow exponentially (vs oscillatory for Bessel).

## Logarithmic Solutions

For repeated root or roots differing by integer, second solution often has form:
$$y_2 = Cy_1\ln x + x^r\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} b_nx^n$$

### Example 12: Verifying Logarithmic Term

For $x^2y'' + xy' + y = 0$ with repeated root $r = 0$, second solution is:

$$y_2 = J_0(x)\ln x + x^0\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} c_nx^{2n}$$

The logarithm captures singularity at origin.

**Answer:** Logarithmic terms arise naturally from repeated indicial roots.

### Example 13: Summary of Method

**Frobenius Method Procedure**:

1. Verify $x_0$ is regular singular point
2. Substitute $y = \sum a_nx^{n+r}$ into equation
3. Find indicial equation from lowest power
4. Solve for roots $r_1, r_2$
5. Determine case (1, 2, or 3) based on root difference
6. For each root, derive recurrence relation
7. Build series solutions
8. For Cases 2-3, may need logarithmic term

**Answer:** Systematic approach handles regular singular points.

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: B**
Definition of regular singular point at $x = 0$:
$(x-x_0)P(x)$ and $(x-x_0)^2Q(x)$ are analytic
For $x_0 = 0$: need $xP(x)$ and $x^2Q(x)$ analytic
This allows $P \sim 1/x$ and $Q \sim 1/x^2$ singularities
But no worse than these

**Problem 2: B**
After substituting Frobenius series, expand in powers of $x$
Lowest power is typically $x^{r-1}$ or $x^r$
Coefficient must vanish: leads to equation for $r$
This is indicial equation
Determines exponent $r$ in $y = x^r \sum a_n x^n$

**Problem 3: A**
Case 1: $r_1 - r_2$ not an integer
$\frac{1}{2}$ is not an integer ✓
Two independent Frobenius series
No logarithmic terms needed
Both solutions are "pure" power series times $x^r$

**Problem 4: B**
$x^2y'' + xy' + (x^2-n^2)y = 0$
$P = 1/x$, $Q = (x^2-n^2)/x^2$
$xP = 1$ analytic ✓
$x^2Q = x^2 - n^2$ analytic ✓
Regular singular point (not irregular)

**Problem 5: C**
Logarithms appear in two situations:
1. Repeated indicial root (Case 2)
2. Roots differ by positive integer (Case 3)
Second solution: $y_2 = Cy_1 \ln x + x^{r_2}\sum b_n x^n$
Constant $C$ may be zero or nonzero depending on equation
