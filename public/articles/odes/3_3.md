# ODE Topic 3.3: Nonlinear Systems and Linearization

Linear systems are tractable—we can solve them exactly using eigenvalues. But most real systems are **nonlinear**. The pendulum equation $\theta'' + \sin\theta = 0$ is nonlinear ($\sin\theta \neq \theta$ for large angles). Predator-prey models with saturation, chemical reactions with nonlinear kinetics, ecosystems with carrying capacities—all nonlinear. We generally can't solve nonlinear systems in closed form.

Yet we can still understand their behavior near equilibrium points through **linearization**. The idea: approximate the nonlinear system by its linear "tangent" at an equilibrium. Just as we approximate $f(x)$ by tangent line $f(a) + f'(a)(x-a)$, we approximate $\vec{f}(\vec{x})$ by **Jacobian matrix** $J$ at equilibrium. If linearization has hyperbolic equilibrium (eigenvalues with nonzero real parts), the nonlinear system behaves similarly near that point.

This article explores nonlinear systems, focusing on finding equilibria, computing the Jacobian, and using linear stability analysis. We'll see applications to competing species, predator-prey dynamics with saturation, and pendulum oscillations. The **Hartman-Grobman theorem** guarantees that linearization predicts local behavior for hyperbolic equilibria—a powerful bridge between tractable linear theory and realistic nonlinear models.

## Nonlinear Systems

A **nonlinear system** has form:
$$\begin{cases} x' = f(x, y) \\ y' = g(x, y) \end{cases}$$

where $f, g$ are nonlinear functions.

**Equilibrium points** (critical points): Solutions to $f(x, y) = 0$ and $g(x, y) = 0$.

At equilibrium: $x' = y' = 0$ (no change, constant solution).

### Example 1: Finding Equilibria

Find equilibria for:
$$\begin{cases} x' = x(1 - x - y) \\ y' = y(0.5 - x) \end{cases}$$

**Solution:**

Set both equations to zero:

From $y(0.5 - x) = 0$: either $y = 0$ or $x = 0.5$

**Case 1**: $y = 0$

From $x(1 - x) = 0$: $x = 0$ or $x = 1$

Equilibria: $(0, 0)$ and $(1, 0)$

**Case 2**: $x = 0.5$

From $0.5(1 - 0.5 - y) = 0$: $0.5 - y = 0$, so $y = 0.5$

Equilibrium: $(0.5, 0.5)$

Also check $(0, y)$: from $x' = 0$, need $x = 0$. From $y' = 0$: $y(0.5 - 0) = 0.5y = 0$, so $y = 0$.

**Answer:** Equilibria at $(0, 0)$, $(1, 0)$, $(0.5, 0.5)$.

### Example 2: Pendulum Equilibria

For pendulum $\theta'' + \sin\theta = 0$, convert to system and find equilibria.

**Solution:**

Let $x = \theta$, $y = \theta'$

System:
$$\begin{cases} x' = y \\ y' = -\sin x \end{cases}$$

Equilibria: $y = 0$ and $\sin x = 0$

$\sin x = 0$ gives $x = n\pi$ for integer $n$

**Answer:** Equilibria at $(n\pi, 0)$ for all integers $n$.

$(0, 0)$: pendulum hanging down (stable)

$(\pi, 0)$: pendulum inverted (unstable)

## The Jacobian Matrix

To linearize $\vec{x}' = \vec{f}(\vec{x})$ at equilibrium $\vec{x}_0$:

**Jacobian matrix**:
$$J = \begin{bmatrix} \frac{\partial f}{\partial x} & \frac{\partial f}{\partial y} \\ \frac{\partial g}{\partial x} & \frac{\partial g}{\partial y} \end{bmatrix}$$

evaluated at $\vec{x}_0$.

**Linearized system**: $\vec{u}' = J\vec{u}$ where $\vec{u} = \vec{x} - \vec{x}_0$ (displacement from equilibrium).

### Example 3: Computing Jacobian

For $\begin{cases} x' = x^2 - y \\ y' = x + y^2 \end{cases}$, find Jacobian.

**Solution:**

$f(x, y) = x^2 - y$, $g(x, y) = x + y^2$

$$J = \begin{bmatrix} \frac{\partial f}{\partial x} & \frac{\partial f}{\partial y} \\ \frac{\partial g}{\partial x} & \frac{\partial g}{\partial y} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 2x & -1 \\ 1 & 2y \end{bmatrix}$$

**Answer:** $ J = \begin{bmatrix} 2x & -1 \\ 1 & 2y \end{bmatrix}$ (depends on $(x, y)$)

### Example 4: Jacobian at Equilibrium

For Example 1, find Jacobian at $(0.5, 0.5)$.

**Solution:**

$f(x, y) = x(1 - x - y)$, $g(x, y) = y(0.5 - x)$

$$\frac{\partial f}{\partial x} = 1 - 2x - y$$

$$\frac{\partial f}{\partial y} = -x$$

$$\frac{\partial g}{\partial x} = -y$$

$$\frac{\partial g}{\partial y} = 0.5 - x$$

At $(0.5, 0.5)$:
$$J = \begin{bmatrix} 1 - 1 - 0.5 & -0.5 \\ -0.5 & 0.5 - 0.5 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -0.5 & -0.5 \\ -0.5 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$$

**Answer:** $ J = \begin{bmatrix} -0.5 & -0.5 \\ -0.5 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$ at $(0.5, 0.5)$

## Stability Analysis

**Hartman-Grobman Theorem**: If linearization at $\vec{x}_0$ has **hyperbolic** equilibrium (all eigenvalues have nonzero real parts), then nonlinear system behaves like linear system near $\vec{x}_0$.

**Stability**:
- All eigenvalues have $\text{Re}(\lambda) < 0$ → **asymptotically stable**
- Any eigenvalue has $\text{Re}(\lambda) > 0$ → **unstable**
- Pure imaginary eigenvalues → **inconclusive** (linearization doesn't determine nonlinear behavior)

### Example 5: Stability of Competing Species

For Example 1 at $(0.5, 0.5)$, determine stability.

**Solution:**

From Example 4: $ J = \begin{bmatrix} -0.5 & -0.5 \\ -0.5 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$

Eigenvalues: $\det(J - \lambda I) = (-0.5-\lambda)(-\lambda) - 0.25 = \lambda^2 + 0.5\lambda - 0.25 = 0$

$$\lambda = \frac{-0.5 \pm \sqrt{0.25 + 1}}{2} = \frac{-0.5 \pm \sqrt{1.25}}{2}$$

$$= \frac{-0.5 \pm 1.118}{2}$$

$\lambda_1 \approx 0.31 > 0$, $\lambda_2 \approx -0.81 < 0$

Opposite signs → **saddle point** (unstable)

**Answer:** Coexistence equilibrium $(0.5, 0.5)$ is unstable saddle.

### Example 6: Pendulum Stability

For pendulum at $(0, 0)$, determine stability.

**Solution:**

System: $x' = y$, $y' = -\sin x$

$$J = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -\cos x & 0 \end{bmatrix}$$

At $(0, 0)$:
$$J = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$$

Eigenvalues: $\lambda^2 + 1 = 0$, so $\lambda = \pm i$ (pure imaginary)

**Inconclusive!** (linearization gives center, but nonlinear system might differ)

The pendulum at bottom is stable (but not asymptotically—small oscillations persist).

**Answer:** Linearization gives center; actual behavior is stable (but neutrally).

### Example 7: Pendulum at Top

Analyze $(\pi, 0)$ for pendulum.

**Solution:**

$\det\begin{bmatrix} -\lambda & 1 \\ 1 & -\lambda \end{bmatrix} = \lambda^2 - 1 = 0$

$\lambda = \pm 1$ (opposite signs!)

**Saddle point** (unstable)

**Answer:** Inverted pendulum is unstable saddle (tiny perturbation causes it to fall).

## Predator-Prey Models

**Lotka-Volterra with logistic growth**:
$$\begin{cases} x' = rx(1 - x/K) - axy \\ y' = -dy + bxy \end{cases}$$

$x$ = prey, $y$ = predator

### Example 8: Equilibria of Modified Lotka-Volterra

Find equilibria for:
$$\begin{cases} x' = x(2 - x - y) \\ y' = y(x - 1) \end{cases}$$

**Solution:**

From $y(x - 1) = 0$: $y = 0$ or $x = 1$

**Case 1**: $y = 0$

$x(2 - x) = 0$ gives $x = 0$ or $x = 2$

Equilibria: $(0, 0)$, $(2, 0)$

**Case 2**: $x = 1$

$1(2 - 1 - y) = 0$ gives $y = 1$

Equilibrium: $(1, 1)$

**Answer:** $(0, 0)$, $(2, 0)$, $(1, 1)$

### Example 9: Stability at Coexistence

For Example 8, analyze $(1, 1)$.

**Solution:**

$f = x(2 - x - y)$, $g = y(x - 1)$

$$J = \begin{bmatrix} 2 - 2x - y & -x \\ y & x - 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

At $(1, 1)$:
$$J = \begin{bmatrix} 2 - 2 - 1 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -1 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$$

Eigenvalues: $\det(J - \lambda I) = -\lambda(-1-\lambda) + 1 = \lambda^2 + \lambda + 1 = 0$

$$\lambda = \frac{-1 \pm \sqrt{1-4}}{2} = \frac{-1 \pm i\sqrt{3}}{2}$$

Complex with $\text{Re}(\lambda) = -1/2 < 0$

**Stable spiral**

**Answer:** Coexistence equilibrium is stable spiral (damped oscillations).

## Nullclines

**$x$-nullcline**: Curve where $x' = 0$ (vertical motion only)

**$y$-nullcline**: Curve where $y' = 0$ (horizontal motion only)

Equilibria occur at **intersections** of nullclines.

### Example 10: Plotting Nullclines

For $\begin{cases} x' = x - xy \\ y' = -y + xy \end{cases}$, find nullclines.

**Solution:**

**$x$-nullcline**: $x - xy = 0$, so $x(1 - y) = 0$

Curves: $x = 0$ (y-axis) or $y = 1$ (horizontal line)

**$y$-nullcline**: $-y + xy = 0$, so $y(x - 1) = 0$

Curves: $y = 0$ (x-axis) or $x = 1$ (vertical line)

**Equilibria** (intersections):
- $x = 0, y = 0$: $(0, 0)$
- $x = 0, x = 1$: impossible
- $y = 1, y = 0$: impossible
- $y = 1, x = 1$: $(1, 1)$

**Answer:** Nullclines divide plane into regions; equilibria at $(0, 0)$ and $(1, 1)$.

### Example 11: Direction Fields from Nullclines

For Example 10, what are signs of $x', y'$ in region $0 < x < 1$, $0 < y < 1$?

**Solution:**

Choose test point $(0.5, 0.5)$:

$x' = 0.5 - 0.5(0.5) = 0.5 - 0.25 = 0.25 > 0$ (moving right)

$y' = -0.5 + 0.5(0.5) = -0.25 < 0$ (moving down)

**Answer:** In this region, trajectories move right and down (toward equilibrium $(1, 1)$ 

## Limit Cycles

Some nonlinear systems have **limit cycles**: isolated periodic orbits that attract nearby trajectories.

Example: Van der Pol oscillator $x'' - \mu(1-x^2)x' + x = 0$ has limit cycle for $\mu > 0$.

### Example 12: Identifying Limit Cycle

Can linear system $\vec{x}' = A\vec{x}$ have limit cycle?

**Solution:**

Linear systems:
- Equilibrium at origin only (if $\det A \neq 0$)
- Trajectories near center are closed, but not isolated
- Cannot have isolated periodic orbit

**Answer:** No, linear systems cannot have limit cycles (Poincaré-Bendixson theorem excludes them).

### Example 13: Multiple Equilibria

For $\begin{cases} x' = y \\ y' = x - x^3 \end{cases}$, find and classify equilibria.

**Solution:**

Equilibria: $y = 0$ and $x - x^3 = 0$

$x(1 - x^2) = 0$ gives $x = 0, \pm 1$

Equilibria: $(0, 0)$, $(1, 0)$, $(-1, 0)$

**Jacobian**:
$$J = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 - 3x^2 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$$

At $(0, 0)$: $J = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$, $\lambda^2 - 1 = 0$, $\lambda = \pm 1$ → **saddle**

At $(\pm 1, 0)$: $J = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -2 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$, $\lambda^2 + 2 = 0$, $\lambda = \pm i\sqrt{2}$ → **center** (linearization)

**Answer:** Origin is saddle; $(\pm 1, 0)$ are centers (by linearization).

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: C**
Equilibrium: constant solution where $\vec{x}' = \vec{0}$
For system $\begin{cases} x' = f(x,y) \\ y' = g(x,y) \end{cases}$: need $f(x,y) = 0$ AND $g(x,y) = 0$
Both derivatives zero (no change in either variable).

**Problem 2: B**
Jacobian: matrix of first partial derivatives
$$J = \begin{bmatrix} \partial f/\partial x & \partial f/\partial y \\ \partial g/\partial x & \partial g/\partial y \end{bmatrix}$$
Linear approximation to nonlinear system near equilibrium.

**Problem 3: C**
Eigenvalues $\lambda_1 = 2 > 0$, $\lambda_2 = -3 < 0$ (opposite signs)
Opposite signs → saddle point
One stable direction, one unstable → overall unstable
Hyperbolic equilibrium (nonzero real parts).

**Problem 4: C**
Pure imaginary: $\lambda = \pm i\beta$ (real part zero)
Linearization gives center (neutrally stable)
Nonlinear system might be stable, unstable, or have limit cycle
Hartman-Grobman doesn't apply (non-hyperbolic)
Need other methods (Lyapunov functions, etc.).

**Problem 5: B**
$x$-nullcline: $x' = f(x,y) = 0$ (vertical motion only)
$y$-nullcline: $y' = g(x,y) = 0$ (horizontal motion only)
Equilibria occur where nullclines intersect
Nullclines divide phase plane into regions with consistent flow direction.
