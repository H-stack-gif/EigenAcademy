# ODE Topic 1.2: Separable Equations

The simplest differential equations to solve are those where you can "separate the variables"—get all $y$ terms on one side and all $x$ terms on the other. Consider $ \frac{dy}{dx} = xy$. We can write this as $ \frac{dy}{y} = x \, dx$. Now each side depends on only one variable, and we can integrate both sides independently. This technique, called **separation of variables**, solves a huge class of equations.

Separable equations appear constantly in applications. Population growth (where growth rate is proportional to current population), radioactive decay (where decay rate is proportional to amount present), Newton's law of cooling (where cooling rate is proportional to temperature difference), chemical reactions, drug metabolism, carbon dating—all lead to separable equations. The technique is straightforward: separate, integrate, solve for $y$ if possible.

This article develops the separation of variables method systematically and applies it to real-world models. We'll see exponential growth and decay, logistic growth with carrying capacity, mixing problems, and Newton's law of cooling. Understanding these models means understanding how rates of change depend on current state—the essence of differential equations.

## Separable Equations

A differential equation is **separable** if it can be written:
$$ \frac{dy}{dx} = g(x)h(y)$$

Equivalently, we can rearrange to:
$$ \frac{dy}{h(y)} = g(x) \, dx$$

**Solution method**:
1. Separate variables: get all $y$ terms (including $dy$) on left, all $x$ terms (including $dx$) on right
2. Integrate both sides: $ \int \frac{dy}{h(y)} = \int g(x) \, dx$
3. Solve for $y$ if possible
4. Use initial condition to find constant

### Example 1: Basic Separation

Solve $ \frac{dy}{dx} = xy$.

**Solution:**

Separate: $ \frac{dy}{y} = x \, dx$

Integrate: $ \int \frac{dy}{y} = \int x \, dx$

$$ \ln|y| = \frac{x^2}{2} + C$$

Exponentiate: $ |y| = e^{x^2/2 + C} = e^C \cdot e^{x^2/2}$

$$ y = Ae^{x^2/2}$$

where $A = \pm e^C$ (arbitrary constant).

**Answer:** $ y = Ae^{x^2/2}$

### Example 2: Using Initial Condition

Solve IVP: $ \frac{dy}{dx} = xy$, $y(0) = 2$.

**Solution:**

From Example 1: $ y = Ae^{x^2/2}$

Apply $y(0) = 2$:
$$ 2 = Ae^{0} = A$$

**Answer:** $ y = 2e^{x^2/2}$

### Example 3: Implicit Solution

Solve $ \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{x}{y}$.

**Solution:**

Separate: $y \, dy = x \, dx$

Integrate: $ \int y \, dy = \int x \, dx$

$$ \frac{y^2}{2} = \frac{x^2}{2} + C$$

$$ y^2 = x^2 + 2C$$

Let $K = 2C$: $y^2 = x^2 + K$

Can solve explicitly: $y = \pm\sqrt{x^2 + K}$

**Answer:** $y^2 = x^2 + K$ (implicit) or $y = \pm\sqrt{x^2 + K}$ (explicit)

### Example 4: Don't Lose Solutions

Solve $ \frac{dy}{dx} = y^2$.

**Solution:**

Separate: $ \frac{dy}{y^2} = dx$

Integrate: $ \int y^{-2} \, dy = \int dx$

$$ -\frac{1}{y} = x + C$$

$$ y = -\frac{1}{x + C}$$

**Important:** When separating, we divided by $y^2$, assuming $y \neq 0$. Check if $y = 0$ is solution:

If $y = 0$: $y' = 0$ and $y^2 = 0$, so $y' = y^2$ ✓

**Answer:** $ y = -\frac{1}{x + C}$ and $y = 0$ (don't lose the equilibrium solution!)

## Exponential Growth and Decay

The equation $ \frac{dy}{dt} = ky$ models exponential growth ($k > 0$) or decay ($k < 0$).

**Solution**: $y = y_0 e^{kt}$ where $y_0 = y(0)$

**Applications**:
- Population growth (unrestricted)
- Radioactive decay
- Bacterial growth
- Compound interest
- Drug concentration decay

### Example 5: Radioactive Decay

Carbon-14 has half-life 5,730 years. If sample starts with 100g, how much remains after 10,000 years?

**Solution:**

Model: $ \frac{dA}{dt} = kA$ with $A(0) = 100$

Solution: $A(t) = 100e^{kt}$

Use half-life to find $k$:
$$ A(5730) = 50 = 100e^{5730k}$$

$$ 0.5 = e^{5730k}$$

$$ k = \frac{\ln(0.5)}{5730} = -\frac{\ln 2}{5730}$$

At $t = 10000$:
$$ A(10000) = 100e^{(-\ln 2/5730)(10000)} = 100e^{-10000\ln 2/5730}$$

$$ = 100 \left(e^{\ln 2}\right)^{-10000/5730} = 100(2)^{-10000/5730}$$

$$ = 100(2)^{-1.746} \approx 100(0.298) = 29.8$$

**Answer:** Approximately 29.8 grams remain.

### Example 6: Population Growth

Bacteria culture grows exponentially. If population is 1000 at $t = 0$ and 3000 at $t = 2$ hours, find population at $t = 5$ hours.

**Solution:**

$ \frac{dP}{dt} = kP$, $P(0) = 1000$

Solution: $P(t) = 1000e^{kt}$

Use $P(2) = 3000$:
$$ 3000 = 1000e^{2k}$$

$$ 3 = e^{2k}$$

$$ k = \frac{\ln 3}{2}$$

At $t = 5$:
$$ P(5) = 1000e^{5k} = 1000e^{5\ln 3/2} = 1000(e^{\ln 3})^{5/2}$$

$$ = 1000(3)^{2.5} = 1000 \cdot 3^2 \cdot \sqrt{3} = 9000\sqrt{3} \approx 15,588$$

**Answer:** Approximately 15,588 bacteria.

## Newton's Law of Cooling

Temperature difference between object and surroundings decreases exponentially:
$$ \frac{dT}{dt} = -k(T - T_{\text{env}})$$

where $T_{\text{env}}$ is ambient temperature.

**Solution**: $T(t) = T_{\text{env}} + (T_0 - T_{\text{env}})e^{-kt}$

### Example 7: Cooling Coffee

Coffee at 90°C is placed in room at 20°C. After 5 minutes, temperature is 70°C. When will it reach 40°C?

**Solution:**

$ \frac{dT}{dt} = -k(T - 20)$

Separate: $ \frac{dT}{T - 20} = -k \, dt$

Integrate: $\ln|T - 20| = -kt + C$

$T - 20 = Ae^{-kt}$, so $T = 20 + Ae^{-kt}$

Use $T(0) = 90$: $90 = 20 + A$, so $A = 70$

Thus $T = 20 + 70e^{-kt}$

Use $T(5) = 70$:
$$ 70 = 20 + 70e^{-5k}$$

$$ 50 = 70e^{-5k}$$

$$ e^{-5k} = \frac{5}{7}$$

$$ k = -\frac{1}{5}\ln\frac{5}{7} = \frac{1}{5}\ln\frac{7}{5}$$

Find $t$ when $T = 40$:
$$ 40 = 20 + 70e^{-kt}$$

$$ 20 = 70e^{-kt}$$

$$ e^{-kt} = \frac{2}{7}$$

$$ -kt = \ln\frac{2}{7}$$

$$ t = \frac{\ln(7/2)}{k} = \frac{\ln(7/2)}{\frac{1}{5}\ln(7/5)} = \frac{5\ln(7/2)}{\ln(7/5)}$$

$$ = \frac{5 \cdot 1.253}{0.336} \approx 18.6 \text{ minutes}$$

**Answer:** Approximately 18.6 minutes.

## Logistic Growth

Exponential growth is unrealistic long-term—resources become limited. **Logistic equation** includes carrying capacity:
$$ \frac{dP}{dt} = kP\left(1 - \frac{P}{M}\right)$$

where $M$ is carrying capacity.

**Key features**:
- Small $P$: grows approximately exponentially ($\approx kP$)
- Near $M$: growth slows (factor $1 - P/M \approx 0$)
- $P = M$: equilibrium (no growth)

### Example 8: Solving Logistic Equation

Solve $ \frac{dP}{dt} = P(1 - P)$ with $P(0) = 0.1$.

**Solution:**

Separate: $ \frac{dP}{P(1-P)} = dt$

Use partial fractions:
$$ \frac{1}{P(1-P)} = \frac{A}{P} + \frac{B}{1-P}$$

$$ 1 = A(1-P) + BP$$

Setting $P = 0$: $1 = A$

Setting $P = 1$: $1 = B$

So $ \frac{1}{P(1-P)} = \frac{1}{P} + \frac{1}{1-P}$

Integrate:
$$ \int \left(\frac{1}{P} + \frac{1}{1-P}\right) dP = \int dt$$

$$ \ln|P| - \ln|1-P| = t + C$$

$$ \ln\left|\frac{P}{1-P}\right| = t + C$$

$$ \frac{P}{1-P} = Ae^t$$

$$ P = Ae^t(1-P) = Ae^t - PAe^t$$

$$ P + PAe^t = Ae^t$$

$$ P(1 + Ae^t) = Ae^t$$

$$ P = \frac{Ae^t}{1 + Ae^t}$$

Use $P(0) = 0.1$:
$$ 0.1 = \frac{A}{1 + A}$$

$$ 0.1(1 + A) = A$$

$$ 0.1 = 0.9A$$

$$ A = \frac{1}{9}$$

**Answer:** $ P = \frac{\frac{1}{9}e^t}{1 + \frac{1}{9}e^t} = \frac{e^t}{9 + e^t} = \frac{1}{1 + 9e^{-t}}$

### Example 9: Logistic Growth Behavior

For logistic equation with $M = 1000$ and $k = 0.1$, starting at $P(0) = 50$, what is $ \lim_{t \to \infty} P(t)$?

**Solution:**

Logistic equation: $ \frac{dP}{dt} = 0.1P\left(1 - \frac{P}{1000}\right)$

For large $t$, population approaches carrying capacity.

**Answer:** $ \lim_{t \to \infty} P(t) = 1000$ (carrying capacity)

## Mixing Problems

Tank contains solution; liquid flows in and out. Find concentration over time.

**Setup**:
- Let $A(t)$ = amount of substance in tank
- Rate in = (concentration in) × (flow rate in)
- Rate out = (concentration out) × (flow rate out)
- $ \frac{dA}{dt} = \text{rate in} - \text{rate out}$

### Example 10: Mixing Problem

Tank contains 100 L of water with 20 kg salt. Pure water flows in at 5 L/min and mixture flows out at 5 L/min (volume constant). Find salt amount after 10 minutes.

**Solution:**

Let $A(t)$ = kg of salt at time $t$

Rate in = 0 (pure water)

Rate out = (concentration out) × (flow out) $ = \frac{A(t)}{100} \cdot 5 = \frac{A}{20}$ kg/min

$$ \frac{dA}{dt} = 0 - \frac{A}{20} = -\frac{A}{20}$$

This is separable:
$$ \frac{dA}{A} = -\frac{dt}{20}$$

$$ \ln|A| = -\frac{t}{20} + C$$

$$ A = Ke^{-t/20}$$

Use $A(0) = 20$: $K = 20$

$$ A(t) = 20e^{-t/20}$$

At $t = 10$:
$$ A(10) = 20e^{-10/20} = 20e^{-0.5} \approx 20(0.606) = 12.1$$

**Answer:** Approximately 12.1 kg of salt remains.

### Example 11: Variable Volume

Tank initially has 50 L water with 10 kg salt. Brine with 0.5 kg/L flows in at 3 L/min; mixture flows out at 2 L/min. Find $A(t)$.

**Solution:**

Volume: $V(t) = 50 + (3-2)t = 50 + t$

Rate in = $0.5 \cdot 3 = 1.5$ kg/min

Rate out = $ \frac{A(t)}{50+t} \cdot 2 = \frac{2A}{50+t}$ kg/min

$$ \frac{dA}{dt} = 1.5 - \frac{2A}{50+t}$$

This is linear first-order (we'll learn this method in next topic).

Rearranging: $ \frac{dA}{dt} + \frac{2A}{50+t} = 1.5$

**Answer:** This requires integrating factor method (covered in Topic 1.3).

### Example 12: Chemical Reaction

Chemical decomposes at rate proportional to amount present. If 50g reduces to 40g in 2 hours, find half-life.

**Solution:**

$ \frac{dA}{dt} = -kA$ (negative for decay)

$A = A_0 e^{-kt}$ with $A_0 = 50$

Use $A(2) = 40$:
$$ 40 = 50e^{-2k}$$

$$ 0.8 = e^{-2k}$$

$$ k = -\frac{\ln 0.8}{2} = \frac{\ln(5/4)}{2}$$

Half-life when $A = 25$:
$$ 25 = 50e^{-kt_{1/2}}$$

$$ 0.5 = e^{-kt_{1/2}}$$

$$ t_{1/2} = \frac{\ln 2}{k} = \frac{2\ln 2}{\ln(5/4)} \approx \frac{1.386}{0.223} \approx 6.2 \text{ hours}$$

**Answer:** Half-life is approximately 6.2 hours.

### Example 13: Orthogonal Trajectories

Find curves that intersect curves $xy = C$ at right angles.

**Solution:**

For family $xy = C$, differentiate implicitly:
$$ x \frac{dy}{dx} + y = 0$$

$$ \frac{dy}{dx} = -\frac{y}{x}$$

Orthogonal trajectories have slopes that are negative reciprocals:
$$ \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{x}{y}$$

Separate: $y \, dy = x \, dx$

Integrate: $ \frac{y^2}{2} = \frac{x^2}{2} + C$

$$ y^2 - x^2 = K$$

**Answer:** Orthogonal trajectories are rectangular hyperbolas $y^2 - x^2 = K$.

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: B**
$ \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{x^2 + 1}{y}$ can be separated:
$y \, dy = (x^2 + 1) \, dx$
Each side depends on one variable only.
Integrate: $ \frac{y^2}{2} = \frac{x^3}{3} + x + C$

**Problem 2: C**
$ \frac{dy}{dx} = 3y$
Separate: $ \frac{dy}{y} = 3 \, dx$
Integrate: $\ln|y| = 3x + C$
$y = e^{3x + C} = e^C \cdot e^{3x} = Ce^{3x}$
Note: $C$ can be any nonzero constant (positive or negative).

**Problem 3: C**
Half-life $t_{1/2}$ satisfies $y(t_{1/2}) = 50 = 100e^{-0.2t_{1/2}}$
$0.5 = e^{-0.2t_{1/2}}$
$\ln(0.5) = -0.2t_{1/2}$
$t_{1/2} = -\frac{\ln(0.5)}{0.2} = \frac{\ln 2}{0.2} \approx 3.47$
General formula: half-life $= \frac{\ln 2}{k}$ for decay $y = y_0 e^{-kt}$

**Problem 4: B**
Carrying capacity $M$ is the limiting population as $t \to \infty$.
When $P = M$: $ \frac{dP}{dt} = kM(1 - M/M) = 0$ (equilibrium)
Population stabilizes at $P = M$.

**Problem 5: B**
$y' = y^2$
Separate: $ \frac{dy}{y^2} = dx$
$-\frac{1}{y} = x + C$
$y = -\frac{1}{x+C}$
Use $y(0) = 1$: $1 = -\frac{1}{C}$, so $C = -1$
$y = -\frac{1}{x-1} = \frac{1}{1-x}$
