# ODE Topic 5.3: Bessel and Legendre Equations

Walk into any physics lab, and you'll encounter Bessel and Legendre functions. Bessel functions describe vibrating circular membranes (drumheads), electromagnetic waves in cylindrical cables, heat diffusion in pipes, and quantum wavefunctions in circular wells. Legendre polynomials appear in gravitational potentials, solutions to Schrödinger's equation for hydrogen, and expansions of functions on spheres. These aren't mathematical curiosities—they're the language of physics in cylindrical and spherical coordinates.

Both equations arise from **separation of variables** in partial differential equations. When you solve the heat equation in a cylinder or Laplace's equation in a sphere, one coordinate satisfies Bessel's equation, another satisfies Legendre's. The solutions form **orthogonal sets**—they're basis functions for expanding arbitrary functions, just as sine and cosine are basis functions for Fourier series.

This article explores Bessel and Legendre equations in depth. We'll derive series solutions using Frobenius method, understand the special properties that make these functions indispensable, and see applications across physics and engineering. We'll learn why some solutions are polynomials (Legendre polynomials for integer order) and others oscillate infinitely (Bessel functions). These special functions unite pure mathematics and physical intuition.

## Bessel's Equation and Solutions

**Bessel's equation of order $n$**:
$$x^2y'' + xy' + (x^2-n^2)y = 0$$

where $n \geq 0$ is a parameter (often integer).

**Solutions**: Bessel functions $J_n(x)$ and $Y_n(x)$

**General solution**: $y = c_1J_n(x) + c_2Y_n(x)$

### Example 1: Bessel Function of First Kind

The **Bessel function of first kind, order $n$** is:
$$J_n(x) = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^k}{k! \, \Gamma(n+k+1)} \left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^{2k+n}$$

For integer $n$: $\Gamma(n+k+1) = (n+k)!$

For $n = 0$:
$$J_0(x) = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^k x^{2k}}{2^{2k}(k!)^2} = 1 - \frac{x^2}{4} + \frac{x^4}{64} - \frac{x^6}{2304} + \cdots$$

**Answer:** $J_0(x)$ is even function, oscillatory, with infinitely many zeros.

### Example 2: Bessel Function Order 1

$$J_1(x) = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^k x^{2k+1}}{2^{2k+1}k!(k+1)!}$$

$$= \frac{x}{2} - \frac{x^3}{16} + \frac{x^5}{384} - \cdots$$

**Property**: $J_1'(0)$ exists (unlike $Y_1(x)$ which is singular at 0).

**Answer:** $J_1(x)$ is odd function, also oscillatory.

### Example 3: Recurrence Relations

Bessel functions satisfy:
$$J_{n-1}(x) + J_{n+1}(x) = \frac{2n}{x}J_n(x)$$

$$J_{n-1}(x) - J_{n+1}(x) = 2J_n'(x)$$

These allow computing higher orders from lower orders.

**Example**: Express $J_2(x)$ in terms of $J_0(x)$ and $J_1(x)$.

From first relation with $n = 1$:
$$J_0(x) + J_2(x) = \frac{2}{x}J_1(x)$$

$$J_2(x) = \frac{2}{x}J_1(x) - J_0(x)$$

**Answer:** $ J_2(x) = \frac{2}{x}J_1(x) - J_0(x)$

## Bessel Functions of Second Kind

**Bessel function of second kind** $Y_n(x)$:

For integer $n$:
$$Y_n(x) = \frac{J_n(x)\cos(n\pi) - J_{-n}(x)}{\sin(n\pi)}$$

(limit as parameter approaches integer)

For $n = 0$:
$$Y_0(x) = \frac{2}{\pi}\left[J_0(x)\ln x + \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^{k+1}H_k x^{2k}}{2^{2k}(k!)^2}\right]$$

where $H_k = 1 + \frac{1}{2} + \cdots + \frac{1}{k}$ (harmonic numbers).

### Example 4: Behavior Near Origin

As $x \to 0^+$:
$$J_0(x) \to 1$$
$$Y_0(x) \sim \frac{2}{\pi}\ln x \to -\infty$$

**Answer:** $Y_n(x)$ is singular at origin (unbounded), while $J_n(x)$ is bounded.

### Example 5: General Solution

For $x^2y'' + xy' + (x^2-4)y = 0$, write general solution.

**Solution:**

This is Bessel equation with $n^2 = 4$, so $n = 2$.

**General solution**: $y = c_1J_2(x) + c_2Y_2(x)$

**Answer:** Linear combination of Bessel functions of order 2.

## Modified Bessel Functions

**Modified Bessel equation**:
$$x^2y'' + xy' - (x^2+n^2)y = 0$$

(Sign change from standard Bessel)

**Solutions**: $I_n(x)$ and $K_n(x)$

### Example 6: Modified Bessel of First Kind

$$I_n(x) = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{1}{k! \, \Gamma(n+k+1)} \left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^{2k+n}$$

(No alternating signs—all positive)

For $n = 0$:
$$I_0(x) = 1 + \frac{x^2}{4} + \frac{x^4}{64} + \frac{x^6}{2304} + \cdots$$

**Relation to $J_n$**: $I_n(x) = i^{-n}J_n(ix)$ where $i = \sqrt{-1}$

**Behavior**: Exponentially growing as $x \to \infty$

**Answer:** $I_n(x)$ grows without bound (no oscillation).

### Example 7: Modified Bessel of Second Kind

$K_n(x)$ is second solution, exponentially decaying:

$$K_0(x) \sim -\ln x \text{ as } x \to 0^+$$

$$K_0(x) \sim \sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2x}}e^{-x} \text{ as } x \to \infty$$

**Answer:** $K_n(x)$ is useful for bounded solutions at infinity.

## Applications of Bessel Functions

### Example 8: Vibrating Circular Membrane

Drumhead displacement $u(r,t)$ satisfies wave equation in polar coordinates.

Separation of variables: $u(r,t) = R(r)\Theta(\theta)T(t)$

Radial equation:
$$r^2R'' + rR' + (\lambda^2 r^2 - n^2)R = 0$$

Let $x = \lambda r$:
$$x^2\frac{d^2R}{dx^2} + x\frac{dR}{dx} + (x^2 - n^2)R = 0$$

**Bessel's equation!**

Bounded solution: $R = J_n(\lambda r)$

**Boundary condition**: $R(a) = 0$ (fixed edge at radius $a$)

Requires $J_n(\lambda a) = 0$, so $\lambda a$ is a zero of $J_n$.

**Answer:** Vibration frequencies determined by zeros of Bessel functions.

### Example 9: Heat Flow in Cylinder

Temperature in cylinder of radius $a$ with boundary condition $u(a,t) = 0$.

After separation: radial equation is Bessel.

Solution: $u(r,t) = J_0(\alpha r)e^{-\alpha^2 kt}$

where $\alpha$ satisfies $J_0(\alpha a) = 0$.

**Answer:** Bessel functions describe temperature distribution.

### Example 10: Zeros of Bessel Functions

$J_0(x)$ has infinitely many positive zeros:
$$\alpha_1 \approx 2.405, \quad \alpha_2 \approx 5.520, \quad \alpha_3 \approx 8.654, \ldots$$

These appear in eigenvalue problems with circular geometry.

**Answer:** Zeros are not equally spaced (unlike $\sin x$).

## Legendre's Equation

**Legendre's equation**:
$$(1-x^2)y'' - 2xy' + n(n+1)y = 0$$

where $n \geq 0$ is parameter.

For integer $n$, one solution is **Legendre polynomial** $P_n(x)$.

### Example 11: Legendre Polynomials

First few Legendre polynomials:
$$P_0(x) = 1$$
$$P_1(x) = x$$
$$P_2(x) = \frac{1}{2}(3x^2-1)$$
$$P_3(x) = \frac{1}{2}(5x^3-3x)$$
$$P_4(x) = \frac{1}{8}(35x^4-30x^2+3)$$

**Rodrigues' formula**:
$$P_n(x) = \frac{1}{2^n n!}\frac{d^n}{dx^n}(x^2-1)^n$$

**Answer:** Legendre polynomials are orthogonal on $[-1,1]$.

### Example 12: Generating Function

$$\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-2xt+t^2}} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} P_n(x)t^n$$

This generates all Legendre polynomials.

**Answer:** Useful for deriving properties.

### Example 13: Orthogonality

$$\int_{-1}^{1} P_m(x)P_n(x) \, dx = \begin{cases} 0 & m \neq n \\ \frac{2}{2n+1} & m = n \end{cases}$$

This orthogonality allows expanding functions:
$$f(x) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} c_nP_n(x)$$

where $ c_n = \frac{2n+1}{2}\int_{-1}^{1} f(x)P_n(x) \, dx$

**Answer:** Legendre series are like Fourier series for $[-1,1]$.

## Applications of Legendre Functions

### Example 14: Gravitational Potential

Potential at distance $r$ from center of sphere (in spherical coordinates):

$$\Phi(r,\theta) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \left(A_nr^n + \frac{B_n}{r^{n+1}}\right)P_n(\cos\theta)$$

Legendre polynomials separate angular dependence.

**Answer:** Essential for multi-pole expansions in physics.

### Example 15: Schrödinger Equation

Hydrogen atom wavefunction in spherical coordinates has angular part:
$$Y_{\ell}^m(\theta,\phi) = P_{\ell}^m(\cos\theta)e^{im\phi}$$

where $P_{\ell}^m$ are **associated Legendre functions**.

These satisfy:
$$(1-x^2)y'' - 2xy' + \left[\ell(\ell+1) - \frac{m^2}{1-x^2}\right]y = 0$$

**Answer:** Quantum mechanics relies on Legendre functions for angular momentum.

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: B**
Standard form of Bessel's equation:
$x^2y'' + xy' + (x^2-n^2)y = 0$
Regular singular point at $x = 0$
Parameter $n$ is order
For $n = 0$: $x^2y'' + xy' + x^2y = 0$

**Problem 2: B**
From series: $ J_0(x) = 1 - \frac{x^2}{4} + \frac{x^4}{64} - \cdots$
$J_0(0) = 1 - 0 + 0 - \cdots = 1$
Bessel functions of first kind are bounded at origin
Unlike $Y_n(x)$ which diverges

**Problem 3: C**
Legendre polynomials are orthogonal on $[-1, 1]$
Natural domain for orthogonality relation
Arise from Laplace equation on sphere
$x = \cos\theta$ where $\theta \in [0, \pi]$

**Problem 4: B**
For integer $n$, one solution to Legendre equation is polynomial
$P_n(x)$ has degree exactly $n$
Series terminates after finite terms
$P_2(x) = \frac{1}{2}(3x^2-1)$ is degree 2

**Problem 5: A**
Modified: $x^2y'' + xy' - (x^2+n^2)y = 0$ (minus sign)
Standard: $x^2y'' + xy' + (x^2-n^2)y = 0$ (plus sign)
Changes oscillatory behavior to exponential
$I_n(x)$ grows, $J_n(x)$ oscillates
Same domain, same singular point
