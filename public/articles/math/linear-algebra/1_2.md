 # Linear Algebra Topic 1.2: Row Reduction and Echelon Forms

 You're staring at a system of four equations in five variables. Maybe you could use substitution—eliminate one variable, then another, then another. But which equation should you start with? Which variable should you eliminate first? One wrong choice and you're drowning in fractions and algebraic chaos.

 There's a better way. The **row reduction algorithm** provides a systematic, foolproof method for solving any linear system. No guessing, no lucky choices—just a clear sequence of operations that always works. Better yet, the algorithm reveals the structure of solutions: whether they exist, whether they're unique, and if not, how to express the infinite solution set.

 This algorithmic approach does more than solve systems. It's the foundation for nearly everything in linear algebra. Understanding row reduction means understanding how to find bases, compute inverses, determine linear independence, and analyze linear transformations. Master this topic, and you've mastered the most fundamental computational tool in the entire course.

 ## Elementary Row Operations

 We can manipulate the rows of an augmented matrix without changing the solution set using three **elementary row operations**:

 1. **Row swap**: Exchange two rows (notation: $R_i \leftrightarrow R_j$)
 2. **Row scaling**: Multiply a row by a nonzero constant (notation: $cR_i \to R_i$)
 3. **Row addition**: Add a multiple of one row to another (notation: $R_i + cR_j \to R_i$)

 These operations preserve solutions because they're just performing the same algebraic manipulations we'd do to the equations themselves.

 ### Example 1: Elementary Row Operations

 Perform the indicated operations on $ \left[\begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & 2 & -1 & 4 \\ 2 & -1 & 3 & 1 \\ -1 & 3 & 2 & 5 \end{array}\right]$:

 a) $R_1 \leftrightarrow R_2$
 b) $3R_3 \to R_3$
 c) $R_2 - 2R_1 \to R_2$

 **Solution:**

 a) Swap rows 1 and 2:
 $$ \left[\begin{array}{ccc|c} 2 & -1 & 3 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 & -1 & 4 \\ -1 & 3 & 2 & 5 \end{array}\right]$$

 b) Starting from the original matrix, multiply row 3 by 3:
 $$ \left[\begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & 2 & -1 & 4 \\ 2 & -1 & 3 & 1 \\ -3 & 9 & 6 & 15 \end{array}\right]$$

 c) Starting from the original matrix, replace row 2 with row 2 minus twice row 1:
 New row 2: $(2, -1, 3, 1) - 2(1, 2, -1, 4) = (0, -5, 5, -7)$

 **Answer:** $ \left[\begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & 2 & -1 & 4 \\ 0 & -5 & 5 & -7 \\ -1 & 3 & 2 & 5 \end{array}\right]$

 ## Row Echelon Form (REF)

 A matrix is in **row echelon form** if:

 1. All nonzero rows are above any rows of all zeros
 2. The **leading entry** (first nonzero number from the left, also called a **pivot**) of each nonzero row is to the right of the leading entry of the row above it
 3. All entries below a leading entry are zero

 **Example of REF:**
 $$ \left[\begin{array}{cccc|c} 2 & -1 & 3 & 1 & 5 \\ 0 & 3 & -2 & 4 & 2 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 5 & -1 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \end{array}\right]$$

 The pivots are 2, 3, and 5 (in positions (1,1), (2,2), and (3,4)).

 **Reduced Row Echelon Form (RREF)** adds two more requirements:

 4. Each leading entry is 1
 5. Each leading entry is the only nonzero entry in its column

 **Example of RREF:**
 $$ \left[\begin{array}{cccc|c} 1 & 0 & 2 & 0 & 3 \\ 0 & 1 & -1 & 0 & 4 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 & -2 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \end{array}\right]$$

 **Key fact**: Every matrix has a unique RREF, but many possible REFs.

 ### Example 2: Identifying Echelon Forms

 Determine whether each matrix is in REF, RREF, both, or neither:

 a) $ \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 0 & 1 & 4 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$

 b) $ \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$

 c) $ \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 2 \end{bmatrix}$

 **Solution:**

 a) REF but not RREF. Pivots (all 1's) are in correct staircase pattern, zeros below pivots. However, columns 2 and 3 have nonzero entries above their pivots (the 2, 3, 4), violating RREF requirement.

 b) Both REF and RREF. This is the $3 \times 3$ identity matrix—all pivots are 1, in staircase pattern, and each is the only nonzero entry in its column.

 c) Neither. The second row is all zeros but appears before the third nonzero row, violating REF requirement 1.

 **Answer:** (a) REF only, (b) Both, (c) Neither

 ## The Row Reduction Algorithm

 Here's the systematic procedure to reduce any matrix to REF, then to RREF:

 **Forward phase (to REF):**

 1. Start with the leftmost nonzero column (pivot column)
 2. If necessary, swap rows to get a nonzero entry at the top of this column
 3. Use row operations to create zeros below this pivot
 4. Move to the next row and next pivot column; repeat until in REF

 **Backward phase (REF to RREF):**

 5. Starting from the rightmost pivot, scale each pivot row so the pivot becomes 1
 6. Use row operations to create zeros above each pivot
 7. Move leftward through pivots until complete

 Let's see this in action.

 ### Example 3: Row Reduction to REF

 Reduce to row echelon form:
 $$ \left[\begin{array}{ccc|c} 0 & 3 & -6 & 6 \\ 3 & -9 & 12 & -9 \\ -2 & 6 & -8 & 6 \end{array}\right]$$

 **Solution:**

 The leftmost nonzero column is column 1. We need a nonzero entry in position (1,1):

 $R_1 \leftrightarrow R_2$:
 $$ \left[\begin{array}{ccc|c} 3 & -9 & 12 & -9 \\ 0 & 3 & -6 & 6 \\ -2 & 6 & -8 & 6 \end{array}\right]$$

 Create zero in position (3,1):

 $R_3 + \frac{2}{3}R_1 \to R_3$:
 New row 3: $(-2, 6, -8, 6) + \frac{2}{3}(3, -9, 12, -9) = (-2, 6, -8, 6) + (2, -6, 8, -6) = (0, 0, 0, 0)$

 $$ \left[\begin{array}{ccc|c} 3 & -9 & 12 & -9 \\ 0 & 3 & -6 & 6 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \end{array}\right]$$

 This is in REF.

 **Answer:** $ \left[\begin{array}{ccc|c} 3 & -9 & 12 & -9 \\ 0 & 3 & -6 & 6 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \end{array}\right]$
