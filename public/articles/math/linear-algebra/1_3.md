 # Linear Algebra Topic 1.3: Matrix Operations

 Matrices are more than just rectangular arrays of numbers—they're computational objects we can add, multiply, and manipulate. A spreadsheet of sales data, a rotation transformation in computer graphics, a network of connections in social media—all are represented by matrices. Understanding how matrices combine and interact is essential for everything from solving systems to analyzing data to modeling physical systems.

 But matrix operations have quirks. Unlike ordinary arithmetic, matrix multiplication isn't commutative: $AB$ generally doesn't equal $BA$. Matrices can fail to have multiplicative inverses. And matrix-vector multiplication connects directly to linear transformations, giving geometric meaning to algebraic operations.

 In this topic, we'll build computational fluency with matrix arithmetic while developing geometric intuition. We'll see how matrix multiplication encodes composition of transformations, how transposes relate to dot products, and how special matrices like diagonal and identity matrices simplify calculations. These operations will be our computational workhorse throughout linear algebra.

 ## Matrix Addition and Scalar Multiplication

 Two matrices of the same size can be added entry-by-entry. Similarly, multiplying a matrix by a scalar multiplies every entry by that scalar.

 For $m \times n$ matrices $A$ and $B$, and scalar $c$:

 **Matrix Addition:**
 $$ (A + B)_{ij} = a_{ij} + b_{ij}$$

 **Scalar Multiplication:**
 $$ (cA)_{ij} = ca_{ij}$$

 These operations satisfy the familiar algebraic properties:
 - Commutativity: $A + B = B + A$
 - Associativity: $(A + B) + C = A + (B + C)$
 - Distributivity: $c(A + B) = cA + cB$ and $(c + d)A = cA + dA$
 - Zero matrix: $A + O = A$ (where $O$ is the matrix of all zeros)
 - Additive inverse: $A + (-A) = O$

 ### Example 1: Basic Matrix Arithmetic

 Compute $3A - 2B$ where:
 $$ A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -2 \\ 3 & 0 \end{bmatrix}, \quad B = \begin{bmatrix} 4 & 1 \\ -1 & 2 \end{bmatrix}$$

 **Solution:**

 $$ 3A = 3\begin{bmatrix} 1 & -2 \\ 3 & 0 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & -6 \\ 9 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$$

 $$ 2B = 2\begin{bmatrix} 4 & 1 \\ -1 & 2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 8 & 2 \\ -2 & 4 \end{bmatrix}$$

 $$ 3A - 2B = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & -6 \\ 9 & 0 \end{bmatrix} - \begin{bmatrix} 8 & 2 \\ -2 & 4 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -5 & -8 \\ 11 & -4 \end{bmatrix}$$

 **Answer:** $ \begin{bmatrix} -5 & -8 \\ 11 & -4 \end{bmatrix}$
