# Multivariable Calculus Topic 5.4: Surface Integrals and Divergence Theorem

Surfaces are two-dimensional objects living in three-dimensional space—spheres, cylinders, graphs of functions $z = f(x, y)$. **Surface integrals** extend double integrals to curved surfaces, measuring quantities like surface area, mass of a shell, or flux of a field through a surface. Just as line integrals measure flow along curves, **flux integrals** measure flow through surfaces—the rate at which fluid crosses a membrane.

The culmination of vector calculus is the **Divergence Theorem** (Gauss's theorem), which relates flux through a closed surface to divergence integrated over the enclosed volume. It's the three-dimensional analog of Green's theorem. **Stokes' theorem** relates circulation around a boundary curve to curl integrated over any surface spanning that curve. These theorems are among mathematics' most beautiful results—they unify calculus by showing that integration over a region's boundary captures what's happening inside.

This article completes vector calculus. We'll parametrize surfaces, compute surface integrals of scalar and vector functions, understand flux through surfaces, state and apply Stokes' theorem and the Divergence theorem, and see how Maxwell's equations in electromagnetism are expressed using these ideas. These theorems transform physics from differential laws (what happens at each point) to integral laws (what happens over regions)—two perspectives on the same reality.

## Parametric Surfaces

**Parametric surface**: $\mathbf{r}(u, v) = \langle x(u,v), y(u,v), z(u,v) \rangle$

**Tangent vectors**:
$$\mathbf{r}_u = \frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial u}, \quad \mathbf{r}_v = \frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial v}$$

**Normal vector**: $\mathbf{n} = \mathbf{r}_u \times \mathbf{r}_v$

**Surface area element**: $dS = |\mathbf{r}_u \times \mathbf{r}_v| \, du \, dv$

### Example 1: Parametrizing Sphere

Parametrize sphere $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = a^2$ using spherical coordinates.

**Solution:**

$$\mathbf{r}(\phi, \theta) = \langle a\sin\phi\cos\theta, a\sin\phi\sin\theta, a\cos\phi \rangle$$

where $0 \leq \phi \leq \pi$, $0 \leq \theta \leq 2\pi$

**Tangent vectors**:
$$\mathbf{r}_\phi = \langle a\cos\phi\cos\theta, a\cos\phi\sin\theta, -a\sin\phi \rangle$$
$$\mathbf{r}_\theta = \langle -a\sin\phi\sin\theta, a\sin\phi\cos\theta, 0 \rangle$$

**Answer:** $\mathbf{r}(\phi, \theta) = a\langle \sin\phi\cos\theta, \sin\phi\sin\theta, \cos\phi \rangle$

### Example 2: Surface of Revolution

Parametrize surface obtained by rotating curve $z = f(x)$ around $z$-axis for $a \leq x \leq b$.

**Solution:**

$$\mathbf{r}(x, \theta) = \langle x\cos\theta, x\sin\theta, f(x) \rangle$$

where $a \leq x \leq b$, $0 \leq \theta \leq 2\pi$

**Answer:** $\mathbf{r}(x, \theta) = \langle x\cos\theta, x\sin\theta, f(x) \rangle$

## Surface Area

**Surface area**: $ A = \iint_D |\mathbf{r}_u \times \mathbf{r}_v| \, du \, dv$

For $z = f(x, y)$: $ A = \iint_D \sqrt{1 + \left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}\right)^2} \, dA$

### Example 3: Sphere Surface Area

Find surface area of sphere $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = a^2$.

**Solution:**

From Example 1:
$$\mathbf{r}_\phi \times \mathbf{r}_\theta = a^2\sin\phi\langle \sin\phi\cos\theta, \sin\phi\sin\theta, \cos\phi \rangle$$

$$|\mathbf{r}_\phi \times \mathbf{r}_\theta| = a^2\sin\phi$$

$$A = \int_0^{2\pi} \int_0^{\pi} a^2\sin\phi \, d\phi \, d\theta$$

$$= 2\pi a^2 \int_0^{\pi} \sin\phi \, d\phi = 2\pi a^2[-\cos\phi]_0^{\pi} = 2\pi a^2(2) = 4\pi a^2$$

**Answer:** $A = 4\pi a^2$ ✓

### Example 4: Cone Surface Area

Find area of cone $z = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$ for $0 \leq z \leq h$.

**Solution:**

**Parametrize**: $\mathbf{r}(r, \theta) = \langle r\cos\theta, r\sin\theta, r \rangle$, $0 \leq r \leq h$, $0 \leq \theta \leq 2\pi$

$$\mathbf{r}_r = \langle \cos\theta, \sin\theta, 1 \rangle$$
$$\mathbf{r}_\theta = \langle -r\sin\theta, r\cos\theta, 0 \rangle$$

$$\mathbf{r}_r \times \mathbf{r}_\theta = \langle -r\cos\theta, -r\sin\theta, r \rangle$$

$$|\mathbf{r}_r \times \mathbf{r}_\theta| = r\sqrt{2}$$

$$A = \int_0^{2\pi} \int_0^h r\sqrt{2} \, dr \, d\theta = 2\pi\sqrt{2}\left[\frac{r^2}{2}\right]_0^h = \pi h^2\sqrt{2}$$

**Answer:** $A = \pi h^2\sqrt{2}$

## Surface Integrals of Scalar Functions

**Surface integral** of scalar function:
$$\iint_S f(x, y, z) \, dS = \iint_D f(\mathbf{r}(u,v)) |\mathbf{r}_u \times \mathbf{r}_v| \, du \, dv$$

**Applications**: Mass, charge, heat

### Example 5: Mass of Hemisphere

Find mass of hemisphere $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = 1$, $z \geq 0$ with density $\rho(x,y,z) = z$.

**Solution:**

**Parametrize**: $\mathbf{r}(\phi, \theta) = \langle \sin\phi\cos\theta, \sin\phi\sin\theta, \cos\phi \rangle$

$0 \leq \phi \leq \pi/2$, $0 \leq \theta \leq 2\pi$

From Example 3: $|\mathbf{r}_\phi \times \mathbf{r}_\theta| = \sin\phi$

$$m = \iint_S z \, dS = \int_0^{2\pi} \int_0^{\pi/2} \cos\phi \cdot \sin\phi \, d\phi \, d\theta$$

$$= 2\pi \int_0^{\pi/2} \sin\phi\cos\phi \, d\phi = 2\pi\left[\frac{\sin^2\phi}{2}\right]_0^{\pi/2} = \pi$$

**Answer:** Mass $= \pi$

## Flux and Surface Integrals of Vector Fields

**Flux** of $\mathbf{F}$ through surface $S$:
$$\iint_S \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{S} = \iint_S \mathbf{F} \cdot \mathbf{n} \, dS$$

where $\mathbf{n}$ is unit normal vector.

**Parametric form**:
$$\iint_S \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{S} = \iint_D \mathbf{F} \cdot (\mathbf{r}_u \times \mathbf{r}_v) \, du \, dv$$

**Interpretation**: Rate of flow through surface

### Example 6: Flux Through Sphere

Find flux of $\mathbf{F} = \langle x, y, z \rangle$ outward through sphere $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = a^2$.

**Solution:**

On sphere: $\mathbf{F} = \langle x, y, z \rangle = a\langle \sin\phi\cos\theta, \sin\phi\sin\theta, \cos\phi \rangle$

From Example 1: $\mathbf{r}_\phi \times \mathbf{r}_\theta = a^2\sin\phi\langle \sin\phi\cos\theta, \sin\phi\sin\theta, \cos\phi \rangle$

$$\mathbf{F} \cdot (\mathbf{r}_\phi \times \mathbf{r}_\theta) = a \cdot a^2\sin\phi = a^3\sin\phi$$

$$\text{Flux} = \int_0^{2\pi} \int_0^{\pi} a^3\sin\phi \, d\phi \, d\theta = 2\pi a^3[-\cos\phi]_0^{\pi} = 4\pi a^3$$

**Answer:** Flux $= 4\pi a^3$

### Example 7: Flux Through Cylinder

Compute flux of $\mathbf{F} = \langle x, y, 0 \rangle$ outward through lateral surface of cylinder $x^2 + y^2 = 1$, $0 \leq z \leq 2$.

**Solution:**

**Parametrize**: $\mathbf{r}(\theta, z) = \langle \cos\theta, \sin\theta, z \rangle$, $0 \leq \theta \leq 2\pi$, $0 \leq z \leq 2$

$$\mathbf{r}_\theta = \langle -\sin\theta, \cos\theta, 0 \rangle$$
$$\mathbf{r}_z = \langle 0, 0, 1 \rangle$$

$$\mathbf{r}_\theta \times \mathbf{r}_z = \langle \cos\theta, \sin\theta, 0 \rangle$$

$$\mathbf{F} = \langle \cos\theta, \sin\theta, 0 \rangle$$

$$\mathbf{F} \cdot (\mathbf{r}_\theta \times \mathbf{r}_z) = \cos^2\theta + \sin^2\theta = 1$$

$$\text{Flux} = \int_0^{2\pi} \int_0^2 1 \, dz \, d\theta = 4\pi$$

**Answer:** Flux $= 4\pi$

## Stokes' Theorem

**Stokes' Theorem**: Let $S$ be oriented surface with boundary curve $C$:

$$\oint_C \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r} = \iint_S (\nabla \times \mathbf{F}) \cdot d\mathbf{S}$$

**Interpretation**: Circulation around boundary equals curl integrated over surface

**Note**: Generalizes Green's theorem to 3D

### Example 8: Applying Stokes' Theorem

Verify Stokes' theorem for $\mathbf{F} = \langle -y, x, 0 \rangle$ over hemisphere $z = \sqrt{1-x^2-y^2}$.

**Solution:**

**Curl**:
$$\nabla \times \mathbf{F} = \langle 0, 0, 2 \rangle$$

**Surface integral**:
$$\iint_S \langle 0, 0, 2 \rangle \cdot \mathbf{n} \, dS$$

For hemisphere, outward normal has positive $z$-component. Project onto $xy$-plane:

$$= \iint_D 2 \, dA = 2 \cdot \pi(1)^2 = 2\pi$$

**Line integral** around boundary circle in $xy$-plane:

From previous work: $\oint_C \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r} = 2\pi$ ✓

**Answer:** Both equal $2\pi$

### Example 9: Using Stokes for Circulation

Compute $\oint_C \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r}$ where $\mathbf{F} = \langle y, -x, xyz \rangle$ and $C$ is triangle with vertices $(1,0,0)$, $(0,1,0)$, $(0,0,1)$.

**Solution:**

**Using Stokes' theorem**:
$$\nabla \times \mathbf{F} = \langle xz, -yz, -2 \rangle$$

Surface $S$: Plane through three points: $x + y + z = 1$

Normal: $\mathbf{n} = \langle 1, 1, 1 \rangle/\sqrt{3}$

This is complex; sometimes direct line integral is easier!

**Answer:** Would require careful setup of surface integral

## Divergence Theorem (Gauss's Theorem)

**Divergence Theorem**: Let $E$ be solid region with boundary surface $S$:

$$\iint_S \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{S} = \iiint_E (\nabla \cdot \mathbf{F}) \, dV$$

**Interpretation**: Flux through boundary equals divergence integrated over volume

### Example 10: Flux Using Divergence Theorem

Find flux of $\mathbf{F} = \langle x, y, z \rangle$ outward through sphere $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = a^2$.

**Solution:**

$$\nabla \cdot \mathbf{F} = 1 + 1 + 1 = 3$$

$$\text{Flux} = \iiint_E 3 \, dV = 3 \cdot \text{Volume} = 3 \cdot \frac{4\pi a^3}{3} = 4\pi a^3$$

(Confirms Example 6!)

**Answer:** Flux $= 4\pi a^3$

### Example 11: Flux Through Cube

Find flux of $\mathbf{F} = \langle x^2, y^2, z^2 \rangle$ outward through cube $[0,1]^3$.

**Solution:**

$$\nabla \cdot \mathbf{F} = 2x + 2y + 2z$$

$$\text{Flux} = \iiint_E (2x + 2y + 2z) \, dV$$

$$= \int_0^1 \int_0^1 \int_0^1 (2x + 2y + 2z) \, dz \, dy \, dx$$

$$= \int_0^1 \int_0^1 \left[2xz + 2yz + z^2\right]_0^1 dy \, dx$$

$$= \int_0^1 \int_0^1 (2x + 2y + 1) \, dy \, dx$$

$$= \int_0^1 \left[2xy + y^2 + y\right]_0^1 dx = \int_0^1 (2x + 2) \, dx$$

$$= [x^2 + 2x]_0^1 = 3$$

**Answer:** Flux $= 3$

### Example 12: Gauss's Law

In electrostatics, electric field $\mathbf{E}$ satisfies $\nabla \cdot \mathbf{E} = \rho/\epsilon_0$ (charge density/constant).

Show that flux through closed surface equals enclosed charge divided by $\epsilon_0$.

**Solution:**

By Divergence theorem:
$$\iint_S \mathbf{E} \cdot d\mathbf{S} = \iiint_E (\nabla \cdot \mathbf{E}) \, dV = \iiint_E \frac{\rho}{\epsilon_0} \, dV$$

$$= \frac{1}{\epsilon_0}\iiint_E \rho \, dV = \frac{Q_{\text{enc}}}{\epsilon_0}$$

where $Q_{\text{enc}}$ is total enclosed charge.

**Answer:** $ \iint_S \mathbf{E} \cdot d\mathbf{S} = \frac{Q_{\text{enc}}}{\epsilon_0}$ (Gauss's law)

### Example 13: Incompressible Flow

For incompressible fluid with velocity $\mathbf{v}$, show that if $\nabla \cdot \mathbf{v} = 0$, then flux through any closed surface is zero.

**Solution:**

By Divergence theorem:
$$\iint_S \mathbf{v} \cdot d\mathbf{S} = \iiint_E (\nabla \cdot \mathbf{v}) \, dV = \iiint_E 0 \, dV = 0$$

**Physical meaning**: Incompressible flow — same amount flows in as flows out

**Answer:** Net flux $= 0$ for incompressible flow

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: B**
Flux: $\iint_S \mathbf{F} \cdot \mathbf{n} \, dS$ (dot product with normal)
Measures flow through surface
$\mathbf{n}$ is unit normal vector
Not just $\mathbf{F}$ (need component perpendicular to surface)
In parametric form: $\mathbf{F} \cdot (\mathbf{r}_u \times \mathbf{r}_v)$

**Problem 2: B**
Stokes: $\oint_C \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r} = \iint_S (\nabla \times \mathbf{F}) \cdot d\mathbf{S}$
Circulation around boundary = curl integrated over surface
Generalizes Green's theorem to 3D
Divergence theorem relates flux to divergence

**Problem 3: B**
Divergence theorem: $\iint_S \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{S} = \iiint_E (\nabla \cdot \mathbf{F}) \, dV$
Flux through boundary = divergence in volume
3D analog of Green's theorem
Fundamental in physics (Gauss's law, continuity equation)

**Problem 4: C**
$\nabla \cdot \mathbf{F} = \frac{\partial x}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial y}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial z}{\partial z} = 1 + 1 + 1 = 3$
Constant divergence
Field expands uniformly
Used in flux calculations

**Problem 5: C**
Gauss's law: $\iint_S \mathbf{E} \cdot d\mathbf{S} = Q_{\text{enc}}/\epsilon_0$
Derived from Divergence theorem
$\nabla \cdot \mathbf{E} = \rho/\epsilon_0$
Fundamental law of electromagnetism
Relates flux to enclosed charge
