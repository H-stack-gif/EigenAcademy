# Multivariable Calculus Topic 5.3: Green's Theorem and Curl and Divergence

A double integral over a region can equal a line integral around its boundary—this remarkable fact is **Green's theorem**, one of the fundamental theorems of vector calculus. It connects the circulation of a vector field around a closed curve to the "microscopic rotation" inside the region. Green's theorem is the two-dimensional version of more general theorems (Stokes' and Divergence theorems) that relate integrals over regions to integrals over their boundaries.

Two new operations on vector fields capture important physical concepts: **curl** measures rotation (how much the field swirls), and **divergence** measures expansion (whether the field flows outward or inward). A whirlpool has high curl. A source (like water flowing from a pipe) has positive divergence; a sink has negative divergence. These concepts pervade physics—curl of electric field relates to changing magnetic field (Faraday's law), divergence of electric field relates to charge density (Gauss's law).

This article develops Green's theorem, curl, and divergence. We'll state and prove Green's theorem, use it to compute areas and line integrals, define curl and divergence, interpret them physically, and understand their relationship to conservative fields (zero curl) and incompressible flow (zero divergence). These tools transform vector calculus from computation to geometric and physical insight.

## Green's Theorem

**Green's Theorem**: Let $C$ be positively oriented (counterclockwise), simple closed curve enclosing region $D$. If $\mathbf{F} = \langle P, Q \rangle$ has continuous partials:

$$\oint_C P \, dx + Q \, dy = \iint_D \left(\frac{\partial Q}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial P}{\partial y}\right) dA$$

**Alternative form**:
$$\oint_C \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r} = \iint_D \left(\frac{\partial Q}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial P}{\partial y}\right) dA$$

**Interpretation**: Circulation around boundary equals "microscopic rotation" integrated over region

### Example 1: Verifying Green's Theorem

Verify Green's theorem for $\mathbf{F} = \langle x^2, xy \rangle$ and $C$ is unit circle.

**Solution:**

**Line integral**:
Parametrize: $\mathbf{r}(t) = \langle \cos t, \sin t \rangle$, $0 \leq t \leq 2\pi$

$$\oint_C x^2 \, dx + xy \, dy = \int_0^{2\pi} [\cos^2 t(-\sin t) + \cos t \sin t(\cos t)] \, dt$$

$$= \int_0^{2\pi} (-\cos^2 t \sin t + \cos^2 t \sin t) \, dt = 0$$

**Double integral**:
$$P = x^2, \quad Q = xy$$
$$\frac{\partial Q}{\partial x} = y, \quad \frac{\partial P}{\partial y} = 0$$

$$\iint_D (y - 0) \, dA = \int_0^{2\pi} \int_0^1 (r\sin\theta) \cdot r \, dr \, d\theta$$

$$= \int_0^{2\pi} \sin\theta \left[\frac{r^3}{3}\right]_0^1 d\theta = \frac{1}{3}\int_0^{2\pi} \sin\theta \, d\theta = 0$$

Both equal $0$ ✓

**Answer:** Verified

### Example 2: Computing with Green's Theorem

Evaluate $\oint_C (y^2 \, dx + xy \, dy)$ where $C$ is triangle with vertices $(0,0)$, $(1,0)$, $(0,1)$.

**Solution:**

**Using Green's theorem**:
$$P = y^2, \quad Q = xy$$
$$\frac{\partial Q}{\partial x} = y, \quad \frac{\partial P}{\partial y} = 2y$$

$$\oint_C = \iint_D (y - 2y) \, dA = \iint_D (-y) \, dA$$

Triangle: $0 \leq x \leq 1$, $0 \leq y \leq 1-x$

$$= \int_0^1 \int_0^{1-x} (-y) \, dy \, dx = \int_0^1 \left[-\frac{y^2}{2}\right]_0^{1-x} dx$$

$$= \int_0^1 -\frac{(1-x)^2}{2} \, dx = -\frac{1}{2}\left[-\frac{(1-x)^3}{3}\right]_0^1 = -\frac{1}{6}$$

**Answer:** $- \frac{1}{6}$

### Example 3: Area Using Green's Theorem

**Area formula**: $ A = \frac{1}{2}\oint_C (x \, dy - y \, dx)$

Find area enclosed by ellipse $ \frac{x^2}{a^2} + \frac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$.

**Solution:**

Parametrize: $x = a\cos t$, $y = b\sin t$, $0 \leq t \leq 2\pi$

$$dx = -a\sin t \, dt, \quad dy = b\cos t \, dt$$

$$A = \frac{1}{2}\oint_C (x \, dy - y \, dx)$$

$$= \frac{1}{2}\int_0^{2\pi} [a\cos t \cdot b\cos t - b\sin t \cdot (-a\sin t)] \, dt$$

$$= \frac{1}{2}\int_0^{2\pi} (ab\cos^2 t + ab\sin^2 t) \, dt = \frac{ab}{2}\int_0^{2\pi} 1 \, dt = \pi ab$$

**Answer:** Area $= \pi ab$ ✓

## Curl in 2D

**Scalar curl** in 2D: $ \text{curl } \mathbf{F} = \frac{\partial Q}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial P}{\partial y}$

**Interpretation**: Measures "microscopic rotation" of field at a point

**Green's theorem**: $ \oint_C \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r} = \iint_D \text{curl } \mathbf{F} \, dA$

### Example 4: Computing Curl

Find curl of $\mathbf{F}(x, y) = \langle -y, x \rangle$.

**Solution:**

$$P = -y, \quad Q = x$$

$$\text{curl } \mathbf{F} = \frac{\partial Q}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial P}{\partial y} = 1 - (-1) = 2$$

**Interpretation**: Constant curl of $2$ — field rotates uniformly

**Answer:** Curl $= 2$

### Example 5: Zero Curl

Show that conservative field has zero curl.

**Solution:**

If $\mathbf{F} = \nabla f = \langle f_x, f_y \rangle$:

$$\text{curl } \mathbf{F} = \frac{\partial f_y}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial f_x}{\partial y} = f_{yx} - f_{xy}$$

By Clairaut's theorem: $f_{xy} = f_{yx}$

Therefore: curl $= 0$

**Answer:** Conservative fields have zero curl (converse true on simply connected domains)

## Divergence

**Divergence** of $\mathbf{F} = \langle P, Q \rangle$:

$$\text{div } \mathbf{F} = \nabla \cdot \mathbf{F} = \frac{\partial P}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial Q}{\partial y}$$

**Interpretation**: Measures "source/sink strength" — rate of expansion

**Flux form of Green's theorem**: $ \oint_C \mathbf{F} \cdot \mathbf{n} \, ds = \iint_D \text{div } \mathbf{F} \, dA$

where $\mathbf{n}$ is outward normal to $C$

### Example 6: Computing Divergence

Find divergence of $\mathbf{F}(x, y) = \langle x^2, y^2 \rangle$.

**Solution:**

$$\text{div } \mathbf{F} = \frac{\partial}{\partial x}(x^2) + \frac{\partial}{\partial y}(y^2) = 2x + 2y$$

**Interpretation**: Divergence varies with position — positive everywhere in first quadrant (expansion)

**Answer:** $\text{div } \mathbf{F} = 2x + 2y$

### Example 7: Incompressible Flow

A vector field $\mathbf{F}$ represents fluid velocity. If $\text{div } \mathbf{F} = 0$, what does this mean?

**Solution:**

$$\text{div } \mathbf{F} = 0$$ means no net expansion/compression

**Incompressible flow**: Fluid density constant (volume conserved)

Example: $\mathbf{F} = \langle -y, x \rangle$ (rotational flow)

$$\text{div } \mathbf{F} = 0 + 0 = 0$$ ✓

**Answer:** Incompressible (divergence-free) flow

## Curl in 3D

**Curl** of $\mathbf{F} = \langle P, Q, R \rangle$:

$$\nabla \times \mathbf{F} = \begin{vmatrix} \mathbf{i} & \mathbf{j} & \mathbf{k} \\ \frac{\partial}{\partial x} & \frac{\partial}{\partial y} & \frac{\partial}{\partial z} \\ P & Q & R \end{vmatrix}$$

$$= \left\langle \frac{\partial R}{\partial y} - \frac{\partial Q}{\partial z}, \frac{\partial P}{\partial z} - \frac{\partial R}{\partial x}, \frac{\partial Q}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial P}{\partial y} \right\rangle$$

**Interpretation**: Vector pointing along axis of rotation, magnitude = rotation rate

### Example 8: Curl in 3D

Find curl of $\mathbf{F}(x, y, z) = \langle -y, x, z \rangle$.

**Solution:**

$$\nabla \times \mathbf{F} = \begin{vmatrix} \mathbf{i} & \mathbf{j} & \mathbf{k} \\ \frac{\partial}{\partial x} & \frac{\partial}{\partial y} & \frac{\partial}{\partial z} \\ -y & x & z \end{vmatrix}$$

$$= \mathbf{i}\left(\frac{\partial z}{\partial y} - \frac{\partial x}{\partial z}\right) - \mathbf{j}\left(\frac{\partial z}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial(-y)}{\partial z}\right) + \mathbf{k}\left(\frac{\partial x}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial(-y)}{\partial y}\right)$$

$$= \mathbf{i}(0) - \mathbf{j}(0) + \mathbf{k}(1 + 1) = \langle 0, 0, 2 \rangle$$

**Answer:** $\nabla \times \mathbf{F} = \langle 0, 0, 2 \rangle$ (rotation around $z$-axis)

### Example 9: Conservative Field Test

Show $\mathbf{F} = \langle yz, xz, xy \rangle$ is conservative using curl.

**Solution:**

$$\nabla \times \mathbf{F} = \begin{vmatrix} \mathbf{i} & \mathbf{j} & \mathbf{k} \\ \frac{\partial}{\partial x} & \frac{\partial}{\partial y} & \frac{\partial}{\partial z} \\ yz & xz & xy \end{vmatrix}$$

$$= \mathbf{i}(x - x) - \mathbf{j}(y - y) + \mathbf{k}(z - z) = \mathbf{0}$$

Since curl is zero, $\mathbf{F}$ is conservative ✓

**Answer:** $\nabla \times \mathbf{F} = \mathbf{0}$; field is conservative

## Divergence in 3D

**Divergence** of $\mathbf{F} = \langle P, Q, R \rangle$:

$$\text{div } \mathbf{F} = \nabla \cdot \mathbf{F} = \frac{\partial P}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial Q}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial R}{\partial z}$$

### Example 10: Divergence Example

Find divergence of $\mathbf{F}(x, y, z) = \langle x^2, y^2, z^2 \rangle$.

**Solution:**

$$\text{div } \mathbf{F} = \frac{\partial}{\partial x}(x^2) + \frac{\partial}{\partial y}(y^2) + \frac{\partial}{\partial z}(z^2)$$

$$= 2x + 2y + 2z$$

**Answer:** $\text{div } \mathbf{F} = 2x + 2y + 2z$

### Example 11: Laplacian

The **Laplacian** $\nabla^2 f = \text{div}(\nabla f)$ combines gradient and divergence.

Compute $\nabla^2 f$ for $f(x, y, z) = x^2 + y^2 + z^2$.

**Solution:**

$$\nabla f = \langle 2x, 2y, 2z \rangle$$

$$\nabla^2 f = \text{div}(\nabla f) = \frac{\partial}{\partial x}(2x) + \frac{\partial}{\partial y}(2y) + \frac{\partial}{\partial z}(2z)$$

$$= 2 + 2 + 2 = 6$$

**Answer:** $\nabla^2 f = 6$

## Vector Identities

**Key identities**:
1. $\nabla \times (\nabla f) = \mathbf{0}$ (curl of gradient is zero)
2. $\nabla \cdot (\nabla \times \mathbf{F}) = 0$ (divergence of curl is zero)
3. $\nabla \times (\nabla \times \mathbf{F}) = \nabla(\nabla \cdot \mathbf{F}) - \nabla^2\mathbf{F}$

### Example 12: Curl of Gradient

Verify $\nabla \times (\nabla f) = \mathbf{0}$ for $f(x, y, z) = xyz$.

**Solution:**

$$\nabla f = \langle yz, xz, xy \rangle$$

From Example 9: $\nabla \times (\nabla f) = \mathbf{0}$ ✓

**General proof**: Component $i$:
$$(\nabla \times \nabla f)_i = f_{zy} - f_{yz} = 0$$ (by Clairaut)

**Answer:** Verified; curl of gradient always zero

### Example 13: Physical Interpretation

In electromagnetism, $\mathbf{E} = -\nabla V$ (electric field is negative gradient of potential). What does $\nabla \times \mathbf{E} = \mathbf{0}$ mean physically?

**Solution:**

$$\nabla \times \mathbf{E} = \nabla \times (-\nabla V) = -\nabla \times (\nabla V) = \mathbf{0}$$

**Physical meaning**: Electric field is **irrotational** (conservative)

In electrostatics, no circulation of electric field around closed loops

(In dynamics, $\nabla \times \mathbf{E} = -\frac{\partial \mathbf{B}}{\partial t}$ by Faraday's law)

**Answer:** Electric field is irrotational (in electrostatics)

A) Rotation
B) Source/sink strength
C) Curl

A) Curl is zero
B) Divergence is zero
C) Field is zero
D) Field is constant
E) Gradient is zero

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: A**
Green's theorem: $\oint_C \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r} = \iint_D \text{curl } \mathbf{F} \, dA$
Relates boundary integral (line) to region integral (double)
Generalizes to Stokes' theorem (surface to boundary)
Key theorem connecting integrals of different dimensions

**Problem 2: B**
Curl measures rotation (angular velocity of fluid)
$\nabla \times \mathbf{F}$ points along rotation axis
Magnitude = twice angular velocity
Divergence measures expansion, not curl

**Problem 3: B**
Zero curl → conservative field (on simply connected domain)
$\nabla \times \mathbf{F} = \mathbf{0} \iff \mathbf{F} = \nabla f$
Conservative fields are irrotational
Not necessarily zero or constant
Divergence-free is different condition

**Problem 4: B**
Divergence measures source/sink strength
$\nabla \cdot \mathbf{F} > 0$: source (flow outward)
$\nabla \cdot \mathbf{F} < 0$: sink (flow inward)
$\nabla \cdot \mathbf{F} = 0$: incompressible
Not related to rotation (that's curl)

**Problem 5: B**
Incompressible flow: $\nabla \cdot \mathbf{F} = 0$ (divergence-free)
Volume preserved in fluid flow
Density constant
Can still have rotation (curl need not be zero)
Example: $\mathbf{F} = \langle -y, x \rangle$
