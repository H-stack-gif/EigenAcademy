# Multivariable Calculus Topic 4.3: Triple Integrals

Just as double integrals extend integration to two dimensions, **triple integrals** extend to three dimensions. We integrate functions $f(x, y, z)$ over solid regions in space. Geometrically, if $f(x, y, z) \geq 0$, the triple integral doesn't represent a four-dimensional "volume" (we can't visualize that), but it computes important quantities: total mass when $f$ is density, electric charge when $f$ is charge density, total heat when $f$ is temperature.

Triple integrals are evaluated by iterating three times—integrate with respect to one variable, then another, then the third. The order matters for computation but not for the final answer (Fubini's theorem again). The region's geometry determines integration limits: $z$ might range from a lower surface to an upper surface, while $x$ and $y$ range over a region in the plane. Visualizing these three-dimensional regions requires spatial reasoning, but systematic techniques make the process manageable.

This article develops triple integrals. We'll evaluate them over boxes and general regions, learn to determine integration limits from geometric descriptions, change the order of integration, and apply triple integrals to find volumes, masses, and centers of mass. In the next article, we'll see how cylindrical and spherical coordinates can dramatically simplify triple integrals with appropriate symmetry.

## Triple Integrals over Boxes

**Triple integral** over box $B = [a, b] \times [c, d] \times [p, q]$:

$$\iiint_B f(x, y, z) \, dV = \int_a^b \int_c^d \int_p^q f(x, y, z) \, dz \, dy \, dx$$

**Fubini's Theorem**: Can integrate in any order (six possibilities)

### Example 1: Box Integration

Evaluate $\displaystyle \int_0^1 \int_0^2 \int_0^3 xyz \, dz \, dy \, dx$.

**Solution:**

**Innermost** (with respect to $z$):
$$\int_0^3 xyz \, dz = xy \int_0^3 z \, dz = xy\left[\frac{z^2}{2}\right]_0^3 = \frac{9xy}{2}$$

**Middle** (with respect to $y$):
$$\int_0^2 \frac{9xy}{2} \, dy = \frac{9x}{2}\int_0^2 y \, dy = \frac{9x}{2}\left[\frac{y^2}{2}\right]_0^2 = \frac{9x}{2}(2) = 9x$$

**Outer** (with respect to $x$):
$$\int_0^1 9x \, dx = 9\left[\frac{x^2}{2}\right]_0^1 = \frac{9}{2}$$

**Answer:** $\displaystyle \frac{9}{2}$

### Example 2: Exponential Function

Evaluate $\displaystyle \iiint_B e^{x+y+z} \, dV$ where $B = [0, 1] \times [0, 1] \times [0, 1]$.

**Solution:**

$$\int_0^1 \int_0^1 \int_0^1 e^{x+y+z} \, dz \, dy \, dx$$

$$= \int_0^1 \int_0^1 e^{x+y}[e^z]_0^1 dy \, dx = \int_0^1 \int_0^1 e^{x+y}(e-1) \, dy \, dx$$

$$= (e-1)\int_0^1 e^x[e^y]_0^1 dx = (e-1)^2\int_0^1 e^x \, dx = (e-1)^3$$

**Answer:** $(e-1)^3$

## Triple Integrals over General Regions

**Type 1 region**: $E = \{(x, y, z) : (x, y) \in D, \, u_1(x, y) \leq z \leq u_2(x, y)\}$

$$\iiint_E f(x, y, z) \, dV = \iint_D \left[\int_{u_1(x,y)}^{u_2(x,y)} f(x, y, z) \, dz\right] dA$$

Then evaluate double integral over $D$.

### Example 3: Tetrahedron

Find volume of tetrahedron bounded by $x + y + z = 1$ and coordinate planes.

**Solution:**

**Region**: $x \geq 0$, $y \geq 0$, $z \geq 0$, $x + y + z \leq 1$

**As Type 1**: For fixed $(x, y)$, $0 \leq z \leq 1 - x - y$

**Projection $D$**: Triangle in $xy$-plane where $x \geq 0$, $y \geq 0$, $x + y \leq 1$

$$V = \iint_D \int_0^{1-x-y} dz \, dA = \iint_D (1-x-y) \, dA$$

$$= \int_0^1 \int_0^{1-x} (1-x-y) \, dy \, dx$$

**Inner**:
$$\int_0^{1-x} (1-x-y) \, dy = (1-x)[y]_0^{1-x} - \left[\frac{y^2}{2}\right]_0^{1-x}$$

$$= (1-x)^2 - \frac{(1-x)^2}{2} = \frac{(1-x)^2}{2}$$

**Outer**:
$$\int_0^1 \frac{(1-x)^2}{2} \, dx = \frac{1}{2}\left[-\frac{(1-x)^3}{3}\right]_0^1 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{3} = \frac{1}{6}$$

**Answer:** Volume $= \displaystyle \frac{1}{6}$

### Example 4: Region Between Surfaces

Set up integral for volume between $z = x^2 + y^2$ and $z = 4$ over disk $x^2 + y^2 \leq 4$.

**Solution:**

**Region**: $(x, y)$ in disk $x^2 + y^2 \leq 4$, $x^2 + y^2 \leq z \leq 4$

$$V = \iint_D \int_{x^2+y^2}^4 dz \, dA$$

In rectangular (less convenient):
$$V = \int_{-2}^2 \int_{-\sqrt{4-x^2}}^{\sqrt{4-x^2}} \int_{x^2+y^2}^4 dz \, dy \, dx$$

In polar (better for next article):
$$V = \int_0^{2\pi} \int_0^2 \int_{r^2}^4 r \, dz \, dr \, d\theta$$

**Answer:** $\displaystyle \int_0^{2\pi} \int_0^2 \int_{r^2}^4 r \, dz \, dr \, d\theta$ (polar form)

### Example 5: Pyramid


$$z = 3(1 - \max(|x|, |y|))$$

For square $|x| \leq 1$, $|y| \leq 1$:

By symmetry, compute one quarter and multiply by 4:

In first quadrant: $0 \leq x \leq 1$, $0 \leq y \leq 1$

Surface depends on which is larger. For $y \leq x$: $z = 3(1-x)$; for $x \leq y$: $z = 3(1-y)$

**Simpler approach**: Volume $= \frac{1}{3} \times \text{base area} \times \text{height} = \frac{1}{3}(2 \times 2)(3) = 4$

**Answer:** Volume $= 4$

## Changing Order of Integration

### Example 6: Determining Limits

Describe the region $E$ given by $\displaystyle \int_0^1 \int_0^x \int_0^{x+y} f(x,y,z) \, dz \, dy \, dx$.

**Solution:**

**Outer**: $0 \leq x \leq 1$

**Middle**: $0 \leq y \leq x$

**Inner**: $0 \leq z \leq x + y$

**Region**: Solid in first octant below plane $z = x + y$, with base a triangle in $xy$-plane ($0 \leq y \leq x \leq 1$).

**Answer:** Region below $z = x+y$, above triangle $\{0 \leq y \leq x \leq 1\}$

### Example 7: Reversing Order

Rewrite $\displaystyle \int_0^1 \int_0^{1-x} \int_0^{1-x-y} f(x,y,z) \, dz \, dy \, dx$ with order $dx \, dy \, dz$.

**Solution:**

**Original region**: Tetrahedron from Example 3

$0 \leq x \leq 1$, $0 \leq y \leq 1-x$, $0 \leq z \leq 1-x-y$

Equivalently: $x + y + z \leq 1$ in first octant.

**Reorder as $dx \, dy \, dz$**:

Fix $z$: $0 \leq z \leq 1$

For fixed $z$, $(x, y)$ in triangle: $x + y \leq 1 - z$, $x, y \geq 0$

**Limits**: $0 \leq z \leq 1$, $0 \leq y \leq 1-z$, $0 \leq x \leq 1-z-y$

$$\int_0^1 \int_0^{1-z} \int_0^{1-z-y} f(x,y,z) \, dx \, dy \, dz$$

**Answer:** $\displaystyle \int_0^1 \int_0^{1-z} \int_0^{1-z-y} f(x,y,z) \, dx \, dy \, dz$

## Applications: Volume

**Volume of region $E$**:
$$V = \iiint_E dV = \iiint_E 1 \, dV$$

### Example 8: Volume of Sphere

Find volume of sphere $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 \leq a^2$ using triple integrals.

**Solution:**

**Upper hemisphere**: $z = \sqrt{a^2 - x^2 - y^2}$

**Lower hemisphere**: $z = -\sqrt{a^2 - x^2 - y^2}$

**Projection**: Disk $x^2 + y^2 \leq a^2$ in $xy$-plane

$$V = \iint_D \int_{-\sqrt{a^2-x^2-y^2}}^{\sqrt{a^2-x^2-y^2}} dz \, dA = \iint_D 2\sqrt{a^2-x^2-y^2} \, dA$$

Better in spherical coordinates (next article), but we can use polar here:

$$V = \int_0^{2\pi} \int_0^a 2\sqrt{a^2-r^2} \cdot r \, dr \, d\theta$$

Let $u = a^2 - r^2$, $du = -2r \, dr$:

$$= 2\pi \int_{a^2}^0 \sqrt{u}(-1) \, du = 2\pi \int_0^{a^2} u^{1/2} \, du = 2\pi\left[\frac{2u^{3/2}}{3}\right]_0^{a^2}$$

$$= 2\pi \cdot \frac{2a^3}{3} = \frac{4\pi a^3}{3}$$

**Answer:** $V = \displaystyle \frac{4\pi a^3}{3}$ ✓

### Example 9: Volume Between Paraboloids

Find volume between $z = x^2 + y^2$ and $z = 2 - x^2 - y^2$.

**Solution:**

Surfaces intersect where $x^2 + y^2 = 2 - x^2 - y^2$:
$$2(x^2 + y^2) = 2$$
$$x^2 + y^2 = 1$$

**Region**: $x^2 + y^2 \leq 1$, $x^2 + y^2 \leq z \leq 2 - x^2 - y^2$

$$V = \int_{-1}^1 \int_{-\sqrt{1-x^2}}^{\sqrt{1-x^2}} \int_{x^2+y^2}^{2-x^2-y^2} dz \, dy \, dx$$

$$= \iint_D (2 - 2(x^2+y^2)) \, dA$$

**In polar**: $0 \leq r \leq 1$, $0 \leq \theta \leq 2\pi$

$$V = \int_0^{2\pi} \int_0^1 (2 - 2r^2) r \, dr \, d\theta = 2\pi \int_0^1 (2r - 2r^3) \, dr$$

$$= 2\pi\left[r^2 - \frac{r^4}{2}\right]_0^1 = 2\pi\left(1 - \frac{1}{2}\right) = \pi$$

**Answer:** Volume $= \pi$

## Applications: Mass and Density

If $\rho(x, y, z)$ is density, then:

**Total mass**: $\displaystyle m = \iiint_E \rho(x, y, z) \, dV$

### Example 10: Mass with Variable Density

Find mass of cube $[0, 1]^3$ with density $\rho(x, y, z) = x + y + z$.

**Solution:**

$$m = \int_0^1 \int_0^1 \int_0^1 (x+y+z) \, dz \, dy \, dx$$

$$= \int_0^1 \int_0^1 \left[xz + yz + \frac{z^2}{2}\right]_0^1 dy \, dx = \int_0^1 \int_0^1 \left(x + y + \frac{1}{2}\right) dy \, dx$$

$$= \int_0^1 \left[xy + \frac{y^2}{2} + \frac{y}{2}\right]_0^1 dx = \int_0^1 \left(x + \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{2}\right) dx$$

$$= \int_0^1 (x + 1) \, dx = \left[\frac{x^2}{2} + x\right]_0^1 = \frac{3}{2}$$

**Answer:** Mass $= \displaystyle \frac{3}{2}$

## Center of Mass

**Center of mass** $(\bar{x}, \bar{y}, \bar{z})$:

$$\bar{x} = \frac{1}{m}\iiint_E x\rho(x,y,z) \, dV$$

$$\bar{y} = \frac{1}{m}\iiint_E y\rho(x,y,z) \, dV$$

$$\bar{z} = \frac{1}{m}\iiint_E z\rho(x,y,z) \, dV$$

### Example 11: Center of Mass

Find center of mass of tetrahedron from Example 3 with constant density $\rho = 1$.

**Solution:**

From Example 3: $m = V = 1/6$ (for constant density, mass equals volume times density)

By symmetry: $\bar{x} = \bar{y} = \bar{z}$

$$\bar{x} = \frac{1}{1/6}\int_0^1 \int_0^{1-x} \int_0^{1-x-y} x \, dz \, dy \, dx$$

$$= 6\int_0^1 \int_0^{1-x} x(1-x-y) \, dy \, dx$$

$$= 6\int_0^1 x\left[(1-x)y - \frac{y^2}{2}\right]_0^{1-x} dx$$

$$= 6\int_0^1 x\left((1-x)^2 - \frac{(1-x)^2}{2}\right) dx = 6\int_0^1 \frac{x(1-x)^2}{2} \, dx$$

$$= 3\int_0^1 x(1-2x+x^2) \, dx = 3\int_0^1 (x - 2x^2 + x^3) \, dx$$

$$= 3\left[\frac{x^2}{2} - \frac{2x^3}{3} + \frac{x^4}{4}\right]_0^1 = 3\left(\frac{1}{2} - \frac{2}{3} + \frac{1}{4}\right) = 3 \cdot \frac{1}{12} = \frac{1}{4}$$

**Answer:** Center of mass at $\left(\displaystyle \frac{1}{4}, \frac{1}{4}, \frac{1}{4}\right)$

### Example 12: Moment of Inertia

The **moment of inertia** about $z$-axis is $\displaystyle I_z = \iiint_E (x^2+y^2)\rho \, dV$.

Find $I_z$ for solid cylinder $x^2 + y^2 \leq a^2$, $0 \leq z \leq h$ with constant density $\rho_0$.

**Solution:**

$$I_z = \rho_0 \int_0^{2\pi} \int_0^a \int_0^h r^2 \cdot r \, dz \, dr \, d\theta$$

$$= \rho_0 \int_0^{2\pi} \int_0^a r^3 [z]_0^h dr \, d\theta = \rho_0 h \int_0^{2\pi} \int_0^a r^3 \, dr \, d\theta$$

$$= \rho_0 h \int_0^{2\pi} \left[\frac{r^4}{4}\right]_0^a d\theta = \rho_0 h \cdot \frac{a^4}{4} \int_0^{2\pi} d\theta = \frac{\rho_0 \pi a^4 h}{2}$$

**Answer:** $I_z = \displaystyle \frac{\rho_0 \pi a^4 h}{2}$

### Example 13: Average Value

Find average value of $f(x, y, z) = xyz$ over box $[0, 1] \times [0, 2] \times [0, 3]$.

**Solution:**

**Volume**: $V = 1 \times 2 \times 3 = 6$

From Example 1: $\displaystyle \iiint_B xyz \, dV = \frac{9}{2}$

$$f_{\text{avg}} = \frac{1}{6} \cdot \frac{9}{2} = \frac{3}{4}$$

**Answer:** Average value $= \displaystyle \frac{3}{4}$

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: C**
Triple integral requires three successive integrations
Iterate: innermost, then middle, then outer
Can be in any of six orders (by Fubini)
Each integration eliminates one variable

**Problem 2: B**
Volume formula: $\displaystyle V = \iiint_E 1 \, dV = \iiint_E dV$
Integrating constant function $f = 1$
Option A only gives volume if $f = 1$
Not $\displaystyle \iint_E dA$ (that's for area in 2D)

**Problem 3: B**
Mass = density × volume (for constant density)
$m = \displaystyle \iiint_E \rho_0 \, dV = \rho_0 \iiint_E dV = \rho_0 V$
For variable density: $m = \displaystyle \iiint_E \rho(x,y,z) \, dV$

**Problem 4: C**
Integration order (from inside out): $z$, then $y$, then $x$
Innermost integral: $\displaystyle \int_0^y f \, dz$
Limits for $z$ can depend on outer variables
Result becomes function of $x, y$ only

**Problem 5: C**
Center of mass: $\bar{x} = \displaystyle \frac{1}{m}\iiint x\rho \, dV$ (and similar for $\bar{y}, \bar{z}$)
Need moments (numerators) and total mass (denominator)
Moments involve coordinate times density
