# Multivariable Calculus Topic 3.4: Optimization and Lagrange Multipliers

Finding maximum and minimum values is one of calculus's most practical applications. A company wants to maximize profit, an engineer wants to minimize material costs, a physicist wants to find stable equilibrium. In single-variable calculus, we found critical points by setting $f'(x) = 0$. For multivariable functions, the idea extends: set **all** partial derivatives to zero. But there's a complication—multivariable functions can have **saddle points** where the surface curves up in one direction and down in another.

The **second derivative test** determines whether critical points are maxima, minima, or saddles. It uses the **discriminant** $D = f_{xx}f_{yy} - (f_{xy})^2$, analogous to the second derivative $f''$ in one dimension. For optimization with **constraints** (maximize profit subject to budget limits, minimize distance subject to staying on a curve), we use **Lagrange multipliers**—an elegant technique that converts a constrained problem into solving a system of equations.

This article develops optimization systematically. We'll find and classify critical points using the second derivative test, optimize on closed and bounded regions (checking boundaries), and master Lagrange multipliers for constrained optimization. These techniques appear everywhere: machine learning (minimize error), economics (maximize utility), physics (minimize energy), statistics (least squares regression). Optimization is where multivariable calculus becomes immediately practical.

## Critical Points

**Critical point** of $f(x, y)$: Point $(a, b)$ where:
- $f_x(a, b) = 0$ and $f_y(a, b) = 0$ (gradient is zero), OR
- One or both partial derivatives don't exist

**Equivalently**: $\nabla f(a, b) = \mathbf{0}$

### Example 1: Finding Critical Points

Find all critical points of $f(x, y) = x^2 + y^2 - 2x + 4y$.

**Solution:**

$$f_x = 2x - 2 = 0 \implies x = 1$$
$$f_y = 2y + 4 = 0 \implies y = -2$$

**Critical point**: $(1, -2)$

**Check**: $f_{xx} = 2 > 0$, $f_{yy} = 2 > 0$, $f_{xy} = 0$

$D = (2)(2) - 0^2 = 4 > 0$ and $f_{xx} > 0$

**Answer:** Critical point $(1, -2)$ is a local minimum (by second derivative test)

### Example 2: Multiple Critical Points

Find critical points of $f(x, y) = x^3 - 3x + y^2$.

**Solution:**

$$f_x = 3x^2 - 3 = 0$$
$$x^2 = 1$$
$$x = \pm 1$$

$$f_y = 2y = 0 \implies y = 0$$

**Critical points**: $(1, 0)$ and $(-1, 0)$

**Answer:** Two critical points: $(1, 0)$ and $(-1, 0)$

## Second Derivative Test

At critical point $(a, b)$, compute:

$$D = f_{xx}(a, b) f_{yy}(a, b) - [f_{xy}(a, b)]^2$$

**Classification**:
1. If $D > 0$ and $f_{xx}(a, b) > 0$: **local minimum**
2. If $D > 0$ and $f_{xx}(a, b) < 0$: **local maximum**
3. If $D < 0$: **saddle point**
4. If $D = 0$: **test inconclusive**

### Example 3: Classifying Critical Points

Classify the critical points of $f(x, y) = x^3 - 3x + y^2$ from Example 2.

**Solution:**

$$f_{xx} = 6x, \quad f_{yy} = 2, \quad f_{xy} = 0$$

**At $(1, 0)$**:
$$D = (6)(2) - 0^2 = 12 > 0$$
$$f_{xx}(1, 0) = 6 > 0$$

**Local minimum** at $(1, 0)$ with $f(1, 0) = 1 - 3 + 0 = -2$

**At $(-1, 0)$**:
$$D = (-6)(2) - 0^2 = -12 < 0$$

**Saddle point** at $(-1, 0)$

**Answer:** $(1, 0)$ is local min; $(-1, 0)$ is saddle point

### Example 4: Saddle Point

Show that $f(x, y) = x^2 - y^2$ has a saddle point at origin.

**Solution:**

$$f_x = 2x = 0 \implies x = 0$$
$$f_y = -2y = 0 \implies y = 0$$

Critical point: $(0, 0)$

$$f_{xx} = 2, \quad f_{yy} = -2, \quad f_{xy} = 0$$

$$D = (2)(-2) - 0^2 = -4 < 0$$

**Saddle point** at origin

**Verification**: On line $y = 0$: $f(x, 0) = x^2 \geq 0$ (minimum at origin)
On line $x = 0$: $f(0, y) = -y^2 \leq 0$ (maximum at origin)

**Answer:** Saddle point at $(0, 0)$

### Example 5: Local Maximum

Find and classify critical points of $f(x, y) = 2 - x^2 - y^2$.

**Solution:**

$$f_x = -2x = 0 \implies x = 0$$
$$f_y = -2y = 0 \implies y = 0$$

$$f_{xx} = -2, \quad f_{yy} = -2, \quad f_{xy} = 0$$

$$D = (-2)(-2) - 0 = 4 > 0$$

Since $f_{xx} = -2 < 0$: **local maximum** at $(0, 0)$

$f(0, 0) = 2$

**Answer:** Local maximum at $(0, 0)$ with value 2 (actually global maximum—paraboloid opening down)

## Absolute Extrema on Closed Regions

**Extreme Value Theorem**: Continuous function on closed, bounded region attains absolute max and min.

**Procedure**:
1. Find critical points in interior
2. Find extreme values on boundary
3. Compare all values

### Example 6: Optimization on Rectangle

Find absolute max and min of $f(x, y) = x^2 + y^2 - 2x$ on rectangle $[0, 3] \times [0, 2]$.

**Solution:**

**Step 1: Interior critical points**

From Example 1: Critical point at $(1, -2)$, but this is **outside** rectangle (since $y = -2 < 0$)

No critical points in interior.

**Step 2: Boundary**

**Edge 1**: $x = 0$, $0 \leq y \leq 2$
$$f(0, y) = y^2$$
Min at $y = 0$: $f(0, 0) = 0$
Max at $y = 2$: $f(0, 2) = 4$

**Edge 2**: $x = 3$, $0 \leq y \leq 2$
$$f(3, y) = 9 + y^2 - 6 = 3 + y^2$$
Min at $y = 0$: $f(3, 0) = 3$
Max at $y = 2$: $f(3, 2) = 7$

**Edge 3**: $y = 0$, $0 \leq x \leq 3$
$$f(x, 0) = x^2 - 2x = (x-1)^2 - 1$$
Min at $x = 1$: $f(1, 0) = -1$
Max at $x = 0$ or $x = 3$: already checked

**Edge 4**: $y = 2$, $0 \leq x \leq 3$
$$f(x, 2) = x^2 - 2x + 4 = (x-1)^2 + 3$$
Min at $x = 1$: $f(1, 2) = 3$
Max at endpoints: already checked

**Step 3: Compare**

Minimum: $f(1, 0) = -1$
Maximum: $f(3, 2) = 7$

**Answer:** Absolute min $= -1$ at $(1, 0)$; absolute max $= 7$ at $(3, 2)$

### Example 7: Optimization on Disk

Find extreme values of $f(x, y) = x^2 + 2y^2$ on disk $x^2 + y^2 \leq 1$.

**Solution:**

**Interior**: Critical point at $(0, 0)$ (gradient is zero)
$$f(0, 0) = 0$$

**Boundary**: $x^2 + y^2 = 1$

Parametrize: $x = \cos\theta$, $y = \sin\theta$

$$f(\cos\theta, \sin\theta) = \cos^2\theta + 2\sin^2\theta = \cos^2\theta + 2(1 - \cos^2\theta)$$
$$= 2 - \cos^2\theta$$

Maximum when $\cos\theta = 0$: $f = 2$ at $(\pm 0, \pm 1)$
Minimum when $\cos^2\theta = 1$: $f = 1$ at $(\pm 1, 0)$

**Compare**: Interior gives $f = 0$ (absolute minimum)

**Answer:** Absolute min $= 0$ at $(0, 0)$; absolute max $= 2$ at $(0, \pm 1)$

## Constrained Optimization: Lagrange Multipliers

**Problem**: Optimize $f(x, y)$ subject to constraint $g(x, y) = c$

**Method of Lagrange Multipliers**:

At extremum, $\nabla f = \lambda \nabla g$ for some scalar $\lambda$ (Lagrange multiplier)

**System to solve**:
$$\nabla f = \lambda \nabla g$$
$$g(x, y) = c$$

### Example 8: Minimizing Distance

Find point on line $2x + y = 5$ closest to origin.

**Solution:**

**Minimize**: $f(x, y) = x^2 + y^2$ (or equivalently $\sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$)

**Constraint**: $g(x, y) = 2x + y = 5$

$$\nabla f = \langle 2x, 2y \rangle$$
$$\nabla g = \langle 2, 1 \rangle$$

**Lagrange equations**:
$$2x = 2\lambda$$
$$2y = \lambda$$
$$2x + y = 5$$

From first two: $x = \lambda$, $y = \lambda/2$

So $y = x/2$

Substitute into constraint:
$$2x + \frac{x}{2} = 5$$
$$\frac{5x}{2} = 5$$
$$x = 2, \quad y = 1$$

**Answer:** Closest point is $(2, 1)$ with distance $\sqrt{5}$

### Example 9: Maximizing on Circle

Find maximum and minimum of $f(x, y) = xy$ on circle $x^2 + y^2 = 1$.

**Solution:**

**Constraint**: $g(x, y) = x^2 + y^2 = 1$

$$\nabla f = \langle y, x \rangle$$
$$\nabla g = \langle 2x, 2y \rangle$$

**Equations**:
$$y = 2\lambda x$$
$$x = 2\lambda y$$
$$x^2 + y^2 = 1$$

From first two: $y = 2\lambda x$ and $x = 2\lambda y$

Substitute: $y = 2\lambda(2\lambda y) = 4\lambda^2 y$

If $y \neq 0$: $1 = 4\lambda^2$, so $\lambda = \pm 1/2$

**Case 1**: $\lambda = 1/2$
$$y = x$$
$$2x^2 = 1$$, so $x = \pm 1/\sqrt{2}$

Points: $\left( \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}, \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)$ and $\left(- \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}, -\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)$

$$f = \frac{1}{2}$$

**Case 2**: $\lambda = -1/2$
$$y = -x$$

Points: $\left( \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}, -\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)$ and $\left(- \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}, \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)$

$$f = -\frac{1}{2}$$

**Answer:** Maximum $= 1/2$; minimum $= -1/2$

### Example 10: Box with Fixed Volume

Find dimensions of rectangular box with volume 1000 cm³ that minimizes surface area.

**Solution:**

**Variables**: dimensions $x, y, z$

**Minimize**: $S = 2(xy + xz + yz)$ (surface area)

**Constraint**: $g(x, y, z) = xyz = 1000$

$$\nabla S = \langle 2y + 2z, 2x + 2z, 2x + 2y \rangle$$
$$\nabla g = \langle yz, xz, xy \rangle$$

**Equations**:
$$2y + 2z = \lambda yz$$
$$2x + 2z = \lambda xz$$
$$2x + 2y = \lambda xy$$
$$xyz = 1000$$

From symmetry, try $x = y = z$:

$$3x^2 = 1000$$
$$x = 10$$

**Verification**: At $x = y = z = 10$, equations satisfied with $\lambda = 0.4$

**Answer:** Cube with side length 10 cm

## Multiple Constraints

For two constraints $g(x, y, z) = c_1$ and $h(x, y, z) = c_2$:

$$\nabla f = \lambda \nabla g + \mu \nabla h$$

### Example 11: Two Constraints

Find extreme values of $f(x, y, z) = x + 2y$ subject to $x^2 + y^2 = 1$ and $z = 0$.

**Solution:**

Second constraint says $z = 0$, so this is really 2D problem on circle.

Reduce to: maximize $f(x, y) = x + 2y$ on $x^2 + y^2 = 1$

$$\nabla f = \langle 1, 2 \rangle$$
$$\nabla g = \langle 2x, 2y \rangle$$

$$1 = 2\lambda x$$
$$2 = 2\lambda y$$

From these: $x = 1/(2\lambda)$, $y = 1/\lambda$

So $y = 2x$

Constraint: $x^2 + (2x)^2 = 1$, giving $5x^2 = 1$

$$x = \pm \frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}, \quad y = \pm \frac{2}{\sqrt{5}}$$

$$f = \frac{1}{\sqrt{5}} + 2 \cdot \frac{2}{\sqrt{5}} = \frac{5}{\sqrt{5}} = \sqrt{5}$$ (maximum)

$$f = -\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}} - \frac{4}{\sqrt{5}} = -\sqrt{5}$$ (minimum)

**Answer:** Max $= \sqrt{5}$, min $= -\sqrt{5}$

### Example 12: Economics Application

Cobb-Douglas utility function $U(x, y) = x^{0.5}y^{0.5}$ with budget $2x + 3y = 60$. Maximize utility.

**Solution:**

$$\nabla U = \left\langle \frac{1}{2}x^{-0.5}y^{0.5}, \frac{1}{2}x^{0.5}y^{-0.5} \right\rangle$$

$$\nabla g = \langle 2, 3 \rangle$$

$$\frac{1}{2}\frac{y^{0.5}}{x^{0.5}} = 2\lambda$$

$$\frac{1}{2}\frac{x^{0.5}}{y^{0.5}} = 3\lambda$$

Divide first by second:
$$\frac{y}{x} = \frac{2}{3}$$

So $y = 2x/3$

Budget: $2x + 3(2x/3) = 60$, giving $4x = 60$, so $x = 15$, $y = 10$

**Answer:** Buy $x = 15$, $y = 10$ for maximum utility

### Example 13: Least Squares

Find line $y = mx + b$ that best fits points $(1, 2)$, $(2, 3)$, $(3, 5)$ (minimize sum of squared errors).

**Solution:**

**Error function**:
$$E(m, b) = (m + b - 2)^2 + (2m + b - 3)^2 + (3m + b - 5)^2$$

**Critical point**:
$$\frac{\partial E}{\partial m} = 0, \quad \frac{\partial E}{\partial b} = 0$$

After computation (expand, differentiate, solve):

$$E_m = 2(m+b-2)(1) + 2(2m+b-3)(2) + 2(3m+b-5)(3) = 0$$

$$E_b = 2(m+b-2) + 2(2m+b-3) + 2(3m+b-5) = 0$$

**System**: $14m + 6b = 28$ and $6m + 3b = 10$

Solution: $m = 1.5$, $b = 7/6$

**Answer:** Best-fit line: $y = 1.5x + 7/6$

## Practice Problems

**Problem 1:** At a critical point $(a, b)$, which is always true?

A) $f$ has a maximum
B) $f$ has a minimum
C) $\nabla f = \mathbf{0}$ or partials undefined
D) $D > 0$
E) $f_{xx} = 0$

**Problem 2:** If $D < 0$ at a critical point, then:

A) Local maximum
B) Local minimum
C) Saddle point
D) Test inconclusive
E) Absolute maximum

**Problem 3:** To find absolute extrema on closed region:

A) Check interior only
B) Check boundary only
C) Check both interior and boundary
D) Use Lagrange multipliers
E) Compute $D$

**Problem 4:** Lagrange multipliers require:

A) $\nabla f = \nabla g$
B) $\nabla f = \lambda \nabla g$
C) $f = \lambda g$
D) $\nabla f \perp \nabla g$
E) $D = 0$

**Problem 5:** For constrained optimization, at extremum:

A) Gradients are perpendicular
B) Gradients are parallel
C) Function is zero
D) Constraint is zero
E) Multiplier is zero

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: C**
Definition of critical point:
$\nabla f = \mathbf{0}$ (both partial derivatives zero)
OR partial derivatives undefined
Not necessarily max or min (could be saddle)
$D > 0$ only at some critical points
Critical point is first step, then classify with second derivative test

**Problem 2: C**
Second derivative test: $D < 0$ → saddle point
Surface curves up in one direction, down in another
Example: $f(x,y) = x^2 - y^2$ at origin
Not max or min (different behavior in different directions)

**Problem 3: C**
Extreme Value Theorem approach:
1. Find critical points in interior
2. Find extrema on boundary (may need parametrization or Lagrange multipliers)
3. Compare all values
Extrema can occur at interior critical points OR on boundary

**Problem 4: B**
Method of Lagrange multipliers:
At extremum of $f$ subject to $g = c$:
Gradients are parallel: $\nabla f = \lambda \nabla g$
$\lambda$ is Lagrange multiplier
Solve together with constraint $g = c$

**Problem 5: B**
At constrained extremum: $\nabla f = \lambda \nabla g$
Gradients are parallel (same or opposite direction)
Geometrically: level curve of $f$ is tangent to constraint curve $g = c$
If perpendicular, could move along constraint to increase/decrease $f$
