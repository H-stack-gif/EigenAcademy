# Multivariable Calculus Topic 4.4: Change of Variables and Jacobians

Integration in rectangular coordinates is sometimes awkward. We've seen how polar coordinates $(r, \theta)$ simplify double integrals with circular symmetry. For triple integrals, **cylindrical coordinates** $(r, \theta, z)$ and **spherical coordinates** $(\rho, \phi, \theta)$ provide similar benefits. A sphere becomes $\rho = a$, a cylinder becomes $r = a$—simple equations for regions that are messy in rectangular coordinates.

The price of changing coordinates is transforming the volume element. In polar coordinates, $dA = r \, dr \, d\theta$. In cylindrical, $dV = r \, dr \, d\theta \, dz$. In spherical, $dV = \rho^2 \sin\phi \, d\rho \, d\phi \, d\theta$. These factors come from the **Jacobian determinant**, which measures how much a coordinate transformation stretches or compresses volume. Understanding Jacobians unlocks the full power of coordinate changes—we can use any transformation that simplifies our problem.

This article develops cylindrical and spherical coordinates for triple integrals, derives the Jacobian for general coordinate transformations, and applies these tools to compute volumes, masses, and other quantities. We'll see how the right coordinates turn intractable integrals into routine calculations. This completes our tour of multiple integration—armed with these techniques, we can integrate over virtually any region in space.

## Cylindrical Coordinates

**Cylindrical coordinates** $(r, \theta, z)$:
$$x = r\cos\theta, \quad y = r\sin\theta, \quad z = z$$

**Conversion back**:
$$r^2 = x^2 + y^2, \quad \theta = \tan^{-1}(y/x), \quad z = z$$

**Volume element**: $dV = r \, dr \, d\theta \, dz$

**Use when**: Problem has circular symmetry around $z$-axis

### Example 1: Cylinder Volume

Find volume of cylinder $x^2 + y^2 \leq 4$, $0 \leq z \leq 5$ using cylindrical coordinates.

**Solution:**

**In cylindrical**: $0 \leq r \leq 2$, $0 \leq \theta \leq 2\pi$, $0 \leq z \leq 5$

$$V = \int_0^{2\pi} \int_0^2 \int_0^5 r \, dz \, dr \, d\theta$$

$$= \int_0^{2\pi} \int_0^2 r[z]_0^5 dr \, d\theta = \int_0^{2\pi} \int_0^2 5r \, dr \, d\theta$$

$$= \int_0^{2\pi} 5\left[\frac{r^2}{2}\right]_0^2 d\theta = \int_0^{2\pi} 10 \, d\theta = 20\pi$$

**Verification**: $V = \pi r^2 h = \pi(2^2)(5) = 20\pi$ ✓

**Answer:** Volume $= 20\pi$

### Example 2: Paraboloid

Find volume under paraboloid $z = 9 - x^2 - y^2$ above $xy$-plane.

**Solution:**

**Paraboloid** intersects $xy$-plane where $z = 0$:
$$9 - x^2 - y^2 = 0$$
$$x^2 + y^2 = 9$$

**Region**: $0 \leq r \leq 3$, $0 \leq \theta \leq 2\pi$, $0 \leq z \leq 9 - r^2$

$$V = \int_0^{2\pi} \int_0^3 \int_0^{9-r^2} r \, dz \, dr \, d\theta$$

$$= \int_0^{2\pi} \int_0^3 r(9-r^2) \, dr \, d\theta = \int_0^{2\pi} \int_0^3 (9r - r^3) \, dr \, d\theta$$

$$= \int_0^{2\pi} \left[\frac{9r^2}{2} - \frac{r^4}{4}\right]_0^3 d\theta = \int_0^{2\pi} \left(\frac{81}{2} - \frac{81}{4}\right) d\theta$$

$$= \int_0^{2\pi} \frac{81}{4} \, d\theta = \frac{81\pi}{2}$$

**Answer:** Volume $= \displaystyle \frac{81\pi}{2}$

### Example 3: Mass in Cylindrical Coordinates

Find mass of solid cylinder $r \leq 2$, $0 \leq z \leq 3$ with density $\rho(r, \theta, z) = r$.

**Solution:**

$$m = \int_0^{2\pi} \int_0^2 \int_0^3 r \cdot r \, dz \, dr \, d\theta = \int_0^{2\pi} \int_0^2 \int_0^3 r^2 \, dz \, dr \, d\theta$$

$$= \int_0^{2\pi} \int_0^2 r^2[z]_0^3 dr \, d\theta = \int_0^{2\pi} \int_0^2 3r^2 \, dr \, d\theta$$

$$= \int_0^{2\pi} 3\left[\frac{r^3}{3}\right]_0^2 d\theta = \int_0^{2\pi} 8 \, d\theta = 16\pi$$

**Answer:** Mass $= 16\pi$

## Spherical Coordinates

**Spherical coordinates** $(\rho, \phi, \theta)$:
- $\rho$: distance from origin
- $\phi$: angle down from positive $z$-axis ($0 \leq \phi \leq \pi$)
- $\theta$: azimuthal angle in $xy$-plane ($0 \leq \theta < 2\pi$)

**Conversion**:
$$x = \rho\sin\phi\cos\theta$$
$$y = \rho\sin\phi\sin\theta$$
$$z = \rho\cos\phi$$

**Inverse**:
$$\rho = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2 + z^2}$$
$$\phi = \cos^{-1}(z/\rho)$$
$$\theta = \tan^{-1}(y/x)$$

**Volume element**: $dV = \rho^2\sin\phi \, d\rho \, d\phi \, d\theta$

### Example 4: Sphere Volume

Find volume of sphere $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 \leq a^2$ using spherical coordinates.

**Solution:**

**In spherical**: $0 \leq \rho \leq a$, $0 \leq \phi \leq \pi$, $0 \leq \theta \leq 2\pi$

$$V = \int_0^{2\pi} \int_0^{\pi} \int_0^a \rho^2\sin\phi \, d\rho \, d\phi \, d\theta$$

$$= \int_0^{2\pi} \int_0^{\pi} \sin\phi\left[\frac{\rho^3}{3}\right]_0^a d\phi \, d\theta$$

$$= \frac{a^3}{3}\int_0^{2\pi} \int_0^{\pi} \sin\phi \, d\phi \, d\theta$$

$$= \frac{a^3}{3}\int_0^{2\pi} [-\cos\phi]_0^{\pi} d\theta = \frac{a^3}{3}\int_0^{2\pi} 2 \, d\theta$$

$$= \frac{2a^3}{3}(2\pi) = \frac{4\pi a^3}{3}$$

**Answer:** $V = \displaystyle \frac{4\pi a^3}{3}$ (standard formula!)

### Example 5: Cone

Find volume of cone $z = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$ below $z = 2$.

**Solution:**

**Cone**: $z = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$ gives $\rho\cos\phi = \rho\sin\phi$, so $\cos\phi = \sin\phi$, thus $\phi = \pi/4$

**Region**: $0 \leq \rho \leq 2/\cos\phi$, $0 \leq \phi \leq \pi/4$, $0 \leq \theta \leq 2\pi$

From $z = 2$: $\rho\cos\phi = 2$, so $\rho = 2/\cos\phi = 2\sec\phi$

$$V = \int_0^{2\pi} \int_0^{\pi/4} \int_0^{2\sec\phi} \rho^2\sin\phi \, d\rho \, d\phi \, d\theta$$

$$= \int_0^{2\pi} \int_0^{\pi/4} \sin\phi\left[\frac{\rho^3}{3}\right]_0^{2\sec\phi} d\phi \, d\theta$$

$$= \int_0^{2\pi} \int_0^{\pi/4} \frac{8\sec^3\phi\sin\phi}{3} \, d\phi \, d\theta$$

$$= \frac{8}{3}\int_0^{2\pi} \int_0^{\pi/4} \frac{\sin\phi}{\cos^3\phi} \, d\phi \, d\theta$$

Let $u = \cos\phi$, $du = -\sin\phi \, d\phi$:

$$= \frac{8}{3}\int_0^{2\pi} \int_1^{1/\sqrt{2}} \frac{-1}{u^3} \, du \, d\theta = \frac{8}{3}\int_0^{2\pi} \left[\frac{1}{2u^2}\right]_1^{1/\sqrt{2}} d\theta$$

$$= \frac{8}{3}\int_0^{2\pi} (1 - \frac{1}{2}) \, d\theta = \frac{8}{3} \cdot \frac{1}{2} \cdot 2\pi = \frac{8\pi}{3}$$

**Answer:** Volume $= \displaystyle \frac{8\pi}{3}$

### Example 6: Spherical Shell

Find volume of region $a \leq \sqrt{x^2+y^2+z^2} \leq b$.

**Solution:**

**In spherical**: $a \leq \rho \leq b$, $0 \leq \phi \leq \pi$, $0 \leq \theta \leq 2\pi$

$$V = \int_0^{2\pi} \int_0^{\pi} \int_a^b \rho^2\sin\phi \, d\rho \, d\phi \, d\theta$$

$$= \int_0^{2\pi} \int_0^{\pi} \sin\phi\left[\frac{\rho^3}{3}\right]_a^b d\phi \, d\theta$$

$$= \frac{b^3 - a^3}{3}\int_0^{2\pi} [-\cos\phi]_0^{\pi} d\theta = \frac{b^3-a^3}{3} \cdot 2 \cdot 2\pi$$

$$= \frac{4\pi(b^3 - a^3)}{3}$$

**Answer:** $V = \displaystyle \frac{4\pi}{3}(b^3 - a^3)$ (difference of sphere volumes)

## The Jacobian

**General change of variables**: $(x, y) = T(u, v)$

**Jacobian determinant**:
$$\frac{\partial(x, y)}{\partial(u, v)} = \begin{vmatrix} \frac{\partial x}{\partial u} & \frac{\partial x}{\partial v} \\ \frac{\partial y}{\partial u} & \frac{\partial y}{\partial v} \end{vmatrix} = \frac{\partial x}{\partial u}\frac{\partial y}{\partial v} - \frac{\partial x}{\partial v}\frac{\partial y}{\partial u}$$

**Change of variables formula**:
$$\iint_R f(x, y) \, dA = \iint_S f(x(u,v), y(u,v)) \left|\frac{\partial(x,y)}{\partial(u,v)}\right| du \, dv$$

### Example 7: Jacobian for Polar Coordinates

Verify $dA = r \, dr \, d\theta$ using the Jacobian.

**Solution:**

$x = r\cos\theta$, $y = r\sin\theta$

$$\frac{\partial(x, y)}{\partial(r, \theta)} = \begin{vmatrix} \cos\theta & -r\sin\theta \\ \sin\theta & r\cos\theta \end{vmatrix}$$

$$= r\cos^2\theta - (-r\sin^2\theta) = r(\cos^2\theta + \sin^2\theta) = r$$

Therefore: $dA = \left|\frac{\partial(x,y)}{\partial(r,\theta)}\right| dr \, d\theta = r \, dr \, d\theta$ ✓

**Answer:** Jacobian $= r$

### Example 8: Jacobian for Cylindrical

Find Jacobian for cylindrical coordinates.

**Solution:**

$x = r\cos\theta$, $y = r\sin\theta$, $z = z$

$$\frac{\partial(x,y,z)}{\partial(r,\theta,z)} = \begin{vmatrix} \cos\theta & -r\sin\theta & 0 \\ \sin\theta & r\cos\theta & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{vmatrix}$$

Expand along third row:
$$= 1 \cdot \begin{vmatrix} \cos\theta & -r\sin\theta \\ \sin\theta & r\cos\theta \end{vmatrix} = r$$

Therefore: $dV = r \, dr \, d\theta \, dz$ ✓

**Answer:** Jacobian $= r$

### Example 9: Jacobian for Spherical

Find Jacobian for spherical coordinates.

**Solution:**

$x = \rho\sin\phi\cos\theta$, $y = \rho\sin\phi\sin\theta$, $z = \rho\cos\phi$

$$\frac{\partial(x,y,z)}{\partial(\rho,\phi,\theta)} = \begin{vmatrix} \sin\phi\cos\theta & \rho\cos\phi\cos\theta & -\rho\sin\phi\sin\theta \\ \sin\phi\sin\theta & \rho\cos\phi\sin\theta & \rho\sin\phi\cos\theta \\ \cos\phi & -\rho\sin\phi & 0 \end{vmatrix}$$

After computation (expand along third row):
$$= \rho^2\sin\phi$$

Therefore: $dV = \rho^2\sin\phi \, d\rho \, d\phi \, d\theta$ ✓

**Answer:** Jacobian $= \rho^2\sin\phi$

## General Transformations

### Example 10: Elliptical Coordinates

Let $x = au$, $y = bv$. Find $\displaystyle \iint_R x^2 + y^2 \, dA$ where $R$ is unit circle in $uv$-plane.

**Solution:**

**Jacobian**:
$$\frac{\partial(x,y)}{\partial(u,v)} = \begin{vmatrix} a & 0 \\ 0 & b \end{vmatrix} = ab$$

**Region $R'$ in $xy$**: Ellipse $\displaystyle \frac{x^2}{a^2} + \frac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$

$$\iint_{R'} (x^2+y^2) \, dA = \int_0^{2\pi} \int_0^1 (a^2u^2 + b^2v^2) \cdot ab \, \cdot r \, dr \, d\theta$$

where $u = r\cos\theta$, $v = r\sin\theta$ in unit circle.

$$= ab\int_0^{2\pi} \int_0^1 (a^2r^2\cos^2\theta + b^2r^2\sin^2\theta) r \, dr \, d\theta$$

$$= ab\int_0^{2\pi} \int_0^1 r^3(a^2\cos^2\theta + b^2\sin^2\theta) \, dr \, d\theta$$

$$= ab\int_0^{2\pi} (a^2\cos^2\theta + b^2\sin^2\theta)\left[\frac{r^4}{4}\right]_0^1 d\theta$$

$$= \frac{ab}{4}\int_0^{2\pi} (a^2\cos^2\theta + b^2\sin^2\theta) \, d\theta$$

Using $\int_0^{2\pi} \cos^2\theta \, d\theta = \int_0^{2\pi} \sin^2\theta \, d\theta = \pi$:

$$= \frac{ab}{4}(a^2\pi + b^2\pi) = \frac{\pi ab(a^2 + b^2)}{4}$$

**Answer:** $\displaystyle \frac{\pi ab(a^2+b^2)}{4}$

### Example 11: Linear Transformation

Transform $\displaystyle \iint_R e^{(x-y)/(x+y)} \, dA$ where $R$ is trapezoid with vertices $(1,0)$, $(2,0)$, $(0,2)$, $(0,1)$ using $u = x+y$, $v = x-y$.

**Solution:**

**Inverse**: $x = (u+v)/2$, $y = (u-v)/2$

**Jacobian**:
$$\frac{\partial(x,y)}{\partial(u,v)} = \begin{vmatrix} 1/2 & 1/2 \\ 1/2 & -1/2 \end{vmatrix} = -\frac{1}{4} - \frac{1}{4} = -\frac{1}{2}$$

$\left|\frac{\partial(x,y)}{\partial(u,v)}\right| = \frac{1}{2}$

**Region in $uv$**: Vertices transform to $(1,-1)$, $(2,2)$, $(2,-2)$, $(1,1)$

Rectangle: $1 \leq u \leq 2$, $-2 \leq v \leq 2$ (after checking)

$$\iint_R e^{v/u} \, dA = \int_1^2 \int_{-2}^2 e^{v/u} \cdot \frac{1}{2} \, dv \, du$$

Bounds need careful analysis. The trapezoid becomes a simpler region.

**Answer:** Integral simplifies using $u, v$ coordinates

### Example 12: Area Scaling

If transformation $T$ has Jacobian $J$, how does area scale?

**Solution:**

$$\text{Area}(T(R)) = \iint_{T(R)} dA = \iint_R |J| \, du \, dv = |J| \cdot \text{Area}(R)$$

if $J$ is constant.

**Answer:** Area scales by $|J|$ (Jacobian measures local area distortion)

### Example 13: Volume in Ellipsoidal Coordinates

Ellipsoid $\displaystyle \frac{x^2}{a^2} + \frac{y^2}{b^2} + \frac{z^2}{c^2} = 1$ can be parametrized as $x = a\rho\sin\phi\cos\theta$, $y = b\rho\sin\phi\sin\theta$, $z = c\rho\cos\phi$. Find volume.

**Solution:**

**Jacobian**: $\displaystyle \frac{\partial(x,y,z)}{\partial(\rho,\phi,\theta)} = abc\rho^2\sin\phi$ (extends spherical)

**Volume**: $0 \leq \rho \leq 1$, $0 \leq \phi \leq \pi$, $0 \leq \theta \leq 2\pi$

$$V = \int_0^{2\pi} \int_0^{\pi} \int_0^1 abc\rho^2\sin\phi \, d\rho \, d\phi \, d\theta$$

$$= abc\int_0^{2\pi} \int_0^{\pi} \sin\phi\left[\frac{\rho^3}{3}\right]_0^1 d\phi \, d\theta$$

$$= \frac{abc}{3}\int_0^{2\pi} [-\cos\phi]_0^{\pi} d\theta = \frac{abc}{3} \cdot 2 \cdot 2\pi = \frac{4\pi abc}{3}$$

**Answer:** Volume of ellipsoid $= \displaystyle \frac{4\pi abc}{3}$

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: B**
Cylindrical coordinates: $dV = r \, dr \, d\theta \, dz$
The $r$ factor comes from Jacobian
Similar to polar $dA = r \, dr \, d\theta$ with extra $dz$
Not $r^2$ (that's spherical)

**Problem 2: B**
$\phi$ measures angle down from positive $z$-axis
Range: $0 \leq \phi \leq \pi$
At $\phi = 0$: on positive $z$-axis
At $\phi = \pi/2$: in $xy$-plane
At $\phi = \pi$: on negative $z$-axis
$\theta$ ranges from $0$ to $2\pi$ (azimuthal angle)

**Problem 3: B**
From Example 7: $\displaystyle \frac{\partial(x,y)}{\partial(r,\theta)} = r$
Therefore $dA = r \, dr \, d\theta$
Jacobian measures area distortion
Not 1 (that would mean no distortion)

**Problem 4: B**
Spherical coordinates natural for spheres
Equation $\rho = a$ describes sphere simply
Also good for cones (constant $\phi$)
Cylinders better in cylindrical coordinates

**Problem 5: B**
Area transformation: Area$(T(R)) = |J| \cdot$ Area$(R)$
If $|J| = 2$: new area is twice original
Jacobian measures local scaling factor
Negative Jacobian: orientation reverses, but area scales by $|J|$
