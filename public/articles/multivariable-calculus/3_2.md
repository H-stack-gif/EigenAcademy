# Multivariable Calculus Topic 3.2: Partial Derivatives

When a function depends on multiple variables, how do we measure its rate of change? If temperature $T(x, y)$ varies across a room, it might increase as you move east (positive $x$-direction) but decrease as you move north (positive $y$-direction). The **partial derivative** $\partial T/\partial x$ measures the rate of change in the $x$-direction alone, holding $y$ fixed. Similarly, $\partial T/\partial y$ measures change in the $y$-direction, holding $x$ fixed.

Partial derivatives extend the derivative concept from single-variable calculus. Instead of one derivative, we have multiple—one for each independent variable. Geometrically, $\partial f/\partial x$ is the slope of the curve obtained by slicing the surface $z = f(x, y)$ with a plane parallel to the $xz$-plane. Each partial derivative captures how the surface tilts in a particular direction.

This article develops partial differentiation systematically. We'll learn computational techniques, geometric interpretations, higher-order partial derivatives, and Clairaut's theorem (mixed partials are equal). We'll write equations of tangent planes—the linear approximation to a surface. We'll see applications in physics (gradients of potential energy), economics (marginal products of labor and capital), and engineering (sensitivity analysis). Partial derivatives are the foundation for everything that follows in multivariable calculus.

## Partial Derivatives: Definition

For $z = f(x, y)$:

**Partial derivative with respect to $x$**:
$$\frac{\partial f}{\partial x} = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x+h, y) - f(x, y)}{h}$$

(treat $y$ as constant, differentiate with respect to $x$)

**Partial derivative with respect to $y$**:
$$\frac{\partial f}{\partial y} = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x, y+h) - f(x, y)}{h}$$

(treat $x$ as constant, differentiate with respect to $y$)

**Notations**: $ \frac{\partial f}{\partial x} = f_x = \partial_x f = D_x f$

### Example 1: Computing Partial Derivatives

Find $f_x$ and $f_y$ for $f(x, y) = x^3 + x^2y + y^2$.

**Solution:**

**For $f_x$**: Treat $y$ as constant
$$f_x = 3x^2 + 2xy + 0 = 3x^2 + 2xy$$

**For $f_y$**: Treat $x$ as constant
$$f_y = 0 + x^2 + 2y = x^2 + 2y$$

**Answer:** $f_x = 3x^2 + 2xy$, $f_y = x^2 + 2y$

### Example 2: Evaluating at a Point

For $f(x, y) = e^{xy}$, find $f_x(1, 0)$ and $f_y(1, 0)$.

**Solution:**

$$f_x = ye^{xy}$$
$$f_x(1, 0) = 0 \cdot e^0 = 0$$

$$f_y = xe^{xy}$$
$$f_y(1, 0) = 1 \cdot e^0 = 1$$

**Answer:** $f_x(1, 0) = 0$, $f_y(1, 0) = 1$

## Geometric Interpretation

**$f_x(a, b)$**: Slope of curve obtained by intersecting surface $z = f(x, y)$ with plane $y = b$

**$f_y(a, b)$**: Slope of curve obtained by intersecting surface $z = f(x, y)$ with plane $x = a$

### Example 3: Interpreting Partial Derivatives

For $f(x, y) = 4 - x^2 - y^2$ (paraboloid), find $f_x(1, 0)$ and interpret geometrically.

**Solution:**

$$f_x = -2x$$
$$f_x(1, 0) = -2$$

**Interpretation**: At point $(1, 0, 3)$ on surface, if you move in positive $x$-direction (holding $y = 0$), surface slopes downward with slope $-2$.

**Trace**: In plane $y = 0$, curve is $z = 4 - x^2$ (parabola opening down)

At $x = 1$: slope of tangent is $-2$ ✓

**Answer:** Slope $= -2$ in $x$-direction

### Example 4: Sign of Partial Derivatives

If $f_x(a, b) > 0$ and $f_y(a, b) < 0$, what does this tell you about $f$?

**Solution:**

**$f_x > 0$**: $f$ increasing as $x$ increases (holding $y$ fixed)

**$f_y < 0$**: $f$ decreasing as $y$ increases (holding $x$ fixed)

**Answer:** $f$ increases eastward, decreases northward

## Higher-Order Partial Derivatives

**Second partial derivatives**:

$$\frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x^2} = (f_x)_x = f_{xx}$$

$$\frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial y^2} = (f_y)_y = f_{yy}$$

**Mixed partial derivatives**:

$$\frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial y \partial x} = (f_x)_y = f_{xy}$$

$$\frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x \partial y} = (f_y)_x = f_{yx}$$

### Example 5: Second Partial Derivatives

Find all second partial derivatives of $f(x, y) = x^3y + xy^2$.

**Solution:**

$$f_x = 3x^2y + y^2$$
$$f_y = x^3 + 2xy$$

$$f_{xx} = 6xy$$
$$f_{yy} = 2x$$

$$f_{xy} = 3x^2 + 2y$$
$$f_{yx} = 3x^2 + 2y$$

**Note**: $f_{xy} = f_{yx}$ (Clairaut's theorem)

**Answer:** $f_{xx} = 6xy$, $f_{yy} = 2x$, $f_{xy} = f_{yx} = 3x^2 + 2y$

## Clairaut's Theorem

**Theorem**: If $f_{xy}$ and $f_{yx}$ are continuous, then $f_{xy} = f_{yx}$

(order of differentiation doesn't matter)

### Example 6: Verifying Clairaut's Theorem

Verify $f_{xy} = f_{yx}$ for $f(x, y) = \sin(xy)$.

**Solution:**

$$f_x = y\cos(xy)$$
$$f_y = x\cos(xy)$$

$$f_{xy} = \frac{\partial}{\partial y}[y\cos(xy)] = \cos(xy) - xy\sin(xy)$$

$$f_{yx} = \frac{\partial}{\partial x}[x\cos(xy)] = \cos(xy) - xy\sin(xy)$$

**Answer:** $f_{xy} = f_{yx}$ ✓

### Example 7: Third-Order Partial Derivatives

For $f(x, y) = x^2y^3$, find $f_{xxy}$.

**Solution:**

$$f_x = 2xy^3$$

$$f_{xx} = 2y^3$$

$$f_{xxy} = 6y^2$$

**Answer:** $f_{xxy} = 6y^2$

## Tangent Planes

**Tangent plane** to surface $z = f(x, y)$ at point $(a, b, f(a, b))$:

$$z = f(a, b) + f_x(a, b)(x - a) + f_y(a, b)(y - b)$$

**Alternative form**:
$$z - z_0 = f_x(x_0, y_0)(x - x_0) + f_y(x_0, y_0)(y - y_0)$$

**Normal vector** to surface: $\langle f_x, f_y, -1 \rangle$

### Example 8: Equation of Tangent Plane

Find the equation of the tangent plane to $z = x^2 + y^2$ at point $(1, 2, 5)$.

**Solution:**

$$f_x = 2x$$, $f_x(1, 2) = 2$

$$f_y = 2y$$, $f_y(1, 2) = 4$

**Tangent plane**:
$$z = 5 + 2(x - 1) + 4(y - 2)$$
$$z = 5 + 2x - 2 + 4y - 8$$
$$z = 2x + 4y - 5$$

**Answer:** $z = 2x + 4y - 5$

### Example 9: Horizontal Tangent Plane

Find points where tangent plane to $z = x^2 - y^2$ is horizontal.

**Solution:**

Horizontal plane has normal vector $\langle 0, 0, -1 \rangle$ (or any vertical vector)

Need: $f_x = 0$ and $f_y = 0$

$$f_x = 2x = 0 \implies x = 0$$
$$f_y = -2y = 0 \implies y = 0$$

At $(0, 0)$: $z = 0$

**Answer:** Only at $(0, 0, 0)$

## Linear Approximation

**Linear approximation** near $(a, b)$:
$$f(x, y) \approx f(a, b) + f_x(a, b)(x - a) + f_y(a, b)(y - b)$$

**Differential**: $df = f_x \, dx + f_y \, dy$

### Example 10: Approximation

Use linear approximation to estimate $f(2.1, 0.9)$ where $f(x, y) = x^2y$.

**Solution:**

Use base point $(2, 1)$ where values are easy:

$$f(2, 1) = 4$$
$$f_x = 2xy$$, $f_x(2, 1) = 4$
$$f_y = x^2$$, $f_y(2, 1) = 4$

$$f(2.1, 0.9) \approx 4 + 4(0.1) + 4(-0.1) = 4 + 0.4 - 0.4 = 4$$

**Exact**: $f(2.1, 0.9) = (2.1)^2(0.9) = 3.969$

**Answer:** Approximation: $4$ (actual: $3.969$, pretty close!)

### Example 11: Differential

Find the differential $dz$ for $z = e^x\sin y$.

**Solution:**

$$\frac{\partial z}{\partial x} = e^x\sin y$$

$$\frac{\partial z}{\partial y} = e^x\cos y$$

$$dz = e^x\sin y \, dx + e^x\cos y \, dy$$

**Answer:** $dz = e^x\sin y \, dx + e^x\cos y \, dy$

## Functions of Three or More Variables

For $w = f(x, y, z)$:

$$\frac{\partial w}{\partial x}, \quad \frac{\partial w}{\partial y}, \quad \frac{\partial w}{\partial z}$$

(hold other two variables constant)

### Example 12: Three Variables

Find $ \frac{\partial w}{\partial x}$, $\frac{\partial w}{\partial y}$, $\frac{\partial w}{\partial z}$ for $w = x^2yz + xyz^2$.

**Solution:**

$$\frac{\partial w}{\partial x} = 2xyz + yz^2$$

$$\frac{\partial w}{\partial y} = x^2z + xz^2$$

$$\frac{\partial w}{\partial z} = x^2y + 2xyz$$

**Answer:** $w_x = 2xyz + yz^2$, $w_y = x^2z + xz^2$, $w_z = x^2y + 2xyz$

### Example 13: Applications

In economics, if $P(L, K) = 100L^{0.6}K^{0.4}$ is production function (Cobb-Douglas), find marginal products.

**Solution:**

**Marginal product of labor**:
$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial L} = 100(0.6)L^{-0.4}K^{0.4} = 60\frac{K^{0.4}}{L^{0.4}}$$

**Marginal product of capital**:
$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial K} = 100(0.4)L^{0.6}K^{-0.6} = 40\frac{L^{0.6}}{K^{0.6}}$$

**Answer:** $P_L = 60(K/L)^{0.4}$, $P_K = 40(L/K)^{0.6}$

A) Vertical
B) Horizontal

D) Undefined
E) A point

**Problem 5:** Clairaut's theorem states that if mixed partials are continuous:

A) $f_{xx} = f_{yy}$
B) $f_{xy} = f_{yx}$
C) $f_x = f_y$
D) $f = 0$
E) $f_{xx} = 0$

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: B**
$f(x, y) = xy^2$
Treat $x$ as constant: $ \frac{\partial}{\partial y}[xy^2] = x(2y) = 2xy$
Not $y^2$ (that's $f_x$)
Power rule applies: bring down exponent

**Problem 2: E**
$f_x = 3x^2$ (derivative of $x^3$, $y^3$ is constant)
$f_{xy} =  \frac{\partial}{\partial y}[3x^2] = 0$ (no $y$ in expression)
Similarly, $f_y = 3y^2$, $f_{yx} = 0$
Verify: $f_{xy} = f_{yx}$ ✓

**Problem 3: A**
$f_x = 2x$, $f_x(1,1) = 2$
$f_y = 4y$, $f_y(1,1) = 4$
Tangent plane: $z = 3 + 2(x-1) + 4(y-1)$
$z = 3 + 2x - 2 + 4y - 4 = 2x + 4y - 3$

**Problem 4: B**
Both partial derivatives zero → no slope in any horizontal direction
Tangent plane: $z = f(a,b)$ (constant)
Horizontal plane
This occurs at critical points

**Problem 5: B**
Clairaut's theorem: mixed partials equal
$f_{xy} = f_{yx}$ (order doesn't matter)
Requires continuity of mixed partials
Not about $f_x = f_y$ (functions rarely have this)
