# Multivariable Calculus Topic 5.1: Vector Fields and Line Integrals

Imagine wind blowing across a landscape. At every point, the wind has a direction and magnitude—a **vector**. A **vector field** assigns a vector to each point in space, describing how something (wind, water, electromagnetic force) varies across a region. Vector fields are fundamental to physics: gravitational fields pull masses together, electric fields exert forces on charges, fluid velocity fields describe flowing water.

**Line integrals** measure cumulative effects along curves. If you walk through a vector field representing force, the **work done** is the line integral of force along your path. If the field represents fluid velocity, the line integral measures **circulation**—how much the fluid swirls around a loop. These integrals generalize single-variable integrals from straight intervals to arbitrary curves in space.

This article develops vector fields and line integrals from the ground up. We'll learn to visualize and interpret vector fields, parametrize curves for integration, compute line integrals of both scalar and vector functions, and apply these tools to calculate work, mass, and circulation. Vector calculus transforms abstract mathematics into the language of physics and engineering—every force, every flow, every field is described by the concepts we develop here.

## Vector Fields

**Vector field** in 2D: $\mathbf{F}(x, y) = \langle P(x, y), Q(x, y) \rangle = P(x,y)\mathbf{i} + Q(x,y)\mathbf{j}$

**Vector field** in 3D: $\mathbf{F}(x, y, z) = \langle P(x,y,z), Q(x,y,z), R(x,y,z) \rangle$

**Visualization**: Draw arrows at sample points showing direction and magnitude

### Example 1: Evaluating Vector Fields

Evaluate $\mathbf{F}(x, y) = \langle x^2, xy \rangle$ at points $(1, 2)$ and $(-1, 0)$.

**Solution:**

$$\mathbf{F}(1, 2) = \langle 1^2, 1 \cdot 2 \rangle = \langle 1, 2 \rangle$$

$$\mathbf{F}(-1, 0) = \langle (-1)^2, (-1)(0) \rangle = \langle 1, 0 \rangle$$

**Answer:** $\mathbf{F}(1, 2) = \langle 1, 2 \rangle$; $\mathbf{F}(-1, 0) = \langle 1, 0 \rangle$

### Example 2: Radial Field

Describe the vector field $\mathbf{F}(x, y) = \langle x, y \rangle$.

**Solution:**

At point $(x, y)$, the vector is $\langle x, y \rangle$—pointing radially outward from origin.

**Magnitude**: $|\mathbf{F}| = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2} = r$ (distance from origin)

**Direction**: Radially outward

**Answer:** Radial field pointing outward, magnitude proportional to distance from origin

### Example 3: Rotational Field

Describe $\mathbf{F}(x, y) = \langle -y, x \rangle$.

**Solution:**

At $(x, y)$, vector is $\langle -y, x \rangle$.

**Check perpendicularity**: $\langle x, y \rangle \cdot \langle -y, x \rangle = -xy + xy = 0$ ✓

Vectors are perpendicular to position vectors (tangent to circles).

**Direction**: Counterclockwise rotation around origin

**Magnitude**: $|\mathbf{F}| = \sqrt{y^2 + x^2} = r$

**Answer:** Rotational field, counterclockwise, magnitude $= r$

### Example 4: Gravitational Field

The gravitational field of mass $M$ at origin is $ \mathbf{F}(x, y, z) = -\frac{GM}{(x^2+y^2+z^2)^{3/2}}\langle x, y, z \rangle$. What is the field at $(1, 0, 0)$?

**Solution:**

$$\mathbf{F}(1, 0, 0) = -\frac{GM}{(1)^{3/2}}\langle 1, 0, 0 \rangle = -GM\langle 1, 0, 0 \rangle$$

**Direction**: Toward origin (negative $x$-direction)

**Magnitude**: $GM$

**Answer:** $\mathbf{F}(1, 0, 0) = \langle -GM, 0, 0 \rangle$ (attractive force toward origin)

## Line Integrals of Scalar Functions

**Line integral** of scalar function $f(x, y)$ along curve $C$:

$$\int_C f(x, y) \, ds = \int_a^b f(x(t), y(t)) \sqrt{\left(\frac{dx}{dt}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{dy}{dt}\right)^2} \, dt$$

where $C$ is parametrized by $\mathbf{r}(t) = \langle x(t), y(t) \rangle$, $a \leq t \leq b$.

**Notation**: $ds = |\mathbf{r}'(t)| \, dt$ is arc length element

**Interpretation**: If $f(x, y)$ is density, $\int_C f \, ds$ is total mass along curve

### Example 5: Line Integral Along Straight Segment

Evaluate $\int_C xy \, ds$ where $C$ is line segment from $(0, 0)$ to $(1, 1)$.

**Solution:**

**Parametrization**: $\mathbf{r}(t) = \langle t, t \rangle$, $0 \leq t \leq 1$

$$\mathbf{r}'(t) = \langle 1, 1 \rangle$$
$$|\mathbf{r}'(t)| = \sqrt{1 + 1} = \sqrt{2}$$

$$\int_C xy \, ds = \int_0^1 t \cdot t \cdot \sqrt{2} \, dt = \sqrt{2}\int_0^1 t^2 \, dt$$

$$= \sqrt{2}\left[\frac{t^3}{3}\right]_0^1 = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{3}$$

**Answer:** $ \frac{\sqrt{2}}{3}$

### Example 6: Line Integral Along Circle

Evaluate $\int_C x^2 \, ds$ where $C$ is circle $x^2 + y^2 = 1$.

**Solution:**

**Parametrization**: $\mathbf{r}(t) = \langle \cos t, \sin t \rangle$, $0 \leq t \leq 2\pi$

$$\mathbf{r}'(t) = \langle -\sin t, \cos t \rangle$$
$$|\mathbf{r}'(t)| = 1$$

$$\int_C x^2 \, ds = \int_0^{2\pi} \cos^2 t \cdot 1 \, dt = \int_0^{2\pi} \frac{1 + \cos 2t}{2} \, dt$$

$$= \left[\frac{t}{2} + \frac{\sin 2t}{4}\right]_0^{2\pi} = \pi$$

**Answer:** $\pi$

## Line Integrals of Vector Fields

**Line integral** of vector field $\mathbf{F} = \langle P, Q \rangle$ along curve $C$:

$$\int_C \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r} = \int_a^b \mathbf{F}(\mathbf{r}(t)) \cdot \mathbf{r}'(t) \, dt$$

**Alternative forms**:
$$\int_C \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r} = \int_C P \, dx + Q \, dy$$

**Interpretation**: **Work** done by force field $\mathbf{F}$ along path $C$

### Example 7: Work Along Straight Path

Find work done by $\mathbf{F}(x, y) = \langle x, y \rangle$ along line from $(0, 0)$ to $(2, 1)$.

**Solution:**

**Parametrization**: $\mathbf{r}(t) = \langle 2t, t \rangle$, $0 \leq t \leq 1$

$$\mathbf{r}'(t) = \langle 2, 1 \rangle$$

$$\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{r}(t)) = \langle 2t, t \rangle$$

$$\mathbf{F} \cdot \mathbf{r}' = \langle 2t, t \rangle \cdot \langle 2, 1 \rangle = 4t + t = 5t$$

$$W = \int_0^1 5t \, dt = 5\left[\frac{t^2}{2}\right]_0^1 = \frac{5}{2}$$

**Answer:** Work $=  \frac{5}{2}$

### Example 8: Work Around Circle

Compute $\int_C \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r}$ where $\mathbf{F} = \langle -y, x \rangle$ and $C$ is unit circle traversed counterclockwise.

**Solution:**

**Parametrization**: $\mathbf{r}(t) = \langle \cos t, \sin t \rangle$, $0 \leq t \leq 2\pi$

$$\mathbf{r}'(t) = \langle -\sin t, \cos t \rangle$$

$$\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{r}(t)) = \langle -\sin t, \cos t \rangle$$

$$\mathbf{F} \cdot \mathbf{r}' = \langle -\sin t, \cos t \rangle \cdot \langle -\sin t, \cos t \rangle = \sin^2 t + \cos^2 t = 1$$

$$\int_C \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r} = \int_0^{2\pi} 1 \, dt = 2\pi$$

**Answer:** $2\pi$ (circulation around circle)

### Example 9: Different Parametrization

Show that line integral is independent of parametrization for $\int_C \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r}$ where $\mathbf{F} = \langle 2x, 3y \rangle$ along $y = x^2$ from $(0,0)$ to $(1,1)$.

**Solution:**

**Parametrization 1**: $\mathbf{r}_1(t) = \langle t, t^2 \rangle$, $0 \leq t \leq 1$

$$\mathbf{r}_1'(t) = \langle 1, 2t \rangle$$
$$\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{r}_1(t)) = \langle 2t, 3t^2 \rangle$$
$$\mathbf{F} \cdot \mathbf{r}_1' = 2t + 6t^3$$

$$\int_0^1 (2t + 6t^3) \, dt = \left[t^2 + \frac{3t^4}{2}\right]_0^1 = 1 + \frac{3}{2} = \frac{5}{2}$$

**Parametrization 2**: $\mathbf{r}_2(s) = \langle s^{1/2}, s \rangle$, $0 \leq s \leq 1$

$$\mathbf{r}_2'(s) = \langle \frac{1}{2}s^{-1/2}, 1 \rangle$$
$$\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{r}_2(s)) = \langle 2s^{1/2}, 3s \rangle$$
$$\mathbf{F} \cdot \mathbf{r}_2' = s^{1/2} \cdot \frac{1}{2}s^{-1/2} + 3s = 1 + 3s$$

$$\int_0^1 (1 + 3s) \, ds = \left[s + \frac{3s^2}{2}\right]_0^1 = 1 + \frac{3}{2} = \frac{5}{2}$$ ✓

**Answer:** Both give $\frac{5}{2}$ (independent of parametrization)

## Properties of Line Integrals

1. **Reversal**: $ \int_{-C} \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r} = -\int_C \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r}$

2. **Additivity**: $ \int_{C_1 + C_2} \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r} = \int_{C_1} \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r} + \int_{C_2} \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r}$

### Example 10: Reversing Direction

If $\int_C \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r} = 5$ where $C$ goes from $A$ to $B$, find the integral from $B$ to $A$.

**Solution:**

Reversing direction changes sign:

$$\int_{-C} \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r} = -\int_C \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r} = -5$$

**Answer:** $-5$

### Example 11: Combining Paths

Compute $\int_C \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r}$ where $\mathbf{F} = \langle y, -x \rangle$ and $C$ is triangle with vertices $(0,0)$, $(1,0)$, $(0,1)$ traversed counterclockwise.

**Solution:**

**Path $C_1$**: $(0,0)$ to $(1,0)$ — $\mathbf{r}_1(t) = \langle t, 0 \rangle$, $0 \leq t \leq 1$

$$\mathbf{r}_1'(t) = \langle 1, 0 \rangle, \quad \mathbf{F} = \langle 0, -t \rangle$$
$$\int_{C_1} = \int_0^1 0 \, dt = 0$$

**Path $C_2$**: $(1,0)$ to $(0,1)$ — $\mathbf{r}_2(t) = \langle 1-t, t \rangle$, $0 \leq t \leq 1$

$$\mathbf{r}_2'(t) = \langle -1, 1 \rangle, \quad \mathbf{F} = \langle t, -(1-t) \rangle$$
$$\int_{C_2} = \int_0^1 [-t + (t-1)] \, dt = \int_0^1 -1 \, dt = -1$$

**Path $C_3$**: $(0,1)$ to $(0,0)$ — $\mathbf{r}_3(t) = \langle 0, 1-t \rangle$, $0 \leq t \leq 1$

$$\mathbf{r}_3'(t) = \langle 0, -1 \rangle, \quad \mathbf{F} = \langle 1-t, 0 \rangle$$
$$\int_{C_3} = \int_0^1 0 \, dt = 0$$

**Total**: $0 + (-1) + 0 = -1$

**Answer:** $-1$

## Applications: Work and Circulation

**Work**: $W = \int_C \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r}$ (force field $\mathbf{F}$ along path $C$)

**Circulation**: $\oint_C \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r}$ around closed curve $C$

### Example 12: Work by Gravity

Find work done by gravity $\mathbf{F} = \langle 0, -mg \rangle$ moving particle from $(0, 0)$ to $(1, 2)$ along parabola $y = 2x^2$.

**Solution:**

**Parametrization**: $\mathbf{r}(t) = \langle t, 2t^2 \rangle$, $0 \leq t \leq 1$

$$\mathbf{r}'(t) = \langle 1, 4t \rangle$$

$$\mathbf{F} = \langle 0, -mg \rangle$$

$$\mathbf{F} \cdot \mathbf{r}' = 0 + (-mg)(4t) = -4mgt$$

$$W = \int_0^1 (-4mgt) \, dt = -4mg\left[\frac{t^2}{2}\right]_0^1 = -2mg$$

**Physical interpretation**: Negative work (gravity opposes upward motion)

**Note**: For conservative fields, work depends only on endpoints: $W = mg(y_{\text{final}} - y_{\text{initial}}) = mg(2 - 0) = 2mg$. Sign reflects direction.

**Answer:** $W = -2mg$ (work against gravity)

### Example 13: Mass of Wire

A wire along helix $\mathbf{r}(t) = \langle \cos t, \sin t, t \rangle$ for $0 \leq t \leq 2\pi$ has density $\rho(x, y, z) = z$. Find total mass.

**Solution:**

$$\mathbf{r}'(t) = \langle -\sin t, \cos t, 1 \rangle$$
$$|\mathbf{r}'(t)| = \sqrt{\sin^2 t + \cos^2 t + 1} = \sqrt{2}$$

$$m = \int_C \rho(x, y, z) \, ds = \int_0^{2\pi} t \cdot \sqrt{2} \, dt$$

$$= \sqrt{2}\left[\frac{t^2}{2}\right]_0^{2\pi} = \sqrt{2} \cdot 2\pi^2 = 2\pi^2\sqrt{2}$$

**Answer:** Mass $= 2\pi^2\sqrt{2}$

C) Circulation
D) Divergence

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: C**
$\mathbf{F} = \langle y, -x \rangle$
At $(1, 0)$: $\mathbf{F} = \langle 0, -1 \rangle$ (downward)
At $(0, 1)$: $\mathbf{F} = \langle 1, 0 \rangle$ (rightward)
This is clockwise rotation around origin
Compare to counterclockwise $\langle -y, x \rangle$

**Problem 2: B**
Arc length element: $ds = |\mathbf{r}'(t)| \, dt$
Magnitude of velocity vector
Measures infinitesimal arc length
Not $dt$ (that's parameter differential)
Not $\mathbf{r}'(t) \, dt$ (that's vector)

**Problem 3: C**
$\int_C \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r}$ = work done by force field $\mathbf{F}$
Dot product: component of force in direction of motion
Integrated along path
Not distance (that would be $\int_C ds$)
Not area (that's double integral)

**Problem 4: B**
$\int_{-C} \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r} = -\int_C \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r}$
Reversing direction flips sign
Physical: work against field vs. with field
Not zero (that would mean no work)

**Problem 5: C**
Circulation: $\oint_C \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r}$ around closed curve
Measures tendency of field to circulate
Related to curl (Green's theorem)
Flux is $\oint_C \mathbf{F} \cdot \mathbf{n} \, ds$ (perpendicular component)
