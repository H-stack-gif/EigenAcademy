# Quantum Field Theory Topic 8.3: Anomaly Cancellation in the Standard Model

The consistency of a chiral gauge theory requires that all gauge anomalies vanish—a non-negotiable mathematical constraint that profoundly shapes the Standard Model's particle content. Chiral fermions coupling to gauge fields generate triangle diagrams (AVV with axial-vector and two vector vertices, or VVV with three vector vertices) that can violate gauge invariance through quantum anomalies. For the Standard Model gauge group SU(3)$_C$ × SU(2)$_L$ × U(1)$_Y$, potential anomalies arise from six types of triangle diagrams: SU(3)$^3$, SU(2)$^3$, U(1)$^3$, SU(3)$^2$ × U(1), SU(2)$^2$ × U(1), and mixed gravitational anomalies. Each anomaly is proportional to the trace over fermion representations of group generators, and miraculously, in the Standard Model these traces vanish when summed over all fermions in one generation. The cancellation requires a precise balance: the number of quark colors must equal three, and the number of quark generations must equal the number of lepton generations. The fact that $\text{Tr}[Y^3] = 0$ for one generation—with leptons and quarks contributing opposite-sign terms that exactly cancel—represents a remarkable conspiracy between seemingly unrelated sectors, hinting at deeper unification principles underlying the Standard Model.

The fundamental principle is that non-Abelian gauge theories must be free of gauge anomalies to be consistent (renormalizable and unitary). An anomaly in a gauge current $\partial_\mu J^\mu \neq 0$ would violate the gauge Ward identities, breaking unitarity and rendering the theory inconsistent. For global symmetries (like chiral U(1)$_A$ in QCD), anomalies are acceptable and even desirable—they're physical effects. But for gauge symmetries, anomalies are fatal and must cancel. The Standard Model's fermion content is precisely arranged so that all gauge anomalies vanish, despite having many chiral fermions. This cancellation is so delicate that adding a fourth generation would require new fermions beyond the simple quark-lepton structure, and discovering new chiral fermions at colliders would immediately raise the question: what cancels their anomalies?

The anomaly coefficients for each triangle diagram are computed from group theory. For a fermion with left-handed component in representation $R$ and right-handed in $R'$ coupled to gauge groups $G_1, G_2, G_3$, the anomaly contribution is:

$$A_{G_1G_2G_3} = \text{Tr}_L[T_{G_1}\{T_{G_2}, T_{G_3}\}] - \text{Tr}_R[T_{G_1}\{T_{G_2}, T_{G_3}\}]$$

where $T_G$ are generators in the respective representations. For purely left-handed fermions (right-handed in trivial representation), only the left trace contributes. The symmetrization $\{T_2, T_3\}$ comes from Bose symmetry of external gauge bosons.

### Example 1: SU(3)$^3$ Anomaly Cancellation

Verify that the SU(3)$_C$ cubic anomaly vanishes in one Standard Model generation.

**Solution:**

The SU(3)$^3$ anomaly coefficient is proportional to:

$$A_{\text{SU}(3)^3} = \sum_{\text{fermions}} \text{Tr}[T^a\{T^b, T^c\}]$$

where $T^a$ are SU(3)$_C$ generators. For representation $R$, using $\{T^a, T^b\} = (1/N_c)\delta^{ab} + d^{abc}T^c$:

$$\text{Tr}[T^a\{T^b, T^c\}] = d_R \delta^{ab}\delta^{ac}$$

where $d_R$ is the Dynkin index of representation $R$. For fundamental: $d_{\mathbf{3}} = 1/2$. For anti-fundamental: $d_{\bar{\mathbf{3}}} = 1/2$. For singlet: $d_{\mathbf{1}} = 0$.

One generation of quarks:
- Left-handed: $Q_L = (u, d)_L$ in $(\mathbf{3}, \mathbf{2}, 1/3)$ → contributes $2 \times (1/2) = 1$ (two weak doublet components, each in fundamental of SU(3))
- Right-handed up: $u_R$ in $(\mathbf{3}, \mathbf{1}, 4/3)$ → contributes $-1 \times (1/2) = -1/2$ (minus for right-handed)
- Right-handed down: $d_R$ in $(\mathbf{3}, \mathbf{1}, -2/3)$ → contributes $-1 \times (1/2) = -1/2$

Leptons:
- All leptons are color singlets → contribute $0$

Total:

$$A_{\text{SU}(3)^3} = 1 - 1/2 - 1/2 = 0$$

The SU(3)$^3$ anomaly vanishes! The left-handed quark doublet exactly cancels the right-handed singlets.

**Answer:** SU(3)$^3$ anomaly: quarks $Q_L$ contribute $+1$, $u_R$ and $d_R$ each $-1/2$; total $= 0$ (cancels).

The SU(2)$^3$ anomaly involves only left-handed fermions since SU(2)$_L$ couples only to left-handed particles. For SU(2), the anomaly for representation $R$ is proportional to the Dynkin index. For doublet: $d_{\mathbf{2}} = 1/2$. For singlet: $d_{\mathbf{1}} = 0$.

### Example 2: SU(2)$^3$ Anomaly Cancellation

Show that the SU(2)$_L$ cubic anomaly vanishes.

**Solution:**

For SU(2), the $\text{Tr}[\sigma^a\{\sigma^b, \sigma^c\}]$ gives zero due to the antisymmetry properties of Pauli matrices:

$$\{\sigma^a, \sigma^b\} = 2\delta^{ab}$$

$$\text{Tr}[\sigma^a \cdot 2\delta^{bc}] = 0$$

because trace of a single Pauli matrix is zero. So:

$$A_{\text{SU}(2)^3} = 0$$

automatically for SU(2) by group theory, regardless of fermion content!

**Answer:** SU(2)$^3$ anomaly vanishes automatically: $\text{Tr}[\sigma^a\{\sigma^b,\sigma^c\}] = 0$ from Pauli matrix properties.

The U(1)$^3$ anomaly is the most constrained and provides the strongest restriction on fermion content. Each fermion contributes its hypercharge cubed, with sign from chirality. This is the anomaly that requires quarks and leptons to balance.

### Example 3: U(1)$_Y^3$ Anomaly Cancellation

Compute the U(1)$_Y$ cubic anomaly for one Standard Model generation and verify cancellation.

**Solution:**

The U(1)$^3$ anomaly coefficient:

$$A_{Y^3} = \sum_{\text{fermions}} Y^3 (\text{left} - \text{right})$$

One generation fermions with hypercharges:

**Leptons:**
- $L = (\nu_e, e^-)_L$: $Y = -1$, left-handed → contributes $2 \times (-1)^3 = -2$
- $e_R$: $Y = -2$, right-handed → contributes $-1 \times (-2)^3 = +8$

**Quarks (3 colors each):**
- $Q = (u,d)_L$: $Y = 1/3$, left-handed → contributes $3 \times 2 \times (1/3)^3 = 2/9$
- $u_R$: $Y = 4/3$, right-handed → contributes $-3 \times (4/3)^3 = -64/9$
- $d_R$: $Y = -2/3$, right-handed → contributes $-3 \times (-2/3)^3 = 8/9$

Total:

$$A_{Y^3} = -2 + 8 + \frac{2}{9} - \frac{64}{9} + \frac{8}{9}$$

$$= 6 + \frac{2 - 64 + 8}{9} = 6 + \frac{-54}{9} = 6 - 6 = 0$$

Perfect cancellation! The lepton sector contributes $+6$, the quark sector contributes $-6$. This requires:
- Exactly 3 colors (factor of 3 from quark colors)
- Equal number of quark and lepton generations

If we had a fourth generation, we'd need to add fermions such that the anomaly still cancels. A sequential fourth generation (same quantum numbers as first three) would cancel within itself, but discovering any new chiral fermion with different quantum numbers would require understanding what cancels its anomaly.

**Answer:** U(1)$_Y^3$ anomaly: leptons contribute $+6$, quarks $-6$; total $= 0$. Requires 3 colors and equal quark/lepton generations.

The mixed SU(3)$^2$ × U(1) anomaly involves two SU(3) vertices and one U(1) vertex. Only colored fermions (quarks) contribute. The coefficient:

$$A_{\text{SU}(3)^2 \times \text{U}(1)} \propto \sum_{\text{quarks}} Y \cdot d_R$$

where $d_R$ is the Dynkin index.

### Example 4: SU(3)$^2$ × U(1)$_Y$ Anomaly

Verify cancellation of the mixed SU(3)$^2$ × U(1)$_Y$ anomaly.

**Solution:**

For each quark, contribution is $Y \times (\text{Dynkin index})$ with sign from chirality. For fundamental of SU(3), Dynkin index $= 1/2$.

**Left-handed quarks:**
- $Q_L$: $Y = 1/3$, doublet (2 components), Dynkin $= 1/2$ → contributes $2 \times (1/3) \times (1/2) = 1/3$

**Right-handed quarks:**
- $u_R$: $Y = 4/3$, singlet, Dynkin $= 1/2$ → contributes $-(4/3) \times (1/2) = -2/3$
- $d_R$: $Y = -2/3$, singlet, Dynkin $= 1/2$ → contributes $-(-2/3) \times (1/2) = 1/3$

Total:

$$A_{\text{SU}(3)^2 \times Y} = \frac{1}{3} - \frac{2}{3} + \frac{1}{3} = 0$$

Cancellation! The left-handed doublet and two right-handed singlets balance.

**Physical interpretation**: The SU(3)$^2$ × U(1) anomaly would violate both color and hypercharge gauge invariances simultaneously. Its cancellation is necessary for consistency of the electroweak and strong interactions together.

**Answer:** SU(3)$^2$ × U(1)$_Y$: $Q_L$ contributes $1/3$, $u_R$ contributes $-2/3$, $d_R$ contributes $1/3$; total $= 0$.

The mixed SU(2)$^2$ × U(1) anomaly involves weak isospin and hypercharge. Only left-handed doublets contribute to SU(2)$^2$.

### Example 5: SU(2)$^2$ × U(1)$_Y$ Anomaly

Show that the SU(2)$^2$ × U(1)$_Y$ anomaly vanishes.

**Solution:**

The coefficient:

$$A_{\text{SU}(2)^2 \times Y} \propto \sum_{\text{left doublets}} Y \cdot d_{\mathbf{2}}$$

where $d_{\mathbf{2}} = 1/2$ for SU(2) doublet.

**Left-handed doublets:**
- $L$: $Y = -1$, Dynkin $= 1/2$ → contributes $(-1) \times (1/2) = -1/2$
- $Q$: $Y = 1/3$, 3 colors, Dynkin $= 1/2$ → contributes $3 \times (1/3) \times (1/2) = 1/2$

Total:

$$A_{\text{SU}(2)^2 \times Y} = -\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{2} = 0$$

The lepton and quark doublets cancel! This again requires exactly 3 colors and equal numbers of generations.

**Key point**: The hypercharges are chosen such that $Y(L) = -1$ and $Y(Q) = 1/3$. The ratio $|Y(L)/Y(Q)| = 3 = N_c$ is not a coincidence—it's required for anomaly cancellation and hints at quark-lepton unification in grand unified theories.

**Answer:** SU(2)$^2$ × U(1)$_Y$: lepton doublet $-1/2$, quark doublet (3 colors) $+1/2$; total $= 0$.

The gravitational anomaly arises from triangle diagrams with two gravitons (energy-momentum tensors) and one gauge boson. For U(1), the coefficient:

$$A_{\text{grav}^2 \times Y} \propto \sum_{\text{fermions}} Y$$

Each chiral fermion contributes its hypercharge with sign from chirality.

### Example 6: Gravitational Anomaly Cancellation

Verify that the mixed gravitational-U(1)$_Y$ anomaly vanishes.

**Solution:**

The coefficient is proportional to:

$$A_{\text{grav}^2 \times Y} = \sum_{\text{fermions}} Y_L - Y_R$$

**Leptons:**
- $L$: $Y = -1$, 2 components → contributes $2 \times (-1) = -2$
- $e_R$: $Y = -2$ → contributes $-(-2) = +2$

**Quarks (3 colors):**
- $Q$: $Y = 1/3$, 2 components → contributes $3 \times 2 \times (1/3) = 2$
- $u_R$: $Y = 4/3$ → contributes $-3 \times (4/3) = -4$
- $d_R$: $Y = -2/3$ → contributes $-3 \times (-2/3) = 2$

Total:

$$A_{\text{grav}^2 \times Y} = (-2 + 2) + (2 - 4 + 2) = 0 + 0 = 0$$

Leptons and quarks each cancel separately! This is a stronger constraint than the cubic anomaly.

**Physical significance**: Gravitational anomalies would violate general covariance (diffeomorphism invariance) coupled with gauge symmetry. Even though gravity is not quantized in the SM, consistency requires these anomalies vanish.

**Answer:** Gravitational-U(1)$_Y$: leptons contribute $0$ (internally cancel), quarks contribute $0$ (internally cancel); total $= 0$.

The requirement of equal numbers of quark and lepton generations is profound. If we have $N_g$ generations, each contributes zero anomaly (by the calculations above). The total anomaly remains zero for any $N_g$. We observe three generations experimentally—but anomaly cancellation would work for any number. The fact that $N_g = 3$ comes from other constraints (flavor physics, cosmology, electroweak precision tests).

Grand Unified Theories (GUTs) embed the SM gauge group into a simple group like SU(5) or SO(10). In SU(5), all fermions of one generation fit into $\bar{\mathbf{5}} \oplus \mathbf{10}$ representations. The beauty: anomaly cancellation is **automatic** because fermions fill complete representations of the simple group, and all anomalies of a simple group with fermions in complete representations vanish by group theory. This is strong evidence for unification.

### Example 7: Number of Colors from Anomaly Cancellation

Show that anomaly cancellation requires exactly 3 colors.

**Solution:**

Consider the U(1)$_Y^3$ anomaly with $N_c$ colors:

$$A_{Y^3} = \sum_{\text{leptons}} Y^3 + N_c \sum_{\text{quarks}} Y^3$$

Leptons:

$$\sum_{\text{lep}} Y^3 = 2(-1)^3 - (-2)^3 = -2 + 8 = 6$$

Quarks (per color):

$$\sum_{\text{quark}} Y^3 = 2(1/3)^3 - (4/3)^3 - (-2/3)^3 = \frac{2}{27} - \frac{64}{27} + \frac{8}{27} = -\frac{54}{27} = -2$$

Total:

$$A_{Y^3} = 6 + N_c(-2) = 6 - 2N_c$$

For cancellation: $6 - 2N_c = 0 \Rightarrow N_c = 3$.

Similarly, SU(2)$^2$ × U(1):

$$A_{\text{SU}(2)^2 \times Y} = (-1) \times \frac{1}{2} + N_c \times \frac{1}{3} \times \frac{1}{2} = -\frac{1}{2} + \frac{N_c}{6}$$

For cancellation: $-1/2 + N_c/6 = 0 \Rightarrow N_c = 3$.

**Both anomalies independently require $N_c = 3$!** This is a powerful prediction: the number of quark colors is fixed by mathematical consistency of the gauge theory. Before QCD was established, anomaly cancellation provided evidence that quarks must come in three colors.

**Answer:** U(1)$_Y^3$ anomaly $= 6 - 2N_c$ vanishes for $N_c = 3$; SU(2)$^2$ × U(1)$_Y$ independently requires $N_c = 3$.

The discovery of anomaly cancellation in the 1970s was crucial for accepting the Standard Model. It showed that the seemingly arbitrary fermion content (three colors, left-handed doublets, etc.) is actually tightly constrained by mathematical consistency. Any proposed extension to the Standard Model (fourth generation, new fermions, etc.) must maintain anomaly cancellation, severely restricting possible new physics.

The implications for beyond-Standard-Model physics are profound. Consider adding right-handed neutrinos to explain neutrino masses through the seesaw mechanism. Since $\nu_R$ is a complete gauge singlet under SU(3)$_C$ × SU(2)$_L$ × U(1)$_Y$ (with $Y = 0$), it contributes nothing to any anomaly—the cancellations remain intact. This is why right-handed neutrinos are the simplest SM extension, requiring no additional structure for consistency. However, if we add a new U(1)$_{B-L}$ gauge symmetry (gauging baryon minus lepton number), the right-handed neutrinos carry charge under this new symmetry and could generate new anomalies. Consistency then requires additional fields or specific charge assignments.

Sequential fourth generation fermions (same quantum numbers as first three) would maintain anomaly cancellation within the fourth generation itself, making them theoretically allowed. However, precision electroweak fits from LEP Z-pole measurements combined with Higgs mass constraints exclude such a fourth generation—the vacuum would destabilize through large top' Yukawa coupling driving Higgs quartic coupling negative at low scales. Vector-like fermions (with both chiralities transforming identically under gauge group) never contribute to anomalies since left and right contributions cancel identically, allowing them as possible new physics even at TeV scales. Many BSM models (little Higgs, composite Higgs, extra dimensions) predict vector-like quarks or leptons for precisely this reason.

The connection between anomaly cancellation and other consistency conditions reveals deep structure. The Witten global anomaly for SU(2) gauge theories requires an even number of SU(2) doublets—automatically satisfied in the SM with 4 doublets per generation (1 lepton + 3 quark colors). Mixed gauge-gravity anomalies constrain theories of quantum gravity coupled to chiral matter, relevant for string theory compactifications. The Green-Schwarz mechanism in string theory cancels anomalies through counterterms involving antisymmetric tensor fields, allowing anomaly cancellation in 10 dimensions. These connections suggest anomaly cancellation is not just a technical requirement but a window into the mathematical structure underlying consistent quantum theories.

Modern perspectives on anomalies include:
- **Anomaly inflow**: Higher-dimensional theories can have anomalies canceled by bulk contributions
- **Gauge/gravity duality**: Anomalies in boundary CFTs match bulk physics
- **Global symmetries**: Can have 't Hooft anomalies that constrain IR physics
- **Topological phases**: Anomalies characterize edge states in condensed matter

The Standard Model's anomaly cancellation remains one of its most beautiful features, hinting at deeper principles yet to be fully understood. The fact that three generations of quarks and leptons, with their specific hypercharge assignments, combine to give exact cancellation across six different anomaly conditions is surely not an accident—it points toward grand unification and perhaps string theory as the ultimate UV completion.

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: B** — Gauge anomalies $\partial_\mu J^\mu_{\text{gauge}} \neq 0$ violate Ward identities $k^\mu \mathcal{M}_\mu = 0$, breaking unitarity ($\sum |S|^2 \neq 1$) and renormalizability. Makes theory mathematically inconsistent. Global anomalies (like U(1)$_A$ in QCD) are acceptable—physical effects. Gauge anomalies are **fatal** and must cancel exactly. Constraint comes from triangle diagrams with chiral fermions.

**Problem 2: B** — One generation: Leptons contribute $2(-1)^3 - (-2)^3 = 6$. Quarks (3 colors) contribute $3[2(1/3)^3 - (4/3)^3 - (-2/3)^3] = 3(-2) = -6$. **Total $= 0$** exactly. Requires $N_c = 3$ colors and balanced quark-lepton content. This miraculous cancellation between seemingly unrelated sectors hints at grand unification (SU(5), SO(10)).

**Problem 3: B** — Anomaly cancellation equations: U(1)$_Y^3$ gives $6 - 2N_c = 0$ → $N_c = 3$. SU(2)$^2$ × U(1)$_Y$ gives $-1/2 + N_c/6 = 0$ → $N_c = 3$. **Both independently require 3 colors!** Predicted before QCD experiments confirmed. Mathematical consistency of gauge theory fixes quark colors. Any other $N_c$ would have inconsistent electroweak theory.

**Problem 4: B** — In SU(5): one generation fills $\bar{\mathbf{5}} \oplus \mathbf{10}$ (complete multiplets). Simple group anomalies with fermions in complete representations **vanish automatically** by group theory. Don't need to check individual triangles—guaranteed by representation theory. Evidence for unification: SM's ad-hoc cancellations become automatic. Also explains charge quantization $Q_e = -3Q_d$.

**Problem 5: B** — Leptons contribute $\sum Y^3 = +6$ to U(1)$_Y^3$ anomaly. Quarks contribute $-6$ (with 3 colors). If unequal numbers of generations, anomaly $\neq 0$ → inconsistent gauge theory. Similarly for SU(2)$^2$ × U(1)$_Y$: lepton doublets and quark doublets must balance. Observing 3 generations of each confirmed by neutrino counting (LEP: $N_\nu = 3$). Fourth generation requires exotic structure.
