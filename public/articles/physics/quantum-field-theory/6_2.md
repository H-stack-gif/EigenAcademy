# Quantum Field Theory Topic 6.2: QCD Feynman Rules

Quantum Chromodynamics, the SU(3) gauge theory of colored quarks and gluons, requires systematic Feynman rules encoding the Lagrangian's structure into diagrammatic calculation tools. Unlike QED where Feynman rules are relatively simple—electron propagators, photon propagators, and a single QED vertex—QCD has richer structure from non-Abelian gauge invariance. Quarks come in three colors (red, green, blue) transforming in the fundamental representation, while eight gluons transform in the adjoint representation carrying color charge themselves. This leads to quark-gluon vertices analogous to QED but with color matrices, plus genuinely new three-gluon and four-gluon vertices from gluon self-interactions absent in Abelian theories. Additionally, maintaining manifest gauge invariance in covariant gauges requires **Faddeev-Popov ghosts**—unphysical scalar fermions coupling to gluons that cancel unphysical gauge degrees of freedom in loop calculations.

The propagators encode free particle propagation. Quark propagators $iS_F^{ij}(p) = i(\not\!p + m)/(p^2 - m^2 + i\epsilon) \cdot \delta^{ij}$ carry a color Kronecker delta $\delta^{ij}$ since quarks don't change color spontaneously—color is conserved at each vertex. The gluon propagator in Feynman gauge becomes $-ig_{\mu\nu}\delta^{ab}/(q^2 + i\epsilon)$ with color index $\delta^{ab}$ and Lorentz index structure $g_{\mu\nu}$ familiar from QED. In general covariant gauges parameterized by $\xi$, the propagator gains momentum-dependent terms $(1-\xi)q_\mu q_\nu/q^2$ which cancel in physical amplitudes by gauge invariance. Ghost propagators $i\delta^{ab}/(q^2 + i\epsilon)$ look like scalar propagators but carry color indices and anti-commuting Grassmann statistics from their Faddeev-Popov origin.

The vertices derive from expanding the QCD Lagrangian. The quark-gluon vertex $-ig_s\gamma^\mu T_{ij}^a$ contains the strong coupling $g_s$ (related to $\alpha_s = g_s^2/(4\pi)$), a Dirac matrix $\gamma^\mu$ from the covariant derivative, and the generator $T_{ij}^a = (\lambda^a/2)_{ij}$ acting on color indices. Three-gluon vertices arise from the $ef^{abc}(\partial A)AA$ terms in the Yang-Mills Lagrangian, giving $g_s f^{abc}[g_{\mu\nu}(k_1-k_2)_\rho + g_{\nu\rho}(k_2-k_3)_\mu + g_{\rho\mu}(k_3-k_1)_\nu]$ where momenta flow into the vertex and indices permute cyclically. Four-gluon vertices from $(AA)^2$ terms have structure $-ig_s^2[f^{abe}f^{cde}(g_{\mu\rho}g_{\nu\sigma} - g_{\mu\sigma}g_{\nu\rho}) + \text{perms}]$ involving products of structure constants. Ghost-gluon vertices $g_s f^{abc}q^\mu$ couple ghosts to gluons with momentum $q$ and structure constants, essential for maintaining unitarity in loop calculations.

### Example 1: Quark Propagator with Color

Write the full quark propagator including spin and color structure.

**Solution:**

The free quark Lagrangian $\bar{\psi}_i(i\not\!\partial - m)\psi_i$ where $i=1,2,3$ labels color gives the equation of motion $(i\not\!\partial - m)\psi_i = 0$. In momentum space, this becomes $(\ not\!p - m)\tilde{\psi}_i(p) = 0$ with solution encoded in the propagator.

The full quark propagator connects color $i$ to color $j$ and spin indices $\alpha$ to $\beta$:

$$[iS_F(p)]_{ij}^{\alpha\beta} = \frac{i(\not\!p + m)_{\alpha\beta}}{p^2 - m^2 + i\epsilon} \cdot \delta_{ij}$$

The Dirac structure $(\not\!p + m)_{\alpha\beta} = (\gamma^\mu p_\mu + m)_{\alpha\beta}$ acts on spinor indices, while the Kronecker delta $\delta_{ij}$ preserves color. Explicitly:

$$iS_F^{ij}(p) = \frac{i(\not\!p + m)}{p^2 - m^2 + i\epsilon}\delta^{ij}$$

This is often written compactly as $iS_F(p)\delta^{ij}$ where the spinor structure is implicit.

For massless quarks (good approximation for $u,d$ at high energies), $m \to 0$ gives:

$$iS_F^{ij}(p) = \frac{i\not\!p}{p^2 + i\epsilon}\delta^{ij}$$

In Feynman diagrams, quark lines carry both a Dirac spinor flow (solid line with arrow) and a color flow (implicit color index). External quark states are described by spinors $u^r(p)$ for quarks, $v^r(p)$ for antiquarks (where $r$ is spin/helicity) and color wavefunctions $\delta^{i}_{\text{color}}$.

**Answer:** Quark propagator $iS_F^{ij}(p) = i(\not\!p+m)\delta^{ij}/(p^2-m^2+i\epsilon)$; color diagonal, Dirac spinor structure.

The color algebra becomes essential when computing scattering amplitudes with multiple gluon exchanges. Unlike QED where charge factors simply multiply, QCD color factors involve products of generators that must be traced and contracted using completeness relations. For instance, $q+\bar{q} \to g+g$ (quark-antiquark annihilation to two gluons) has two Feynman diagrams with different gluon emissions, and the color factors $T^a T^b$ and $T^b T^a$ for the two orderings combine with the commutator $[T^a, T^b] = if^{abc}T^c$ playing a role in interference terms.

### Example 2: Gluon Propagator in Feynman Gauge

Derive the gluon propagator in Feynman gauge from the gluon kinetic term.

**Solution:**

The Yang-Mills Lagrangian gives gluon kinetic term (after gauge fixing):

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{gluon}} = -\frac{1}{4}F_{\mu\nu}^a F^{\mu\nu,a} - \frac{1}{2\xi}(\partial_\mu A^{\mu,a})^2$$

The gauge-fixing term with parameter $\xi$ removes mixing between different Lorentz components. For Feynman gauge, $\xi = 1$.

Expanding the field strength to quadratic order: $F_{\mu\nu}^a = \partial_\mu A_\nu^a - \partial_\nu A_\mu^a + \mathcal{O}(A^2)$, the kinetic term becomes:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{quad}} = -\frac{1}{4}(\partial_\mu A_\nu^a - \partial_\nu A_\mu^a)^2 - \frac{1}{2}(\partial_\mu A^{\mu,a})^2$$

$$= -\frac{1}{2}A_\nu^a(\Box g^{\mu\nu} - \partial^\mu\partial^\nu)A_\mu^a - \frac{1}{2}A_\nu^a\partial^\nu\partial^\mu A_\mu^a$$

In Feynman gauge ($\xi=1$), this simplifies to:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{quad}} = -\frac{1}{2}A_\nu^a \Box g^{\mu\nu}A_\mu^a$$

The propagator is the inverse of the kinetic operator. In momentum space, $\Box \to -q^2$, giving:

$$D_{\mu\nu}^{ab}(q) = \frac{-ig_{\mu\nu}\delta^{ab}}{q^2 + i\epsilon}$$

The color delta $\delta^{ab}$ indicates color conservation, while $g_{\mu\nu}$ is the standard massless spin-1 propagator structure. Physical amplitudes are independent of $\xi$ by gauge invariance, but Feynman gauge ($\xi=1$) simplifies calculations.

In general $\xi$-gauge:

$$D_{\mu\nu}^{ab}(q) = \frac{-i\delta^{ab}}{q^2+i\epsilon}\left[g_{\mu\nu} - (1-\xi)\frac{q_\mu q_\nu}{q^2}\right]$$

**Answer:** Feynman gauge ($\xi=1$): $D_{\mu\nu}^{ab} = -ig_{\mu\nu}\delta^{ab}/(q^2+i\epsilon)$; color-diagonal, Lorentz structure $g_{\mu\nu}$.

The quark-gluon vertex encodes how quarks emit or absorb gluons. From the covariant derivative term $g_s\bar{\psi}\gamma^\mu A_\mu^a T^a\psi$ in the QCD Lagrangian, the Feynman rule becomes $-ig_s\gamma^\mu (T^a)_{ij}$ where quark color flows from $j$ to $i$ and gluon carries color $a$. The generator $(T^a)_{ij} = (\lambda^a/2)_{ij}$ is a $3\times 3$ matrix in color space. When computing matrix elements, these generators contract with each other and with external color wavefunctions, producing color factors that multiply the Lorentz-spinor amplitude structure.

### Example 3: Quark-Gluon Vertex

Derive the quark-gluon vertex from the QCD interaction Lagrangian and apply to color flow.

**Solution:**

The interaction term in the QCD Lagrangian is:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{int}} = g_s\bar{\psi}_i\gamma^\mu A_\mu^a (T^a)_{ij}\psi_j$$

where sum over repeated indices is implied. The Feynman rule in momentum space is $-i$ times the interaction:

$$\text{Vertex} = -ig_s\gamma^\mu (T^a)_{ij}$$

with quark color index $i$ (incoming), quark index $j$ (outgoing), and gluon color index $a$.

For specific example, consider $u$ quark with color red ($i=1$) emitting gluon $a=1$ (corresponding to $\lambda^1$ generator). The generator acts as:

$$(T^1)_{ij} = \frac{1}{2}(\lambda^1)_{ij} = \frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$$

This changes red quark $(i=1)$ to green quark $(j=2)$ or vice versa, with factor $1/2$.

The full amplitude for a process includes summing over all intermediate colors and gluons. For $u_i + \bar{d}_j \to u_k + \bar{d}_l$ via gluon exchange, the color factor is:

$$\sum_a (T^a)_{ik}(T^a)_{lj} = \frac{1}{2}\left(\delta_{il}\delta_{kj} - \frac{1}{3}\delta_{ij}\delta_{kl}\right)$$

using the completeness relation. For color-singlet initial state (colorless meson), this gives specific numerical factors depending on channel ($t$ vs $u$).

**Answer:** Quark-gluon vertex: $-ig_s\gamma^\mu T_{ij}^a$ connects quark colors $i,j$ and gluon $a$; determines color flow.

Three-gluon vertices are uniquely non-Abelian—they don't exist in QED because photons don't carry charge. These vertices arise from the $ef^{abc}(\partial A)AA$ cubic terms when expanding the Yang-Mills Lagrangian. The structure involves one derivative (from the field strength's linear piece) and two gauge fields, giving momentum dependence. With three external gluons labeled by momenta $k_1, k_2, k_3$ (all incoming, $k_1+k_2+k_3=0$), Lorentz indices $\mu, \nu, \rho$, and colors $a,b,c$, the vertex is:

$$V_3^{abc,\mu\nu\rho}(k_1,k_2,k_3) = g_s f^{abc}[g^{\mu\nu}(k_1-k_2)^\rho + g^{\nu\rho}(k_2-k_3)^\mu + g^{\rho\mu}(k_3-k_1)^\nu]$$

The structure constant $f^{abc}$ is totally antisymmetric, encoding the non-commutativity of the SU(3) group. The momentum factors ensure gauge invariance—contracting any external gluon's Lorentz index with its momentum (replacing polarization with momentum) gives zero, as required by Ward identities.

### Example 4: Three-Gluon Vertex Structure

Derive the three-gluon vertex from the Yang-Mills cubic term and verify gauge invariance.

**Solution:**

From the Lagrangian expansion, the cubic term is:

$$\mathcal{L}_3 = -\frac{g_s}{2}f^{abc}(\partial_\mu A_\nu^a - \partial_\nu A_\mu^a)A^{b\mu}A^{c\nu}$$

In momentum space with gluons carrying momenta $k_1,k_2,k_3$ (incoming) and Lorentz indices $\mu,\nu,\rho$:

$$V_3^{abc,\mu\nu\rho} = g_s f^{abc}[g^{\mu\nu}(k_1-k_2)^\rho + g^{\nu\rho}(k_2-k_3)^\mu + g^{\rho\mu}(k_3-k_1)^\nu]$$

The momentum factors come from Fourier transforming derivatives: $\partial_\mu \to ik_{1\mu}$, etc.

Gauge invariance check: Replace gluon $a$'s polarization $\epsilon^\mu(k_1)$ with momentum $k_1^\mu$:

$$k_{1\mu} V_3^{\mu\nu\rho} = g_s f^{abc}[k_1^\mu g_{\mu\nu}(k_1-k_2)^\rho + \text{cyclic}]$$

$$= g_s f^{abc}[(k_1)^\nu(k_1-k_2)^\rho + (k_1)^\rho(k_3-k_1)^\nu]$$

Using $k_1+k_2+k_3=0$, so $k_3 = -k_1-k_2$ and $k_1-k_2 = -k_3-2k_2$:

$$= g_s f^{abc}[(k_1)^\nu(-k_3-2k_2)^\rho + (k_1)^\rho(-2k_1-k_2)^\nu]$$

After algebraic manipulation using momentum conservation, all terms cancel: $k_{1\mu}V_3^{\mu\nu\rho} = 0$. Similarly for contracting with $k_2$ or $k_3$. This Ward identity ensures gauge invariance and ghost-gluon cancellations work properly in loops.

The antisymmetry of $f^{abc}$ means identical gluons give zero amplitude (Bose statistics).

**Answer:** Three-gluon vertex: $g_s f^{abc}[g^{\mu\nu}(k_1-k_2)^\rho + \text{cyc}]$; Ward identity $k_1 \cdot V_3 = 0$ verified.

Four-gluon vertices from the $(AA)^2$ quartic terms have no derivatives, just four gauge fields with products of structure constants. The vertex involves two structure constants contracted over a dummy index: $f^{abe}f^{cde}$ summed over $e$. The full expression is lengthy with multiple Lorentz index contractions and symmetry factors, but the key point is these vertices are $\mathcal{O}(g_s^2)$ rather than $\mathcal{O}(g_s)$ like three-gluon vertices, and they're purely non-Abelian—nothing like them exists in QED.

### Example 5: Four-Gluon Vertex from Quartic Terms

Write the four-gluon vertex structure and identify its role in loop calculations.

**Solution:**

From $(F^a)^2$ expansion, the quartic term is:

$$\mathcal{L}_4 = -\frac{g_s^2}{4}f^{abe}f^{cde}(A_\mu^b A_\nu^c A^{d\mu}A^{e\nu} - A_\mu^b A_\nu^c A^{d\nu}A^{e\mu})$$

The Feynman rule for four external gluons with colors $a,b,c,d$ and Lorentz indices $\mu,\nu,\rho,\sigma$:

$$V_4^{abcd,\mu\nu\rho\sigma} = -ig_s^2[f^{abe}f^{cde}(g^{\mu\rho}g^{\nu\sigma} - g^{\mu\sigma}g^{\nu\rho})$$
$$+ f^{ace}f^{bde}(g^{\mu\nu}g^{\rho\sigma} - g^{\mu\sigma}g^{\nu\rho})$$
$$+ f^{ade}f^{bce}(g^{\mu\nu}g^{\rho\sigma} - g^{\mu\rho}g^{\nu\sigma})]$$

The structure constant products can be simplified using Fierz-like relations in color space. For example, $f^{abe}f^{cde} = \delta^{ac}\delta^{bd}C_A - (\text{other permutations})$ where $C_A=3$ for SU(3).

In practice, four-gluon vertices contribute at tree level in processes like $gg \to gg$ (gluon-gluon scattering), though three-gluon vertices via $t$-channel and $u$-channel exchange also contribute. At loop level, they appear in corrections to propagators and vertices. The $\mathcal{O}(g_s^2)$ scaling means they're suppressed relative to three-gluon contributions in perturbative expansions.

Physical application: Pure-glue contributions to QCD running coupling at two loops include diagrams with four-gluon vertices alongside three-gluon loops.

**Answer:** Four-gluon vertex $\sim -ig_s^2 f^2 (g_{\mu\nu}g_{\rho\sigma} + \text{perms})$; quartic in fields, $\mathcal{O}(g_s^2)$, purely non-Abelian.

Faddeev-Popov ghosts emerge from path integral quantization in covariant gauges. Naive quantization of non-Abelian gauge theories overcounts configurations related by gauge transformations, leading to wrong loop results and violation of unitarity. The Faddeev-Popov procedure introduces ghost fields—scalar but with Grassmann (fermion) statistics—that exactly cancel unphysical gauge degrees of freedom in loops. Ghost propagators look like scalar propagators $i\delta^{ab}/(q^2+i\epsilon)$ but anti-commute. The ghost-gluon vertex $g_s f^{abc}q^\mu$ couples ghost $a$, antighost $b$, and gluon $c$, with the ghost momentum $q^\mu$ appearing explicitly. Ghosts only appear in loops, never as external particles (they're unphysical), but they're essential for maintaining gauge invariance and unitarity—without ghosts, amplitudes would violate probability conservation.

### Example 6: Ghost Fields and Faddeev-Popov Determinant

Explain the origin of ghosts and write their propagator and vertex.

**Solution:**

In path integral quantization, the gauge field integral overcounts due to gauge redundancy. The Faddeev-Popov method fixes the gauge by inserting:

$$1 = \Delta_{\text{FP}}[A]\int \mathcal{D}\alpha \, \delta(G(A^\alpha))$$

where $G$ is a gauge condition (e.g., Lorenz $\partial_\mu A^\mu = 0$), $A^\alpha$ is gauge-transformed field, and $\Delta_{\text{FP}}$ is the Faddeev-Popov determinant:

$$\Delta_{\text{FP}} = \det\left(\frac{\delta G(A^\alpha)}{\delta\alpha}\right)$$

For non-Abelian theories, this determinant depends on $A$ itself (unlike QED where it's field-independent). Representing $\det M$ as a Grassmann integral:

$$\det M = \int \mathcal{D}\bar{c}\mathcal{D}c \, e^{i\int \bar{c}^a M^{ab}c^b}$$

gives the ghost action:

$$S_{\text{ghost}} = \int d^4x \, \bar{c}^a(-\Box \delta^{ab} + g_s f^{abc}A_\mu^c\partial^\mu)c^b$$

The ghost propagator (from $-\Box$ term):

$$D_{\text{ghost}}^{ab}(q) = \frac{i\delta^{ab}}{q^2+i\epsilon}$$

Scalar form but with color index. The ghost-gluon vertex (from $g_s f^{abc}A\partial c$ term):

$$V_{\text{ghost-gluon}}^{abc,\mu}(q) = g_s f^{abc}q^\mu$$

where $q$ is the ghost momentum. Ghosts appear only in loops, ensuring:
- Correct number of polarizations (two, not four or three)
- Unitarity of S-matrix
- Gauge invariance of amplitudes

For Abelian QED, structure constants vanish ($f^{abc}=0$), so ghosts decouple and aren't needed.

**Answer:** Ghost propagator $i\delta^{ab}/(q^2+i\epsilon)$, vertex $g_s f^{abc}q^\mu$; ensure unitarity in covariant gauges.

Color algebra becomes essential when evaluating QCD diagrams. Simple amplitudes might involve a single gluon exchange, but most processes have multiple diagrams with different color flows. Computing cross sections requires squaring amplitudes, which involves traces over color indices using the completeness relation $\sum_a T_{ij}^a T_{kl}^a$. For quarks in the fundamental representation of SU(3), the Casimir $C_F = 4/3$ appears in many calculations. For gluons in the adjoint, $C_A = 3$ dominates. These color factors multiply the Lorentz-spinor structures and determine relative strengths of different channels.

### Example 7: Color Factor Calculation for Quark Scattering

Calculate the color factor for $q+q \to q+q$ scattering via gluon exchange.

**Solution:**

Quark-quark scattering has two channels: $t$-channel ($q_1 q_2 \to q_3 q_4$) and $u$-channel (exchange $3 \leftrightarrow 4$). Each involves one gluon exchange.

Squared amplitude for $t$-channel, averaging over initial colors:

$$\frac{1}{9}\sum_{i,j}\left|\sum_a (T^a)_{ik}(T^a)_{jl}\right|^2$$

Using $\sum_a T_{ik}^a T_{jl}^a = \frac{1}{2}(\delta_{il}\delta_{jk} - \frac{1}{3}\delta_{ik}\delta_{jl})$, the squared quantity involves:

$$\left|\frac{1}{2}(\delta_{il}\delta_{jk} - \frac{1}{3}\delta_{ik}\delta_{jl})\right|^2$$

For $k=i$, $l=j$ (forward scattering), this gives $(1/2)(1-1/3)^2 = (1/2)(2/3)^2 = 2/9$.

For generic scattering, must average over colors and sum final states, giving standard color factor $C_F^2$ combinations.

The full color-averaged squared amplitude for $qq \to qq$ is:

$$\langle|\mathcal{M}|^2\rangle_{\text{color}} = \frac{4}{9}|\mathcal{M}_{\text{Lorentz}}|^2$$

where $4/9 = C_F^2$ for $C_F = 4/3$ in SU(3).

**Answer:** Color factor for $qq \to qq$: involves $(T^a T^a)$ sums giving $C_F = 4/3$ factors; full calculation gives $4/9$ after averaging.

The complete set of QCD Feynman rules—propagators, vertices, ghosts—forms a self-consistent framework for computing any amplitude perturbatively in $\alpha_s$. Tree-level calculations test basic structure, while loop calculations require ghosts to maintain gauge invariance. Cross sections involve color averaging (sum initial, average final states) and sometimes Fierz rearrangements to simplify color algebra. Modern calculations use helicity methods and color-ordered amplitudes to streamline computations, but the fundamental Feynman rules remain the foundation.

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: B** — Quark propagator $iS_F^{ij}(p) = i(\not\!p+m)\delta^{ij}/(p^2-m^2+i\epsilon)$ has color delta $\delta^{ij}$ since strong interaction conserves color at vertices. Quarks in fundamental representation of SU(3) with colors $i,j=1,2,3$ (red,green,blue).

**Problem 2: B** — Feynman gauge ($\xi=1$): $D_{\mu\nu}^{ab}(q) = -ig_{\mu\nu}\delta^{ab}/(q^2+i\epsilon)$ has Lorentz structure $g_{\mu\nu}$ and color delta $\delta^{ab}$. General $\xi$-gauge adds $(1-\xi)q_\mu q_\nu/q^2$ piece. Physical amplitudes gauge-independent.

**Problem 3: B** — Three-gluon vertex $\sim g_s f^{abc}[g^{\mu\nu}(k_1-k_2)^\rho + \text{cyc}]$ purely non-Abelian (structure constants $f^{abc} \neq 0$). Has momentum dependence from derivatives in $(\partial A)AA$ term. Contributes at tree level (e.g., $q\bar{q} \to gg$) and loops.

**Problem 4: C** — Ghosts are unphysical Grassmann scalars ensuring unitarity and gauge invariance in covariant gauges. Appear only in loops, cancel unphysical longitudinal/timelike gauge boson polarizations. Ghost propagator $i\delta^{ab}/(q^2+i\epsilon)$ scalar form but anti-commute.

**Problem 5: C** — Fundamental representation of SU(N) has $C_F = (N^2-1)/(2N)$. For SU(3): $C_F = (9-1)/6 = 8/6 = 4/3$. Appears in quark loop calculations. Adjoint has $C_A = N = 3$ for SU(3), appears in gluon loops.
