# Quantum Field Theory Topic 2.1: Interaction Picture and Dyson Series

Free field theory is exactly solvable—particles propagate independently, and we can construct the complete energy spectrum. But nature isn't free. Particles interact: electrons scatter off photons, quarks bind into protons, the Higgs couples to matter. Understanding these interactions requires **perturbation theory**, treating the interaction Hamiltonian $\hat{H}_{int}$ as a small correction to the free theory $\hat{H}_0$. But there's a conceptual obstacle: in the Schrödinger picture, states evolve while operators stay fixed, whereas in the Heisenberg picture, operators evolve while states remain constant. Neither is convenient for perturbation theory. The solution is the **interaction picture**, a hybrid where both states and operators evolve—states carry the interaction dynamics, while operators evolve freely.

The interaction picture leads to the **Dyson series**, an expansion of the time evolution operator $\hat{U}(t, t_0)$ in powers of $\hat{H}_{int}$. This series, when extended to infinite past and future with adiabatic switching, defines the **S-matrix** (scattering matrix) $\hat{S}$—the fundamental object encoding all scattering amplitudes. Each term in the Dyson series corresponds to a specific number of interactions, and computing S-matrix elements reduces to evaluating time-ordered products of interaction Hamiltonians. This framework underlies all perturbative calculations in quantum field theory, from QED to the Standard Model.

This article develops the interaction picture from its definition through the Dyson series to the S-matrix. We'll construct the interaction picture operators, derive the time evolution operator, expand it perturbatively, understand time ordering, apply the formalism to $\phi^4$ theory, implement adiabatic switching, and see how the S-matrix encodes scattering processes.

## Interaction Picture Definition

The interaction picture interpolates between Schrödinger and Heisenberg pictures, separating free evolution from interaction dynamics.

### Example 1: Interaction Picture Operators

Define interaction picture field operators and their evolution.

**Solution:**

Full Hamiltonian: $\hat{H} = \hat{H}_0 + \hat{H}_{int}$

**Interaction picture field**:
$$\hat{\phi}_I(t) = e^{i\hat{H}_0 t}\hat{\phi}_S e^{-i\hat{H}_0 t}$$

where $\hat{\phi}_S$ is the Schrödinger picture field (time-independent).

Time evolution:
$$i\frac{d\hat{\phi}_I}{dt} = [\hat{\phi}_I, \hat{H}_0]$$

This is **free field evolution**—$\hat{\phi}_I$ evolves as if there were no interactions.

For Klein-Gordon field:
$$\hat{\phi}_I(\mathbf{x}, t) = \int\frac{d^3k}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\omega_k}}[\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k}}e^{i(\mathbf{k} \cdot \mathbf{x} - \omega_k t)} + \hat{a}^\dagger_{\mathbf{k}}e^{-i(\mathbf{k} \cdot \mathbf{x} - \omega_k t)}]$$

(Same form as free field, but now embedded in interacting theory.)

**Interaction Hamiltonian** in interaction picture:
$$\hat{H}_{int,I}(t) = e^{i\hat{H}_0 t}\hat{H}_{int,S}e^{-i\hat{H}_0 t}$$

**Answer:** Interaction picture: $\hat{\phi}_I = e^{i\hat{H}_0 t}\hat{\phi}_S e^{-i\hat{H}_0 t}$ (operators evolve with $\hat{H}_0$).

### Example 2: State Evolution in Interaction Picture

Derive the evolution equation for states in the interaction picture.

**Solution:**

**Interaction picture state**:
$$|\psi_I(t)\rangle = e^{i\hat{H}_0 t}|\psi_S(t)\rangle$$

Time evolution of Schrödinger state: $i\frac{d}{dt}|\psi_S\rangle = \hat{H}|\psi_S\rangle$

$$i\frac{d|\psi_I\rangle}{dt} = i\frac{d}{dt}\left(e^{i\hat{H}_0 t}|\psi_S\rangle\right)$$

$$= -\hat{H}_0 e^{i\hat{H}_0 t}|\psi_S\rangle + e^{i\hat{H}_0 t}i\frac{d|\psi_S\rangle}{dt}$$

$$= -\hat{H}_0|\psi_I\rangle + e^{i\hat{H}_0 t}\hat{H}|\psi_S\rangle$$

$$= -\hat{H}_0|\psi_I\rangle + e^{i\hat{H}_0 t}(\hat{H}_0 + \hat{H}_{int})e^{-i\hat{H}_0 t}|\psi_I\rangle$$

$$= \hat{H}_{int,I}(t)|\psi_I\rangle$$

**Evolution equation**:
$$i\frac{d|\psi_I(t)\rangle}{dt} = \hat{H}_{int,I}(t)|\psi_I(t)\rangle$$

States evolve due to interactions only. This is the key equation of the interaction picture.

**Answer:** State evolution: $i\frac{d|\psi_I\rangle}{dt} = \hat{H}_{int,I}(t)|\psi_I\rangle$ (interactions drive dynamics).

## Time Evolution Operator and Dyson Series

### Example 3: Time Evolution Operator

Construct the time evolution operator in the interaction picture.

**Solution:**

Define:
$$|\psi_I(t)\rangle = \hat{U}(t, t_0)|\psi_I(t_0)\rangle$$

From $i\frac{d|\psi_I\rangle}{dt} = \hat{H}_{int,I}(t)|\psi_I\rangle$:

$$i\frac{d\hat{U}(t, t_0)}{dt} = \hat{H}_{int,I}(t)\hat{U}(t, t_0)$$

with boundary condition $\hat{U}(t_0, t_0) = \mathbb{1}$.

Formal solution:
$$\hat{U}(t, t_0) = \mathbb{1} + (-i)\int_{t_0}^t dt_1 \, \hat{H}_{int,I}(t_1)\hat{U}(t_1, t_0)$$

This is an **integral equation**. Iterating:

$$\hat{U}(t, t_0) = \mathbb{1} + (-i)\int_{t_0}^t dt_1 \, \hat{H}_{int,I}(t_1)$$

$$+ (-i)^2\int_{t_0}^t dt_1\int_{t_0}^{t_1} dt_2 \, \hat{H}_{int,I}(t_1)\hat{H}_{int,I}(t_2) + \cdots$$

**Dyson series**: Perturbative expansion in powers of $\hat{H}_{int}$.

**Answer:** Time evolution $\hat{U}(t, t_0)$ satisfies $i\frac{d\hat{U}}{dt} = \hat{H}_{int,I}(t)\hat{U}$, expanded perturbatively.

### Example 4: Time-Ordered Exponential

Express the Dyson series as a time-ordered exponential.

**Solution:**

The nested time integrals can be written compactly using **time ordering**.

Define time-ordering operator $T$:
$$T[\hat{A}(t_1)\hat{B}(t_2)] = \begin{cases}
\hat{A}(t_1)\hat{B}(t_2) & t_1 > t_2 \\
\hat{B}(t_2)\hat{A}(t_1) & t_2 > t_1
\end{cases}$$

The Dyson series becomes:

$$\hat{U}(t, t_0) = T\exp\left[-i\int_{t_0}^t dt' \, \hat{H}_{int,I}(t')\right]$$

$$= \sum_{n=0}^\infty\frac{(-i)^n}{n!}\int_{t_0}^t dt_1 \cdots \int_{t_0}^t dt_n \, T[\hat{H}_{int,I}(t_1)\cdots\hat{H}_{int,I}(t_n)]$$

Time ordering ensures operators at later times appear to the left.

**Physical interpretation**:
- $n=0$: No interactions (free propagation)
- $n=1$: Single interaction
- $n=2$: Two interactions, properly time-ordered
- Higher orders: Multiple scatterings

**Answer:** Dyson series: $\hat{U}(t, t_0) = T\exp[-i\int_{t_0}^t \hat{H}_{int,I}(t')dt']$ (time-ordered exponential).

## S-Matrix and Adiabatic Switching

### Example 5: S-Matrix Definition

Define the S-matrix and its physical interpretation.

**Solution:**

**S-matrix**: Evolution from infinite past to infinite future.

$$\hat{S} = \hat{U}(\infty, -\infty) = T\exp\left[-i\int_{-\infty}^\infty dt \, \hat{H}_{int,I}(t)\right]$$

S-matrix elements:
$$S_{fi} = \langle f|\hat{S}|i\rangle$$

where $|i\rangle$ and $|f\rangle$ are free particle states (asymptotic states).

**Physical meaning**:
- $|S_{fi}|^2$ = probability for transition $i \to f$
- Diagonal: $S_{ii} = \langle i|\hat{S}|i\rangle$ (no scattering)
- Off-diagonal: $S_{fi}$ ($f \neq i$) encodes scattering amplitude

For scattering process $|i\rangle \to |f\rangle$:
$$S_{fi} = \delta_{fi} - i(2\pi)^4\delta^4(p_i - p_f)\mathcal{M}_{fi}$$

where $\mathcal{M}_{fi}$ is the **invariant amplitude** (Lorentz scalar).

**Unitarity**: $\hat{S}^\dagger\hat{S} = \mathbb{1}$ (probability conservation).

**Answer:** S-matrix $\hat{S} = \hat{U}(\infty, -\infty)$ with $S_{fi} = \langle f|\hat{S}|i\rangle$ (scattering amplitude).

### Example 6: Adiabatic Switching

Implement adiabatic switching to handle infinite time limits.

**Solution:**

**Problem**: $\hat{H}_{int}$ constant from $t = -\infty$ leads to divergences.

**Solution**: Introduce slowly varying envelope:
$$\hat{H}_{int}(t) \to \hat{H}_{int}(t)e^{-\epsilon|t|}$$

where $\epsilon \to 0^+$ after calculations.

Modified S-matrix:
$$\hat{S}_\epsilon = T\exp\left[-i\int_{-\infty}^\infty dt \, e^{-\epsilon|t|}\hat{H}_{int,I}(t)\right]$$

**Physical interpretation**:
- Interactions "turned on" adiabatically as $t \to -\infty$
- States in far past/future are free particles
- $\epsilon \to 0$: Recover true S-matrix

Advantages:
1. Well-defined integrals (convergent)
2. Separates bound states from scattering states
3. Regularizes vacuum energy contributions

In practice, $\epsilon$ appears in propagators: $i/(p^2 - m^2 + i\epsilon)$.

**Answer:** Adiabatic switching: $\hat{H}_{int} \to e^{-\epsilon|t|}\hat{H}_{int}$ ensures convergence, $\epsilon \to 0^+$ at end.

### Example 7: φ⁴ Theory Example

Apply the Dyson series to $\phi^4$ theory.

**Solution:**

Lagrangian: $\mathcal{L} = \frac{1}{2}\partial_\mu\phi\partial^\mu\phi - \frac{1}{2}m^2\phi^2 - \frac{\lambda}{4!}\phi^4$

Interaction Hamiltonian:
$$\hat{H}_{int} = \int d^3x \, \frac{\lambda}{4!}\hat{\phi}^4(\mathbf{x})$$

First-order term in Dyson series:
$$\hat{S}^{(1)} = -i\int d^4x \, \frac{\lambda}{4!}\hat{\phi}_I^4(x)$$

This represents a **four-point vertex**: four scalar fields meeting at spacetime point $x$.

Coupling constant: $\lambda$ (dimensionless for $d=4$ spacetime dimensions).

Second-order:
$$\hat{S}^{(2)} = \frac{(-i)^2}{2!}\int d^4x_1 d^4x_2 \, \left(\frac{\lambda}{4!}\right)^2 T[\hat{\phi}_I^4(x_1)\hat{\phi}_I^4(x_2)]$$

Two vertices connected by propagators. Time ordering $T$ ensures causality.

This expansion generates all Feynman diagrams for $\phi^4$ scattering.

**Answer:** φ⁴ theory: $\hat{H}_{int} = \int d^3x \frac{\lambda}{4!}\phi^4$, Dyson series generates multi-vertex processes.

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: B**
Interaction picture operators: Evolve with $\hat{H}_0$
$\hat{\phi}_I(t) = e^{i\hat{H}_0 t}\hat{\phi}_S e^{-i\hat{H}_0 t}$
Free evolution for operators: $i\frac{d\hat{\phi}_I}{dt} = [\hat{\phi}_I, \hat{H}_0]$
States evolve with $\hat{H}_{int,I}$: $i\frac{d|\psi_I\rangle}{dt} = \hat{H}_{int,I}|\psi_I\rangle$
Hybrid picture separating free/interaction dynamics

**Problem 2: B**
Dyson series: Perturbative expansion in $\hat{H}_{int}$
$\hat{U} = \sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{(-i)^n}{n!}\int T[\hat{H}_{int}(t_1)\cdots\hat{H}_{int}(t_n)]$
Each order: $n$ interactions
Valid when $\hat{H}_{int} \ll \hat{H}_0$ (weak coupling)
Foundation for Feynman diagram expansion

**Problem 3: B**
S-matrix: Maps asymptotic free states
$|i\rangle_{t \to -\infty}$ (far past) to $|f\rangle_{t \to +\infty}$ (far future)
$S_{fi} = \langle f|\hat{S}|i\rangle$ (scattering amplitude)
States are free particles (no interactions) at $t = \pm\infty$
Contains all scattering information

**Problem 4: B**
Time ordering: Handles non-commuting operators
$[\hat{\phi}(t_1), \hat{\phi}(t_2)] \neq 0$ for $t_1 \neq t_2$
$T$ arranges later times to left
Essential for Dyson series consistency
Ensures causal propagation in diagrams

**Problem 5: C**
Adiabatic switching: $\hat{H}_{int} \to e^{-\epsilon|t|}\hat{H}_{int}$
Regularizes $\int_{-\infty}^\infty$ (makes convergent)
$\epsilon \to 0^+$ at end of calculation
Separates bound states from continuum
Leads to $i\epsilon$ prescription in propagators
