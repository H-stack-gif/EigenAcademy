# Quantum Field Theory Topic 4.2: Euclidean Path Integrals

The Minkowski path integral $\int \mathcal{D}\phi \, e^{iS[\phi]}$ is a highly oscillatory integral—the phase $e^{iS}$ rotates rapidly, making numerical evaluation nearly impossible and even the mathematical definition subtle. But there's a powerful trick: **Wick rotation** to imaginary time $t \to -i\tau$ transforms the oscillatory Minkowski integral into a **convergent Euclidean integral** $\int \mathcal{D}\phi \, e^{-S_E[\phi]}$ with a real, positive weight function. This isn't just a mathematical convenience—it reveals a profound connection between quantum field theory and statistical mechanics. The Euclidean path integral is precisely the partition function of a statistical field theory at temperature $T = \hbar/(k_B\beta)$. This connection enables Monte Carlo simulations of QCD on the lattice, provides non-perturbative definitions of quantum field theories, and allows the study of **instantons**—non-perturbative field configurations that mediate tunneling between topologically distinct vacua.

The Wick rotation $t = -i\tau$ analytically continues from Minkowski spacetime (signature $+---$) to Euclidean spacetime (signature $++++$). The action transforms as $S_M = \int d^4x \, \mathcal{L}_M \to S_E = \int d^4x_E \, \mathcal{L}_E$ where the Euclidean Lagrangian has the form of energy density in statistical mechanics. Correlation functions become **Schwinger functions** satisfying reflection positivity—a key property ensuring unitarity when analytically continued back to Minkowski space. Temperature is introduced through **periodic boundary conditions** in imaginary time with period $\beta = 1/(k_B T)$. This formalism is essential for studying QCD at finite temperature (quark-gluon plasma), computing non-perturbative effects, and implementing lattice gauge theory—the only known method for computing hadron masses from first principles.

This article develops Euclidean path integrals from Wick rotation through statistical mechanics connections to lattice field theory. We'll perform the Wick rotation explicitly, derive the Euclidean action, prove convergence properties, connect to statistical partition functions, introduce thermal field theory via periodic imaginary time, examine instantons as Euclidean classical solutions, preview lattice QFT, and understand how Euclidean methods enable non-perturbative calculations.

## Wick Rotation and Euclidean Action

Wick rotation transforms oscillatory Minkowski integrals into convergent Euclidean integrals through analytic continuation in time.

### Example 1: Wick Rotation Procedure

Perform the Wick rotation for a scalar field.

**Solution:**

**Minkowski metric**: $g_{\mu\nu} = \text{diag}(1, -1, -1, -1)$, signature $(+---)$

**Wick rotation**: $t \to -i\tau$ (imaginary time)

Define Euclidean time: $x^0 = t \to x^0_E = -it = \tau$ (takes $t \in \mathbb{R}$ to $\tau \in \mathbb{R}$)

Spatial coordinates unchanged: $\mathbf{x}_E = \mathbf{x}$

**Euclidean metric**: $\delta_{\mu\nu} = \text{diag}(1, 1, 1, 1)$, signature $(++++)$

All directions equivalent (no distinguished time direction).

**Integration measure**:
$$d^4x = dt \, d^3x \to d^4x_E = d\tau \, d^3x = -i \, dt \, d^3x$$

Thus: $\int d^4x \to i\int d^4x_E$

**Derivatives**:
$$\partial_t \to \partial_{-it} = i\partial_\tau, \quad \partial_i \to \partial_i$$

**Action transformation**:
$$S_M = \int d^4x \, \mathcal{L}_M \to i\int d^4x_E \, \mathcal{L}_E = iS_E$$

**Path integral**:
$$Z_M = \int \mathcal{D}\phi \, e^{iS_M} \to Z_E = \int \mathcal{D}\phi \, e^{-S_E}$$

Oscillatory weight $e^{iS}$ becomes damping weight $e^{-S_E}$ (convergent!).

**Answer:** Wick rotation $t \to -i\tau$ gives $e^{iS_M} \to e^{-S_E}$, transforming oscillatory to convergent integral.

### Example 2: Euclidean Scalar Field Action

Derive the Euclidean action for a Klein-Gordon field.

**Solution:**

**Minkowski action**:
$$S_M = \int d^4x \left[\frac{1}{2}(\partial_t\phi)^2 - \frac{1}{2}(\nabla\phi)^2 - \frac{1}{2}m^2\phi^2\right]$$

$$= \int d^4x \left[\frac{1}{2}\partial_\mu\phi\partial^\mu\phi - \frac{1}{2}m^2\phi^2\right]$$

**After Wick rotation** $t \to -i\tau$:
$$(\partial_t\phi)^2 \to (i\partial_\tau\phi)^2 = -(\partial_\tau\phi)^2$$

**Euclidean action**:
$$S_E = \int d^4x_E \left[\frac{1}{2}(\partial_\tau\phi)^2 + \frac{1}{2}(\nabla\phi)^2 + \frac{1}{2}m^2\phi^2\right]$$

$$= \int d^4x_E \left[\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\mu=1}^4(\partial_\mu\phi)^2 + \frac{1}{2}m^2\phi^2\right]$$

All positive signs! This is energy-like (bounded below).

**Euclidean Lagrangian**:
$$\mathcal{L}_E = \frac{1}{2}(\partial_\mu\phi)^2 + \frac{1}{2}m^2\phi^2$$

Looks like potential energy (no kinetic-potential difference).

**Path integral weight**: $e^{-S_E}$ favors configurations with small action (minimum action principle, but now truly minimum).

**Convergence**: Integral convergent for reasonable field configurations (Gaussian decay at large $\phi$).

**Answer:** Euclidean action $S_E = \int d^4x_E[\frac{1}{2}(\partial_\mu\phi)^2 + \frac{1}{2}m^2\phi^2]$ (all positive, energy-like).

### Example 3: Euclidean Propagator

Compute the propagator in Euclidean space.

**Solution:**

**Equation of motion**: $(-\partial_\tau^2 - \nabla^2 + m^2)\phi = 0$

Or: $(-\partial_\mu\partial_\mu + m^2)\phi = 0$ (Euclidean d'Alembertian $\partial_\mu\partial_\mu = \partial_\tau^2 + \nabla^2$)

**Euclidean propagator** $D_E(x_E - y_E)$ satisfies:
$$(-\partial_\mu\partial_\mu + m^2)D_E(x_E - y_E) = \delta^4(x_E - y_E)$$

**Momentum space**:
$$D_E(p_E) = \frac{1}{p_E^2 + m^2}$$

where $p_E^2 = p_\tau^2 + \mathbf{p}^2$ (Euclidean norm, all positive).

No $i\epsilon$ prescription needed—propagator already well-defined!

**Position space** (radial symmetry):
$$D_E(r) = \frac{1}{4\pi^2 r^2}K_1(mr)$$

where $K_1$ is modified Bessel function (exponential decay at large $r$).

**Analytic continuation back**: Replace $p_\tau = ip_0$ to get Minkowski propagator.
$$D_M(p) = \frac{i}{p_0^2 - \mathbf{p}^2 - m^2 + i\epsilon}$$

**Answer:** Euclidean propagator $D_E(p_E) = \frac{1}{p_E^2 + m^2}$ (well-defined, no $i\epsilon$).

## Connection to Statistical Mechanics

Euclidean path integrals are identical in form to partition functions in statistical mechanics, revealing deep connections between QFT and stat mech.

### Example 4: Euclidean Path Integral as Partition Function

Show the equivalence between Euclidean QFT and statistical field theory.

**Solution:**

**Statistical mechanics**: Partition function for classical field $\phi(\mathbf{x})$ with energy functional $E[\phi]$:
$$Z_{\text{stat}} = \int \mathcal{D}\phi \, e^{-\beta E[\phi]}$$

where $\beta = 1/(k_B T)$.

**Euclidean QFT**: After Wick rotation:
$$Z_E = \int \mathcal{D}\phi \, e^{-S_E[\phi]}$$

where $S_E = \int d^4x_E \, \mathcal{L}_E$.

**Identification**: Set $\mathcal{L}_E \leftrightarrow$ energy density, $\int d\tau \leftrightarrow \beta$

**d-dimensional QFT** $\leftrightarrow$ **d-dimensional statistical field theory**

Example: 2D Euclidean QFT = 2D classical stat mech (Ising model, etc.)

**Correlation functions**:
- QFT: $\langle\phi(x)\phi(y)\rangle_E = \frac{1}{Z_E}\int\mathcal{D}\phi \, \phi(x)\phi(y)e^{-S_E}$
- Stat mech: $\langle\phi(x)\phi(y)\rangle_{\text{thermal}}$ (thermal expectation)

Identical mathematical structure!

**Physical implications**:
- Critical phenomena in stat mech ↔ Massless QFT (scale invariance)
- Phase transitions ↔ Vacuum structure
- Renormalization group (both theories)

**Answer:** Euclidean QFT path integral = Statistical mechanics partition function (mathematical equivalence).

### Example 5: Finite Temperature Field Theory

Introduce temperature through periodic imaginary time.

**Solution:**

**Thermal field theory**: QFT at temperature $T > 0$.

**Wick rotation**: $t \to -i\tau$ with **periodic boundary conditions**:
$$\phi(\tau + \beta, \mathbf{x}) = \phi(\tau, \mathbf{x})$$

where $\beta = 1/(k_B T)$ (inverse temperature).

**Interpretation**: Imaginary time circle of circumference $\beta$.

**Partition function**:
$$Z(\beta) = \text{Tr}[e^{-\beta \hat{H}}] = \int \mathcal{D}\phi \, e^{-S_E[\phi]}$$

with periodic $\tau \in [0, \beta]$.

**Matsubara frequencies**: Fourier modes in time direction quantized:
$$\phi(\tau, \mathbf{x}) = \frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{n=-\infty}^\infty e^{-i\omega_n\tau}\tilde{\phi}_n(\mathbf{x})$$

where $\omega_n = 2\pi n/\beta$ (bosons) or $\omega_n = (2n+1)\pi/\beta$ (fermions).

**High/low temperature limits**:
- $T \to \infty$ ($\beta \to 0$): Thermal wavelength shorter than Compton wavelength, classical limit
- $T \to 0$ ($\beta \to \infty$): Ground state dominates, vacuum QFT

**Applications**: QCD at finite temperature (quark-gluon plasma), early universe cosmology.

**Answer:** Finite temperature: Periodic imaginary time $\phi(\tau+\beta) = \phi(\tau)$ with $\beta = 1/(k_BT)$.

## Instantons and Non-Perturbative Effects

Euclidean formulation enables study of non-perturbative field configurations—instantons—that mediate tunneling.

### Example 6: Instantons in Euclidean Field Theory

Introduce instantons as classical Euclidean solutions.

**Solution:**

**Instanton**: Classical solution to Euclidean equations of motion with finite action.

**Euclidean equation**: $\frac{\delta S_E}{\delta\phi} = 0$

$$-\partial_\mu\partial_\mu\phi + V'(\phi) = 0$$

**Boundary conditions**: $\phi(\mathbf{x}_E) \to \phi_{\text{vac}}$ as $|\mathbf{x}_E| \to \infty$

(Approaches vacuum at spatial infinity.)

**Action**: $S_E[\phi_{\text{inst}}] = S_0$ (finite, classical value)

**Example**: Double-well potential $V(\phi) = \lambda(\phi^2 - v^2)^2$

Vacuum: $\phi = \pm v$ (two degenerate minima)

Instanton: Solution interpolating between $\phi(-\infty) = -v$ and $\phi(+\infty) = +v$ in Euclidean time.

**Physical interpretation**: 
- Minkowski: Tunneling between vacua (forbidden classically)
- Euclidean: Classical path connecting vacua
- Path integral: $e^{-S_E[\text{inst}]} = e^{-S_0}$ contribution

**Non-perturbative**: Action $S_0 \sim 1/\lambda$ → contribution $e^{-S_0/\lambda} \not\sim \lambda^n$ (not power series in $\lambda$).

Essential singularity at $\lambda = 0$—invisible in perturbation theory!

**Answer:** Instantons: Finite-action Euclidean solutions, mediate vacuum tunneling, non-perturbative $\sim e^{-S_0/g^2}$.

### Example 7: Lattice QFT Preview

Introduce lattice field theory as discretized Euclidean path integral.

**Solution:**

**Lattice regularization**: Replace continuous spacetime with discrete lattice.

**Euclidean lattice**: Points $n = (n_0, n_1, n_2, n_3)$ with spacing $a$ (lattice cutoff).

**Field**: $\phi_n \equiv \phi(x_E = na)$ (values at lattice sites)

**Derivatives**: $\partial_\mu\phi \to \frac{\phi_{n+\hat{\mu}} - \phi_n}{a}$ (finite differences)

**Action**:
$$S_E^{\text{latt}} = a^4\sum_n\left[\frac{1}{2}\sum_\mu\left(\frac{\phi_{n+\hat{\mu}} - \phi_n}{a}\right)^2 + \frac{1}{2}m^2\phi_n^2\right]$$

**Path integral**:
$$Z = \prod_n\int d\phi_n \, e^{-S_E^{\text{latt}}}$$

Product over finite number of sites (finite-dimensional integral—well-defined!).

**Continuum limit**: $a \to 0$ (remove cutoff), recover continuum QFT.

**Advantages**:
1. UV-regulated (maximum momentum $\sim 1/a$)
2. Non-perturbative (can compute numerically via Monte Carlo)
3. Gauge theories natural (Wilson loops, plaquettes)

**Applications**: **Lattice QCD** computes hadron masses, QCD phase diagram, confined spectrum from first principles.

**Answer:** Lattice QFT: Discretized Euclidean path integral on lattice, UV-regulated, enables numerical calculations.

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: D**
Wick rotation: $t \to -i\tau$ or $x^0 = t \to x^0_E = \tau = it$
Imaginary time: $\tau \in \mathbb{R}$ (real Euclidean time)
Convention varies: Some use $t \to it$, equivalent up to sign
Euclidean metric: $(++++)$ signature
Analytic continuation from Minkowski

**Problem 2: B**
Weight: $e^{iS_M} \to e^{iS_E \cdot i} = e^{-S_E}$
Real, positive (or negative if $S_E < 0$)
Convergent integral (Gaussian-like)
No longer oscillatory
Statistical mechanics interpretation

**Problem 3: C**
Euclidean QFT = Statistical field theory
Same mathematical structure
$Z_E = \int \mathcal{D}\phi e^{-S_E}$ (partition function)
$S_E \leftrightarrow \beta E$ (action ↔ energy)
Critical phenomena ↔ Massless fields

**Problem 4: B**
Temperature: Periodic imaginary time
$\phi(\tau + \beta) = \phi(\tau)$ with $\beta = 1/(k_BT)$
Imaginary time circle circumference $\beta$
High $T$: Small $\beta$ (short circle)
Low $T$: Large $\beta$ (long circle, $T \to 0$ recovers vacuum)

**Problem 5: C**
Instantons: Finite-action classical Euclidean solutions
$-\partial_\mu\partial_\mu\phi + V'(\phi) = 0$ in Euclidean space
Interpolate between degenerate vacua
Non-perturbative: $e^{-S_0/g^2}$ contribution
Invisible in perturbation theory (essential singularity)
