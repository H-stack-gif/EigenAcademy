# Quantum Field Theory Topic 3.3: Ward Identity and Gauge Invariance

Gauge invariance isn't just a symmetry—it's a **constraint** that profoundly restricts the form of scattering amplitudes. The **Ward identity** (and its generalized form, the Ward-Takahashi identity) expresses this constraint mathematically: amplitudes must vanish when external photon polarizations are replaced by their momenta. This isn't merely a consistency check; it ensures that only physical transverse photon polarizations contribute to observables, guarantees charge conservation to all orders in perturbation theory, and protects the photon from acquiring mass through quantum corrections. Ward identities are so powerful that they determine the structure of vertex corrections, constrain form factors, and provide non-perturbative relations between different Green's functions.

The physical content of Ward identities is profound. They embody the statement that gauge transformations are **unphysical**—observable quantities cannot depend on our choice of gauge-fixing parameter $\xi$. Consider Compton scattering: if we compute the amplitude in Feynman gauge and in Landau gauge, we get different intermediate expressions, but the final S-matrix element (contracted with physical polarization vectors) is **identical**. This gauge-independence extends to all orders: loop corrections respect Ward identities order-by-order, ensuring the photon remains massless and charge is conserved despite quantum fluctuations. The Ward-Takahashi identity generalizes this to off-shell Green's functions, relating vertex functions to propagator inverses—a relation that survives non-perturbative effects and remains exact in the full quantum theory.

This article develops Ward identities from their simplest form through gauge-independence checks to the generalized Ward-Takahashi relations. We'll prove the basic Ward identity, understand its physical meaning, verify gauge-independence of scattering amplitudes, derive charge conservation from Ward identities, extend to off-shell Ward-Takahashi identities, examine implications for photon mass, and see how gauge invariance constrains the entire structure of QED.

## Basic Ward Identity

The simplest Ward identity relates tree-level amplitudes to conservation laws, arising directly from gauge invariance.

### Example 1: Ward Identity for Tree-Level Vertex

Prove the Ward identity for the QED vertex at tree level.

**Solution:**

Consider amplitude with external photon of momentum $k$ and polarization $\epsilon^\mu$:
$$\mathcal{M}^\mu = \bar{u}(p')(-ie\gamma^\mu)u(p)$$

where $p' = p + k$ (momentum conservation).

**Ward identity** states:
$$k_\mu\mathcal{M}^\mu = 0$$

**Proof**:
$$k_\mu\mathcal{M}^\mu = -ie\bar{u}(p')\not\!k u(p)$$

Using Dirac equations:
- $(i\not\!p' - m)\bar{u}(p') = 0$ → $\bar{u}(p')\not\!p' = m\bar{u}(p')$
- $(i\not\!p - m)u(p) = 0$ → $\not\!p u(p) = mu(p)$

We need:
$$\bar{u}(p')\not\!k u(p) = \bar{u}(p')(\not\!p + \not\!k - \not\!p)u(p) = m\bar{u}(p')u(p) - \bar{u}(p')mu(p) = 0$$

using $\bar{u}(p')\not\!p' = m\bar{u}(p')$ and $\not\!p u(p) = mu(p)$.

**Physical interpretation**: Replacing polarization $\epsilon^\mu$ with momentum $k^\mu$ gives zero—gauge transformation of photon field doesn't change physics.

**Answer:** Ward identity $k_\mu\mathcal{M}^\mu = 0$ follows from on-shell Dirac equations, ensures gauge invariance.

### Example 2: Physical Meaning of Ward Identity

Explain the physical content of the Ward identity.

**Solution:**

**Gauge transformation** of photon field:
$$A^\mu(x) \to A^\mu(x) + \partial^\mu\chi(x)$$

In momentum space: $\tilde{A}^\mu(k) \to \tilde{A}^\mu(k) + k^\mu\tilde{\chi}(k)$

Polarization vector shifts: $\epsilon^\mu \to \epsilon^\mu + \alpha k^\mu$ for arbitrary $\alpha$.

**Ward identity**: $k_\mu\mathcal{M}^\mu = 0$ ensures amplitude unchanged.

$$\mathcal{M}(\epsilon + \alpha k) = \mathcal{M}(\epsilon) + \alpha k_\mu\mathcal{M}^\mu = \mathcal{M}(\epsilon) + 0 = \mathcal{M}(\epsilon)$$

**Physical polarizations**: Transverse $k \cdot \epsilon = 0$ (only two physical states).

**Unphysical polarizations**: Longitudinal $\epsilon^\mu \propto k^\mu$ and timelike $\epsilon^0$ decouple by Ward identity.

**Consequence**: S-matrix depends only on physical polarizations—gauge choice irrelevant for observables.

**Example**: In Compton scattering, replace one photon polarization with its momentum—amplitude vanishes.

**Answer:** Ward identity ensures gauge transformations don't affect physical amplitudes, only transverse polarizations contribute.

## Gauge Independence of Amplitudes

Physical amplitudes must be independent of gauge-fixing parameter $\xi$ appearing in photon propagator.

### Example 3: Gauge Independence Check

Verify gauge independence for a simple scattering process.

**Solution:**

Consider tree-level process with one internal photon (e.g., $e^-e^- \to e^-e^-$ Møller scattering).

**Photon propagator** (general gauge):
$$D^{\mu\nu}_F(k) = \frac{-i}{k^2 + i\epsilon}\left[g^{\mu\nu} - (1-\xi)\frac{k^\mu k^\nu}{k^2}\right]$$

**Amplitude structure**:
$$\mathcal{M} = \bar{u}(p_3)\Gamma^\mu u(p_1) \cdot D_{\mu\nu}(k) \cdot \bar{u}(p_4)\Gamma^\nu u(p_2)$$

where $\Gamma^\mu = -ie\gamma^\mu$ (vertex).

**Gauge-dependent term**:
$$(1-\xi)\frac{k^\mu k^\nu}{k^2}\bar{u}(p_3)\Gamma^\mu u(p_1) \cdot \bar{u}(p_4)\Gamma^\nu u(p_2)$$

$$= (1-\xi)\frac{1}{k^2}[k_\mu\bar{u}(p_3)\Gamma^\mu u(p_1)][k_\nu\bar{u}(p_4)\Gamma^\nu u(p_2)]$$

By **Ward identity**: Each bracket vanishes (on-shell external particles).

$$k_\mu\bar{u}(p_3)\Gamma^\mu u(p_1) = 0, \quad k_\nu\bar{u}(p_4)\Gamma^\nu u(p_2) = 0$$

Therefore, gauge-dependent term vanishes!

**Result**: $\mathcal{M}$ independent of $\xi$ for physical (on-shell) external states.

**Answer:** Gauge-dependent terms in propagator canceled by Ward identity, amplitudes gauge-independent.

### Example 4: Charge Conservation from Ward Identity

Derive charge conservation from the Ward identity.

**Solution:**

**Ward identity** (tree-level): $k_\mu\mathcal{M}^\mu = 0$ for amplitude with photon momentum $k$.

Consider $e^-(p) \to e^-(p') + \gamma(k)$ (impossible kinematically but formal).

$$\mathcal{M}^\mu = \bar{u}(p')(-ie\gamma^\mu)u(p)$$

Ward identity:
$$k_\mu\mathcal{M}^\mu = -ie\bar{u}(p')\not\!k u(p) = 0$$

This follows from $k = p' - p$ and Dirac equations, implying **momentum conservation**.

More generally, for any process with photon emission:

Ward identity → Current conservation $\partial_\mu j^\mu = 0$ → Charge conservation $dQ/dt = 0$

**Multi-photon**: Ward identity for each photon ensures total charge conserved.

**Quantum corrections**: Ward identities hold to all orders → charge conservation exact in full quantum theory.

**Physical consequence**: Electric charge cannot change through interactions, electrons always carry charge $-e$.

**Answer:** Ward identity $k_\mu\mathcal{M}^\mu = 0$ encodes current conservation $\partial_\mu j^\mu = 0$, ensuring charge conservation.

## Ward-Takahashi Identity

The generalized Ward-Takahashi identity relates off-shell vertex functions to propagators, providing non-perturbative constraints.

### Example 5: Off-Shell Ward-Takahashi Identity

Derive the Ward-Takahashi identity for the one-particle irreducible vertex.

**Solution:**

**Full vertex function** $\Gamma^\mu(p', p)$: Sum of all 1PI diagrams with two fermion legs and one photon.

At tree level: $\Gamma^\mu_0 = \gamma^\mu$

With corrections: $\Gamma^\mu(p', p) = \gamma^\mu + \text{loops}$

**Full fermion propagator**: $S_F(p) = [i\not\!p - m - \Sigma(p)]^{-1}$

where $\Sigma(p)$ is self-energy.

**Ward-Takahashi identity**:
$$q_\mu\Gamma^\mu(p', p) = S_F^{-1}(p') - S_F^{-1}(p)$$

where $q = p' - p$ (photon momentum).

**Proof sketch**: Consider gauge transformation of generating functional, derive relation between vertex and propagator corrections.

At tree level ($\Gamma^\mu = \gamma^\mu$, $S_F^{-1} = i\not\!p - m$):
$$q_\mu\gamma^\mu = \not\!q = (i\not\!p' - m) - (i\not\!p - m)$$ ✓

**Physical meaning**: Vertex corrections and self-energy corrections related—both constrained by gauge invariance.

**Non-perturbative**: Holds exactly, not just to any finite order in $\alpha$.

**Answer:** Ward-Takahashi: $q_\mu\Gamma^\mu(p', p) = S_F^{-1}(p') - S_F^{-1}(p)$ (relates vertex to propagators, exact).

### Example 6: Photon Mass and Ward Identity

Explain why Ward identities ensure the photon remains massless.

**Solution:**

**Photon self-energy**: Loop corrections to photon propagator.

One-loop vacuum polarization $\Pi^{\mu\nu}(k)$ modifies propagator:
$$D^{\mu\nu}_F(k) = \frac{-i}{k^2}\left[g^{\mu\nu} - \frac{k^\mu k^\nu}{k^2}\right] + \text{corrections}$$

**Full propagator**:
$$D^{\mu\nu}_F(k) = \frac{-i}{k^2 - \Pi(k^2)}\left[g^{\mu\nu} - \frac{k^\mu k^\nu}{k^2}\right]$$

where $\Pi(k^2)$ is Lorentz-invariant part of $\Pi^{\mu\nu}$.

**Ward identity** for vacuum polarization:
$$k_\mu\Pi^{\mu\nu}(k) = 0$$

This constrains structure: $\Pi^{\mu\nu}(k) = (k^2 g^{\mu\nu} - k^\mu k^\nu)\Pi(k^2)$

**Photon mass**: Would shift denominator to $k^2 - m_\gamma^2$.

But Ward identity forces $\Pi^{\mu\nu}$ structure → no mass term can be generated!

**Result**: $\Pi(0) = 0$ (photon pole at $k^2 = 0$), photon remains massless to all orders.

**Physical consequence**: Long-range Coulomb force $V \propto 1/r$ preserved (massive photon would give Yukawa $V \propto e^{-m_\gamma r}/r$).

**Answer:** Ward identity constrains vacuum polarization structure, forbids photon mass generation, ensures $m_\gamma = 0$ exactly.

### Example 7: Application to Form Factors

Use Ward identities to constrain electromagnetic form factors.

**Solution:**

**Electron-photon vertex** with momentum transfer $q = p' - p$:

Most general decomposition:
$$\Gamma^\mu(p', p) = \gamma^\mu F_1(q^2) + \frac{i\sigma^{\mu\nu}q_\nu}{2m}F_2(q^2)$$

where $F_1, F_2$ are **form factors** and $\sigma^{\mu\nu} = \frac{i}{2}[\gamma^\mu, \gamma^\nu]$.

**Ward-Takahashi identity**: $q_\mu\Gamma^\mu = S_F^{-1}(p') - S_F^{-1}(p)$

For on-shell fermions: $S_F^{-1}(p) = i\not\!p - m = 0$

$$q_\mu\Gamma^\mu(p', p) = 0$$

Computing left side:
$$q_\mu\left[\gamma^\mu F_1 + \frac{i\sigma^{\mu\nu}q_\nu}{2m}F_2\right] = \not\!q F_1 + 0$$

(Second term vanishes: $q_\mu\sigma^{\mu\nu}q_\nu = 0$ by antisymmetry.)

Using $\not\!q = \not\!p' - \not\!p = 0$ on-shell:

Requires: $F_1(q^2 = 0) = 1$ (**charge normalization**)

**Physical meaning**: Form factor $F_1$ must be unity at zero momentum transfer (static limit) to preserve charge.

$F_2$ unconstrained at $q^2 = 0$—gives **anomalous magnetic moment** $a_e = F_2(0)$.

**Answer:** Ward identity constrains $F_1(0) = 1$ (charge normalization), allows $F_2 \neq 0$ (anomalous moment).
