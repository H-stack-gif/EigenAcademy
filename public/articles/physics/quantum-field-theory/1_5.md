# Quantum Field Theory Topic 1.5: Quantization of EM Field

The electromagnetic field presents unique quantization challenges. Unlike scalar or Dirac fields, the four-component vector potential $A_\mu$ contains redundancy—gauge transformations $A_\mu \to A_\mu + \partial_\mu\chi$ leave physics unchanged. This **gauge freedom** isn't just mathematical pedantry; it's essential for describing massless spin-1 particles. A massive vector field has three polarization states, but the photon has only two. The missing degree of freedom is "eaten" by gauge invariance, reducing four components to two physical polarizations. Quantization must respect this structure while maintaining Lorentz covariance.

Two approaches handle gauge quantization: **Coulomb gauge** breaks manifest Lorentz invariance but clearly separates physical from unphysical degrees of freedom, making canonical quantization straightforward. **Covariant quantization** (Gupta-Bleuler) maintains Lorentz covariance by allowing all four components of $A_\mu$ as operators, at the cost of introducing an indefinite metric Hilbert space with negative norm states. Physical states satisfy subsidiary conditions that eliminate these ghosts. Both approaches yield identical S-matrix elements—the gauge-invariant observables.

This article develops EM field quantization from gauge fixing through mode expansion to photon creation operators. We'll impose Coulomb gauge, count physical degrees of freedom, construct commutation relations, build the Hamiltonian, understand photon states, examine polarization vectors, address the Casimir effect for photons, and preview covariant quantization.

## Coulomb Gauge Quantization

The Coulomb gauge $\nabla \cdot \mathbf{A} = 0$ eliminates longitudinal photons and simplifies quantization while breaking manifest Lorentz covariance.

### Example 1: Coulomb Gauge Condition

Implement the Coulomb gauge and count degrees of freedom.

**Solution:**

Maxwell Lagrangian:
$$\mathcal{L} = -\frac{1}{4}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu} = \frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{E}^2 - \mathbf{B}^2)$$

with $\mathbf{E} = -\nabla A^0 - \dot{\mathbf{A}}$ and $\mathbf{B} = \nabla \times \mathbf{A}$.

**Coulomb gauge**: $\nabla \cdot \mathbf{A} = 0$ (transversality condition).

Degrees of freedom:
- Start: 4 components $(A^0, \mathbf{A})$
- Gauge freedom: Eliminates 1 (choose $\chi$ to set $\nabla \cdot \mathbf{A} = 0$)
- Equation of motion: $\Box A^0 = 0$ → set $A^0 = 0$ for free field
- Physical: 2 transverse polarizations

For plane wave $\mathbf{A} \sim \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_\lambda e^{i\mathbf{k} \cdot \mathbf{x}}$:
$$\mathbf{k} \cdot \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_\lambda = 0 \quad (\lambda = 1, 2)$$

Two orthonormal polarization vectors $\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_1, \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_2$ perpendicular to $\mathbf{k}$.

**Answer:** Coulomb gauge $\nabla \cdot \mathbf{A} = 0$ leaves 2 transverse polarizations (physical photons).

### Example 2: Mode Expansion in Coulomb Gauge

Write the vector potential mode expansion.

**Solution:**

Free field ($A^0 = 0$ in Coulomb gauge):

$$\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{x}, t) = \sum_{\mathbf{k}, \lambda}\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2\omega_k V\epsilon_0}}\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\mathbf{k}, \lambda}[\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k}, \lambda}e^{i(\mathbf{k} \cdot \mathbf{x} - \omega_k t)} + \hat{a}^\dagger_{\mathbf{k}, \lambda}e^{-i(\mathbf{k} \cdot \mathbf{x} - \omega_k t)}]$$

where:
- $\omega_k = c|\mathbf{k}|$ (massless dispersion)
- $\lambda = 1, 2$ labels two polarizations
- $\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\mathbf{k}, \lambda}$ are transverse: $\mathbf{k} \cdot \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\mathbf{k}, \lambda} = 0$
- Orthonormality: $\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\mathbf{k}, \lambda} \cdot \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\mathbf{k}, \lambda'} = \delta_{\lambda\lambda'}$

Completeness (transverse subspace):
$$\sum_{\lambda=1,2}\epsilon^i_{\mathbf{k}, \lambda}\epsilon^{j*}_{\mathbf{k}, \lambda} = \delta^{ij} - \frac{k^i k^j}{k^2}$$

(Projects onto plane perpendicular to $\mathbf{k}$.)

**Answer:** Mode expansion $\mathbf{A} = \sum_{\mathbf{k}, \lambda}\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2\omega_k V\epsilon_0}}\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\mathbf{k}, \lambda}[\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k}, \lambda}e^{i\mathbf{k} \cdot \mathbf{x}}e^{-i\omega_k t} + \text{h.c.}]$.

## Commutation Relations and Hamiltonian

### Example 3: Photon Creation and Annihilation Operators

Derive the commutation relations for photon operators.

**Solution:**

Canonical momentum (in Coulomb gauge):
$$\pi^i(\mathbf{x}, t) = \frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\dot{A}^i} = \epsilon_0 \dot{A}^i = \epsilon_0 \mathbf{E}^i$$

Canonical commutators:
$$[\hat{A}^i(\mathbf{x}, t), \hat{\pi}^j(\mathbf{y}, t)] = i\hbar\delta^{ij}_T(\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{y})$$

where $\delta^{ij}_T = \delta^{ij} - \frac{\partial^i\partial^j}{\nabla^2}$ is the transverse delta function (projects onto $\nabla \cdot \mathbf{A} = 0$).

From mode expansion:

$$[\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k}, \lambda}, \hat{a}^\dagger_{\mathbf{k}', \lambda'}] = \delta_{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{k}'}\delta_{\lambda\lambda'}$$

$$[\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k}, \lambda}, \hat{a}_{\mathbf{k}', \lambda'}] = 0, \quad [\hat{a}^\dagger_{\mathbf{k}, \lambda}, \hat{a}^\dagger_{\mathbf{k}', \lambda'}] = 0$$

Identical to scalar field—**photons are bosons** (commutation, not anticommutation).

**Answer:** Photon operators $[\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k}, \lambda}, \hat{a}^\dagger_{\mathbf{k}', \lambda'}] = \delta_{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{k}'}\delta_{\lambda\lambda'}$ (bosonic commutation).

### Example 4: Photon Hamiltonian

Express the Hamiltonian in terms of photon number operators.

**Solution:**

Energy:
$$H = \frac{1}{2}\int d^3x \, (\epsilon_0 E^2 + \frac{1}{\mu_0}B^2)$$

Substituting mode expansions:

$$\hat{H} = \sum_{\mathbf{k}, \lambda}\hbar\omega_k\left(\hat{a}^\dagger_{\mathbf{k}, \lambda}\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k}, \lambda} + \frac{1}{2}\right)$$

where $\omega_k = c|\mathbf{k}|$ (massless).

Each mode: quantum harmonic oscillator with frequency $\omega_k$.

**Normal ordering**:
$$:\hat{H}: = \sum_{\mathbf{k}, \lambda}\hbar\omega_k\hat{a}^\dagger_{\mathbf{k}, \lambda}\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k}, \lambda}$$

Photon number operator: $\hat{N}_{\mathbf{k}, \lambda} = \hat{a}^\dagger_{\mathbf{k}, \lambda}\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k}, \lambda}$

Energy eigenvalues:
$$E = \sum_{\mathbf{k}, \lambda}\hbar\omega_k n_{\mathbf{k}, \lambda}$$

where $n_{\mathbf{k}, \lambda} = 0, 1, 2, \ldots$ (photons are bosons, no restriction).

**Answer:** Photon Hamiltonian $:\hat{H}: = \sum_{\mathbf{k}, \lambda}\hbar\omega_k\hat{N}_{\mathbf{k}, \lambda}$ (sum over two polarizations).

## Photon States and Polarizations

### Example 5: Single-Photon States

Construct and characterize single-photon states.

**Solution:**

**Vacuum**: $\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k}, \lambda}|0\rangle = 0$ for all $\mathbf{k}, \lambda$.

**One-photon state**:
$$|\mathbf{k}, \lambda\rangle = \hat{a}^\dagger_{\mathbf{k}, \lambda}|0\rangle$$

Properties:
- Energy: $E = \hbar\omega_k = \hbar c|\mathbf{k}|$
- Momentum: $\mathbf{p} = \hbar\mathbf{k}$
- Massless: $E = pc$ (dispersion $\omega = c|\mathbf{k}|$)
- Polarization: $\lambda = 1, 2$ (transverse)
- Spin: 1 (vector boson)
- Helicity: $\pm 1$ (circular polarizations)

**Circular polarizations** (eigenstates of helicity):
$$\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_\pm = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_1 \pm i\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_2)$$

Helicity $h = \pm 1$: projection of spin onto momentum direction.

**Linear polarizations**: $\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_1, \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_2$ (superpositions of $\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_\pm$).

**Multi-photon states**:
$$|\mathbf{k}_1, \lambda_1; \mathbf{k}_2, \lambda_2; \ldots\rangle = \hat{a}^\dagger_{\mathbf{k}_1, \lambda_1}\hat{a}^\dagger_{\mathbf{k}_2, \lambda_2}\cdots|0\rangle$$

Symmetric (bosons).

**Answer:** Single photon $|\mathbf{k}, \lambda\rangle = \hat{a}^\dagger_{\mathbf{k}, \lambda}|0\rangle$ with energy $\hbar c|\mathbf{k}|$, two polarizations.

### Example 6: Photon Casimir Effect

Relate the Casimir effect to photon vacuum energy.

**Solution:**

Vacuum energy for EM field:
$$E_0 = \sum_{\mathbf{k}, \lambda}\frac{\hbar\omega_k}{2}$$

Factor of 2 from two polarizations.

Between parallel plates (Casimir setup):
- Boundary conditions modify allowed modes
- $k_\perp$ quantized: $k_z = n\pi/a$ ($n = 1, 2, 3, \ldots$)

Energy per unit area:
$$\frac{E(a)}{A} = 2 \times \frac{1}{2}\sum_n\int\frac{dk_x dk_y}{(2\pi)^2}\hbar c\sqrt{k_x^2 + k_y^2 + (n\pi/a)^2}$$

After regularization:
$$E(a)/A = -\frac{\pi^2\hbar c}{720a^3}$$

Casimir force:
$$F/A = -\frac{\partial E/\partial a}{A} = -\frac{\pi^2\hbar c}{240a^4}$$

**Measurable**: Agrees with experiment (Lamoreaux 1997: within 5%).

Purely quantum: $F \propto \hbar$ (vanishes classically).

**Answer:** Photon Casimir force $F/A = -\frac{\pi^2\hbar c}{240a^4}$ from vacuum energy modification.

### Example 7: Covariant Quantization Preview

Outline the Gupta-Bleuler method for covariant quantization.

**Solution:**

**Problem with Coulomb gauge**: Breaks manifest Lorentz covariance.

**Gupta-Bleuler approach**:
1. Keep all four components $A_\mu$ as operators
2. Use Lorenz gauge $\partial_\mu A^\mu = 0$ (Lorentz covariant)
3. Impose commutation: $[A_\mu, A_\nu] \sim g_{\mu\nu}$ (indefinite metric!)
4. This introduces **negative norm states** (unphysical "ghosts")

**Solution**: Physical states satisfy:
$$(\partial_\mu A^\mu)^{(+)}|\text{phys}\rangle = 0$$

where $(+)$ denotes positive frequency part.

This eliminates ghosts while maintaining covariance.

**Result**: Physical subspace has positive norm, gauge-invariant S-matrix.

Advantage: Manifestly Lorentz covariant calculations.

Disadvantage: More complex (negative norm intermediate states).

Both Coulomb and Gupta-Bleuler give identical physical predictions.

**Answer:** Covariant quantization: Keep all $A_\mu$, use Lorenz gauge, impose physical state conditions to eliminate ghosts.

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: C**
Coulomb gauge: $\nabla \cdot \mathbf{A} = 0$
Transverse polarizations only: 2
Start with 4 components $A_\mu$
Gauge freedom eliminates 1 (longitudinal)
Equation of motion: $A^0 = 0$ for free field
Physical: 2 polarizations (massless spin-1)

**Problem 2: B**
Photons: Bosons (integer spin $s = 1$)
Commutation relations $[\hat{a}, \hat{a}^\dagger] = 1$
Occupation $n = 0, 1, 2, 3, \ldots$ (no restriction)
Bose-Einstein statistics
Laser: Many photons in same mode (coherent state)

**Problem 3: B**
Massless photon: $\omega_k = c|\mathbf{k}|$
Linear dispersion (not relativistic $\sqrt{k^2 + m^2}$)
$E = pc$ (massless particle relation)
Travels at speed of light $c$
Different from massive particles

**Problem 4: B**
Massless spin-1: Helicity $h = \pm 1$
Projection of spin onto momentum
Circular polarizations: $\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_\pm$ (left/right)
No $h = 0$ (longitudinal eliminated by gauge)
Two states (not three like massive spin-1)

**Problem 5: D**
Casimir: Attractive force $F \propto -1/a^4$
From photon vacuum energy between plates
Quantum effect ($F \propto \hbar$, vanishes classically)
Measured experimentally (Lamoreaux 1997, within 5%)
Boundary conditions modify vacuum energy
