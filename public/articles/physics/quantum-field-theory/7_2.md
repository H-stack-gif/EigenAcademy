# Quantum Field Theory Topic 7.2: Goldstone's Theorem

Continuous global symmetries, when spontaneously broken, mandate the existence of massless excitations—**Goldstone bosons**—representing the system's ability to move freely along the vacuum manifold without energy cost. Consider a field theory invariant under continuous global transformations $\phi \to e^{i\alpha^a Q^a}\phi$ where $Q^a$ are symmetry generators satisfying Lie algebra $[Q^a, Q^b] = if^{abc}Q^c$. If the vacuum breaks this symmetry by acquiring a VEV $\langle 0|\phi|0\rangle = v \neq 0$ such that $Q^a v \neq 0$ for some generator $Q^a$, then that generator is spontaneously broken, and Goldstone's theorem guarantees one massless scalar boson corresponding to each broken generator. The proof relies on conserved currents $j^\mu_a$ associated with global symmetries via Noether's theorem, whose conservation $\partial_\mu j^\mu_a = 0$ combined with non-vanishing vacuum charge $Q^a = \int d^3x\, j^0_a$ acting on the vacuum produces a pole at $q^2 = 0$ in the current-current correlation function, signifying a massless particle.

The vacuum manifold's geometry directly counts Goldstones. For a symmetry group $G$ spontaneously broken to subgroup $H$, the vacuum manifold is the coset space $G/H$, whose dimension equals the number of broken generators and thus the number of Goldstones. Examples:

- U(1) → nothing: 1 Goldstone (complex scalar case)
- SU(2) → U(1): 2 Goldstones (dimension of $S^2$ sphere)  
- SU(3) → SU(2): 5 Goldstones (dimension of $S^5$ sphere)

Physical realizations include pions as approximate Goldstones from chiral symmetry breaking in QCD where $SU(2)_L \times SU(2)_R \to SU(2)_V$, magnons in ferromagnets from broken spin rotation symmetry, and phonons in crystals from broken translational symmetry.

### Example 1: Noether Current and Charge for Global U(1)

Derive the conserved current for global U(1) symmetry $\phi \to e^{i\alpha}\phi$ and compute the charge.

**Solution:**

The Lagrangian $\mathcal{L} = |\partial_\mu\phi|^2 - V(|\phi|^2)$ is invariant under $\phi \to e^{i\alpha}\phi$. The infinitesimal transformation:

$$\delta\phi = i\alpha\phi, \quad \delta\phi^* = -i\alpha\phi^*$$

Noether's theorem gives the conserved current:

$$j^\mu = \frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi)}\delta\phi + \frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi^*)}\delta\phi^*$$

Computing:

$$\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi)} = \partial^\mu\phi^*, \quad \frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi^*)} = \partial^\mu\phi$$

$$j^\mu = \partial^\mu\phi^* \cdot i\alpha\phi + \partial^\mu\phi \cdot (-i\alpha\phi^*) = i\alpha(\phi^*\partial^\mu\phi - \phi\partial^\mu\phi^*)$$

Dropping the constant $\alpha$:

$$j^\mu = i(\phi^*\partial^\mu\phi - \phi\partial^\mu\phi^*)$$

This is conserved: $\partial_\mu j^\mu = 0$ (verify using equations of motion).

The conserved charge:

$$Q = \int d^3x\, j^0 = i\int d^3x\, (\phi^*\dot{\phi} - \phi\dot{\phi}^*)$$

For $\phi = (v+h+i\chi)/\sqrt{2}$:

$$Q = \int d^3x\, \frac{1}{2}[((v+h) - i\chi)(\dot{h} + i\dot{\chi}) - ((v+h) + i\chi)(\dot{h} - i\dot{\chi})]$$

$$= \int d^3x\, [(v+h)\dot{\chi} + \chi\dot{h}] \approx \int d^3x\, [v\dot{\chi} + \mathcal{O}(\text{fluct}^2)]$$

The vacuum $\phi_0 = v/\sqrt{2}$ breaks this symmetry: $Q|0\rangle \neq 0$ since $Q$ acts like $\partial_\chi$, creating Goldstone quanta.

**Answer:** Noether current $j^\mu = i(\phi^*\partial^\mu\phi - \phi\partial^\mu\phi^*)$ conserved; charge $Q = \int d^3x\,v\dot{\chi}$ non-zero on vacuum.

The formal proof of Goldstone's theorem proceeds through Ward identities and correlation functions. The key steps:

1. Global symmetry → Conserved current $\partial_\mu j^\mu_a = 0$ (Noether)
2. SSB → Vacuum not invariant: $Q^a|0\rangle \neq 0$ for broken $Q^a$  
3. Correlation function: $\langle 0|[j^0_a(x), \phi(y)]|0\rangle = i\delta^3(\vec{x}-\vec{y})Q^a\langle\phi\rangle$
4. Insert complete states, Fourier transform
5. Pole at $q^2 = 0$ → massless particle (Goldstone)

The logic is: if current conservation holds but vacuum charge doesn't annihilate the vacuum, there must exist intermediate states $|G\rangle$ accessible at zero energy cost, which are the Goldstones.

### Example 2: Goldstone's Theorem via Pole Analysis

Sketch the proof of massless Goldstone from current correlation function.

**Solution:**

Consider the two-point function of conserved currents:

$$\Pi^{\mu\nu}(q) = i\int d^4x\, e^{iq \cdot x}\langle 0|T[j^\mu(x)j^\nu(0)]|0\rangle$$

Current conservation $\partial_\mu j^\mu = 0$ implies:

$$q_\mu \Pi^{\mu\nu}(q) = 0, \quad \Pi^{\mu\nu}(q)q_\nu = 0$$

Decompose using tensor structure:

$$\Pi^{\mu\nu}(q) = (q^\mu q^\nu - g^{\mu\nu}q^2)\Pi(q^2)$$

Now consider the commutator $[Q, \phi]$ where $Q = \int d^3x\, j^0$ is the charge:

$$\langle 0|[\phi(x), Q]|0\rangle = \langle 0|\phi(x)Q|0\rangle - \langle 0|Q\phi(x)|0\rangle$$

If $Q|0\rangle \neq 0$ (SSB), then $\langle 0|\phi Q|0\rangle \neq 0$. The current-field commutator:

$$\langle 0|[j^0(x), \phi(0)]|0\rangle = i\delta^3(\vec{x})\langle\phi\rangle$$

Fourier transforming and inserting complete states:

$$\sum_n \frac{\langle 0|j^0|n\rangle\langle n|\phi|0\rangle}{E_n} \propto \langle\phi\rangle \neq 0$$

As $E_n \to 0$, this sum must have a contribution from massless states $|G\rangle$ (Goldstones). The pole structure:

$$\Pi^{00}(q) \sim \frac{F^2}{q^2}\quad \text{as } q^2 \to 0$$

signals massless particle with decay constant $F$ (pion decay constant analogue).

**Answer:** Current conservation + SSB ($Q|0\rangle \neq 0$) → Pole at $q^2 = 0$ in correlation function → Massless Goldstone boson.

For non-Abelian groups, the counting is more intricate. The vacuum picks a direction in group space, breaking generators that don't leave the vacuum invariant while preserving those that do. The unbroken subgroup $H$ consists of generators $T^a$ satisfying $T^a v = 0$, while broken generators $T^b$ have $T^b v \neq 0$. The number of Goldstones equals $\dim(G) - \dim(H) = \dim(G/H)$.

### Example 3: SU(2) Breaking to U(1)

For SU(2) symmetry broken to U(1), count Goldstone bosons and identify the vacuum manifold.

**Solution:**

Consider a complex doublet transforming under SU(2):

$$\Phi = \begin{pmatrix} \phi_1 \\ \phi_2 \end{pmatrix}, \quad \Phi \to e^{i\alpha^a \sigma^a/2}\Phi$$

with potential $V(\Phi) = -\mu^2\Phi^\dagger\Phi + \lambda(\Phi^\dagger\Phi)^2$. The minimum occurs at:

$$\Phi^\dagger\Phi = \frac{v^2}{2}$$

Choose vacuum (aligned with $\sigma^3$ direction):

$$\Phi_0 = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ v/\sqrt{2} \end{pmatrix}$$

This breaks SU(2) to U(1). Check generators:

$$T^3\Phi_0 = \frac{\sigma^3}{2}\begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ v/\sqrt{2} \end{pmatrix} = \frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ v/\sqrt{2} \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ -v/(2\sqrt{2}) \end{pmatrix} \propto \Phi_0$$

So $T^3$ is unbroken (leaves vacuum invariant up to phase).

$$T^1\Phi_0 = \frac{\sigma^1}{2}\begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ v/\sqrt{2} \end{pmatrix} = \frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ v/\sqrt{2} \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} v/(2\sqrt{2}) \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} \not\propto \Phi_0$$

$T^1$ broken! Similarly $T^2$ broken.

Number of Goldstones: $\dim(\text{SU}(2)) - \dim(\text{U}(1)) = 3 - 1 = 2$.

Vacuum manifold: All $\Phi$ with $|\Phi|^2 = v^2/2$, which is $S^3$ (3-sphere in 4D real space). Fixing U(1) phase reduces to $S^3/S^1 \cong S^2$ (2-sphere), confirming 2-dimensional manifold → 2 Goldstones.

**Answer:** SU(2) → U(1): Two broken generators ($T^1, T^2$), vacuum manifold $S^2$, **two Goldstones**.

Physical examples abound. In QCD, the approximate $SU(2)_L \times SU(2)_R$ chiral symmetry (exact for massless $u,d$ quarks) breaks spontaneously to vector $SU(2)_V$ (isospin), producing three (nearly) massless Goldstones: $\pi^+, \pi^0, \pi^-$. Their small masses $m_\pi \approx 140$ MeV arise from explicit symmetry breaking by quark masses. The pion decay constant $f_\pi \approx 92$ MeV relates to the QCD scale $\Lambda_{\text{QCD}}$.

### Example 4: Pions as Approximate Goldstones

Explain how pions emerge from chiral symmetry breaking in QCD.

**Solution:**

For massless $u,d$ quarks, QCD Lagrangian has chiral symmetry $SU(2)_L \times SU(2)_R$:

$$\mathcal{L} = \bar{q}_L i\not\!D q_L + \bar{q}_R i\not\!D q_R - \frac{1}{4}F^a_{\mu\nu}F^{a\mu\nu}$$

where $q_L = P_L q$, $q_R = P_R q$, and $q = (u, d)^T$ is the quark doublet. The symmetry:

$$q_L \to e^{i\alpha^a_L \sigma^a/2}q_L, \quad q_R \to e^{i\alpha^a_R \sigma^a/2}q_R$$

independent rotations on left- and right-handed quarks.

Non-perturbative dynamics generate quark condensate:

$$\langle\bar{q}q\rangle = \langle\bar{q}_L q_R + \bar{q}_R q_L\rangle \neq 0$$

This breaks $SU(2)_L \times SU(2)_R \to SU(2)_V$ (vector, diagonal subgroup). The breaking pattern:

$$\dim(SU(2)_L \times SU(2)_R) - \dim(SU(2)_V) = (3 + 3) - 3 = 3$$

Three Goldstones: $\pi^+, \pi^0, \pi^-$ (pion triplet).

The pion decay constant:

$$\langle 0|j^\mu_a|\pi^b(p)\rangle = i\delta^{ab}f_\pi p^\mu$$

with $f_\pi \approx 92$ MeV. Actual pions have mass $m_\pi \approx 140$ MeV from explicit breaking by quark masses:

$$m_\pi^2 \sim \frac{m_u + m_d}{f_\pi^2}\langle\bar{q}q\rangle$$

in chiral perturbation theory. The ratio $m_\pi/f_\pi \sim 1.5$ confirms pions as pseudo-Goldstones (approximate).

**Answer:** Chiral $SU(2)_L \times SU(2)_R \to SU(2)_V$ broken by $\langle\bar{q}q\rangle$; three Goldstones (pions) with $f_\pi \approx 92$ MeV.

Goldstone bosons couple derivatively, suppressed by the symmetry-breaking scale. The effective Lagrangian for Goldstones $\pi^a$ takes the form:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{eff}} = \frac{f^2}{4}\text{Tr}[\partial_\mu U\partial^\mu U^\dagger] + \cdots$$

where $U = \exp(i\pi^a T^a/f)$ is the non-linear realization and $f$ is the decay constant. Expanding in powers of $\pi/f$ gives derivative couplings $\sim (\partial\pi)^2/f^2$, explaining why Goldstones interact weakly at low energies—the **Adler zero**: pion scattering amplitudes vanish as all momenta → 0.

### Example 5: Effective Lagrangian for Goldstones

Write the leading-order effective Lagrangian for Goldstone bosons and discuss derivative expansion.

**Solution:**

For $n$ Goldstones $\pi^a$ (a = 1, ..., n) from breaking $G \to H$, the effective theory is:

$$\mathcal{L} = \frac{f^2}{2}(\partial_\mu\pi^a)(\partial^\mu\pi^a) + \frac{c_1}{f^2}(\partial_\mu\pi^a\partial^\mu\pi^a)(\partial_\nu\pi^b\partial^\nu\pi^b) + \cdots$$

The leading term has two derivatives (canonical kinetic), next has four derivatives (suppressed by $1/f^2$).

For non-Abelian case, use exponential parametrization:

$$U(\pi) = \exp\left(\frac{i\pi^a T^a}{f}\right)$$

where $T^a$ are broken generators. The Lagrangian:

$$\mathcal{L} = \frac{f^2}{4}\text{Tr}[\partial_\mu U \partial^\mu U^\dagger]$$

Expanding $U \approx 1 + i\pi^a T^a/f - \pi^a\pi^b T^aT^b/(2f^2) + \cdots$:

$$\partial_\mu U \approx \frac{i}{f}\partial_\mu\pi^a T^a - \frac{1}{2f^2}\pi^a\partial_\mu\pi^b T^aT^b + \cdots$$

$$\mathcal{L} = \frac{1}{2}(\partial_\mu\pi^a)^2 + \frac{1}{4f^2}\pi^a\pi^b(\partial_\mu\pi^c)^2[\text{Tr}(T^aT^bT^cT^c)] + \cdots$$

Every additional Goldstone field comes with $1/f$ suppression. At low energy $E \ll f$:

$$\mathcal{M}(\pi\pi \to \pi\pi) \sim \frac{E^2}{f^2}$$

vanishes as $E \to 0$ (Adler zero).

**Answer:** Leading effective Lagrangian $\mathcal{L} = \frac{f^2}{2}(\partial\pi)^2$; higher-derivative terms suppressed by $1/f^2$, giving weak low-energy interactions.

The generalization to fermions involves non-linear realizations. When global symmetries acting on fermions break, Goldstones couple through covariant derivatives $D_\mu\psi = \partial_\mu\psi + \Gamma_\mu\psi$ where $\Gamma_\mu \sim (\partial_\mu\pi)/f$ contains Goldstone fields. This produces interactions like $\bar{\psi}\gamma^\mu\psi(\partial_\mu\pi)/f$, giving pion-nucleon couplings in nuclear physics.

### Example 6: Goldstone Coupling to Matter Fields

Derive how Goldstones couple to fermions in the non-linear sigma model.

**Solution:**

Consider fermions $\psi$ transforming under $G$ broken to $H$. Introduce Goldstone matrix $U(\pi) = e^{i\pi^a T^a/f}$. The fermion transforms:

$$\psi \to g\psi, \quad U \to gUh^\dagger$$

where $g \in G$, $h(g,\pi) \in H$ (compensating transformation).

Define covariant derivative:

$$D_\mu\psi = \partial_\mu\psi + i\Gamma_\mu\psi$$

where $\Gamma_\mu = \frac{i}{2}(U^\dagger\partial_\mu U + U\partial_\mu U^\dagger)$ transforms as $H$ connection.

Expanding $U \approx 1 + i\pi^a T^a/f$:

$$\Gamma_\mu \approx \frac{1}{f}\partial_\mu\pi^a T^a + \frac{i}{2f^2}[\pi^a, \partial_\mu\pi^b]T^aT^b + \cdots$$

The fermion kinetic term:

$$\bar{\psi}i\gamma^\mu D_\mu\psi = \bar{\psi}i\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu\psi + \frac{1}{f}\bar{\psi}\gamma^\mu\psi \partial_\mu\pi^a T^a + \cdots$$

This gives derivative coupling $\sim \bar{\psi}\gamma^\mu\psi \partial_\mu\pi/f$, as in pion-nucleon interactions with coupling $g_{\pi NN}/m_N \sim 1/f_\pi$.

For nucleons ($N = p,n$):

$$\mathcal{L}_{\pi N} = \frac{g_A}{2f_\pi}\bar{N}\gamma^\mu\gamma_5\tau^a N \partial_\mu\pi^a$$

where $g_A \approx 1.27$ is the axial coupling constant, $\tau^a$ are isospin matrices.

**Answer:** Goldstone-fermion coupling $\sim \bar{\psi}\gamma^\mu\psi \partial_\mu\pi/f$ (derivative, suppressed by $f$); pion-nucleon $\sim g_A\bar{N}\gamma^\mu\gamma_5\tau \partial\pi/(2f_\pi)$.

The number of Goldstones can be subtle when symmetries are gauged or when multiple fields participate. For Coleman-Mandula theorem considerations, spacetime symmetries (Poincaré) and internal symmetries cannot be non-trivially combined in relativistic QFT, except through supersymmetry (Haag-Lopuszanski-Sohnius extension). Goldstone's theorem plays a role in classifying possible symmetry realizations.

### Example 7: Counting Goldstones for General Breaking

For symmetry breaking $G \to H$, derive the number of Goldstones from the vacuum manifold.

**Solution:**

The vacuum manifold consists of all field configurations related by $G$ transformations that minimize the potential. If $v$ is one vacuum, the full manifold is:

$$\mathcal{M}_{\text{vac}} = \{gv : g \in G\}$$

Two vacua $g_1 v$ and $g_2 v$ are equivalent if related by unbroken symmetry $h \in H$:

$$g_2 v = h(g_1 v) \quad \Rightarrow \quad g_2 = hg_1$$

The equivalence classes form the coset space:

$$\mathcal{M}_{\text{vac}} \cong G/H$$

The dimension of this manifold equals the number of independent directions one can move in vacuum space without cost—each is a Goldstone:

$$N_G = \dim(G/H) = \dim(G) - \dim(H)$$

For example:

SU(3) → SU(2): $\dim(SU(3)) = 8$, $\dim(SU(2)) = 3$, so $N_G = 5$ Goldstones.

SU(3) → SU(2)×U(1): $\dim = 8 - (3+1) = 4$ Goldstones.

SO(3) → SO(2): $\dim = 3 - 1 = 2$ Goldstones (magnons in 3D ferromagnet).

Each broken generator corresponds to one Goldstone. The generators $T^a$ of $G$ split into:
- Unbroken: $T^a v = 0$ (elements of $\mathfrak{h}$, Lie algebra of $H$)
- Broken: $T^a v \neq 0$ (complement generators)

Number broken = $N_G$.

**Answer:** Number of Goldstones $N_G = \dim(G) - \dim(H) = \dim(G/H)$, one per broken generator; vacuum manifold is coset space.

The universality of Goldstone's theorem makes it one of the most powerful results in theoretical physics, applicable across condensed matter (superfluidity, superconductivity, magnetism), particle physics (chiral symmetry, electroweak breaking), and cosmology (axions, domain walls). The only way to avoid massless Goldstones is either to explicitly break the symmetry (giving small masses $\propto$ explicit breaking) or to gauge the symmetry (Higgs mechanism, next topic).

The theorem's robustness extends even to strongly-coupled theories where perturbation theory fails. Lattice QCD simulations confirm pion properties from chiral symmetry breaking despite QCD's non-perturbative nature at low energies. The Goldstone counting remains exact: three broken generators of $SU(2)_L \times SU(2)_R / SU(2)_V$ yield exactly three pions, verified numerically through correlation functions computed on spacetime lattices. This demonstrates Goldstone's theorem transcends weak-coupling approximations.

Modern applications include **axions**—hypothetical Goldstones from Peccei-Quinn symmetry breaking proposed to solve the strong CP problem in QCD. The axion decay constant $f_a$ sets the scale, with $f_a \sim 10^{12}$ GeV giving axion mass $m_a \sim 10^{-5}$ eV from explicit breaking by QCD instantons. If they exist, axions could constitute dark matter, detectable through conversion to photons in strong magnetic fields (ADMX experiment). The axion demonstrates how Goldstone physics connects particle physics to cosmology.

Another profound application involves **Nambu-Goldstone modes in condensed matter**. Superfluids exhibit broken U(1) symmetry from Bose-Einstein condensation, with phonons as Goldstones. Helium-4 below $T_\lambda = 2.17$ K flows without viscosity, the superfluid velocity field related to the Goldstone phase gradient $\vec{v}_s = (\hbar/m)\nabla\theta$. Quantized vortices carry circulation in units of $h/m$, direct manifestation of Goldstone mode topology. Similarly, superconductors break gauge symmetry with Cooper pairs, though photon mass from Higgs mechanism modifies the naive Goldstone picture (Anderson-Higgs mechanism).

The connection to topology deepens through homotopy theory. The vacuum manifold's topology determines defect structures: for $G/H \cong S^1$, line defects (vortices) can exist; for $G/H \cong S^2$, point defects (monopoles) occur; for $G/H \cong S^3$, textures form. These topological defects could have been produced during cosmological phase transitions in the early universe, potentially leaving observable signatures in the cosmic microwave background or gravitational wave spectrum.

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: B** — Goldstone's theorem (Goldstone 1961): Spontaneously broken continuous global symmetry produces one massless Goldstone boson per broken generator. Proof via conserved currents and Ward identities. Discrete symmetries produce no Goldstones. Local (gauge) symmetries combine with SSB differently (Higgs mechanism).

**Problem 2: D** — $\dim(SU(3)) = 8$ (eight generators), $\dim(SU(2)) = 3$ (three generators), difference $8 - 3 = 5$ Goldstones. Vacuum manifold $SU(3)/SU(2) \cong S^5$ (5-sphere), confirming 5-dimensional space. Each broken generator → one Goldstone.

**Problem 3: B** — Pions ($\pi^+, \pi^0, \pi^-$) are approximate Goldstones from spontaneous chiral symmetry breaking in QCD. For massless $u,d$ quarks: $SU(2)_L \times SU(2)_R$ broken by quark condensate $\langle\bar{q}q\rangle$ to vector $SU(2)_V$. Explicit quark masses give $m_\pi \approx 140$ MeV (pseudo-Goldstones).

**Problem 4: B** — Goldstone effective Lagrangian $\mathcal{L} = \frac{f^2}{2}(\partial\pi)^2 + \mathcal{O}((\partial\pi)^4/f^2)$ has only derivative couplings. Shift symmetry $\pi \to \pi + c$ forbids potential terms. Low-energy amplitudes $\sim E^2/f^2$ vanish as $E \to 0$ (Adler zero).

**Problem 5: C** — Vacuum manifold: All degenerate vacua related by $G$ transformations, modulo unbroken $H$ equivalences, giving coset $G/H$. Dimension = number of broken generators = number of Goldstones. Examples: U(1) → nothing gives $S^1$; SU(2) → U(1) gives $S^2$.
