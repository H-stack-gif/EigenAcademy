# Quantum Field Theory Topic 3.1: QED Lagrangian and Feynman Rules

Quantum electrodynamics (QED) is the quantum field theory of light and matter—electrons, positrons, and photons interacting through electromagnetic forces. It's the first successfully quantized gauge theory and remains the most precisely tested theory in physics, with predictions matching experiment to better than one part in a billion. The **QED Lagrangian** combines the Dirac equation for fermions with Maxwell's equations for photons, linked through **minimal coupling** $\partial_\mu \to D_\mu = \partial_\mu + ieA_\mu$. This coupling isn't arbitrary—it's demanded by **local U(1) gauge invariance**, the requirement that physics remains unchanged under position-dependent phase transformations $\psi(x) \to e^{i\alpha(x)}\psi(x)$. Without the photon field $A_\mu$ transforming compensatively, this symmetry would be broken. Gauge invariance thus explains why photons exist and why they couple to electric charge.

The QED Lagrangian $\mathcal{L} = \bar{\psi}(i\gamma^\mu D_\mu - m)\psi - \frac{1}{4}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}$ encapsulates all electromagnetic interactions. The Dirac term describes electron propagation, the Maxwell term describes photon propagation, and the covariant derivative contains the interaction $-e\bar{\psi}\gamma^\mu\psi A_\mu$. From this compact expression flow the **Feynman rules**—the systematic prescription for computing scattering amplitudes. Fermion propagators carry spinor indices and propagate as $i(\gamma^\mu p_\mu + m)/(p^2 - m^2 + i\epsilon)$, photon propagators carry Lorentz indices with gauge-dependent forms, and vertices contribute $-ie\gamma^\mu$ connecting two fermion lines to one photon. Different gauge choices simplify different calculations: Feynman gauge $\xi = 1$ gives the simplest propagator, Coulomb gauge separates longitudinal and transverse modes, and Landau gauge $\xi = 0$ makes certain Ward identities manifest.

This article develops the QED Lagrangian from gauge invariance through minimal coupling to the complete Feynman rules. We'll construct the gauge-invariant Lagrangian, derive the interaction term, understand the photon propagator in different gauges, write down fermion propagators with spinor structure, specify the electromagnetic vertex, examine the conserved current, and see how gauge invariance constrains the theory's structure at every step.

## QED Lagrangian from Gauge Invariance

The electromagnetic interaction emerges from demanding local U(1) gauge invariance—a profound connection between symmetry and force.

### Example 1: Minimal Coupling from Local Gauge Invariance

Derive the QED interaction from local U(1) gauge symmetry.

**Solution:**

Free Dirac Lagrangian: $\mathcal{L}_0 = \bar{\psi}(i\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu - m)\psi$

**Global U(1) symmetry**: $\psi \to e^{i\alpha}\psi$ with constant $\alpha$.

This leaves $\mathcal{L}_0$ invariant since $\bar{\psi} \to e^{-i\alpha}\bar{\psi}$ cancels the phase.

**Local U(1)**: Make $\alpha = \alpha(x)$ spacetime-dependent.

Problem: Derivative term fails.
$$\partial_\mu\psi \to \partial_\mu(e^{i\alpha(x)}\psi) = e^{i\alpha(x)}[\partial_\mu\psi + i(\partial_\mu\alpha)\psi]$$

Extra term $i(\partial_\mu\alpha)\psi$ breaks invariance!

**Solution**: Introduce gauge field $A_\mu$ transforming as:
$$A_\mu \to A_\mu - \frac{1}{e}\partial_\mu\alpha$$

Define **covariant derivative**:
$$D_\mu \equiv \partial_\mu + ieA_\mu$$

Now:
$$D_\mu\psi \to e^{i\alpha(x)}[\partial_\mu + ieA_\mu - i\partial_\mu\alpha]\psi = e^{i\alpha(x)}D_\mu\psi$$

Transforms covariantly! The gauge field $A_\mu$ (photon) must exist to maintain local gauge invariance.

**QED Lagrangian**:
$$\mathcal{L}_{QED} = \bar{\psi}(i\gamma^\mu D_\mu - m)\psi - \frac{1}{4}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}$$

where $F_{\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu A_\nu - \partial_\nu A_\mu$ (gauge-invariant field strength).

**Answer:** Local U(1) requires introducing $A_\mu$, minimal coupling $D_\mu = \partial_\mu + ieA_\mu$ ensures gauge invariance.

### Example 2: Interaction Term and Conserved Current

Extract the interaction Hamiltonian and identify the conserved current.

**Solution:**

Expand covariant derivative:
$$\mathcal{L}_{QED} = \bar{\psi}(i\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu - m)\psi + e\bar{\psi}\gamma^\mu\psi A_\mu - \frac{1}{4}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}$$

**Interaction term**:
$$\mathcal{L}_{int} = e\bar{\psi}\gamma^\mu\psi A_\mu = eJ^\mu A_\mu$$

where **electromagnetic current**:
$$J^\mu = \bar{\psi}\gamma^\mu\psi$$

This is the fundamental interaction: photon field couples to fermion current.

**Conserved current** (Noether's theorem from U(1) symmetry):
$$j^\mu = -e\bar{\psi}\gamma^\mu\psi$$

Conservation: $\partial_\mu j^\mu = 0$ (from equations of motion).

Electric charge:
$$Q = \int d^3x \, j^0 = -e\int d^3x \, \psi^\dagger\psi$$

For electron: $e < 0$ (negative charge), so $Q = +|e|$ (one electron).

Coupling constant: $e^2/(4\pi) = \alpha \approx 1/137$ (fine structure constant).

**Physical interpretation**: Photons couple to charged fermions with strength $e$, conserved current guarantees charge conservation.

**Answer:** Interaction $\mathcal{L}_{int} = e\bar{\psi}\gamma^\mu\psi A_\mu$, conserved current $j^\mu = -e\bar{\psi}\gamma^\mu\psi$, charge conservation automatic.

## Feynman Rules for QED

The QED Feynman rules translate Lagrangian terms into graphical elements and mathematical factors for amplitude calculations.

### Example 3: Fermion Propagator

Derive the fermion propagator and its spinor structure.

**Solution:**

From Dirac Lagrangian $\bar{\psi}(i\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu - m)\psi$:

Equation of motion: $(i\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu - m)\psi = 0$ or $(i\not\!\partial - m)\psi = 0$ using Feynman slash notation $\not\!A \equiv \gamma^\mu A_\mu$.

**Fourier space**: $(i\not\!p - m)\tilde{\psi}(p) = 0$

**Propagator** (Greens function):
$$(i\not\!p - m)S_F(p) = \mathbb{1}$$

Solving:
$$S_F(p) = \frac{i(\not\!p + m)}{p^2 - m^2 + i\epsilon}$$

The numerator $(\not\!p + m) = \gamma^\mu p_\mu + m$ comes from:
$$(i\not\!p - m) \cdot \frac{i(\not\!p + m)}{p^2 - m^2 + i\epsilon} = \frac{-(\not\!p)^2 + m^2}{p^2 - m^2 + i\epsilon} = \mathbb{1}$$

using $(\not\!p)^2 = p^2$ (from $\{\gamma^\mu, \gamma^\nu\} = 2g^{\mu\nu}$).

**Spinor indices**: $S_F$ is a $4 \times 4$ matrix in spinor space.

Graphically:
```
------>----- = iS_F(p) = i(γ⋅p + m)/(p² - m² + iε)
  fermion line
```

**Answer:** Fermion propagator $S_F(p) = \frac{i(\not\!p + m)}{p^2 - m^2 + i\epsilon}$ ($4 \times 4$ spinor matrix).

### Example 4: Photon Propagator in Different Gauges

Derive the photon propagator and understand gauge dependence.

**Solution:**

From Maxwell Lagrangian $\mathcal{L} = -\frac{1}{4}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}$:

Equation of motion: $\partial^\mu F_{\mu\nu} = 0$ or $\Box A_\nu - \partial_\nu(\partial^\mu A_\mu) = 0$

**Gauge fixing**: Add gauge-fixing term to Lagrangian:
$$\mathcal{L}_{gf} = -\frac{1}{2\xi}(\partial^\mu A_\mu)^2$$

where $\xi$ is the gauge parameter.

Modified equation: $\Box A_\nu - (1 - \frac{1}{\xi})\partial_\nu(\partial^\mu A_\mu) = 0$

**Photon propagator** (Fourier space):
$$D^{\mu\nu}_F(k) = \frac{-i}{k^2 + i\epsilon}\left[g^{\mu\nu} - (1-\xi)\frac{k^\mu k^\nu}{k^2}\right]$$

**Gauge choices**:

1. **Feynman gauge** ($\xi = 1$):
$$D^{\mu\nu}_F = \frac{-ig^{\mu\nu}}{k^2 + i\epsilon}$$

Simplest form, manifestly Lorentz covariant, most convenient for calculations.

2. **Landau gauge** ($\xi = 0$):
$$D^{\mu\nu}_F = \frac{-i}{k^2 + i\epsilon}\left[g^{\mu\nu} - \frac{k^\mu k^\nu}{k^2}\right]$$

Transverse propagator, $k_\mu D^{\mu\nu} = 0$.

3. **Coulomb gauge** (non-covariant): $\nabla \cdot \mathbf{A} = 0$, separates longitudinal/transverse.

**Physical result**: S-matrix elements are **gauge-independent** (only physical polarizations contribute).

**Answer:** Photon propagator $D^{\mu\nu}_F = \frac{-i}{k^2 + i\epsilon}[g^{\mu\nu} - (1-\xi)\frac{k^\mu k^\nu}{k^2}]$, Feynman gauge $\xi=1$ simplest.

### Example 5: QED Vertex

Write the Feynman rule for the electromagnetic vertex.

**Solution:**

From interaction $\mathcal{L}_{int} = e\bar{\psi}\gamma^\mu\psi A_\mu$:

**Vertex factor**: $-ie\gamma^\mu$

Graphically:
```
  fermion in
      |
      | γμ
   ---⚬---  photon (wavy line)
      |
  fermion out
```

The vertex connects:
- Two fermion lines (incoming/outgoing electron or positron)
- One photon line

**Spinor indices**: $\gamma^\mu$ is $4 \times 4$ matrix acting on fermion spinors.

**Lorentz index**: $\mu$ contracted with photon momentum or polarization.

**Sign convention**: $-ie$ (minus from Feynman rules, $i$ from time ordering, $e$ from coupling).

For electron: $e < 0$, so actual coupling is $-ie = -i(-|e|) = i|e|$ (positive).

**Conservation**: Momentum conservation at vertex $p_{in} = p_{out} + k_\gamma$.

**Answer:** QED vertex: $-ie\gamma^\mu$ connecting two fermion lines and one photon line.

## Complete QED Feynman Rules

### Example 6: Summary of QED Feynman Rules

State the complete set of QED Feynman rules in momentum space.

**Solution:**

**QED Feynman rules** (momentum space):

1. **External fermion** (incoming): $u^{(s)}(p)$ (spinor wavefunction, spin $s$)

2. **External fermion** (outgoing): $\bar{u}^{(s)}(p)$ (adjoint spinor)

3. **External antifermion** (incoming): $\bar{v}^{(s)}(p)$

4. **External antifermion** (outgoing): $v^{(s)}(p)$

5. **External photon**: $\epsilon^\mu(k, \lambda)$ (polarization vector, helicity $\lambda = \pm 1$)

6. **Fermion propagator**: $\frac{i(\not\!p + m)}{p^2 - m^2 + i\epsilon}$

7. **Photon propagator** (Feynman gauge): $\frac{-ig^{\mu\nu}}{k^2 + i\epsilon}$

8. **Vertex**: $-ie\gamma^\mu$ (momentum conservation at vertex)

9. **Integration**: $\int\frac{d^4p}{(2\pi)^4}$ for each internal momentum loop

10. **Fermion loop**: Factor $(-1)$ (from anticommutation)

11. **Symmetry factor**: $1/S$ where $S$ = number of diagram automorphisms

12. **Overall factor**: $(2\pi)^4\delta^4(P_f - P_i)$ (momentum conservation)

**Matrix element**: $i\mathcal{M} = \sum(\text{all connected diagrams})$

Spinor indices contracted left-to-right along fermion lines, Lorentz indices contracted between vertices and propagators.

**Answer:** Complete QED rules: External spinors/polarizations, propagators $S_F$ and $D_F$, vertex $-ie\gamma^\mu$, integrate loops.

### Example 7: Spinor Algebra and Trace Techniques

Outline methods for evaluating spinor expressions.

**Solution:**

QED amplitudes involve spinor chains like:
$$\mathcal{M} = \bar{u}(p_1)\gamma^\mu u(p_2) \cdot \frac{-ig_{\mu\nu}}{q^2} \cdot \bar{v}(p_3)\gamma^\nu v(p_4)$$

**Computing $|\mathcal{M}|^2$**: Sum over final spins, average over initial spins.

$$\sum_{\text{spins}}|\mathcal{M}|^2 = \frac{1}{4}\sum_{s_1, s_2, s_3, s_4}|\mathcal{M}|^2$$

**Casimir's trick**: Use completeness relations.
$$\sum_{s}u^{(s)}(p)\bar{u}^{(s)}(p) = \not\!p + m$$

$$\sum_{s}v^{(s)}(p)\bar{v}^{(s)}(p) = \not\!p - m$$

This converts spinor sums into **traces**:
$$\sum_{\text{spins}}|\mathcal{M}|^2 = \text{Tr}[(\not\!p_1 + m)\gamma^\mu(\not\!p_2 + m)\gamma^\nu] \times \text{Tr}[(\not\!p_3 - m)\gamma_\mu(\not\!p_4 - m)\gamma_\nu]$$

**Trace identities**:
- $\text{Tr}[\text{odd } \gamma] = 0$
- $\text{Tr}[\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu] = 4g^{\mu\nu}$
- $\text{Tr}[\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu\gamma^\rho\gamma^\sigma] = 4(g^{\mu\nu}g^{\rho\sigma} - g^{\mu\rho}g^{\nu\sigma} + g^{\mu\sigma}g^{\nu\rho})$

These reduce complicated spinor expressions to algebraic functions of momenta and masses.

**Answer:** Spin sums use completeness $\sum u\bar{u} = \not\!p + m$, convert to traces of gamma matrices, evaluate with trace identities.
