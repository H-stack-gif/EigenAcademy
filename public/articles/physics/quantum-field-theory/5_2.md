# Quantum Field Theory Topic 5.2: Renormalization in QED

The systematic procedure of renormalization in quantum electrodynamics separates the bare parameters appearing in the classical Lagrangian from the physical renormalized quantities measured in experiments. The bare electron mass $m_0$ and charge $e_0$ diverge as we remove the regulator, but physical observables remain finite through precise cancellations between divergent bare parameters and equally divergent quantum corrections. We write $e_0 = Z_e e$, $m_0 = m + \delta m$, and $\psi_0 = \sqrt{Z_\psi}\psi$ where $Z_e$, $Z_\psi$ are wave function renormalization constants and $\delta m$ is the mass counterterm—all containing infinities that exactly cancel loop divergences order by order in perturbation theory. At one-loop, the photon wave function renormalization $Z_A = 1 - e^2/(12\pi^2\epsilon) + \text{finite}$ arises from vacuum polarization, the electron wave function renormalization $Z_\psi = 1 - e^2/(8\pi^2\epsilon) + \text{finite}$ from self-energy corrections, and the mass shift $\delta m = 3e^2 m/(16\pi^2\epsilon) + \text{finite}$ from on-shell renormalization conditions. The Ward identity $Z_e = \sqrt{Z_A}$ constrains the charge renormalization, reducing independent constants and ensuring gauge invariance. The choice of renormalization scheme—on-shell conditions defining physical pole masses and residues versus $\overline{\text{MS}}$ minimal subtraction of dimensional regularization poles—affects intermediate expressions but yields identical physical predictions when properly implemented.

The bare QED Lagrangian in terms of bare fields and parameters:

$$\mathcal{L}_0 = -\frac{1}{4}F_{\mu\nu}^0 F^{0\mu\nu} + \bar{\psi}_0(i\gamma^\mu D_\mu - m_0)\psi_0$$

becomes the renormalized Lagrangian after field redefinitions $A_\mu^0 = \sqrt{Z_A}A_\mu$ and $\psi_0 = \sqrt{Z_\psi}\psi$:

$$\mathcal{L} = -\frac{1}{4}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu} + \bar{\psi}(i\gamma^\mu D_\mu - m)\psi + \mathcal{L}_{\text{CT}}$$

where the counterterm Lagrangian:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{CT}} = -\frac{Z_A-1}{4}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu} + \bar{\psi}[(Z_\psi-1)i\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu - \delta m]\psi - (Z_e-1)e\bar{\psi}\gamma^\mu\psi A_\mu$$

contains terms $(Z_i - 1)$ that diverge as the regulator is removed but precisely cancel divergences from loop integrals. This systematic organization treats counterterms as vertices in Feynman diagrams—each divergent loop diagram has corresponding counterterm diagram that cancels the infinity when renormalization conditions are imposed.

The vacuum polarization contribution to the photon propagator modifies the bare propagator $-ig_{\mu\nu}/q^2$ through the one-loop fermion bubble. The photon self-energy tensor $-i\Pi_{\mu\nu}(q)$ has the general form:

$$-i\Pi_{\mu\nu}(q) = -i(q^2 g_{\mu\nu} - q_\mu q_\nu)\Pi(q^2)$$

where gauge invariance (Ward identity $q^\mu\Pi_{\mu\nu} = 0$) enforces the transverse structure. Computing the fermion loop in dimensional regularization:

$$\Pi(q^2) = -\frac{e^2}{12\pi^2\epsilon} + \frac{e^2}{6\pi^2}\int_0^1 dx\, x(1-x)\ln\frac{\mu^2}{m^2 - x(1-x)q^2} + O(\epsilon)$$

The divergent part gives the photon wave function renormalization:

$$Z_A = 1 - \frac{e^2}{12\pi^2\epsilon} + O(e^4)$$

The finite part contributes to the running of the fine structure constant, discussed in the next topic.

### Example 1: One-Loop Photon Self-Energy Calculation

Derive the vacuum polarization $\Pi(q^2)$ in QED at one-loop using dimensional regularization.

**Solution:**

The fermion loop diagram contributes:

$$-i\Pi_{\mu\nu}(q) = (-ie)^2\int \frac{d^dk}{(2\pi)^d}\text{Tr}\left[\gamma_\mu\frac{i(k\!\!\!/ + m)}{k^2 - m^2}\gamma_\nu\frac{i(k\!\!\!/ + q\!\!\!/ + m)}{(k+q)^2 - m^2}\right]$$

The trace over Dirac matrices in $d$ dimensions:

$$\text{Tr}[\gamma_\mu(k\!\!\!/ + m)\gamma_\nu(k\!\!\!/ + q\!\!\!/ + m)] = 4[k_\mu(k+q)_\nu + k_\nu(k+q)_\mu - g_{\mu\nu}(k \cdot (k+q) - m^2)]$$

Using Feynman parameters to combine denominators:

$$\frac{1}{AB} = \int_0^1 \frac{dx}{[xA + (1-x)B]^2}$$

The integral becomes:

$$\Pi_{\mu\nu} = \frac{8e^2}{(2\pi)^d}\int_0^1 dx\int d^dk\, \frac{k_\mu k_\nu + \text{other terms}}{[k^2 + 2xk \cdot q + xq^2 - m^2 + x(1-x)q^2]^2}$$

Shift $k \to k - xq$ to eliminate linear term:

$$\Pi_{\mu\nu} = \frac{8e^2}{(2\pi)^d}\int_0^1 dx\int d^dk\, \frac{(k_\mu - xq_\mu)(k_\nu - xq_\nu) + \cdots}{[k^2 - \Delta]^2}$$

where $\Delta = m^2 - x(1-x)q^2$. The tensor structure after integration:

$$\Pi_{\mu\nu} = (q^2 g_{\mu\nu} - q_\mu q_\nu)\Pi(q^2)$$

The scalar function:

$$\Pi(q^2) = -\frac{4e^2}{(4\pi)^{d/2}}\int_0^1 dx\, x(1-x)\int d^dk\, \frac{1}{[k^2-\Delta]^2}$$

Using standard integral $\int d^dk/(k^2-\Delta)^2 = i\pi^{d/2}\Delta^{d/2-2}/\Gamma(2)$:

$$\Pi(q^2) = -\frac{4e^2 i}{(4\pi)^{d/2}}\int_0^1 dx\, x(1-x)\Gamma(2-d/2)\Delta^{d/2-2}$$

For $d = 4-2\epsilon$:

$$\Gamma(\epsilon) = \frac{1}{\epsilon} - \gamma_E + O(\epsilon)$$

$$\Delta^{-\epsilon} = \left(\frac{\mu^2}{\Delta}\right)^\epsilon \approx 1 + \epsilon\ln\frac{\mu^2}{\Delta}$$

$$\Pi(q^2) = -\frac{e^2}{4\pi^2}\int_0^1 dx\, x(1-x)\left[\frac{1}{\epsilon} - \gamma_E + \ln\frac{4\pi\mu^2}{\Delta}\right]$$

The $x$ integral of $x(1-x)$:

$$\int_0^1 x(1-x)dx = \frac{1}{6}$$

Divergent part:

$$\Pi_{\text{div}} = -\frac{e^2}{24\pi^2\epsilon}$$

So $Z_A = 1 - e^2/(12\pi^2\epsilon)$ accounting for factor of 2 from definition.

**Answer:** Vacuum polarization $\Pi(q^2) = -(e^2/12\pi^2\epsilon) + \text{finite}$; gives $Z_A = 1 - e^2/(12\pi^2\epsilon) + O(e^4)$.

The electron self-energy diagram modifies the fermion propagator through radiative corrections. The one-loop self-energy:

$$-i\Sigma(p\!\!\!/) = (-ie)^2\int \frac{d^dk}{(2\pi)^d}\gamma^\mu\frac{i(p\!\!\!/ - k\!\!\!/ + m)}{(p-k)^2 - m^2}\frac{-ig_{\mu\nu}}{k^2}\gamma^\nu$$

has both divergent and finite pieces. Dimensional analysis suggests $\Sigma(p) = A p\!\!\!/ + B m$ where $A$ and $B$ are functions of $p^2/m^2$ containing divergences. The wave function renormalization $Z_\psi$ and mass counterterm $\delta m$ are fixed by on-shell conditions: the physical pole at $p^2 = m^2$ with residue 1. Computing explicitly in Feynman gauge:

$$Z_\psi = 1 - \frac{e^2}{8\pi^2\epsilon} + O(e^4)$$

$$\delta m = \frac{3e^2 m}{16\pi^2\epsilon} + O(e^4)$$

The mass counterterm diverges linearly in the cutoff, reflecting the fact that the electron's bare mass is completely obscured by its electromagnetic self-energy cloud—the physical mass is entirely a quantum effect in the limit $m_0 \to 0$.

### Example 2: Electron Self-Energy and Mass Renormalization

Calculate the mass counterterm $\delta m$ at one-loop in on-shell scheme.

**Solution:**

The fermion self-energy in Feynman gauge:

$$-i\Sigma(p) = (-ie)^2\int \frac{d^4k}{(2\pi)^4}\gamma^\mu\frac{i(p\!\!\!/ - k\!\!\!/ + m)}{(p-k)^2 - m^2 + i\epsilon}\frac{-i}{k^2 + i\epsilon}\gamma_\mu$$

Simplify the numerator using $\gamma^\mu(p\!\!\!/ - k\!\!\!/ + m)\gamma_\mu = -2(p\!\!\!/ - k\!\!\!/) + 4m$ in $d=4$:

$$\Sigma(p) = -e^2\int \frac{d^4k}{(2\pi)^4}\frac{-2(p\!\!\!/ - k\!\!\!/) + 4m}{[(p-k)^2 - m^2]k^2}$$

Use Feynman parameters:

$$\Sigma(p) = -e^2\int_0^1 dx\int \frac{d^4k}{(2\pi)^4}\frac{-2(p\!\!\!/ - k\!\!\!/) + 4m}{[k^2 - 2xk \cdot p + xp^2 + x(1-x)m^2]^2}$$

Shift $k \to k + xp$:

$$\Sigma(p) = -e^2\int_0^1 dx\int \frac{d^4k}{(2\pi)^4}\frac{-2(1-x)p\!\!\!/ - 2k\!\!\!/ + 4m}{[k^2 - \Delta]^2}$$

where $\Delta = -xp^2 - x(1-x)m^2$. The $k\!\!\!/$ term vanishes by symmetry. Remaining:

$$\Sigma(p) = -e^2\int_0^1 dx[-2(1-x)p\!\!\!/ + 4m]\int \frac{d^4k}{(2\pi)^4}\frac{1}{[k^2-\Delta]^2}$$

Using $\int d^4k/(k^2-\Delta)^2 = i/(16\pi^2)\Delta^{-1}[1/\epsilon + \text{finite}]$:

$$\Sigma(p) = \frac{e^2}{16\pi^2}\int_0^1 dx\,[2(1-x)p\!\!\!/ - 4m]\left[\frac{1}{\epsilon} + \ln\frac{\mu^2}{\Delta}\right]$$

Separate momentum and mass terms:

$$\Sigma(p) = (p\!\!\!/ - m)A + B m$$

where:

$$A = \frac{e^2}{16\pi^2}\int_0^1 2(1-x)\left[\frac{1}{\epsilon} + \ln\frac{\mu^2}{\Delta}\right]dx$$

$$B = -\frac{4e^2}{16\pi^2}\int_0^1 \left[\frac{1}{\epsilon} + \ln\frac{\mu^2}{\Delta}\right]dx + m\text{-dependent}$$

On-shell renormalization: $\Sigma(p\!\!\!/ = m) = 0$ (pole at physical mass). This gives:

$$\delta m = -\Sigma(m)|_{p=m} = \frac{3e^2 m}{16\pi^2\epsilon} + \text{finite}$$

The wave function renormalization from residue condition:

$$Z_\psi = 1 - \frac{\partial\Sigma}{\partial p\!\!\!/}\bigg|_{p=m} = 1 - \frac{e^2}{8\pi^2\epsilon} + \text{finite}$$

**Answer:** On-shell mass counterterm $\delta m = 3e^2 m/(16\pi^2\epsilon) + \text{finite}$; $Z_\psi = 1 - e^2/(8\pi^2\epsilon) + O(e^4)$.

The vertex correction represents the third primitively divergent structure in QED. The one-loop triangle diagram with external photon and two fermions:

$$-ie\Lambda^\mu(p', p) = (-ie)^3\int \frac{d^dk}{(2\pi)^d}\gamma^\nu\frac{i(p'\!\!\!/ - k\!\!\!/ + m)}{(p'-k)^2 - m^2}\gamma^\mu\frac{i(p\!\!\!/ - k\!\!\!/ + m)}{(p-k)^2 - m^2}\frac{-ig_{\nu\rho}}{k^2}\gamma^\rho$$

is logarithmically divergent. The Ward identity from gauge invariance constrains:

$$q^\mu\Lambda_\mu(p', p) = S^{-1}(p') - S^{-1}(p)$$

where $q = p' - p$ and $S(p)$ is the electron propagator. This implies $Z_e = \sqrt{Z_A}$ relating charge and photon field renormalizations—a remarkable constraint reducing independent renormalization constants. At one-loop:

$$Z_e = 1 - \frac{e^2}{24\pi^2\epsilon} + O(e^4) = \sqrt{1 - \frac{e^2}{12\pi^2\epsilon}} + O(e^4)$$

consistent with $Z_A$ computed from vacuum polarization.

### Example 3: Ward Identity and Charge Renormalization

Prove the Ward identity $Z_e = \sqrt{Z_A}$ at one-loop in QED.

**Solution:**

The Ward identity for the vertex function:

$$q^\mu\Gamma_\mu(p', p) = S^{-1}(p') - S^{-1}(p)$$

where $q = p' - p$, $S(p)$ is the full electron propagator including self-energy.

At one-loop, write $\Gamma^\mu = \gamma^\mu + \Lambda^\mu$ where $\Lambda^\mu$ is the vertex correction. The Ward identity becomes:

$$q^\mu\Lambda_\mu = \Sigma(p'\!\!\!/) - \Sigma(p\!\!\!/)$$

The bare vertex couples as $-ie_0\gamma^\mu = -ie Z_e\gamma^\mu$. After renormalization:

$$\Gamma^\mu(p', p) = Z_e^{-1}\gamma^\mu + \text{finite vertex corrections}$$

On-shell ($p^2 = p'^2 = m^2$), the vertex function at zero momentum transfer $q=0$:

$$\Gamma^\mu(p, p) = Z_e^{-1}\gamma^\mu$$

From photon wave function renormalization, the effective charge at one-loop:

$$e_{\text{eff}}^2 = \frac{e_0^2}{Z_A} = \frac{Z_e^2 e^2}{Z_A}$$

Physical charge $e_{\text{phys}} = e$ requires:

$$e_0 = Z_A^{1/2} e_{\text{phys}}$$

But also $e_0 = Z_e e$ from vertex. Thus:

$$Z_e = Z_A^{1/2}$$

At one-loop with $Z_A = 1 - e^2/(12\pi^2\epsilon)$:

$$Z_e = \left(1 - \frac{e^2}{12\pi^2\epsilon}\right)^{1/2} = 1 - \frac{e^2}{24\pi^2\epsilon} + O(e^4)$$

This Ward identity is a consequence of gauge invariance—without it, QED would be inconsistent (unitarity violated). Dimensional regularization preserves gauge invariance, automatically ensuring Ward identities hold.

**Answer:** Ward identity $q^\mu\Gamma_\mu = S^{-1}(p') - S^{-1}(p)$ plus gauge invariance implies $Z_e = Z_A^{1/2} = 1 - e^2/(24\pi^2\epsilon) + O(e^4)$.

The choice between on-shell and $\overline{\text{MS}}$ renormalization schemes reflects different philosophies. On-shell renormalization defines physical parameters directly: the electron mass is the pole of the propagator, the charge is measured at zero momentum transfer (Thomson scattering limit), and wave function renormalizations set residues to unity. This scheme has clear physical interpretation but becomes cumbersome at high energies where virtual particles go far off-shell. The $\overline{\text{MS}}$ scheme simply subtracts divergent poles $1/\epsilon - \gamma_E + \ln(4\pi)$ without reference to physical kinematics, yielding simpler expressions for beta functions and anomalous dimensions. Modern high-energy calculations universally employ $\overline{\text{MS}}$, while low-energy precision QED often uses on-shell conditions.

### Example 4: Counterterm Feynman Rules

Write the Feynman rules for QED counterterm vertices.

**Solution:**

The counterterm Lagrangian:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{CT}} = -\frac{Z_A-1}{4}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu} + (Z_\psi-1)\bar{\psi}i\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu\psi - \delta m\bar{\psi}\psi - (Z_e-1)e\bar{\psi}\gamma^\mu\psi A_\mu$$

generates counterterm vertices:

**Photon propagator counterterm**: From $-(Z_A-1)F^2/4$, the momentum-space contribution:

$$-iq^2(Z_A - 1)(g_{\mu\nu} - q_\mu q_\nu/q^2)$$

In Feynman gauge where original propagator is $-ig_{\mu\nu}/q^2$, this gives correction:

$$\frac{-ig_{\mu\nu}}{q^2}[1 + q^2(Z_A-1)] = \frac{-ig_{\mu\nu}}{q^2}\left[1 + \frac{e^2}{12\pi^2\epsilon}\right]$$

**Fermion propagator counterterm**: From $(Z_\psi-1)i\bar{\psi}\gamma \cdot \partial\psi - \delta m\bar{\psi}\psi$:

$$i[(Z_\psi - 1)p\!\!\!/ - \delta m]$$

This cancels divergences from self-energy $-i\Sigma(p)$:

$$iS(p) = \frac{i}{p\!\!\!/ - m - \Sigma(p)} \approx \frac{i}{p\!\!\!/ - m}\left[1 + \frac{\Sigma(p)}{p\!\!\!/ - m}\right]$$

The counterterms ensure:

$$\Sigma(p) + (Z_\psi-1)p\!\!\!/ - \delta m = \text{finite}$$

**Vertex counterterm**: From $-(Z_e-1)e\bar{\psi}\gamma^\mu\psi A_\mu$:

$$-ie(Z_e - 1)\gamma^\mu = -ie\left(-\frac{e^2}{24\pi^2\epsilon}\right)\gamma^\mu$$

This cancels the divergence in vertex correction $\Lambda^\mu$.

**Practical use**: In multiloop calculations, include counterterm diagrams alongside loop diagrams. Each divergence from loops is canceled by corresponding counterterm, order by order in $\alpha = e^2/(4\pi)$.

**Answer:** Counterterm vertices: photon $-iq^2(Z_A-1)g_{\mu\nu}$, fermion $i[(Z_\psi-1)p\!\!\!/ - \delta m]$, vertex $-ie(Z_e-1)\gamma^\mu$; cancel loop divergences systematically.

The systematic nature of renormalization extends to all orders. The BPHZ (Bogoliubov-Parasiuk-Hepp-Zimmermann) theorem proves that renormalizability—the property that finitely many counterterms suffice to all orders—holds for theories with appropriate power counting. QED, with only three primitively divergent structures, is proven renormalizable to all orders. The proof involves careful analysis of overlapping divergences in multiloop diagrams and establishing that forest formulas correctly organize all subtractions.

### Example 5: Two-Loop Renormalization Structure

Describe the two-loop renormalization of QED vertex.

**Solution:**

At two-loop order $O(e^4)$, the vertex function receives corrections from multiple topologies:

**Diagram 1**: Nested photon bubbles (vacuum polarization insertions on internal photon lines).

**Diagram 2**: Vertex correction with one-loop photon self-energy on external photon.

**Diagram 3**: Double vertex correction (two vertex insertions).

**Diagram 4**: Overlapping divergence (fermion box with two photons).

Each individually divergent piece combines with one-loop counterterms inserted into one-loop diagrams. For example, the vertex with one-loop $Z_A$ correction:

$$\Gamma^\mu = \gamma^\mu + \Lambda^\mu_{1-\text{loop}} + \Lambda^\mu_{2-\text{loop}} + (Z_e^{(1)} + Z_e^{(2)})\gamma^\mu + Z_e^{(1)}\Lambda^\mu_{1-\text{loop}}$$

The structure at $O(e^{2n})$:

$$Z_e^{(n)} = \sum_{k=1}^n (\text{divergences from }k\text{-loop vertex diagrams})$$

At two-loop:

$$Z_e^{(2)} = 1 - \frac{e^2}{24\pi^2\epsilon} + \frac{e^4}{(24\pi^2)^2}\left[\frac{c_2}{\epsilon^2} + \frac{c_1}{\epsilon}\right] + O(e^6)$$

The $1/\epsilon^2$ pole appears from products of one-loop divergences. The Ward identity continues to hold:

$$Z_e^{(2)} = \left[Z_A^{(2)}\right]^{1/2} = 1 - \frac{e^2}{24\pi^2\epsilon} + \cdots$$

All divergences cancel order by order, yielding finite, renormalized vertex function.

**Practical calculation**: Modern tools (FeynCalc, Package-X) automate two-loop and higher calculations. The $(g-2)_\mu$ anomalous magnetic moment of the muon is known to five-loop order $O(\alpha^5)$, requiring evaluation of 12,672 Feynman diagrams!

**Answer:** Two-loop vertex has multiple topologies; counterterms from one-loop $Z$ factors plus new two-loop $Z^{(2)}$ cancel all divergences; Ward identity $Z_e = \sqrt{Z_A}$ holds order-by-order.

The extension to mass renormalization in different schemes illustrates scheme dependence. In on-shell scheme, $m$ is the physical pole mass. In $\overline{\text{MS}}$ scheme, $m(\mu)$ becomes a running parameter depending on renormalization scale $\mu$, related to pole mass by:

$$m_{\text{pole}} = m(\mu)\left[1 + \frac{\alpha(\mu)}{\pi} + O(\alpha^2)\right]$$

The $\overline{\text{MS}}$ mass runs with $\mu$ according to the mass anomalous dimension, while pole mass is scale-independent. For QCD quark masses, $\overline{\text{MS}}$ masses are standard: $m_b(\mu = m_b) = 4.18$ GeV is the running bottom quark mass, differing from pole mass by $\sim 10\%$.

### Example 6: Renormalization Group Equation for Mass

Derive how the mass counterterm leads to running mass in $\overline{\text{MS}}$ scheme.

**Solution:**

The bare mass $m_0$ is scale-independent:

$$\mu\frac{d}{d\mu}m_0 = 0$$

Writing $m_0 = Z_m(\mu, \epsilon)m(\mu)$ where $Z_m$ contains $1/\epsilon$ poles:

$$0 = \mu\frac{d}{d\mu}[Z_m m] = m\mu\frac{dZ_m}{d\mu} + Z_m\mu\frac{dm}{d\mu}$$

Rearranging:

$$\mu\frac{dm}{d\mu} = -\frac{m}{Z_m}\mu\frac{dZ_m}{d\mu} = -\gamma_m m$$

where the **mass anomalous dimension**:

$$\gamma_m = \frac{\mu}{Z_m}\frac{dZ_m}{d\mu}$$

At one-loop in QED with $Z_m = 1 + (3\alpha)/(4\pi\epsilon)$:

$$\mu\frac{dZ_m}{d\mu} = \frac{3}{4\pi\epsilon}\mu\frac{d\alpha}{d\mu}$$

Using beta function $\beta(\alpha) = \mu d\alpha/d\mu$:

$$\gamma_m = \frac{3}{4\pi\epsilon}\beta(\alpha)$$

In $\overline{\text{MS}}$, extract the finite part as $\epsilon \to 0$ with $\beta(\alpha) = 2\alpha^2/(3\pi)$:

$$\gamma_m = \frac{3\alpha}{2\pi} + O(\alpha^2)$$

The mass evolves:

$$\mu\frac{dm}{d\mu} = -\gamma_m m = -\frac{3\alpha}{2\pi}m$$

Solving:

$$m(\mu_2) = m(\mu_1)\exp\left(-\int_{\mu_1}^{\mu_2}\frac{d\mu'}{\mu'}\gamma_m(\alpha(\mu'))\right)$$

For QED with slowly varying $\alpha$:

$$m(\mu_2) \approx m(\mu_1)\left(\frac{\mu_2}{\mu_1}\right)^{-3\alpha/(2\pi)}$$

The mass decreases logarithmically with scale—a tiny effect in QED (exponent $\sim 10^{-3}$) but important in QCD where $\alpha_s$ is large.

**Answer:** RG equation $\mu dm/d\mu = -\gamma_m m$ with $\gamma_m = 3\alpha/(2\pi)$ at one-loop; mass runs slowly in QED, significantly in QCD.

### Example 7: Comparing On-Shell and $\overline{\text{MS}}$ Results

Calculate the one-loop photon propagator correction in both schemes and relate them.

**Solution:**

The vacuum polarization in dimensional regularization:

$$\Pi(q^2) = -\frac{e^2}{12\pi^2}\left[\frac{1}{\epsilon} - \gamma_E + \ln(4\pi)\right] + \frac{e^2}{6\pi^2}\int_0^1 dx\,x(1-x)\ln\frac{\mu^2}{m^2 - x(1-x)q^2}$$

**On-shell scheme**: Define $Z_A$ such that the photon propagator has residue 1 at $q^2 = 0$ (massless photon). The finite part at $q^2 = 0$:

$$\Pi(0) = \frac{e^2}{6\pi^2}\int_0^1 dx\,x(1-x)\ln\frac{\mu^2}{m^2}$$

$$= \frac{e^2}{6\pi^2}\cdot\frac{1}{6}\ln\frac{\mu^2}{m^2} = \frac{e^2}{36\pi^2}\ln\frac{\mu^2}{m^2}$$

Subtracting this along with the pole:

$$\Pi^{\text{OS}}(q^2) = \frac{e^2}{6\pi^2}\int_0^1 dx\,x(1-x)\ln\frac{m^2}{m^2 - x(1-x)q^2}$$

**$\overline{\text{MS}}$ scheme**: Subtract only $1/\epsilon - \gamma_E + \ln(4\pi)$:

$$\Pi^{\overline{\text{MS}}}(q^2) = \frac{e^2}{6\pi^2}\int_0^1 dx\,x(1-x)\ln\frac{\mu^2}{m^2 - x(1-x)q^2}$$

The difference:

$$\Pi^{\overline{\text{MS}}}(q^2) - \Pi^{\text{OS}}(q^2) = \frac{e^2}{36\pi^2}\ln\frac{\mu^2}{m^2}$$

This constant shift doesn't affect physical observables like cross sections, which depend on $\Pi(q^2) - \Pi(q'^2)$.

**Charge relation**: The effective charge at scale $Q^2$:

$$\alpha(Q^2) = \frac{\alpha}{1 - \Pi(Q^2)}$$

differs between schemes by a constant rescaling absorbed into the initial condition $\alpha(\mu_0)$. Running from one scale to another gives scheme-independent result.

**Answer:** On-shell subtracts finite part at $q^2=0$; $\overline{\text{MS}}$ subtracts only poles. Differ by constant; physical running $\alpha(\mu_2)/\alpha(\mu_1)$ scheme-independent.

The renormalization of QED stands as the prototype for all quantum field theories. The systematic procedure—regulate divergences, introduce counterterms, impose renormalization conditions, verify Ward identities—applies universally to QCD, electroweak theory, and effective field theories. Modern precision tests like the electron $(g-2)$ measured to 13 decimal places and predicted theoretically to $O(\alpha^5)$ validate renormalization to extraordinary accuracy, confirming that this seemingly baroque procedure captures real physics with unparalleled precision.

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: B** — Bare parameters $e_0, m_0$ diverge as regulator removed. Physical charge $e$ finite through $e_0 = Z_e e$ where $Z_e = 1 - e^2/(24\pi^2\epsilon) + O(e^4)$ at one-loop. **Problem 2: B** — Ward identity $q^\mu\Gamma_\mu = S^{-1}(p') - S^{-1}(p)$ constrains vertex. Consequence: $Z_e = \sqrt{Z_A}$ relating charge and photon field renormalization. Violated if regularization breaks gauge symmetry (cutoff does, dimensional reg preserves).

**Problem 3: B** — Vacuum polarization one-loop gives $\Pi(q^2) = -(e^2/(12\pi^2\epsilon)) + \text{finite}$. Photon propagator correction $\to Z_A = 1 - e^2/(12\pi^2\epsilon) + O(e^4)$. Negative sign: virtual fermion pairs screen charge. Finite part contributes to running $\alpha(q^2)$. Two-loop $O(e^4)$ gives additional $1/\epsilon^2, 1/\epsilon$ poles from nested loops.

**Problem 4: B** — Electron self-energy requires mass counterterm $\delta m = 3e^2m/(16\pi^2\epsilon) + \text{finite}$ in on-shell scheme (pole at physical mass). Diverges linearly in cutoff regularization. Physical mass $m$ completely determined by quantum corrections—bare $m_0 \to 0$ limit meaningful. In $\overline{\text{MS}}$, mass runs with scale $\mu$ via anomalous dimension $\gamma_m = 3\alpha/(2\pi)$.

**Problem 5: B** — Modified minimal subtraction subtracts pole plus scheme constants: $1/\epsilon - \gamma_E + \ln(4\pi)$. Every dim reg integral gives factor $(4\pi)^{-\epsilon}\Gamma(\epsilon) \approx 1 + \epsilon(\ln(4\pi)-\gamma_E)$. Subtracting these simplifies beta functions. Leaves finite parts $\ln(\mu^2/m^2)$ encoding physics. On-shell scheme subtracts to enforce physical conditions ($p^2=m^2$ pole). Both schemes give identical cross sections when matched properly.
