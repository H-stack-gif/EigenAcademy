# Quantum Field Theory Topic 2.2: Wick's Theorem and Contractions

The Dyson series expresses the S-matrix as time-ordered products of field operators: $T[\hat{\phi}(x_1)\hat{\phi}(x_2)\cdots\hat{\phi}(x_n)]$. But field operators are sums of creation and annihilation operators, making these products combinatorially complex. Computing $\langle 0|T[\hat{\phi}^4(x_1)\hat{\phi}^4(x_2)]|0\rangle$ directly would require expanding eight factors, evaluating all possible pairings, and keeping track of which operators annihilate which. This is impractical beyond the simplest cases. **Wick's theorem** provides the essential tool: it systematically reduces time-ordered products to **normal-ordered** products plus **contractions**. Normal ordering eliminates vacuum expectation values, while contractions are simply propagators—c-number functions encoding how particles propagate between spacetime points.

Wick's theorem transforms operator algebra into combinatorics. Each term in the expansion corresponds to a specific pattern of field pairings (contractions), and fully contracted terms—those where every field is paired—give vacuum-to-vacuum amplitudes. This is precisely the structure of Feynman diagrams: each contraction becomes a propagator line, each uncontracted field becomes an external leg, and vertices arise from the interaction Hamiltonian. For fermions, an extra subtlety appears: antisymmetry under operator exchange introduces minus signs that become crucial for ensuring Fermi-Dirac statistics in scattering amplitudes.

This article develops Wick's theorem from normal ordering through contractions to the complete expansion formula. We'll define contractions, prove Wick's theorem by induction, understand fully contracted terms, handle fermion signs, apply the theorem to compute correlation functions, and see how it generates Feynman diagrams systematically.

## Normal Ordering and Contractions

### Example 1: Normal Ordering Review

Define normal ordering and its properties for bosons.

**Solution:**

**Normal ordering** $:\hat{A}:$ places all creation operators $\hat{a}^\dagger$ to the left of annihilation operators $\hat{a}$.

Examples:
$$:\hat{a}\hat{a}^\dagger: = \hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a}$$

$$:\hat{\phi}(x)\hat{\phi}(y): = \text{reorder mode expansion with all } \hat{a}^\dagger \text{ left}$$

Properties:
- $\langle 0|:\hat{A}:|0\rangle = 0$ (vacuum expectation vanishes)
- Normal-ordered Hamiltonian: $:\hat{H}: = \sum \omega_k\hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a}$ (removes vacuum energy)
- Linear: $:(c_1\hat{A} + c_2\hat{B}): = c_1:\hat{A}: + c_2:\hat{B}:$

For product of fields:
$$:\hat{\phi}(x_1)\hat{\phi}(x_2): = \int\frac{d^3k_1 d^3k_2}{(2\pi)^6}\frac{1}{\sqrt{4\omega_1\omega_2}}[\hat{a}_{k_1}\hat{a}_{k_2} + \hat{a}_{k_1}\hat{a}^\dagger_{k_2} + \hat{a}^\dagger_{k_1}\hat{a}_{k_2} + \hat{a}^\dagger_{k_1}\hat{a}^\dagger_{k_2}]e^{-ik_1 \cdot x_1}e^{-ik_2 \cdot x_2} + \text{h.c.}$$

All terms with $\hat{a}^\dagger$ moved left.

**Answer:** Normal ordering $:\cdots:$ moves all $\hat{a}^\dagger$ left, $\langle 0|:\hat{A}:|0\rangle = 0$ for any $\hat{A}$.

### Example 2: Contraction Definition

Define the contraction of two fields.

**Solution:**

**Contraction**: Difference between time-ordered and normal-ordered products.

For scalar fields:
$$\wick{\c\phi(x)\c\phi(y)} = T[\hat{\phi}(x)\hat{\phi}(y)] - :\hat{\phi}(x)\hat{\phi}(y):$$

Computing explicitly:
$$T[\hat{\phi}(x)\hat{\phi}(y)] = \theta(t_x - t_y)\hat{\phi}(x)\hat{\phi}(y) + \theta(t_y - t_x)\hat{\phi}(y)\hat{\phi}(x)$$

The contraction equals:
$$\wick{\c\phi(x)\c\phi(y)} = D_F(x - y) = \langle 0|T[\hat{\phi}(x)\hat{\phi}(y)]|0\rangle$$

This is the **Feynman propagator**—a c-number function (not an operator).

Key property: Contraction is **independent of field ordering** (symmetric for bosons):
$$\wick{\c\phi(x)\c\phi(y)} = \wick{\c\phi(y)\c\phi(x)}$$

For fermions (later): Antisymmetric under exchange.

**Answer:** Contraction $\wick{\c\phi(x)\c\phi(y)} = T[\phi\phi] - :\phi\phi: = D_F(x-y)$ (Feynman propagator).

### Example 3: Simple Wick's Theorem Application

Apply Wick's theorem to two fields.

**Solution:**

**Wick's theorem** (two fields):
$$T[\hat{\phi}(x)\hat{\phi}(y)] = :\hat{\phi}(x)\hat{\phi}(y): + \wick{\c\phi(x)\c\phi(y)}$$

The time-ordered product splits into:
1. Normal-ordered term: $:\hat{\phi}(x)\hat{\phi}(y):$ (creates/annihilates particles)
2. Fully contracted term: $\wick{\c\phi(x)\c\phi(y)} = D_F(x-y)$ (propagator)

Vacuum expectation:
$$\langle 0|T[\hat{\phi}(x)\hat{\phi}(y)]|0\rangle = \langle 0|:\hat{\phi}(x)\hat{\phi}(y):|0\rangle + D_F(x-y)$$

$$= 0 + D_F(x-y) = D_F(x-y)$$

The normal-ordered term vanishes when sandwiched between vacua.

**Physical meaning**: Propagator $D_F$ is the amplitude for a particle to propagate from $y$ to $x$.

**Answer:** Two fields: $T[\phi(x)\phi(y)] = :\phi(x)\phi(y): + D_F(x-y)$ (Wick's theorem).

## General Wick's Theorem

### Example 4: Wick's Theorem for Multiple Fields

State the general form of Wick's theorem.

**Solution:**

**Wick's theorem**: Time-ordered product = normal ordering + all possible contractions.

$$T[\hat{\phi}(x_1)\hat{\phi}(x_2)\cdots\hat{\phi}(x_n)] = :\hat{\phi}(x_1)\hat{\phi}(x_2)\cdots\hat{\phi}(x_n):$$

$$+ \sum_{\text{single contractions}}:\wick{\c\phi(x_i)\c\phi(x_j)}\hat{\phi}(\text{others}):$$

$$+ \sum_{\text{double contractions}}:\wick{\c\phi(x_i)\c\phi(x_j)}\wick{\c\phi(x_k)\c\phi(x_l)}\hat{\phi}(\text{others}):$$

$$+ \cdots + \sum_{\text{all fields contracted}}\wick{\c\phi(x_1)\c\phi(x_2)}\wick{\c\phi(x_3)\c\phi(x_4)}\cdots$$

**Combinatorics**: For $n$ fields, number of ways to pair them into $n/2$ contractions (for $n$ even):
$$\frac{n!}{2^{n/2}(n/2)!}$$

**Fully contracted term** (all fields paired): Contributes to vacuum amplitude $\langle 0|T[\cdots]|0\rangle$.

Example ($n=4$):
$$T[\phi_1\phi_2\phi_3\phi_4] = :\phi_1\phi_2\phi_3\phi_4: + :\wick{\c\phi_1\c\phi_2}\phi_3\phi_4: + 5 \text{ other single contractions}$$

$$+ \wick{\c\phi_1\c\phi_2}\wick{\c\phi_3\c\phi_4} + \wick{\c\phi_1\c\phi_3}\wick{\c\phi_2\c\phi_4} + \wick{\c\phi_1\c\phi_4}\wick{\c\phi_2\c\phi_3}$$

**Answer:** Wick's theorem: $T[\phi_1\cdots\phi_n] = :\phi_1\cdots\phi_n: + \sum(\text{all contractions})$.

### Example 5: Proof Strategy for Wick's Theorem

Outline the inductive proof of Wick's theorem.

**Solution:**

**Proof by induction** on number of fields.

**Base case** ($n=2$): Already verified—$T[\phi\phi] = :\phi\phi: + \text{contraction}$.

**Inductive step**: Assume true for $n-1$ fields, prove for $n$ fields.

Key identity:
$$T[\hat{\phi}(x_1)\hat{\phi}(x_2)\cdots\hat{\phi}(x_n)] = \hat{\phi}(x_1):\hat{\phi}(x_2)\cdots\hat{\phi}(x_n): + \sum_{i=2}^n\wick{\c\phi(x_1)\c\phi(x_i)}:\hat{\phi}(x_2)\cdots\hat\phi(x_{i-1})\hat\phi(x_{i+1})\cdots\hat{\phi}(x_n):$$

(assuming $t_1$ is earliest time).

For general times, split into regions where different $t_i$ is earliest, apply above formula to each region, and sum.

The combinatorics works out: all possible contractions appear exactly once.

**Fermions**: Same proof structure, but track minus signs from anticommutation.

**Answer:** Proof: Induction on $n$, using contraction definition and time-ordering properties.

## Vacuum Expectation Values and Fully Contracted Terms

### Example 6: Vacuum Expectation from Wick's Theorem

Calculate $\langle 0|T[\phi(x_1)\phi(x_2)\phi(x_3)\phi(x_4)]|0\rangle$.

**Solution:**

Apply Wick's theorem:
$$\langle 0|T[\phi_1\phi_2\phi_3\phi_4]|0\rangle = \langle 0|:\phi_1\phi_2\phi_3\phi_4:|0\rangle + \text{single contractions} + \text{double contractions}$$

Normal-ordered terms: $\langle 0|:\cdots:|0\rangle = 0$ (always).

Single contractions: Still have uncontracted fields → vanish in vacuum.

**Only fully contracted terms survive**:
$$\langle 0|T[\phi_1\phi_2\phi_3\phi_4]|0\rangle = \wick{\c\phi_1\c\phi_2}\wick{\c\phi_3\c\phi_4} + \wick{\c\phi_1\c\phi_3}\wick{\c\phi_2\c\phi_4} + \wick{\c\phi_1\c\phi_4}\wick{\c\phi_2\c\phi_3}$$

$$= D_F(x_1 - x_2)D_F(x_3 - x_4) + D_F(x_1 - x_3)D_F(x_2 - x_4) + D_F(x_1 - x_4)D_F(x_2 - x_3)$$

Three ways to pair four fields into two propagators.

**Physical interpretation**: Three distinct Feynman diagrams contributing to 4-point function.

**Answer:** Vacuum expectation: Sum of all fully contracted terms (products of propagators).

### Example 7: Fermion Contractions and Signs

Extend Wick's theorem to fermions.

**Solution:**

For **Dirac fields** $\hat{\psi}$, $\hat{\bar{\psi}}$:

Contraction:
$$\wick{\c\psi(x)\c{\bar\psi}(y)} = S_F(x - y) = \langle 0|T[\hat{\psi}(x)\hat{\bar{\psi}}(y)]|0\rangle$$

where $S_F$ is the **fermion propagator**.

**Crucial difference**: Anticommutation introduces **minus signs**.

Wick's theorem for fermions:
$$T[\psi_1\psi_2\cdots\psi_n] = (-1)^\sigma:\psi_1\psi_2\cdots\psi_n: + \text{contractions with signs}$$

where $\sigma$ is the number of operator exchanges needed to achieve time ordering.

Example:
$$T[\psi_1\bar{\psi}_2] = \theta(t_1 - t_2)\psi_1\bar{\psi}_2 - \theta(t_2 - t_1)\bar{\psi}_2\psi_1$$

(Minus sign from anticommutation!)

**General rule**: Each fermion contraction contributes $(-1)^p$ where $p$ is the number of fermion field exchanges required.

This ensures correct **Fermi-Dirac statistics** in scattering amplitudes.

**Answer:** Fermions: Wick's theorem with $(-1)^\sigma$ signs from anticommutation, contractions give $S_F$.
