# Quantum Field Theory Topic 1.4: Quantization of Dirac Fields

Scalar fields obey commutation relations and describe bosons. But electrons, quarks, and all matter particles are **fermions** with half-integer spin, obeying the Pauli exclusion principle. Quantizing the Dirac field requires a fundamentally different procedure: **anticommutation relations** instead of commutation. This seemingly small change has profound consequences—it prevents negative energy states from catastrophically populating, enforces the exclusion principle, and leads to Fermi-Dirac statistics. The **spin-statistics theorem** connects these dots: half-integer spin requires anticommutation (fermions), integer spin requires commutation (bosons). This deep result follows from combining quantum mechanics, special relativity, and causality.

When we quantize the Dirac field using anticommutators $\{\hat{\psi}, \hat{\psi}^\dagger\} = \delta^3(\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{y})$, the annihilation operators for negative energy solutions become **creation operators for antiparticles**. The positron emerges naturally—not as an ad hoc fix but as a mathematical necessity. Each momentum mode has two particle states (spin up/down) and two antiparticle states, all satisfying fermionic statistics. The Hamiltonian, when properly normal ordered, is positive definite with no instability. Charge conjugation swaps particles and antiparticles, revealing a beautiful symmetry structure.

This article develops Dirac field quantization from the classical spinor field through anticommutation relations to Fock space construction for fermions. We'll impose anticommutators, construct fermionic creation/annihilation operators, derive the energy spectrum, understand the spin-statistics connection, construct the charge operator, and see how anticommutation enforces the Pauli exclusion principle.

## Anticommutation Relations

For the Dirac field, canonical momentum is $\pi = i\psi^\dagger$. But imposing commutation relations leads to inconsistencies—negative norm states and causality violation. The solution: anticommutation.

### Example 1: Fermionic Equal-Time Anticommutators

Derive the canonical anticommutation relations for the Dirac field.

**Solution:**

For Dirac Lagrangian $\mathcal{L} = \bar{\psi}(i\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu - m)\psi$:

Conjugate momentum:
$$\pi_a(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\dot{\psi}_a} = i\psi_a^\dagger(\mathbf{x})$$

**Canonical anticommutation relations** (CAR):

$$\{\hat{\psi}_a(\mathbf{x}, t), \hat{\psi}^\dagger_b(\mathbf{y}, t)\} = \delta_{ab}\delta^3(\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{y})$$

$$\{\hat{\psi}_a(\mathbf{x}, t), \hat{\psi}_b(\mathbf{y}, t)\} = 0$$

$$\{\hat{\psi}^\dagger_a(\mathbf{x}, t), \hat{\psi}^\dagger_b(\mathbf{y}, t)\} = 0$$

where $a, b$ are spinor indices ($a = 1, 2, 3, 4$).

Anticommutator: $\{A, B\} = AB + BA$

These replace the commutators used for bosons. The anticommutator ensures:
1. Positive energy spectrum
2. Causality (spacelike-separated fields anticommute)
3. Pauli exclusion principle

**Answer:** Fermionic CAR: $\{\hat{\psi}_a(\mathbf{x}), \hat{\psi}^\dagger_b(\mathbf{y})\} = \delta_{ab}\delta^3(\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{y})$, others zero.

### Example 2: Fermionic Creation and Annihilation Operators

Construct fermionic ladder operators from the mode expansion.

**Solution:**

Mode expansion (discrete):
$$\hat{\psi}(\mathbf{x}, t) = \sum_{\mathbf{p}, s}\frac{1}{\sqrt{V}}[\hat{b}_{\mathbf{p}, s}u^{(s)}(\mathbf{p})e^{-ip \cdot x} + \hat{d}^\dagger_{\mathbf{p}, s}v^{(s)}(\mathbf{p})e^{+ip \cdot x}]$$

where:
- $\hat{b}_{\mathbf{p}, s}$: annihilates particle (electron) with momentum $\mathbf{p}$, spin $s$
- $\hat{d}^\dagger_{\mathbf{p}, s}$: creates antiparticle (positron) with momentum $\mathbf{p}$, spin $s$
- $u^{(s)}(\mathbf{p})$, $v^{(s)}(\mathbf{p})$: positive/negative energy spinors

From CAR:

$$\{\hat{b}_{\mathbf{p}, s}, \hat{b}^\dagger_{\mathbf{p}', s'}\} = \delta_{\mathbf{p}\mathbf{p}'}\delta_{ss'}$$

$$\{\hat{d}_{\mathbf{p}, s}, \hat{d}^\dagger_{\mathbf{p}', s'}\} = \delta_{\mathbf{p}\mathbf{p}'}\delta_{ss'}$$

$$\{\hat{b}_{\mathbf{p}, s}, \hat{b}_{\mathbf{p}', s'}\} = 0, \quad \text{etc.}$$

All other anticommutators vanish. Note: $\{b, b\} = 0$ implies $b^2 = 0$—you can't create two identical fermions in the same state!

**Answer:** Fermionic operators $\hat{b}, \hat{d}$ satisfy $\{\hat{b}_{\mathbf{p}, s}, \hat{b}^\dagger_{\mathbf{p}', s'}\} = \delta_{\mathbf{p}\mathbf{p}'}\delta_{ss'}$ (anticommutation).

## Hamiltonian and Energy Spectrum

### Example 3: Dirac Hamiltonian in Second Quantization

Express the Hamiltonian using normal ordering.

**Solution:**

Classical Hamiltonian density:
$$\mathcal{H} = \psi^\dagger(i\boldsymbol{\alpha} \cdot \nabla + \beta m)\psi$$

where $\boldsymbol{\alpha} = \gamma^0\boldsymbol{\gamma}$ and $\beta = \gamma^0$.

Substituting mode expansion before normal ordering:

$$\hat{H} = \sum_{\mathbf{p}, s}E_p[\hat{b}^\dagger_{\mathbf{p}, s}\hat{b}_{\mathbf{p}, s} - \hat{d}_{\mathbf{p}, s}\hat{d}^\dagger_{\mathbf{p}, s}]$$

The negative sign on the $d$ term is disastrous—it makes antiparticle states have negative energy!

**Normal ordering**:
$$:\hat{H}: = \sum_{\mathbf{p}, s}E_p[\hat{b}^\dagger_{\mathbf{p}, s}\hat{b}_{\mathbf{p}, s} + \hat{d}^\dagger_{\mathbf{p}, s}\hat{d}_{\mathbf{p}, s}]$$

Using $\hat{d}_{\mathbf{p}, s}\hat{d}^\dagger_{\mathbf{p}, s} = \delta_{\mathbf{p}\mathbf{p}'}\delta_{ss'} - \hat{d}^\dagger_{\mathbf{p}, s}\hat{d}_{\mathbf{p}, s}$:

Now both particles and antiparticles contribute **positive energy** $E_p = \sqrt{p^2 + m^2}$.

Vacuum: $:|0\rangle = 0$ (no infinite negative energy sea).

Energy eigenvalues:
$$E = \sum_{\mathbf{p}, s}E_p(n^b_{\mathbf{p}, s} + n^d_{\mathbf{p}, s})$$

where $n^b, n^d = 0, 1$ (fermions: occupation 0 or 1 only!).

**Answer:** Normal-ordered $:\hat{H}: = \sum_{\mathbf{p}, s}E_p(\hat{b}^\dagger\hat{b} + \hat{d}^\dagger\hat{d})$ (positive energy for particles and antiparticles).

### Example 4: Fermionic Fock Space

Construct the Fock space for fermions.

**Solution:**

**Vacuum**: $\hat{b}_{\mathbf{p}, s}|0\rangle = \hat{d}_{\mathbf{p}, s}|0\rangle = 0$

**One-particle states**:
$$|\mathbf{p}, s\rangle = \hat{b}^\dagger_{\mathbf{p}, s}|0\rangle$$ (electron)

$$|\bar{\mathbf{p}}, s\rangle = \hat{d}^\dagger_{\mathbf{p}, s}|0\rangle$$ (positron)

**Two-particle states**:
$$|\mathbf{p}_1, s_1; \mathbf{p}_2, s_2\rangle = \hat{b}^\dagger_{\mathbf{p}_1, s_1}\hat{b}^\dagger_{\mathbf{p}_2, s_2}|0\rangle$$

**Antisymmetry**: If $(\mathbf{p}_1, s_1) = (\mathbf{p}_2, s_2)$:
$$\hat{b}^\dagger_{\mathbf{p}, s}\hat{b}^\dagger_{\mathbf{p}, s}|0\rangle = -\hat{b}^\dagger_{\mathbf{p}, s}\hat{b}^\dagger_{\mathbf{p}, s}|0\rangle = 0$$

(Pauli exclusion: Can't put two identical fermions in same state!)

Exchange symmetry:
$$|\mathbf{p}_1, s_1; \mathbf{p}_2, s_2\rangle = -|\mathbf{p}_2, s_2; \mathbf{p}_1, s_1\rangle$$

Fermionic Fock space: Antisymmetric tensor product.

**Answer:** Fermionic Fock space with antisymmetric states, $n_{\mathbf{p}, s} = 0, 1$ only (Pauli exclusion).

## Charge Operator and Particle-Antiparticle Symmetry

### Example 5: Conserved Charge Operator

Construct the electric charge operator.

**Solution:**

From Noether's theorem, the U(1) current for Dirac field:
$$j^\mu = \bar{\psi}\gamma^\mu\psi$$

Charge operator:
$$\hat{Q} = \int d^3x \, \hat{j}^0 = \int d^3x \, \hat{\psi}^\dagger\hat{\psi}$$

Substituting mode expansion and normal ordering:

$$\hat{Q} = \sum_{\mathbf{p}, s}(\hat{b}^\dagger_{\mathbf{p}, s}\hat{b}_{\mathbf{p}, s} - \hat{d}^\dagger_{\mathbf{p}, s}\hat{d}_{\mathbf{p}, s})$$

Interpretation:
- $\hat{b}^\dagger\hat{b}$: particle (electron) number, charge $+|e|$
- $\hat{d}^\dagger\hat{d}$: antiparticle (positron) number, charge $-|e|$

Sign difference: Antiparticles carry opposite charge!

Eigenvalues: $Q = N_e - N_{\bar{e}}$ (particles minus antiparticles).

Vacuum: $\hat{Q}|0\rangle = 0$ (charge neutral).

Conservation: $[\hat{Q}, \hat{H}] = 0$ (charge conserved).

**Answer:** Charge operator $\hat{Q} = \sum_{\mathbf{p}, s}(\hat{b}^\dagger\hat{b} - \hat{d}^\dagger\hat{d})$ (particles minus antiparticles).

## Spin-Statistics Theorem

### Example 6: Spin-Statistics Connection

Explain why fermions must anticommute and bosons must commute.

**Solution:**

**Spin-statistics theorem** (Pauli, 1940):
- Half-integer spin ($s = 1/2, 3/2, \ldots$) → Anticommutation (fermions)
- Integer spin ($s = 0, 1, 2, \ldots$) → Commutation (bosons)

**Proof sketch** (requires QFT + special relativity):

1. **Causality**: Fields at spacelike separation must (anti)commute.
   
2. **Lorentz invariance**: Under boosts, spacelike → timelike or vice versa.

3. **Energy positivity**: Require vacuum to be lowest energy state.

4. **Connection**: For half-integer spin, only anticommutation satisfies all three.

If Dirac field obeyed commutation:
- $[\psi, \psi^\dagger]$ would give negative norm states
- Vacuum would be unstable (infinite negative energy)
- Causality violated at spacelike separation

**Experimental confirmation**:
- Electrons obey Fermi-Dirac statistics (anticommutation)
- Photons obey Bose-Einstein statistics (commutation)
- Never observed violations

Deep connection between **symmetry**, **statistics**, and **spin**.

**Answer:** Spin-statistics: Half-integer spin requires anticommutation (fermions), integer spin requires commutation (bosons).

### Example 7: Pauli Exclusion Principle

Derive the Pauli exclusion principle from anticommutation.

**Solution:**

For fermionic creation operators:

$$\{\hat{b}^\dagger_{\mathbf{p}, s}, \hat{b}^\dagger_{\mathbf{p}', s'}\} = 0$$

For identical state $(\mathbf{p}, s) = (\mathbf{p}', s')$:

$$\hat{b}^\dagger_{\mathbf{p}, s}\hat{b}^\dagger_{\mathbf{p}, s} + \hat{b}^\dagger_{\mathbf{p}, s}\hat{b}^\dagger_{\mathbf{p}, s} = 0$$

$$2(\hat{b}^\dagger_{\mathbf{p}, s})^2 = 0$$

$$(\hat{b}^\dagger_{\mathbf{p}, s})^2 = 0$$

Therefore:
$$(\hat{b}^\dagger_{\mathbf{p}, s})^2|0\rangle = 0$$

**Cannot create two fermions in the same quantum state!**

Occupation number:
$$\hat{n}_{\mathbf{p}, s} = \hat{b}^\dagger_{\mathbf{p}, s}\hat{b}_{\mathbf{p}, s}$$

has eigenvalues $n = 0, 1$ only (not 2, 3, ...).

This is the **Pauli exclusion principle**—a direct consequence of fermionic anticommutation.

Explains:
- Atomic structure (shells)
- Periodic table
- Stability of matter
- Degeneracy pressure in white dwarfs/neutron stars

**Answer:** Anticommutation $\{\hat{b}^\dagger, \hat{b}^\dagger\} = 0$ implies $(\hat{b}^\dagger)^2 = 0$ (Pauli exclusion: $n = 0, 1$ only).

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: B**
Dirac fields: Fermionic, anticommutation relations
$\{\hat{\psi}_a, \hat{\psi}^\dagger_b\} = \delta_{ab}\delta^3(\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{y})$
All other anticommutators zero
Required for positive energy, causality
Commutation would give negative norm states

**Problem 2: D**
Charge: $\hat{Q} = \sum_{\mathbf{p}, s}(\hat{b}^\dagger\hat{b} - \hat{d}^\dagger\hat{d})$
Particles $\hat{b}$: positive charge
Antiparticles $\hat{d}$: negative charge
Sign difference crucial
Conserved: $[\hat{Q}, \hat{H}] = 0$

**Problem 3: B**
Fermions: $n = 0, 1$ only (Pauli exclusion)
From $(\hat{b}^\dagger)^2 = 0$ (anticommutation)
Cannot have $n = 2, 3, \ldots$
Bosons: $n = 0, 1, 2, 3, \ldots$ (no restriction)
Explains atomic structure, stability of matter

**Problem 4: B**
Spin-statistics theorem: Deep result from QFT + relativity
Half-integer spin ($s = 1/2, 3/2, \ldots$) → Fermions (anticommute)
Integer spin ($s = 0, 1, 2, \ldots$) → Bosons (commute)
Required by causality + energy positivity
Experimentally confirmed

**Problem 5: C**
Normal ordering: $:\hat{H}:$ places $\hat{b}^\dagger$ left of $\hat{b}$
Without: $\hat{H} = \sum E_p(\hat{b}^\dagger\hat{b} - \hat{d}\hat{d}^\dagger)$ (negative!)
With: $:\hat{H}: = \sum E_p(\hat{b}^\dagger\hat{b} + \hat{d}^\dagger\hat{d})$ (positive!)
Crucial for stability
Vacuum: lowest energy state
