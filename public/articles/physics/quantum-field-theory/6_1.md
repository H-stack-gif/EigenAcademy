# Quantum Field Theory Topic 6.1: Yang-Mills Lagrangian and Gauge Symmetry

The electromagnetic force emerges from requiring local U(1) gauge invariance—the freedom to rotate the phase of charged fields independently at each spacetime point. Yang and Mills in 1954 asked a profound question: what if we demand invariance under more general transformations, where the symmetry group itself has non-commuting elements? Replacing the Abelian group U(1) with non-Abelian groups like SU(2) or SU(3) leads to gauge theories where the gauge bosons themselves carry charge and interact with each other. A photon is electrically neutral and photons don't scatter off photons at tree level, but in non-Abelian theories, gauge bosons carry the very charge they mediate—gluons carry color charge, W and Z bosons carry weak isospin. This self-interaction, absent in electromagnetism, produces phenomena completely foreign to QED: asymptotic freedom where interactions weaken at short distances, confinement where colored particles can never exist in isolation, and a rich vacuum structure with instantons and topological winding.

The mathematical structure begins with Lie groups and their algebras. A Lie group $G$ like SU(N) has generators $T^a$ (Hermitian, traceless $N \times N$ matrices) satisfying the commutation relation $[T^a, T^b] = if^{abc}T^c$ where the structure constants $f^{abc}$ encode the group's non-commutativity. For SU(2), these are just the Pauli matrices with $f^{abc} = \epsilon^{abc}$, while SU(3) has eight generators (Gell-Mann matrices) with more complex structure constants. The dimensionality is $\dim[\text{SU}(N)] = N^2 - 1$, giving three gauge bosons for weak SU(2) and eight gluons for color SU(3). Under a local gauge transformation, matter fields transform as $\psi(x) \to g(x)\psi(x)$ where $g(x) = \exp(i\alpha^a(x)T^a)$ varies with spacetime position. The gauge field must transform non-trivially to maintain covariance: introducing the gauge potential matrix $\mathcal{A}_\mu = A_\mu^a T^a$, the transformation law becomes $\mathcal{A}_\mu \to g\mathcal{A}_\mu g^{-1} - (i/e)(\partial_\mu g)g^{-1}$, reducing to the familiar QED form $A_\mu \to A_\mu + (1/e)\partial_\mu\alpha$ when the group is Abelian.

The covariant derivative $D_\mu = \partial_\mu + ie\mathcal{A}_\mu = \partial_\mu + ieA_\mu^a T^a$ ensures that $D_\mu\psi$ transforms like $\psi$ itself under gauge transformations, allowing construction of gauge-invariant kinetic terms. Computing the commutator $[D_\mu, D_\nu]$ reveals the field strength tensor: $[D_\mu, D_\nu] = ie\mathcal{F}_{\mu\nu}$ where $\mathcal{F}_{\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu\mathcal{A}_\nu - \partial_\nu\mathcal{A}_\mu + ie[\mathcal{A}_\mu, \mathcal{A}_\nu]$. Writing this in components gives $F_{\mu\nu}^a = \partial_\mu A_\nu^a - \partial_\nu A_\mu^a + ef^{abc}A_\mu^b A_\nu^c$, where the crucial non-linear term $ef^{abc}A_\mu^b A_\nu^c$ distinguishes non-Abelian from Abelian theories. This term, proportional to the structure constants, mandates that gauge bosons couple to themselves. The Yang-Mills Lagrangian $\mathcal{L}_{YM} = -\frac{1}{4}F_{\mu\nu}^a F^{\mu\nu,a}$ when expanded contains not just the free gauge field kinetic term but also cubic and quartic self-interactions: three-gluon vertices from the $A^3$ terms and four-gluon vertices from $A^4$ terms in the field strength squared.

### Example 1: SU(N) Generators and Structure Constants

For SU(2) and SU(3), write the generators explicitly and derive key structure constant relations.

**Solution:**

SU(2) generators are $T^a = \sigma^a/2$ where $\sigma^a$ are Pauli matrices:

$$T^1 = \frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \quad T^2 = \frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix} 0 & -i \\ i & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \quad T^3 = \frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$$

The commutation relation $[T^a, T^b] = i\epsilon^{abc}T^c$ gives structure constants $f^{abc} = \epsilon^{abc}$ (Levi-Civita symbol). Computing directly: $[T^1, T^2] = iT^3$, confirming the angular momentum algebra.

For SU(3), generators are $T^a = \lambda^a/2$ where $\lambda^a$ are the eight Gell-Mann matrices. The structure constants satisfy antisymmetry $f^{abc} = -f^{bac}$ and the Jacobi identity $f^{abe}f^{ecd} + f^{bce}f^{ead} + f^{cae}f^{ebd} = 0$. Key values include $f^{123} = 1$, $f^{147} = f^{246} = f^{257} = f^{345} = 1/2$, $f^{458} = f^{678} = \sqrt{3}/2$. The normalization $\text{Tr}[T^a T^b] = \frac{1}{2}\delta^{ab}$ fixes conventional factors.

The Casimir operators characterize representations. For the fundamental representation of SU(N), $\sum_a (T^a)^2 = C_F \mathbb{1}$ where $C_F = (N^2-1)/(2N)$. For SU(3), $C_F = 4/3$. For the adjoint representation (gluons), $\sum_a f^{acd}f^{bcd} = C_A\delta^{ab}$ where $C_A = N$ for SU(N). These Casimirs appear ubiquitously in loop calculations and beta function computations.

**Answer:** SU(2) has $f^{abc} = \epsilon^{abc}$; SU(3) has 8 generators with $C_F = 4/3$, $C_A = 3$ determining loop corrections.

The gauge transformation properties reveal why non-Abelian theories are fundamentally different from QED. In Abelian U(1), the gauge field transforms as $A_\mu \to A_\mu + \partial_\mu\alpha$ which is purely additive—the photon doesn't carry charge. But for non-Abelian groups, the transformation $\mathcal{A}_\mu \to g\mathcal{A}_\mu g^{-1} - (i/e)(\partial_\mu g)g^{-1}$ mixes different gauge field components through the group element $g$. Infinitesimally, this becomes $\delta A_\mu^a = (1/e)\partial_\mu\alpha^a + f^{abc}A_\mu^b\alpha^c$, where the second term proportional to structure constants shows that gauge bosons transform in the adjoint representation—they carry the charge themselves. This is why gluons interact with each other while photons do not.

### Example 2: Gauge Transformation of Matter and Gauge Fields

Derive the infinitesimal gauge transformations and verify covariance of the covariant derivative.

**Solution:**

Under gauge transformation $g(x) = e^{i\alpha^a(x)T^a}$, matter field transforms as $\psi \to g\psi$. Infinitesimally (small $\alpha^a$):

$$\delta\psi = i\alpha^a T^a \psi$$

For the gauge potential matrix $\mathcal{A}_\mu = A_\mu^a T^a$, the transformation $\mathcal{A}_\mu \to g\mathcal{A}_\mu g^{-1} - (i/e)(\partial_\mu g)g^{-1}$ gives infinitesimally:

$$\delta\mathcal{A}_\mu = i[\alpha^a T^a, \mathcal{A}_\mu] - \frac{i}{e}\partial_\mu(\alpha^a T^a) = i\alpha^a[T^a, A_\mu^b T^b] - \frac{i}{e}\partial_\mu\alpha^a T^a$$

Using $[T^a, T^b] = if^{abc}T^c$:

$$\delta\mathcal{A}_\mu = i\alpha^a A_\mu^b(if^{abc}T^c) - \frac{i}{e}\partial_\mu\alpha^a T^a = -\alpha^a f^{abc}A_\mu^b T^c - \frac{i}{e}\partial_\mu\alpha^a T^a$$

In components: $\delta A_\mu^a = \frac{1}{e}\partial_\mu\alpha^a + f^{abc}A_\mu^b\alpha^c$. The covariant derivative $D_\mu\psi = \partial_\mu\psi + ie\mathcal{A}_\mu\psi$ transforms as:

$$\delta(D_\mu\psi) = \partial_\mu(\delta\psi) + ie(\delta\mathcal{A}_\mu)\psi + ie\mathcal{A}_\mu(\delta\psi)$$

After substitution and using the Leibniz rule on $g(\partial_\mu\psi)$, terms cancel leaving $\delta(D_\mu\psi) = i\alpha^a T^a(D_\mu\psi)$, proving $D_\mu\psi$ transforms covariantly like $\psi$ itself.

**Answer:** Infinitesimal transformations: $\delta\psi = i\alpha^a T^a\psi$, $\delta A_\mu^a = \frac{1}{e}\partial_\mu\alpha^a + f^{abc}A_\mu^b\alpha^c$; covariance verified.

The field strength tensor inherits gauge covariance from the covariant derivative. Since $[D_\mu, D_\nu] = ie\mathcal{F}_{\mu\nu}$ and both $D_\mu$ and $D_\nu$ transform covariantly, the commutator must as well: $\mathcal{F}_{\mu\nu} \to g\mathcal{F}_{\mu\nu}g^{-1}$. In components, this means $F_{\mu\nu}^a$ transforms in the adjoint representation, picking up structure constant factors. The explicit form $F_{\mu\nu}^a = \partial_\mu A_\nu^a - \partial_\nu A_\mu^a + ef^{abc}A_\mu^b A_\nu^c$ makes the non-linearity manifest—the field strength depends quadratically on the gauge potential through the $ef^{abc}A_\mu^b A_\nu^c$ term. This stands in stark contrast to electromagnetism where $F_{\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu A_\nu - \partial_\nu A_\mu$ is linear. The non-linear term generates self-interactions when we construct the Lagrangian.

### Example 3: Field Strength Tensor Derivation

Calculate the field strength from the covariant derivative commutator and identify the non-linear term.

**Solution:**

Start with the covariant derivative $D_\mu = \partial_\mu + ie\mathcal{A}_\mu$ where $\mathcal{A}_\mu = A_\mu^a T^a$. Compute the commutator:

$$[D_\mu, D_\nu] = [\partial_\mu + ie\mathcal{A}_\mu, \partial_\nu + ie\mathcal{A}_\nu]$$

Expanding: $[D_\mu, D_\nu] = \partial_\mu(ie\mathcal{A}_\nu) - \partial_\nu(ie\mathcal{A}_\mu) + (ie)^2[\mathcal{A}_\mu, \mathcal{A}_\nu]$

$$= ie(\partial_\mu\mathcal{A}_\nu - \partial_\nu\mathcal{A}_\mu) - e^2[\mathcal{A}_\mu, \mathcal{A}_\nu]$$

Define field strength: $\mathcal{F}_{\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu\mathcal{A}_\nu - \partial_\nu\mathcal{A}_\mu + ie[\mathcal{A}_\mu, \mathcal{A}_\nu]$, giving $[D_\mu, D_\nu] = ie\mathcal{F}_{\mu\nu}$.

In components with $\mathcal{A}_\mu = A_\mu^a T^a$:

$$[\mathcal{A}_\mu, \mathcal{A}_\nu] = A_\mu^a A_\nu^b [T^a, T^b] = A_\mu^a A_\nu^b (if^{abc}T^c) = if^{abc}A_\mu^a A_\nu^b T^c$$

Therefore:

$$\mathcal{F}_{\mu\nu} = (\partial_\mu A_\nu^a - \partial_\nu A_\mu^a)T^a + ie(if^{abc}A_\mu^a A_\nu^b T^c) = F_{\mu\nu}^c T^c$$

where $F_{\mu\nu}^c = \partial_\mu A_\nu^c - \partial_\nu A_\mu^c + ef^{cab}A_\mu^a A_\nu^b = \partial_\mu A_\nu^c - \partial_\nu A_\mu^c + ef^{abc}A_\mu^b A_\nu^c$ (relabeling).

The non-linear term $ef^{abc}A_\mu^b A_\nu^c$ is quadratic in gauge fields. For Abelian theories, $f^{abc} = 0$ and we recover Maxwell's linear field strength. The Bianchi identity $D_\mu\mathcal{F}_{\nu\rho} + D_\nu\mathcal{F}_{\rho\mu} + D_\rho\mathcal{F}_{\mu\nu} = 0$ still holds with the covariant derivative.

**Answer:** $F_{\mu\nu}^a = \partial_\mu A_\nu^a - \partial_\nu A_\mu^a + ef^{abc}A_\mu^b A_\nu^c$; non-linear term $\sim A^2$ from structure constants.

Writing the Yang-Mills Lagrangian $\mathcal{L}_{YM} = -\frac{1}{4}F_{\mu\nu}^a F^{\mu\nu,a}$ and expanding the field strength reveals the interaction structure. The quadratic part $-\frac{1}{4}(\partial_\mu A_\nu^a - \partial_\nu A_\mu^a)^2$ gives the free gauge boson kinetic term, propagating the massless gauge bosons. The cubic terms come from mixing the linear and non-linear pieces: $-\frac{1}{2}(\partial_\mu A_\nu^a - \partial_\nu A_\mu^a)(ef^{abc}A^{b\mu}A^{c\nu})$, yielding three-gluon vertices with one derivative and two gauge fields. The quartic terms arise from $(ef^{abc}A_\mu^b A_\nu^c)^2$, producing four-gluon vertices with no derivatives and four gauge fields. These self-interactions are mandatory—removing them would break gauge invariance. The precise vertex structures, including momentum dependence from derivatives, are determined by expanding the Lagrangian carefully.

### Example 4: Yang-Mills Lagrangian Expansion

Expand the Yang-Mills Lagrangian to identify kinetic, cubic, and quartic terms explicitly.

**Solution:**

Start with $\mathcal{L}_{YM} = -\frac{1}{4}F_{\mu\nu}^a F^{\mu\nu,a}$ where $F_{\mu\nu}^a = \partial_\mu A_\nu^a - \partial_\nu A_\mu^a + ef^{abc}A_\mu^b A_\nu^c$. Expanding:

$$F_{\mu\nu}^a F^{\mu\nu,a} = (\partial_\mu A_\nu^a - \partial_\nu A_\mu^a + ef^{abc}A_\mu^b A_\nu^c)(\partial^\mu A^{\nu,a} - \partial^\nu A^{\mu,a} + ef^{ade}A^{d\mu}A^{e\nu})$$

The pure derivative term gives: $(\partial_\mu A_\nu^a)^2 - 2(\partial_\mu A_\nu^a)(\partial^\nu A^{\mu,a}) + (\partial_\nu A_\mu^a)^2$. Using antisymmetry and partial integration, this becomes the standard kinetic term $-\frac{1}{2}(\partial_\mu A_\nu^a)(\partial^\mu A^{\nu,a}) + \frac{1}{2}(\partial_\mu A_\nu^a)(\partial^\nu A^{\mu,a})$.

The cubic terms (mixing linear and quadratic pieces):

$$\mathcal{L}_3 = -\frac{1}{2}ef^{abc}(\partial_\mu A_\nu^a - \partial_\nu A_\mu^a)(A^{b\mu}A^{c\nu})$$

The quartic terms (pure quadratic):

$$\mathcal{L}_4 = -\frac{1}{4}e^2 f^{abc}f^{ade}(A_\mu^b A_\nu^c)(A^{d\mu}A^{e\nu})$$

Simplifying using $f^{abc}f^{ade} = \delta^{be}\delta^{cd}C_A - (\text{permutations})$ where $C_A$ is the adjoint Casimir:

$$\mathcal{L}_4 = -\frac{e^2}{4}[f^{abe}f^{cde}A_\mu^b A^{c\mu}A_\nu^d A^{e\nu} + f^{abe}f^{cde}A_\mu^b A_\nu^c A^{d\mu}A^{e\nu}]$$

These terms generate Feynman rules: three-gluon vertex $\sim ef^{abc}[g_{\mu\nu}(k_1-k_2)_\rho + \text{permutations}]$ and four-gluon vertex $\sim e^2[\cdots]$ with contracted structure constants.

**Answer:** Expansion gives kinetic $\sim (\partial A)^2$, cubic $\sim ef^{abc}(\partial A)AA$, quartic $\sim e^2 f^2 A^4$; self-interactions.

Coupling matter fermions to Yang-Mills fields requires the covariant derivative in the fermion kinetic term. The Lagrangian $\mathcal{L}_{\text{matter}} = \bar{\psi}(i\gamma^\mu D_\mu - m)\psi$ with $D_\mu = \partial_\mu + ieA_\mu^a T^a$ is manifestly gauge invariant since both $\bar{\psi}$ and $D_\mu\psi$ transform covariantly. Expanding gives the free Dirac term $\bar{\psi}(i\not\!\partial - m)\psi$ plus the interaction $e\bar{\psi}\gamma^\mu A_\mu^a T^a\psi$. The generator $T^a$ acts on the fermion's color or isospin index, making this vertex color-dependent. For QCD with quarks transforming in the fundamental representation of SU(3), we have $T_{ij}^a = (\lambda^a/2)_{ij}$ where $i,j = 1,2,3$ are color indices. The vertex connects quark color $i$, antiquark color $j$, and gluon $a$ with amplitude $-ig_s\gamma^\mu T_{ij}^a$ where $g_s$ is the strong coupling constant.

### Example 5: Matter Coupling and Gauge Invariance

Show that the matter Lagrangian $\bar{\psi}(i\gamma^\mu D_\mu - m)\psi$ is gauge invariant.

**Solution:**

Under gauge transformation $\psi \to g\psi = e^{i\alpha^a T^a}\psi$ and $\bar{\psi} \to \bar{\psi}g^\dagger = \bar{\psi}e^{-i\alpha^a T^a}$ (since $T^a$ are Hermitian). The covariant derivative transforms as $D_\mu\psi \to gD_\mu\psi$. Therefore:

$$\bar{\psi}(i\gamma^\mu D_\mu - m)\psi \to \bar{\psi}g^\dagger g(i\gamma^\mu D_\mu - m)\psi = \bar{\psi}(i\gamma^\mu D_\mu - m)\psi$$

using $g^\dagger g = 1$ for unitary transformations. The Lagrangian is gauge invariant.

Expanding the covariant derivative: $D_\mu\psi = \partial_\mu\psi + ieA_\mu^a T^a\psi$, so:

$$\bar{\psi}i\gamma^\mu D_\mu\psi = \bar{\psi}i\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu\psi + ie\bar{\psi}\gamma^\mu A_\mu^a T^a\psi$$

The first term is the free Dirac Lagrangian. The second gives the fermion-gauge boson vertex with Feynman rule $-ie\gamma^\mu T^a$ in momentum space. For QCD quarks in fundamental SU(3), $T^a = \lambda^a/2$ acts on color indices: vertex connects quark $i$, antiquark $j$, gluon $a$ with factor $(T^a)_{ij} = (\lambda^a/2)_{ij}$.

The full QCD Lagrangian combines Yang-Mills and quark terms:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{QCD}} = -\frac{1}{4}F_{\mu\nu}^a F^{\mu\nu,a} + \sum_f \bar{\psi}_f(i\gamma^\mu D_\mu - m_f)\psi_f$$

where sum runs over quark flavors $f = u,d,s,c,b,t$, each a color triplet.

**Answer:** Gauge invariance proven: $\bar{\psi}g^\dagger g D_\mu\psi = \bar{\psi}D_\mu\psi$; vertex $-ie\gamma^\mu T^a$ couples fermions to gauge bosons.

Physical degrees of freedom require careful counting due to gauge redundancy. A gauge field $A_\mu^a$ naively has $4 \times (N^2-1)$ components—four spacetime indices times the number of group generators. However, gauge transformations $\delta A_\mu^a = \partial_\mu\alpha^a + \cdots$ provide $(N^2-1)$ redundancies (one per gauge parameter $\alpha^a$), while Gauss's law constraints remove another $(N^2-1)$ unphysical time components. This leaves $2(N^2-1)$ physical polarizations—two transverse modes per gauge boson, as required for massless spin-1 particles propagating at light speed. For SU(3) QCD, this gives $2 \times 8 = 16$ physical gluon degrees of freedom. When gauge bosons acquire mass through the Higgs mechanism as in electroweak theory, they gain a longitudinal polarization and have three physical degrees of freedom each.

### Example 6: Counting Physical Polarizations

Count physical degrees of freedom for non-Abelian gauge bosons including gauge fixing.

**Solution:**

Starting with gauge field $A_\mu^a$ having four spacetime components and $(N^2-1)$ group indices:

Total components: $4 \times (N^2-1)$

Gauge transformations $\delta A_\mu^a = \frac{1}{e}\partial_\mu\alpha^a + f^{abc}A_\mu^b\alpha^c$ have $(N^2-1)$ independent parameters $\alpha^a$. Each gauge transformation removes one degree of freedom, leaving:

After gauge fixing: $4 \times (N^2-1) - (N^2-1) = 3(N^2-1)$

Gauss's law $D^i E_i^a = \rho^a$ (where $E_i^a$ is the electric field, $\rho^a$ the charge density) imposes $(N^2-1)$ constraints—one per color component. These are first-class constraints eliminating the time components $A_0^a$:

Physical degrees of freedom: $3(N^2-1) - (N^2-1) = 2(N^2-1)$

These are two transverse polarizations per gauge boson, as required for massless spin-1 particles.

For SU(2): $N=2$, giving $2(2^2-1) = 2 \times 3 = 6$ physical DOF (three $W^\pm, Z$ bosons times two polarizations each before mass).

For SU(3): $N=3$, giving $2(3^2-1) = 2 \times 8 = 16$ physical DOF (eight gluons times two polarizations).

After electroweak symmetry breaking, W and Z acquire mass and gain longitudinal polarization, having 3 DOF each.

**Answer:** Non-Abelian gauge field has $2(N^2-1)$ physical DOF; two transverse polarizations per massless gauge boson.

The structure constants satisfy important algebraic identities beyond just antisymmetry. The Jacobi identity $f^{abe}f^{ecd} + f^{bce}f^{ead} + f^{cae}f^{ebd} = 0$ ensures consistency of the Lie algebra and plays a role in verifying gauge invariance of amplitudes. Completeness relations like $\sum_a T_{ij}^a T_{kl}^a = \frac{1}{2}(\delta_{il}\delta_{kj} - \frac{1}{N}\delta_{ij}\delta_{kl})$ for the fundamental representation of SU(N) appear constantly in color algebra calculations when simplifying Feynman diagrams with multiple gluon exchanges. The Fierz identity allows reshuffling of color indices in four-quark operators, essential for computing processes like quark-quark scattering where $t$-channel and $u$-channel gluon exchanges interfere.

### Example 7: Color Algebra Identities

Derive the fundamental representation completeness relation and apply to color factor calculation.

**Solution:**

For SU(N) fundamental representation, generators satisfy $\text{Tr}[T^a T^b] = T_F\delta^{ab}$ where $T_F = 1/2$. The completeness relation follows from the identity:

$$\sum_a T_{ij}^a T_{kl}^a = \frac{1}{2}\left(\delta_{il}\delta_{kj} - \frac{1}{N}\delta_{ij}\delta_{kl}\right)$$

This can be proven by considering both sides as operators in the tensor product space and using SU(N) representation theory. The first term $\delta_{il}\delta_{kj}/2$ comes from the identity representation, while the second $-\delta_{ij}\delta_{kl}/(2N)$ subtracts the SU(N) singlet projection.

Application: Calculate the color factor for $q_i + \bar{q}_j \to q_k + \bar{q}_l$ via gluon exchange. The amplitude has structure $T_{ik}^a T_{lj}^a$ (sum over gluon color $a$). Using completeness:

$$\sum_a T_{ik}^a T_{lj}^a = \frac{1}{2}\left(\delta_{il}\delta_{kj} - \frac{1}{N}\delta_{ik}\delta_{lj}\right)$$

For $N=3$ (QCD) and identical colors $i=k$, $j=l$ (forward scattering):

$$\sum_a T_{ii}^a T_{jj}^a = \frac{1}{2}\left(\delta_{ij}\delta_{ij} - \frac{1}{3}\delta_{ii}\delta_{jj}\right) = \frac{1}{2}\left(1 - \frac{1}{3} \cdot 3 \cdot 1\right) = 0$$

The color factor vanishes for identical final-state colors due to Bose symmetry and antisymmetry of generators.

The Casimir $C_F = \frac{N^2-1}{2N}$ for fundamental gives $C_F = 4/3$ for SU(3), while $C_A = N$ for adjoint gives $C_A = 3$.

**Answer:** Completeness: $\sum_a T_{ij}^a T_{kl}^a = \frac{1}{2}(\delta_{il}\delta_{kj} - \frac{1}{N}\delta_{ij}\delta_{kl})$; determines color factors in amplitudes.

The full structure of Yang-Mills theory with matter combines all these elements: the non-Abelian gauge group with its structure constants, the covariant derivative ensuring gauge covariance, the non-linear field strength generating self-interactions, and the matter coupling through generators in appropriate representations. For QCD, this is SU(3) with quarks in the fundamental representation and gluons in the adjoint, giving eight colored gluons mediating the strong force. For electroweak SU(2)×U(1), the structure is more intricate with Higgs mechanism breaking the symmetry, but the underlying Yang-Mills framework remains the same. The self-interactions of gauge bosons—absent in QED, mandatory in non-Abelian theories—are the key to asymptotic freedom and confinement, the defining features of strong interactions.

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: C** — SU(N) special unitary $N \times N$ matrices have $N^2$ real parameters, minus 1 constraint from unit determinant gives $N^2-1$ generators. SU(2) has 3 (Pauli), SU(3) has 8 (Gell-Mann), general formula $\dim[\text{SU}(N)] = N^2-1$.

**Problem 2: B** — Non-Abelian field strength $F_{\mu\nu}^a = \partial_\mu A_\nu^a - \partial_\nu A_\mu^a + ef^{abc}A_\mu^b A_\nu^c$ has linear derivative terms plus non-linear $A^2$ term from structure constants. Maxwell (Abelian) has only $\partial A$, difference produces self-interactions.

**Problem 3: B** — Expanding $(F^a)^2$ gives kinetic $\sim (\partial A)^2$ plus cubic $\sim (\partial A)A^2$ from cross terms plus quartic $\sim (A^2)^2$. Three-gluon vertices from cubic, four-gluon from quartic—self-interactions mandatory for gauge invariance. QED lacks these (Abelian).

**Problem 4: B** — Non-Abelian: gauge bosons carry gauge charge (gluons carry color, W/Z carry weak charge), unlike neutral photon. Transformation $A_\mu \to gA_\mu g^{-1} + \cdots$ shows gauge fields transform non-trivially. Source of asymptotic freedom and distinctive QCD phenomenology.

**Problem 5: B** — Covariant derivative transforms as $D_\mu\psi \to g(D_\mu\psi)$, ensuring $\bar{\psi}\gamma^\mu D_\mu\psi$ is gauge invariant. Without gauge potential term, ordinary derivative fails: $\partial_\mu(g\psi) = g\partial_\mu\psi + (\partial_\mu g)\psi$ has extra term breaking covariance.
