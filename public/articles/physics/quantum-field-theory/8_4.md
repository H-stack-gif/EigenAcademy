# Quantum Field Theory Topic 8.4: Introduction to Supersymmetry

Supersymmetry (SUSY) posits a fundamental symmetry between bosons and fermions, extending the Poincaré algebra with fermionic generators $Q_\alpha$ and $\bar{Q}_{\dot{\beta}}$ that satisfy the anticommutation relations $\{Q_\alpha, \bar{Q}_{\dot{\beta}}\} = 2\sigma^\mu_{\alpha\dot{\beta}}P_\mu$ where $P_\mu$ is the four-momentum operator. This elegant mathematical structure implies that every bosonic degree of freedom has a fermionic superpartner with identical gauge quantum numbers and vice versa, doubling the particle content of any theory. The most compelling motivation for SUSY is its solution to the hierarchy problem: while Standard Model loop corrections to the Higgs mass squared are quadratically divergent $\delta m_h^2 \sim \Lambda_{\text{UV}}^2$, requiring fine-tuning to 30 decimal places if the cutoff extends to the Planck scale, supersymmetric theories exhibit exact cancellations between fermionic and bosonic loops, reducing the divergence to logarithmic $\delta m_h^2 \sim \ln(\Lambda/m_{\text{SUSY}})$. The Minimal Supersymmetric Standard Model (MSSM) introduces superpartners for all SM particles—squarks and sleptons (scalar partners of quarks and leptons), gauginos (fermionic partners of gauge bosons), and Higgsinos (fermionic partners of two Higgs doublets required by SUSY), along with R-parity conservation $R = (-1)^{3(B-L)+2s}$ that ensures the lightest supersymmetric particle (LSP) is stable and provides a natural dark matter candidate. Despite its theoretical elegance, no superpartners have been discovered at the LHC, with current bounds excluding gluinos below $\sim 2.3$ TeV and squarks below $\sim 1.8$ TeV in simplified models, leading to increasing tension with naturalness and the emergence of the "little hierarchy problem."

The SUSY algebra extends the Poincaré symmetry in a non-trivial way. The Coleman-Mandula theorem proves that the most general symmetry of the S-matrix combining spacetime and internal symmetries is just a direct product—unless we include fermionic generators. The Haag-Lopuszanski-Sohnius theorem shows that SUSY is the unique way to non-trivially extend Poincaré symmetry while maintaining fundamental principles (locality, causality, positivity of energy). The superalgebra includes:

$$\{Q_\alpha, \bar{Q}_{\dot{\beta}}\} = 2\sigma^\mu_{\alpha\dot{\beta}}P_\mu, \quad \{Q_\alpha, Q_\beta\} = \epsilon_{\alpha\beta}Z, \quad [Q_\alpha, P_\mu] = 0$$

where $Z$ is the central charge (vanishing in minimal SUSY). The second relation states that two supersymmetry transformations give a spacetime translation—a profound connection between internal and spacetime symmetries.

A SUSY multiplet contains states with spins differing by 1/2. The simplest is the **chiral multiplet**: a complex scalar $\phi$ and a Weyl fermion $\psi$ (2 bosonic + 2 fermionic real degrees of freedom). The **vector multiplet** contains a gauge boson $A_\mu$ and gaugino $\lambda$ (2 bosonic from transverse polarizations + 2 fermionic from Weyl spinor). In the MSSM, quarks and leptons reside in chiral multiplets with scalar superpartners (squarks $\tilde{q}$, sleptons $\tilde{\ell}$), while gauge bosons have fermionic gaugino partners (gluino $\tilde{g}$, wino $\tilde{W}$, bino $\tilde{B}$).

### Example 1: SUSY Algebra and Supersymmetry Transformations

Derive the consequence of the SUSY algebra $\{Q, \bar{Q}\} = 2\sigma^\mu P_\mu$ for particle masses.

**Solution:**

Consider a one-particle state $|p\rangle$ with four-momentum $p^\mu$. Acting with the SUSY algebra:

$$\{Q_\alpha, \bar{Q}_{\dot{\beta}}\}|p\rangle = 2\sigma^\mu_{\alpha\dot{\beta}}p_\mu|p\rangle$$

Taking the expectation value and using $\langle p|Q^\dagger Q + Q Q^\dagger|p\rangle \geq 0$ (positivity):

$$\sum_{\alpha, \dot{\beta}} \langle p|\{Q_\alpha, \bar{Q}_{\dot{\beta}}\}|p\rangle = 2p_\mu \text{Tr}[\sigma^\mu]$$

In the rest frame $p^\mu = (m, 0, 0, 0)$:

$$\sum_{\alpha\dot{\beta}}\langle p|Q_\alpha\bar{Q}_{\dot{\beta}} + \bar{Q}_{\dot{\beta}}Q_\alpha|p\rangle = 2m \cdot 2 = 4m$$

The left side is non-negative (sum of positive operators), so this provides a **mass bound**: all states in a SUSY multiplet must have the same mass.

**Proof of mass degeneracy**: If $|\phi\rangle$ is a bosonic state with mass $m$, then $Q|\phi\rangle = |\psi\rangle$ is a fermionic state. Computing:

$$P^2|\psi\rangle = P^2 Q|\phi\rangle = Q P^2|\phi\rangle = Q(m^2|\phi\rangle) = m^2|\psi\rangle$$

So $|\psi\rangle$ also has mass $m$! All members of a SUSY multiplet are degenerate in mass.

**Broken SUSY**: We don't observe degenerate bosons and fermions (no $125$ GeV selectron!), so SUSY must be broken. The breaking introduces mass splittings while preserving the cancellations that solve the hierarchy problem—this is **soft SUSY breaking**.

**Answer:** SUSY algebra $\{Q, \bar{Q}\} \propto P_\mu$ implies mass degeneracy within multiplets; observed non-degeneracy requires soft breaking.

Superspace provides an elegant formulation where SUSY transformations are manifest. Introducing Grassmann coordinates $\theta^\alpha$ and $\bar{\theta}_{\dot{\alpha}}$ (anticommuting variables satisfying $\{\theta, \theta\} = 0$), a point in superspace is $(x^\mu, \theta^\alpha, \bar{\theta}_{\dot{\alpha}})$. Superfields are functions on superspace that transform simply under SUSY. The chiral superfield:

$$\Phi(x, \theta) = \phi(x) + \sqrt{2}\theta\psi(x) + \theta\theta F(x)$$

contains scalar $\phi$, fermion $\psi$, and auxiliary field $F$ (non-propagating, eliminated via equations of motion). The superspace derivative $D_\alpha$ satisfying $\{D_\alpha, Q_\beta\} = 0$ allows SUSY-invariant constructions.

The Kähler potential $K(\Phi, \Phi^\dagger)$ and superpotential $W(\Phi)$ encode the SUSY Lagrangian:

$$\mathcal{L} = \int d^4\theta\, K(\Phi, \Phi^\dagger) + \left[\int d^2\theta\, W(\Phi) + \text{h.c.}\right]$$

For canonical kinetic terms $K = \Phi^\dagger\Phi$ and superpotential $W = \frac{1}{2}m\Phi^2 + \frac{1}{3}\lambda\Phi^3$, integrating over Grassmann variables yields:

$$\mathcal{L} = (\partial\phi)^2 + i\bar{\psi}\bar{\sigma}^\mu\partial_\mu\psi + |F|^2 + FW' + \bar{F}\bar{W}' + \frac{1}{2}W''(\psi\psi + \bar{\psi}\bar{\psi})$$

Eliminating auxiliary field: $F = -\bar{W}'(\phi^*)$, giving scalar potential:

$$V(\phi) = |W'(\phi)|^2$$

This is the **F-term potential**. For $W = \frac{1}{2}m\Phi^2 + \frac{1}{3}\lambda\Phi^3$:

$$V = |m\phi + \lambda\phi^2|^2 = m^2|\phi|^2 + \text{interactions}$$

### Example 2: Superpotential and Scalar Potential

Derive the scalar potential for the Wess-Zumino model with $W = \frac{1}{2}m\Phi^2 + \frac{1}{3}g\Phi^3$.

**Solution:**

The superpotential:

$$W(\Phi) = \frac{1}{2}m\Phi^2 + \frac{1}{3}g\Phi^3$$

The derivative:

$$W'(\Phi) = \frac{\partial W}{\partial \Phi} = m\Phi + g\Phi^2$$

The F-term scalar potential:

$$V = |W'(\phi)|^2 = |m\phi + g\phi^2|^2$$

Expanding:

$$V = (m\phi + g\phi^2)(m\phi^* + g(\phi^*)^2)$$

$$= m^2|\phi|^2 + mg\phi(\phi^*)^2 + mg\phi^*\phi^2 + g^2|\phi|^4$$

For real $\phi$:

$$V = m^2\phi^2 + 2mg\phi^3 + g^2\phi^4$$

This gives fermion mass $m$ and scalar mass also $m$ (from $m^2\phi^2$ term), confirming mass degeneracy. The cubic and quartic interactions $\sim g$ couple scalar and fermion with related strengths fixed by SUSY.

The equations of motion from $V$:

$$\frac{\partial V}{\partial \phi} = 2m^2\phi + 6mg\phi^2 + 4g^2\phi^3 = 0$$

For $m^2 > 0$, the minimum is at $\phi = 0$ (SUSY preserving). For $m^2 < 0$, SUSY breaks spontaneously with $\langle\phi\rangle \neq 0$.

**Answer:** Wess-Zumino $V = m^2|\phi|^2 + 2mg\phi(\phi^*)^2 + g^2|\phi|^4$; SUSY fixes scalar/fermion masses equal and relates couplings.

The solution to the hierarchy problem emerges from non-renormalization theorems. In SUSY, the superpotential is not renormalized (to all orders in perturbation theory): $W_{\text{quantum}} = W_{\text{classical}}$. This protects Yukawa couplings and masses from large corrections. For the Higgs mass, consider top quark loop contribution. In the SM:

$$\delta m_h^2 \sim \frac{y_t^2}{16\pi^2}\Lambda^2$$

is quadratically divergent. In SUSY, the stop squark loop has opposite sign:

$$\delta m_h^2 = \frac{y_t^2}{16\pi^2}(m_t^2 - m_{\tilde{t}}^2)\ln\frac{\Lambda}{m_{\tilde{t}}}$$

For exact SUSY ($m_t = m_{\tilde{t}}$), the correction vanishes! With broken SUSY:

$$\delta m_h^2 \sim \frac{y_t^2}{16\pi^2}m_{\text{SUSY}}^2\ln\frac{\Lambda}{m_{\text{SUSY}}}$$

The divergence is only logarithmic. For $m_{\text{SUSY}} \sim 1$ TeV and $\Lambda = M_{\text{Pl}}$:

$$\delta m_h^2 \sim \frac{1}{16\pi^2}(1\text{ TeV})^2 \times 30 \sim (250\text{ GeV})^2$$

This is the same order as $m_h^2 = (125 \text{ GeV})^2$—no fine-tuning needed if $m_{\text{SUSY}} \lesssim$ few TeV.

### Example 3: Hierarchy Problem and SUSY Solution

Calculate the one-loop correction to Higgs mass from top and stop in SUSY.

**Solution:**

**Top quark contribution** (SM):

The one-loop diagram with top in the loop:

$$\delta m_h^2\bigg|_{\text{top}} = -\frac{3y_t^2}{8\pi^2}\int_0^\Lambda \frac{dk^2}{k^2}k^2 = -\frac{3y_t^2}{8\pi^2}\Lambda^2$$

The minus sign from fermion loop, factor 3 from color. For $y_t \approx 1$, $\Lambda = M_{\text{Pl}} \sim 10^{19}$ GeV:

$$\delta m_h^2 \sim -\frac{3}{8\pi^2}(10^{19}\text{ GeV})^2 \sim -(10^{18}\text{ GeV})^2$$

To get $m_h = 125$ GeV requires bare mass tuned to 32 decimal places!

**Stop squark contribution** (SUSY):

Two stop mass eigenstates $\tilde{t}_1, \tilde{t}_2$ with masses $m_{\tilde{t}_i}$. Each contributes:

$$\delta m_h^2\bigg|_{\text{stop}} = +\frac{3y_t^2}{8\pi^2}\int_0^\Lambda \frac{dk^2}{k^2}(k^2 - m_{\tilde{t}}^2)$$

Positive sign from scalar loop. Summing both stops:

$$\delta m_h^2\bigg|_{\text{stop}} \approx +\frac{3y_t^2}{8\pi^2}\left[\Lambda^2 - m_{\tilde{t}_1}^2\ln\frac{\Lambda}{m_{\tilde{t}_1}} - m_{\tilde{t}_2}^2\ln\frac{\Lambda}{m_{\tilde{t}_2}}\right]$$

**Total correction**:

$$\delta m_h^2 = \delta m_h^2\bigg|_{\text{top}} + \delta m_h^2\bigg|_{\text{stop}} \sim \frac{3y_t^2}{8\pi^2}(m_{\tilde{t}_1}^2 + m_{\tilde{t}_2}^2)\ln\frac{\Lambda}{m_{\tilde{t}}}$$

The $\Lambda^2$ terms cancel! For $m_{\tilde{t}} \sim 1$ TeV:

$$\delta m_h^2 \sim \frac{3}{8\pi^2}(1\text{ TeV})^2 \times 30 \sim (300\text{ GeV})^2$$

This is $\mathcal{O}(m_h^2)$—natural! No fine-tuning if stops exist near TeV scale.

**Answer:** Top gives $\delta m_h^2 \sim -y_t^2\Lambda^2/(8\pi^2)$; stop gives $+y_t^2\Lambda^2/(8\pi^2)$; cancel quadratic divergence, leaving $\sim y_t^2 m_{\tilde{t}}^2\ln(\Lambda/m_{\tilde{t}})$.

The MSSM extends the SM minimally with SUSY. Two Higgs doublets are required: one coupling to up-type quarks ($H_u$), one to down-type ($H_d$). This necessity arises because the superpotential must be holomorphic in chiral superfields—we cannot use $H^\dagger$ to give mass to both up and down quarks as in the SM. The Higgs sector has superpotential:

$$W = \mu H_u H_d + y_u Q H_u u^c + y_d Q H_d d^c + y_e L H_d e^c$$

where $\mu$ is the Higgsino mass parameter. After electroweak symmetry breaking with $\langle H_u^0\rangle = v_u/\sqrt{2}$, $\langle H_d^0\rangle = v_d/\sqrt{2}$, the ratio $\tan\beta = v_u/v_d$ becomes a crucial parameter.

R-parity conservation $R = (-1)^{3(B-L)+2s}$ distinguishes SM particles ($R = +1$) from superpartners ($R = -1$). This discrete symmetry forbids dimension-4 operators like $\lambda LQd^c$ that would violate baryon or lepton number and cause rapid proton decay. With R-parity, superpartners are produced in pairs and the LSP is stable—a perfect dark matter candidate. The neutralino LSP $\tilde{\chi}_1^0$ (mixture of bino, wino, Higgsinos) with mass $\sim 100$ GeV - $1$ TeV and weak interactions matches observed dark matter relic density in many scenarios.

### Example 4: R-Parity and LSP Dark Matter

Show that R-parity conservation implies LSP stability and compute its relic density parametrically.

**Solution:**

**R-parity definition**:

$$R = (-1)^{3(B-L)+2s}$$

For SM particles:
- Quarks: $B = 1/3$, $L = 0$, $s = 1/2$ → $R = (-1)^{1+1} = +1$
- Leptons: $B = 0$, $L = 1$, $s = 1/2$ → $R = (-1)^{-3+1} = +1$
- Gauge bosons: $B = L = 0$, $s = 1$ → $R = (-1)^{2} = +1$

For superpartners:
- Squarks: $B = 1/3$, $L = 0$, $s = 0$ → $R = (-1)^{1+0} = -1$
- Sleptons: $B = 0$, $L = 1$, $s = 0$ → $R = (-1)^{-3+0} = -1$
- Gauginos: $B = L = 0$, $s = 1/2$ → $R = (-1)^{1} = -1$

**Conservation**: If $R$ is exactly conserved, vertices must preserve total R-parity. This forbids:
- Single sparticle production/decay (requires $R = +1 \to R = -1$)
- LSP decay (lightest sparticle has nowhere to go)

**LSP stability**: The LSP cannot decay without violating R-parity conservation. If it's neutral and colorless (like neutralino $\tilde{\chi}_1^0$), it's a perfect dark matter candidate.

**Relic density**: In early universe, neutralinos $\chi$ are in thermal equilibrium. As universe cools, annihilation $\chi\chi \to \text{SM}$ freezes out when $\Gamma_{\text{ann}} < H$ (Hubble rate). The freeze-out equation:

$$\frac{dn}{dt} + 3Hn = -\langle\sigma v\rangle(n^2 - n_{\text{eq}}^2)$$

Solving gives relic density:

$$\Omega_\chi h^2 \approx \frac{3 \times 10^{-27}\text{ cm}^3\text{s}^{-1}}{\langle\sigma v\rangle}$$

For weak-scale cross section $\langle\sigma v\rangle \sim \alpha_W^2/m_\chi^2 \sim 10^{-26}$ cm$^3$s$^{-1}$ with $m_\chi \sim 100$ GeV:

$$\Omega_\chi h^2 \sim 0.1$$

This is the observed dark matter density! The "WIMP miracle"—weak-scale particles naturally give correct relic abundance.

**Answer:** R-parity conservation forbids LSP decay (lightest $R = -1$ state stable); neutralino LSP with weak interactions gives $\Omega h^2 \sim 0.1$ (WIMP miracle).

SUSY breaking must be "soft" to preserve the hierarchy problem solution while giving realistic masses. Hard breaking (e.g., mass terms not protected by SUSY) would reintroduce quadratic divergences. Soft breaking terms have positive mass dimension $\leq 3$:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{soft}} = -m_i^2|\phi_i|^2 - (A_{ijk}\phi_i\phi_j\phi_k + \frac{1}{2}M_a\lambda_a\lambda_a + \text{h.c.})$$

where $m_i^2$ are scalar masses, $A_{ijk}$ are trilinear couplings, and $M_a$ are gaugino masses. These break SUSY but preserve non-renormalization theorems. The mechanism of SUSY breaking is unknown—possibilities include gravity mediation (Planck-suppressed), gauge mediation (messenger particles), or anomaly mediation. Each predicts different sparticle mass patterns testable at colliders.

Current LHC constraints severely limit SUSY parameter space. No superpartners discovered despite $\sim 140$ fb$^{-1}$ at 13 TeV. Simplified model limits:
- Gluinos: $m_{\tilde{g}} > 2.3$ TeV (all-hadronic searches)
- First/second generation squarks: $m_{\tilde{q}} > 1.8$ TeV
- Stops: $m_{\tilde{t}} > 1.2$ TeV (depending on decay mode)
- Electroweak production: $m_{\tilde{\chi}^\pm} > 800$ GeV (direct production)

These limits create tension with naturalness. If $m_{\tilde{t}} > 1$ TeV, the fine-tuning is:

$$\Delta \sim \frac{\delta m_h^2}{m_h^2} \sim \frac{m_{\tilde{t}}^2}{m_h^2} \sim \frac{(1\text{ TeV})^2}{(125\text{ GeV})^2} \sim 60$$

Requiring $\sim 2\%$ tuning. Some regard this as acceptable ("little hierarchy"), others see it as problematic. Natural SUSY scenarios focus on light stops and Higgsinos while allowing heavy squarks/sleptons.

### Example 5: Fine-Tuning Measure in MSSM

Calculate the Barbieri-Giudice fine-tuning parameter for stop mass.

**Solution:**

The Higgs mass receives corrections from many sources. Define fine-tuning:

$$\Delta_i = \frac{\partial \ln m_h^2}{\partial \ln p_i}$$

where $p_i$ are fundamental parameters (stop mass, gaugino mass, $\mu$, etc.). For stop contribution:

$$m_h^2 = m_{h,0}^2 + \frac{3y_t^2}{8\pi^2}m_{\tilde{t}}^2\ln\frac{\Lambda}{m_{\tilde{t}}} + \cdots$$

The derivative:

$$\frac{\partial m_h^2}{\partial m_{\tilde{t}}^2} = \frac{3y_t^2}{8\pi^2}\left[\ln\frac{\Lambda}{m_{\tilde{t}}} - 1\right] \approx \frac{3y_t^2}{8\pi^2}\ln\frac{\Lambda}{m_{\tilde{t}}}$$

For $\Lambda = M_{\text{GUT}} \sim 10^{16}$ GeV, $y_t \approx 1$:

$$\frac{\partial \ln m_h^2}{\partial \ln m_{\tilde{t}}^2} \approx \frac{3}{8\pi^2}\ln\frac{10^{16}}{m_{\tilde{t}}} \approx \frac{3 \times 37}{80} \approx 1.4$$

For $m_{\tilde{t}} = 1$ TeV, this gives moderate tuning. But this is just one contribution! The $\mu$ parameter also contributes:

$$\Delta_\mu = \frac{\mu^2}{m_h^2}$$

For $\mu \sim 1$ TeV:

$$\Delta_\mu \sim \frac{(1000)^2}{(125)^2} \sim 60$$

The total fine-tuning is roughly the maximum of all $\Delta_i$. Current LHC limits push $\Delta_{\text{total}} \gtrsim 50$-$100$, concerning but not yet excluding SUSY.

**Naturalness target**: To maintain $\Delta < 10$ (10% tuning), need $m_{\tilde{t}} < 700$ GeV and $\mu < 400$ GeV—increasingly difficult given LHC constraints.

**Answer:** Fine-tuning $\Delta_{\tilde{t}} \sim m_{\tilde{t}}^2/m_h^2 \times \ln(\Lambda/m_{\tilde{t}})/(8\pi^2)$; for $m_{\tilde{t}} > 1$ TeV, $\Delta \gtrsim 50$ (little hierarchy problem).

Beyond minimal SUSY, variations attempt to improve naturalness or explain Higgs mass. The Next-to-MSSM (NMSSM) adds a singlet superfield, generating effective $\mu$ term dynamically. Split SUSY allows heavy scalars ($\sim 10^{10}$ GeV) but light gauginos, preserving gauge coupling unification and dark matter while abandoning hierarchy naturalness. R-parity violation allows lepton-number-violating couplings, removing stable LSP (no dark matter) but avoiding cosmological issues. Gauge mediation transmits SUSY breaking through messenger fields charged under SM, predicting flavor-blind soft terms and characteristic decay chains through light gravitino.

The future of SUSY depends on LHC discoveries and precision measurements. The High-Luminosity LHC with 3000 fb$^{-1}$ will push sensitivity to $m_{\tilde{g}} \sim 3$ TeV and electroweak production to $m_{\tilde{\chi}} \sim 1$ TeV. Future 100 TeV colliders could access $m_{\tilde{g}} \sim 15$ TeV. Indirect probes include flavor physics (e.g., $B_s \to \mu\mu$ sensitive to SUSY loops), electric dipole moments (probing CP-violating SUSY phases), and dark matter direct detection (neutralino-nucleon scattering). Despite null results so far, SUSY remains the most developed solution to the hierarchy problem and a cornerstone of BSM physics, with its ultimate fate to be decided by upcoming experiments.

### Example 6: Gauge Coupling Unification in MSSM

Show that MSSM gauge couplings unify while SM couplings do not.

**Solution:**

The one-loop running of gauge couplings:

$$\frac{d\alpha_i^{-1}}{d\ln\mu} = -\frac{b_i}{2\pi}$$

where $b_i$ are beta function coefficients. For SM:

$$b_1^{\text{SM}} = \frac{41}{10}, \quad b_2^{\text{SM}} = -\frac{19}{6}, \quad b_3^{\text{SM}} = -7$$

(using GUT normalization $\alpha_1 = (5/3)\alpha_Y$). The couplings evolve:

$$\alpha_i^{-1}(\mu) = \alpha_i^{-1}(m_Z) - \frac{b_i}{2\pi}\ln\frac{\mu}{m_Z}$$

At $m_Z$: $\alpha_1^{-1} \approx 59$, $\alpha_2^{-1} \approx 30$, $\alpha_3^{-1} \approx 9$.

Evolving to high energy, the SM couplings **almost** meet around $10^{15}$ GeV but miss by $\sim 15\%$—not true unification.

For MSSM with SUSY scale $m_{\text{SUSY}} \sim 1$ TeV:

$$b_1^{\text{MSSM}} = \frac{33}{5}, \quad b_2^{\text{MSSM}} = 1, \quad b_3^{\text{MSSM}} = -3$$

The changes from additional superpartners modify running. Evolving from $m_Z$ to $m_{\text{SUSY}}$ with SM beta functions, then above $m_{\text{SUSY}}$ with MSSM beta functions:

The three couplings **meet precisely** at $M_{\text{GUT}} \approx 2 \times 10^{16}$ GeV with unified coupling $\alpha_{\text{GUT}} \approx 1/24$.

Numerically:
- SM: couplings differ by $\sim 15\%$ at $10^{16}$ GeV
- MSSM: couplings unify to $< 1\%$ accuracy

This is **striking evidence** for both SUSY and grand unification! The MSSM correctly predicts unification while SM fails.

**Implications**: If SUSY exists around TeV scale, it strongly suggests GUT physics at $10^{16}$ GeV. The precise unification is not a coincidence but points to a unified gauge group (SU(5), SO(10), etc.) at high energy.

**Answer:** SM couplings nearly meet but miss by $\sim 15\%$; MSSM couplings unify precisely at $M_{\text{GUT}} \sim 2 \times 10^{16}$ GeV with $\alpha_{\text{GUT}} \sim 1/24$.

### Example 7: Neutralino Mass Matrix and Dark Matter

Write down the neutralino mass matrix in MSSM and discuss LSP composition.

**Solution:**

The four neutralinos are mixtures of bino $\tilde{B}$, neutral wino $\tilde{W}^0$, and two Higgsinos $\tilde{H}_u^0$, $\tilde{H}_d^0$. In the basis $(\tilde{B}, \tilde{W}^0, \tilde{H}_u^0, \tilde{H}_d^0)$, the mass matrix:

$$M_{\tilde{\chi}^0} = \begin{pmatrix}
M_1 & 0 & -m_Z s_W c_\beta & m_Z s_W s_\beta \\
0 & M_2 & m_Z c_W c_\beta & -m_Z c_W s_\beta \\
-m_Z s_W c_\beta & m_Z c_W c_\beta & 0 & -\mu \\
m_Z s_W s_\beta & -m_Z c_W s_\beta & -\mu & 0
\end{pmatrix}$$

where $M_1$, $M_2$ are bino/wino masses, $\mu$ is Higgsino mass parameter, $s_W = \sin\theta_W$, and $s_\beta = \sin\beta$, $c_\beta = \cos\beta$.

Diagonalizing gives four mass eigenstates $\tilde{\chi}_i^0$ ($i = 1,2,3,4$) with $m_{\tilde{\chi}_1^0} < m_{\tilde{\chi}_2^0} < \cdots$. The LSP $\tilde{\chi}_1^0$ composition depends on parameters:

**Bino-like LSP**: If $M_1 < M_2, |\mu|$, then $\tilde{\chi}_1^0 \approx \tilde{B}$. Mass $m_{\tilde{\chi}_1^0} \approx M_1$. This is common in mSUGRA/CMSSM. Bino couples weakly to Z (no SU(2) charge), giving small annihilation cross section—tends to overproduce dark matter unless coannihilation with stau or stop.

**Wino-like LSP**: If $M_2 < M_1, |\mu|$, then $\tilde{\chi}_1^0 \approx \tilde{W}^0$. Couples strongly to W/Z (SU(2) triplet), giving larger annihilation. For pure wino, correct relic density at $m_{\tilde{\chi}} \approx 2.7$ TeV (too heavy for current LHC).

**Higgsino-like LSP**: If $|\mu| < M_1, M_2$, then $\tilde{\chi}_1^0$ is mixture of $\tilde{H}_u^0$, $\tilde{H}_d^0$. Strong coupling to Higgs and Z, efficient annihilation. Correct relic density at $m_{\tilde{\chi}} \approx 1$ TeV.

**Mixed LSP**: Generic case with $M_1 \sim M_2 \sim |\mu|$ gives complicated mixing. Relic density varies significantly with composition.

**Current constraints**: Direct detection (XENON1T, LUX) excludes large swaths of parameter space, especially spin-independent scattering for bino/Higgsino LSP. Indirect detection (Fermi, AMS-02) constrains annihilation cross sections. LHC electroweak production limits push Higgsino masses above $\sim 400$ GeV in simplified models.

**Answer:** Neutralino mass matrix mixes $\tilde{B}$, $\tilde{W}^0$, $\tilde{H}_{u,d}^0$; LSP composition (bino/wino/Higgsino) determines dark matter properties and detection prospects.

Supersymmetry remains a beautiful theoretical framework addressing fundamental questions about naturalness, unification, and dark matter. While the lack of discoveries at LHC has dampened initial optimism, SUSY still provides the most complete solution to the hierarchy problem and predicts a rich phenomenology worth pursuing. The next generation of experiments—HL-LHC, future colliders, improved dark matter detectors, and precision flavor measurements—will decisively test whether nature is supersymmetric at accessible energy scales.

Multiplets are mass-degenerate in exact SUSY. **Observed non-degeneracy** (no 125 GeV selectron!) requires soft SUSY breaking: mass splittings $\sim m_{\text{soft}} \sim$ TeV while preserving hierarchy solution. Coleman-Mandula: SUSY is unique non-trivial spacetime-internal symmetry extension.

**Problem 2: B** — SM: top loop gives $\delta m_h^2 \sim -y_t^2\Lambda^2/(16\pi^2) \sim -(10^{18}\text{ GeV})^2$ for $\Lambda = M_{\text{Pl}}$, requiring 30-decimal-place tuning. SUSY: stop loop gives $+y_t^2\Lambda^2/(16\pi^2)$, **exact cancellation** of quadratic divergence. Broken SUSY: $\delta m_h^2 \sim y_t^2 m_{\tilde{t}}^2\ln(\Lambda/m_{\tilde{t}})/(16\pi^2)$—logarithmic, natural if $m_{\tilde{t}} \lesssim$ few TeV. Non-renormalization theorems protect.

**Problem 3: A** — R-parity distinguishes SM ($R=+1$) from sparticles ($R=-1$). Conservation forbids: (1) Dimension-4 operators $\lambda LQd^c$ causing rapid $p \to e^+\pi^0$ (observed $\tau_p > 10^{34}$ years requires R-parity or alternative), (2) Single sparticle production, (3) LSP decay → stable neutral LSP (neutralino $\tilde{\chi}_1^0$) = dark matter. WIMP miracle: weak-scale annihilation gives $\Omega h^2 \sim 0.1$ naturally. Without R-parity: no DM candidate but allows RPV decays.

**Problem 4: B** — Superpotential $W(\Phi)$ must be **holomorphic** (analytic function) in chiral superfields $\Phi$, cannot contain $\Phi^\dagger$. SM uses $H$ and $H^\dagger \equiv H^c$ for up/down Yukawa: $y_u QH^c u^c + y_d QHd^c$. SUSY: can't use $H^\dagger$! Need two doublets: $W = y_u QH_u u^c + y_d QH_d d^c$ with $H_u$, $H_d$ both chiral superfields. Bonus: solves Higgs mass bound (tree-level $m_h < m_Z$ in 1HDM, lifted by radiative corrections in 2HDM).

**Problem 5: B** — LHC Run 2 ($\sqrt{s}=13$ TeV, $\sim 140$ fb$^{-1}$): No sparticles found. Limits (simplified models): gluinos $m_{\tilde{g}} > 2.3$ TeV, squarks $m_{\tilde{q}} > 1.8$ TeV, stops $m_{\tilde{t}} > 1.2$ TeV (model-dependent). **Fine-tuning**: $\Delta \sim m_{\tilde{t}}^2/m_h^2 \sim 60$ for $m_{\tilde{t}} = 1$ TeV ("little hierarchy"). Not excluded but increasingly unnatural. HL-LHC ($3000$ fb$^{-1}$) reaches $m_{\tilde{g}} \sim 3$ TeV. Split SUSY, compressed spectra, RPV escape limits.
