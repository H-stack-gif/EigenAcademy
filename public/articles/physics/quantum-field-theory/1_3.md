# Quantum Field Theory Topic 1.3: Quantization of Scalar Fields

Classical field theory describes fields as c-number functions $\phi(\mathbf{x}, t)$ evolving deterministically. But experiments reveal discrete quanta—particles. The photoelectric effect shows light comes in photons; particle detectors click discretely. How do we bridge this gap? **Canonical quantization** promotes classical fields to operators $\hat{\phi}(\mathbf{x}, t)$ satisfying commutation relations analogous to $[x, p] = i\hbar$. Each mode of the field becomes a quantum harmonic oscillator. Excitations of these oscillators are particles—photons for the electromagnetic field, mesons for scalar fields, and so forth. This procedure transforms field theory from a wave description to a particle picture.

The quantization of the Klein-Gordon field serves as the simplest example. We expand $\phi$ in plane wave modes, identify conjugate momenta, impose canonical commutation relations, and discover creation and annihilation operators $\hat{a}^\dagger_{\mathbf{k}}$ and $\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k}}$ that add or remove particles. The vacuum $|0\rangle$ is not empty—it has zero-point energy $\sum \frac{\hbar\omega_k}{2}$, leading to the famous Casimir effect. Multi-particle states form **Fock space**, the appropriate Hilbert space for quantum field theory where particle number is not fixed. This framework extends immediately to all bosonic fields.

This article develops scalar field quantization from classical mode expansion through canonical commutation relations to the construction of Fock space. We'll derive the commutation relations, build creation/annihilation operators, construct the Hamiltonian as a sum of harmonic oscillators, understand normal ordering, calculate the vacuum energy and Casimir effect, and see how quantum fields naturally describe variable particle number.

## Canonical Commutation Relations

In classical mechanics, canonical quantization replaces Poisson brackets with commutators: $\{q, p\}_{PB} = 1 \to [q, p] = i\hbar$. For fields, the canonical variables are $\phi(\mathbf{x}, t)$ and $\pi(\mathbf{x}, t) = \frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\dot{\phi}}$.

### Example 1: Equal-Time Commutation Relations

Derive the canonical commutation relations for the scalar field.

**Solution:**

For Klein-Gordon Lagrangian $\mathcal{L} = \frac{1}{2}\dot{\phi}^2 - \frac{1}{2}(\nabla\phi)^2 - \frac{1}{2}m^2\phi^2$:

Conjugate momentum:
$$\pi(\mathbf{x}, t) = \frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\dot{\phi}} = \dot{\phi}(\mathbf{x}, t)$$

**Canonical quantization**: Promote to operators satisfying:

$$[\hat{\phi}(\mathbf{x}, t), \hat{\pi}(\mathbf{y}, t)] = i\hbar\delta^3(\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{y})$$

$$[\hat{\phi}(\mathbf{x}, t), \hat{\phi}(\mathbf{y}, t)] = 0$$

$$[\hat{\pi}(\mathbf{x}, t), \hat{\pi}(\mathbf{y}, t)] = 0$$

These are **equal-time commutation relations** (fields at the same time $t$ but different spatial points).

The delta function $\delta^3(\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{y})$ ensures local commutation—fields commute unless at the same spatial point.

Units: $[\phi] = \text{mass}$, $[\pi] = \text{mass}^2$ in natural units ($\hbar = c = 1$).

**Answer:** Canonical commutators $[\hat{\phi}(\mathbf{x}, t), \hat{\pi}(\mathbf{y}, t)] = i\hbar\delta^3(\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{y})$, others zero.

### Example 2: Creation and Annihilation Operators

Construct creation and annihilation operators from the mode expansion.

**Solution:**

Mode expansion:
$$\hat{\phi}(\mathbf{x}, t) = \int\frac{d^3k}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\omega_k}}[\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k}}e^{i(\mathbf{k} \cdot \mathbf{x} - \omega_k t)} + \hat{a}^\dagger_{\mathbf{k}}e^{-i(\mathbf{k} \cdot \mathbf{x} - \omega_k t)}]$$

$$\hat{\pi}(\mathbf{x}, t) = \int\frac{d^3k}{(2\pi)^3}(-i)\sqrt{\frac{\omega_k}{2}}[\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k}}e^{i(\mathbf{k} \cdot \mathbf{x} - \omega_k t)} - \hat{a}^\dagger_{\mathbf{k}}e^{-i(\mathbf{k} \cdot \mathbf{x} - \omega_k t)}]$$

Inverting (Fourier transform):

$$\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k}} = \int d^3x \, e^{-i\mathbf{k} \cdot \mathbf{x}}\left[\omega_k\hat{\phi}(\mathbf{x}) + i\hat{\pi}(\mathbf{x})\right]\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\omega_k}}$$

From canonical commutators:

$$[\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k}}, \hat{a}^\dagger_{\mathbf{k}'} ] = (2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf{k} - \mathbf{k}')$$

$$[\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k}}, \hat{a}_{\mathbf{k}'}] = 0, \quad [\hat{a}^\dagger_{\mathbf{k}}, \hat{a}^\dagger_{\mathbf{k}'}] = 0$$

These are exactly the commutation relations for **harmonic oscillator operators**, one for each mode $\mathbf{k}$.

**Answer:** Creation/annihilation operators $\hat{a}^\dagger_{\mathbf{k}}, \hat{a}_{\mathbf{k}}$ satisfy $[\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k}}, \hat{a}^\dagger_{\mathbf{k}'}] = (2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf{k} - \mathbf{k}')$.

## Hamiltonian and Energy Spectrum

### Example 3: Field Hamiltonian in Second Quantization

Express the Hamiltonian in terms of creation and annihilation operators.

**Solution:**

Classical Hamiltonian density:
$$\mathcal{H} = \frac{1}{2}\pi^2 + \frac{1}{2}(\nabla\phi)^2 + \frac{1}{2}m^2\phi^2$$

Total energy:
$$H = \int d^3x \, \mathcal{H}$$

Substituting operator mode expansions and integrating:

$$\hat{H} = \int\frac{d^3k}{(2\pi)^3}\omega_k\left(\hat{a}^\dagger_{\mathbf{k}}\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k}} + \frac{1}{2}(2\pi)^3\delta^3(0)\right)$$

The $\delta^3(0)$ term is the **infinite vacuum energy**. In a finite volume $V$:

$$\hat{H} = \sum_{\mathbf{k}}\omega_k\left(\hat{a}^\dagger_{\mathbf{k}}\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k}} + \frac{1}{2}\right)$$

This is a sum of independent harmonic oscillators!

Define **number operator**: $\hat{N}_{\mathbf{k}} = \hat{a}^\dagger_{\mathbf{k}}\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k}}$ with eigenvalues $n_{\mathbf{k}} = 0, 1, 2, \ldots$

Energy eigenvalues:
$$E = \sum_{\mathbf{k}}\omega_k\left(n_{\mathbf{k}} + \frac{1}{2}\right)$$

Each excitation adds energy $\omega_k = \sqrt{k^2 + m^2}$—interpreted as a particle with momentum $\mathbf{k}$.

**Answer:** Hamiltonian $\hat{H} = \sum_{\mathbf{k}}\omega_k(\hat{N}_{\mathbf{k}} + \frac{1}{2})$ (sum of harmonic oscillators).

### Example 4: Fock Space Construction

Construct the Fock space of multi-particle states.

**Solution:**

**Vacuum state**: No particles in any mode.
$$\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k}}|0\rangle = 0 \quad \forall \mathbf{k}$$

**Single-particle states**:
$$|\mathbf{k}\rangle = \hat{a}^\dagger_{\mathbf{k}}|0\rangle$$

Energy: $E = \omega_k + E_0$ (one quantum above vacuum).

**Two-particle states**:
$$|\mathbf{k}_1, \mathbf{k}_2\rangle = \hat{a}^\dagger_{\mathbf{k}_1}\hat{a}^\dagger_{\mathbf{k}_2}|0\rangle$$

Since $[\hat{a}^\dagger_{\mathbf{k}_1}, \hat{a}^\dagger_{\mathbf{k}_2}] = 0$, particles are **bosons** (symmetric under exchange).

**General Fock state**:
$$|n_{\mathbf{k}_1}, n_{\mathbf{k}_2}, \ldots\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{\mathbf{k}_1}! n_{\mathbf{k}_2}! \cdots}}(\hat{a}^\dagger_{\mathbf{k}_1})^{n_{\mathbf{k}_1}}(\hat{a}^\dagger_{\mathbf{k}_2})^{n_{\mathbf{k}_2}}\cdots|0\rangle$$

**Fock space**: Direct sum of all sectors with fixed particle number.
$$\mathcal{F} = \bigoplus_{n=0}^\infty \mathcal{H}^{(n)}$$

where $\mathcal{H}^{(n)}$ is the $n$-particle subspace.

This automatically accommodates particle creation and annihilation.

**Answer:** Fock space $\mathcal{F} = \bigoplus_{n=0}^\infty \mathcal{H}^{(n)}$ with basis $|n_{\mathbf{k}_1}, n_{\mathbf{k}_2}, \ldots\rangle$ (variable particle number).

## Normal Ordering and Vacuum Energy

### Example 5: Normal Ordering

Define normal ordering and its purpose.

**Solution:**

**Normal ordering** (Wick ordering): Place all creation operators to the left of annihilation operators.

$$:\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k}}\hat{a}^\dagger_{\mathbf{k}}: \equiv \hat{a}^\dagger_{\mathbf{k}}\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k}}$$

For the Hamiltonian:
$$:\hat{H}: = \sum_{\mathbf{k}}\omega_k\hat{a}^\dagger_{\mathbf{k}}\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k}}$$

Difference from original:
$$\hat{H} - :\hat{H}: = \sum_{\mathbf{k}}\frac{\omega_k}{2} = E_0$$

**Purpose**:
1. Removes infinite vacuum energy
2. Sets vacuum as zero energy reference: $:\hat{H}:|0\rangle = 0$
3. Simplifies calculations (no vacuum bubbles)

Normal ordering is denoted by colons $:\cdots:$ around operators.

For any product:
$$:\hat{A}\hat{B}: = \begin{cases}
\hat{A}\hat{B} & \text{if } \hat{A} \text{ is creation} \\
\hat{B}\hat{A} & \text{if } \hat{A} \text{ is annihilation}
\end{cases}$$

**Answer:** Normal ordering $:\hat{H}:$ removes vacuum energy, places $\hat{a}^\dagger$ left of $\hat{a}$.

### Example 6: Casimir Effect

Calculate the Casimir force between parallel conducting plates.

**Solution:**

Vacuum energy in volume $V$:
$$E_0 = \frac{1}{2}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\omega_k \to \frac{V}{2}\int\frac{d^3k}{(2\pi)^3}\sqrt{k^2 + m^2}$$

This diverges! But energy differences are finite.

**Setup**: Two parallel plates at $z = 0$ and $z = a$, perfect conductors.

**Boundary conditions**: $\phi = 0$ at plates (Dirichlet) quantizes $k_z$:
$$k_z = \frac{n\pi}{a}, \quad n = 1, 2, 3, \ldots$$

Energy per unit area:
$$\frac{E(a)}{A} = \frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=1}^\infty\int\frac{dk_x dk_y}{(2\pi)^2}\sqrt{k_x^2 + k_y^2 + (n\pi/a)^2 + m^2}$$

For massless field ($m = 0$), after regularization:

$$\frac{E(a)}{A} = -\frac{\pi^2\hbar c}{720a^3}$$

(Negative: energy lower than free space!)

**Casimir force**:
$$F = -\frac{\partial E}{\partial a} = -\frac{\pi^2\hbar c}{240a^4} \cdot A$$

Force per unit area: $P = -\frac{\pi^2\hbar c}{240a^4}$ (attractive).

For $a = 1$ μm: $P \approx 0.013$ Pa—measurable and confirmed experimentally!

**Answer:** Casimir force $F/A = -\frac{\pi^2\hbar c}{240a^4}$ (attractive, from boundary-modified vacuum energy).

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: B**
Field CCR: $[\hat{\phi}(\mathbf{x}, t), \hat{\pi}(\mathbf{y}, t)] = i\hbar\delta^3(\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{y})$
Delta function: Localized in space
Equal-time commutator (same $t$)
Spatial derivatives: $[\phi(\mathbf{x}), \phi(\mathbf{y})] = 0$
Foundation for canonical quantization

**Problem 2: B**
Continuum normalization: $[\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k}}, \hat{a}^\dagger_{\mathbf{k}'}] = (2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf{k} - \mathbf{k}')$
Discrete (box): $[\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k}}, \hat{a}^\dagger_{\mathbf{k}'}] = \delta_{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{k}'}$
Bosonic commutation (scalars are bosons)
All others: $[\hat{a}, \hat{a}] = [\hat{a}^\dagger, \hat{a}^\dagger] = 0$
Harmonic oscillator algebra per mode

**Problem 3: C**
Vacuum energy: $E_0 = \sum_{\mathbf{k}}\frac{\omega_k}{2} \to \infty$
Sum over all modes diverges
Zero-point energy per oscillator: $\hbar\omega/2$
Removed by normal ordering: $:\hat{H}:$
Energy differences finite (Casimir)

**Problem 4: C**
Fock space: $\mathcal{F} = \bigoplus_{n=0}^\infty \mathcal{H}^{(n)}$
Variable particle number (0, 1, 2, ...)
$\hat{a}^\dagger$ creates, $\hat{a}$ annihilates particles
Appropriate for relativistic QFT
Vacuum: $|0\rangle$ (no particles)

**Problem 5: C**
Casimir: Observable vacuum energy effect
Boundary conditions modify zero-point energy
Attractive force $F \propto -1/a^4$
Measured experimentally (agreement!)
Purely quantum (vanishes as $\hbar \to 0$)
