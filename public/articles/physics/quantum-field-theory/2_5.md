# Quantum Field Theory Topic 2.5: Cross Sections and Decay Rates

Computing Feynman diagrams gives us the scattering amplitude $\mathcal{M}_{fi}$—but this isn't what experiments measure. Detectors count particles: how many scattered into a particular direction, what energies they carry, whether unstable particles decayed. These observables are **cross sections** (for scattering processes) and **decay rates** (for unstable particles). The cross section $\sigma$ has dimensions of area and quantifies the effective target size for a scattering event—larger $\sigma$ means more probable scattering. The decay rate $\Gamma$ has dimensions of inverse time and determines how quickly unstable particles decay via $\Gamma = \hbar/\tau$ where $\tau$ is the lifetime. Both are computed from $|\mathcal{M}|^2$ through **phase space integrals**, weighting over all possible final states.

Phase space integrals are Lorentz invariant but look different in different frames. The **center-of-mass frame** simplifies 2→2 scattering, where the differential cross section depends only on the scattering angle. **Mandelstam variables** $s$, $t$, $u$ provide Lorentz-invariant kinematic variables encoding energy and momentum transfer. The **optical theorem** connects the imaginary part of the forward scattering amplitude to the total cross section—a profound consequence of unitarity. These formulas bridge abstract QFT calculations to experimental measurements, making theoretical predictions testable.

This article develops cross sections and decay rates from their definitions through phase space integration to practical calculations. We'll understand Lorentz-invariant phase space, compute differential cross sections for 2→2 scattering, define Mandelstam variables, calculate decay rates for unstable particles, prove the optical theorem, and connect amplitudes to experimental observables.

## Lorentz-Invariant Phase Space

### Example 1: Phase Space Measure

Define Lorentz-invariant phase space (LIPS) for final states.

**Solution:**

For final state with $n$ particles, momenta $p_1, \ldots, p_n$:

**Phase space measure**:
$$d\text{LIPS}_n = (2\pi)^4\delta^4\left(\sum p_i - P\right)\prod_{i=1}^n\frac{d^3p_i}{(2\pi)^3 2E_i}$$

where:
- $P$ = total incoming momentum
- $\delta^4$ enforces overall 4-momentum conservation
- $\frac{d^3p_i}{2E_i}$ is Lorentz-invariant measure for each particle
- Factor $2\pi$ conventions vary (ours: $(2\pi)^4$ with delta function)

**Lorentz invariance**:
$$\frac{d^3p}{2E} = \frac{d^3p}{2\sqrt{|\mathbf{p}|^2 + m^2}}$$

is invariant under Lorentz boosts (proven using Jacobian).

**Two-body phase space** ($n=2$):
$$d\text{LIPS}_2 = (2\pi)^4\delta^4(p_1 + p_2 - P)\frac{d^3p_1}{(2\pi)^3 2E_1}\frac{d^3p_2}{(2\pi)^3 2E_2}$$

Using delta function: $\int d^3p_2 \delta^3(\mathbf{p}_1 + \mathbf{p}_2 - \mathbf{P})$ eliminates $\mathbf{p}_2$.

**Answer:** LIPS: $d\text{LIPS}_n = (2\pi)^4\delta^4(\sum p_i - P)\prod_i \frac{d^3p_i}{(2\pi)^3 2E_i}$ (Lorentz invariant).

### Example 2: Two-Body Phase Space in CM Frame

Evaluate two-body phase space in the center-of-mass frame.

**Solution:**

**Center-of-mass (CM) frame**: Total momentum $\mathbf{P} = 0$.

For $a + b \to 1 + 2$:

Initial: $E_a + E_b = \sqrt{s}$ (CM energy)

Final: $E_1 + E_2 = \sqrt{s}$, $\mathbf{p}_1 + \mathbf{p}_2 = 0$

Two-body phase space:
$$d\text{LIPS}_2 = \delta(E_1 + E_2 - \sqrt{s})\frac{d^3p_1}{(2\pi)^2 4E_1 E_2}$$

Switch to spherical coordinates: $d^3p_1 = |\mathbf{p}_1|^2 d|\mathbf{p}_1| d\Omega$

Energy delta function: $\delta(E_1 + E_2 - \sqrt{s}) = \frac{1}{|\partial(E_1+E_2)/\partial|\mathbf{p}_1||}\delta(|\mathbf{p}_1| - |\mathbf{p}_1^*|)$

where $|\mathbf{p}_1^*|$ is the solution to $E_1 + E_2 = \sqrt{s}$ (on-shell condition).

Result:
$$d\text{LIPS}_2 = \frac{|\mathbf{p}_f|}{16\pi^2\sqrt{s}}d\Omega$$

where $|\mathbf{p}_f| = |\mathbf{p}_1| = |\mathbf{p}_2|$ (final momentum magnitude).

**Answer:** CM frame: $d\text{LIPS}_2 = \frac{|\mathbf{p}_f|}{16\pi^2\sqrt{s}}d\Omega$ (depends only on angle).

## Differential Cross Sections

### Example 3: 2→2 Differential Cross Section

Derive the differential cross section formula for 2→2 scattering.

**Solution:**

**Definition**: Scattering rate per unit flux per unit phase space.

$$\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega} = \frac{1}{\text{flux}}\frac{1}{(2\pi)^2}|\mathcal{M}|^2 \times d\text{LIPS}_2$$

**Flux**: Relative velocity times particle densities.

In CM frame with identical masses:
$$\text{Flux} = 2E_a \times 2E_b \times v_{rel} = 4\sqrt{(p_a \cdot p_b)^2 - m_a^2 m_b^2}$$

For $m_a = m_b = m$ in CM: $\text{Flux} = 4|\mathbf{p}_i|\sqrt{s}$

Combining:
$$\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega} = \frac{1}{4|\mathbf{p}_i|\sqrt{s}} \times \frac{1}{(2\pi)^2}|\mathcal{M}|^2 \times \frac{|\mathbf{p}_f|}{16\pi^2\sqrt{s}}$$

**Simplifying**:
$$\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega} = \frac{1}{64\pi^2 s}\frac{|\mathbf{p}_f|}{|\mathbf{p}_i|}|\mathcal{M}|^2$$

For **elastic scattering** ($m_1 = m_3$, $m_2 = m_4$): $|\mathbf{p}_f| = |\mathbf{p}_i|$

$$\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega} = \frac{1}{64\pi^2 s}|\mathcal{M}|^2$$

**Answer:** Differential cross section: $\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega} = \frac{1}{64\pi^2 s}\frac{|\mathbf{p}_f|}{|\mathbf{p}_i|}|\mathcal{M}|^2$ (CM frame).

### Example 4: Mandelstam Variables

Define Mandelstam variables and their physical meaning.

**Solution:**

For $a + b \to c + d$ with momenta $p_a, p_b, p_c, p_d$:

**Mandelstam variables**:
$$s = (p_a + p_b)^2 = (p_c + p_d)^2 \quad \text{(total CM energy squared)}$$

$$t = (p_a - p_c)^2 = (p_b - p_d)^2 \quad \text{(momentum transfer squared)}$$

$$u = (p_a - p_d)^2 = (p_b - p_c)^2 \quad \text{(crossed channel)}$$

**Constraint**: $s + t + u = \sum_i m_i^2$ (sum of squared masses).

**Physical interpretation**:
- $s$-channel: Total energy in CM frame, $\sqrt{s}$ = CM energy
- $t$-channel: Momentum transfer (e.g., in scattering angle)
- $u$-channel: Crossed process (exchange $c \leftrightarrow d$)

**Lorentz invariant**: Same values in all frames.

Scattering angle in CM:
$$\cos\theta = \frac{s(s+t-\sum m^2)}{|\mathbf{p}_i||\mathbf{p}_f|}$$

(Relates $t$ to scattering angle.)

**Answer:** Mandelstam: $s = (p_a+p_b)^2$, $t = (p_a-p_c)^2$, $u = (p_a-p_d)^2$ (Lorentz-invariant kinematics).

## Decay Rates

### Example 5: Two-Body Decay Rate

Calculate the decay rate for $A \to B + C$.

**Solution:**

Unstable particle $A$ (mass $M$, momentum $P$) decays to $B$ (mass $m_B$) and $C$ (mass $m_C$).

**Decay rate**:
$$\Gamma = \frac{1}{2M}\int d\text{LIPS}_2 \, |\mathcal{M}|^2$$

where factor $1/(2M)$ normalizes per particle in rest frame.

**Rest frame** ($\mathbf{P} = 0$, $E = M$):
$$\Gamma = \frac{1}{2M}\int\frac{d^3p_B}{(2\pi)^3 2E_B}\frac{d^3p_C}{(2\pi)^3 2E_C}(2\pi)^4\delta^4(P - p_B - p_C)|\mathcal{M}|^2$$

Using delta functions and integrating:
$$\Gamma = \frac{1}{2M}\frac{|\mathbf{p}^*|}{8\pi M^2}|\mathcal{M}|^2$$

where $|\mathbf{p}^*|$ is the magnitude of $\mathbf{p}_B = -\mathbf{p}_C$ in rest frame:
$$|\mathbf{p}^*| = \frac{1}{2M}\sqrt{[M^2 - (m_B + m_C)^2][M^2 - (m_B - m_C)^2]}$$

**Lifetime**: $\tau = 1/\Gamma$ (mean decay time).

For constant $|\mathcal{M}|^2$:
$$\Gamma = \frac{|\mathbf{p}^*|}{16\pi M^2}|\mathcal{M}|^2$$

**Answer:** Decay rate: $\Gamma = \frac{|\mathbf{p}^*|}{16\pi M^2}|\mathcal{M}|^2$ (rest frame, two-body).

### Example 6: Three-Body Decay Phase Space

Describe the phase space structure for $A \to B + C + D$.

**Solution:**

**Three-body decay**: More complex phase space (two independent variables).

$$\Gamma = \frac{1}{2M}\int d\text{LIPS}_3 \, |\mathcal{M}|^2$$

$$d\text{LIPS}_3 = (2\pi)^4\delta^4(P - p_B - p_C - p_D)\prod_{i=B,C,D}\frac{d^3p_i}{(2\pi)^3 2E_i}$$

After integrating one momentum using delta function, two degrees of freedom remain.

Common choice: **Dalitz plot** variables.
- $s_{BC} = (p_B + p_C)^2$ (invariant mass of $BC$ pair)
- $s_{CD} = (p_C + p_D)^2$ (invariant mass of $CD$ pair)

Phase space boundary determined by kinematics:
$$(m_B + m_C)^2 \leq s_{BC} \leq (M - m_D)^2$$

and similar for $s_{CD}$.

**Dalitz plot**: 2D plot of $s_{BC}$ vs. $s_{CD}$, density shows dynamics.

**Answer:** Three-body: Two degrees of freedom, often plotted as Dalitz plot $(s_{BC}, s_{CD})$.

## Optical Theorem

### Example 7: Optical Theorem and Unitarity

Derive the optical theorem connecting forward amplitude to total cross section.

**Solution:**

**Unitarity** of S-matrix: $\hat{S}^\dagger\hat{S} = \mathbb{1}$

Implies:
$$\sum_n\langle i|\hat{S}^\dagger|n\rangle\langle n|\hat{S}|i\rangle = 1$$

Write $\hat{S} = \mathbb{1} + i\hat{T}$ where $\hat{T}$ contains interactions.

$$i(\hat{T} - \hat{T}^\dagger) = \hat{T}^\dagger\hat{T}$$

For forward scattering ($\mathbf{p}_f = \mathbf{p}_i$, $t = 0$):

$$2\text{Im}[\mathcal{M}(s, t=0)] = \sum_n(2\pi)^4\delta^4(P_i - P_n)|\mathcal{M}_{i \to n}|^2$$

Right side is proportional to **total cross section**:
$$\sigma_{tot} = \frac{1}{2E_a 2E_b v_{rel}}\sum_n\int d\text{LIPS}_n \, |\mathcal{M}_{i \to n}|^2$$

Result:
$$\text{Im}[\mathcal{M}(s, 0)] = \sqrt{s} \cdot \sigma_{tot}$$

**Physical meaning**:
- Forward scattering amplitude's imaginary part encodes total probability for **any** final state
- Unitarity connects elastic amplitude to all possible channels
- Experimental test: Measure $\sigma_{tot}$ and compare to $\text{Im}[\mathcal{M}]$

**Answer:** Optical theorem: $\text{Im}[\mathcal{M}(s,0)] = \sqrt{s}\sigma_{tot}$ (unitarity consequence).

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: C**
Lorentz-invariant measure: $\frac{d^3p}{2E}$
Invariant under boosts (Jacobian cancels)
$d^3p$ alone not invariant ($\gamma$ factor)
Factor 2: Convention (some use $\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3 2E}$)
Essential for LIPS definition

**Problem 2: A**
CM frame: $\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega} = f(\sqrt{s}, \theta)$
$\sqrt{s}$ = total CM energy
$\theta$ = scattering angle
Independent of individual energies (fixed by kinematics)
Simple form: $\frac{1}{64\pi^2 s}|\mathcal{M}|^2$

**Problem 3: C**
Mandelstam constraint: $s + t + u = \sum_i m_i^2$
From 4-momentum conservation
Lorentz invariant
Only two independent (third determined)
Useful for expressing kinematics

**Problem 4: B**
Decay rate: $\Gamma = 1/\tau$
$\tau$ = mean lifetime
$\Gamma$ = inverse time (decay probability per unit time)
$N(t) = N_0 e^{-\Gamma t} = N_0 e^{-t/\tau}$
Exponential decay law

**Problem 5: B**
Optical theorem: $\text{Im}[\mathcal{M}(s,0)] = \sqrt{s}\sigma_{tot}$
Forward amplitude ($t=0$) imaginary part
Total cross section (sum over all final states)
Consequence of unitarity ($\hat{S}^\dagger\hat{S} = \mathbb{1}$)
Powerful consistency check
