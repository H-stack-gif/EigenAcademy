# Quantum Field Theory Topic 7.3: Higgs Mechanism (Abelian)

When spontaneous symmetry breaking combines with local gauge invariance, something remarkable occurs: the would-be Goldstone boson disappears from the physical spectrum, instead providing the longitudinal polarization that transforms a massless gauge boson into a massive one. This **Higgs mechanism** resolves the apparent conflict between gauge symmetry (requiring massless gauge bosons) and phenomenology (demanding massive W and Z bosons in weak interactions). For the simplest case—Abelian U(1) with scalar field minimally coupled—the Lagrangian $\mathcal{L} = -\frac{1}{4}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu} + |D_\mu\phi|^2 - V(\phi)$ where $D_\mu = \partial_\mu + ieA_\mu$ and $V(\phi) = -\mu^2|\phi|^2 + \lambda|\phi|^4$ exhibits SSB with $\langle\phi\rangle = v/\sqrt{2}$, but the Goldstone mode gets "eaten" by the gauge boson, leaving one massive vector boson $m_A = ev$ and one massive scalar Higgs $m_h = \sqrt{2\lambda}v$.

The key is **unitary gauge**, choosing field parametrization $\phi = (v+h)/\sqrt{2}$ (purely real) which eliminates the Goldstone explicitly. The covariant derivative term becomes:

$$|D_\mu\phi|^2 = |(\partial_\mu + ieA_\mu)\frac{v+h}{\sqrt{2}}|^2 = \frac{1}{2}(\partial_\mu h)^2 + \frac{e^2v^2}{2}A_\mu A^\mu + e^2vhA_\mu A^\mu + \frac{e^2h^2}{2}A_\mu A^\mu$$

The term $\frac{e^2v^2}{2}A_\mu A^\mu$ is a gauge boson mass! Degree-of-freedom counting confirms consistency: before SSB, we have a massless gauge field (2 polarizations) plus complex scalar (2 degrees of freedom) = 4 total. After SSB: massive gauge field (3 polarizations) plus real Higgs (1 degree of freedom) = 4 total. The Goldstone became the gauge boson's longitudinal mode.

### Example 1: Higgs Mechanism in Unitary Gauge

Expand the Abelian Higgs Lagrangian in unitary gauge and identify mass terms.

**Solution:**

Start with:

$$\mathcal{L} = -\frac{1}{4}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu} + |D_\mu\phi|^2 - V(\phi)$$

with $\phi = (v+h)/\sqrt{2}$ (unitary gauge), $D_\mu = \partial_\mu + ieA_\mu$. The covariant derivative:

$$D_\mu\phi = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\partial_\mu h + ieA_\mu(v+h))$$

$$|D_\mu\phi|^2 = \frac{1}{2}|(\partial_\mu h + ieA_\mu(v+h))|^2 = \frac{1}{2}(\partial_\mu h)^2 + ie(\partial_\mu h)A^\mu(v+h) - \frac{e^2}{2}A_\mu A^\mu(v+h)^2$$

The cross term $(\partial_\mu h)A^\mu$ vanishes after integration by parts (gauge invariance). We get:

$$|D_\mu\phi|^2 = \frac{1}{2}(\partial_\mu h)^2 + \frac{e^2}{2}(v+h)^2 A_\mu A^\mu$$

Expanding $(v+h)^2 = v^2 + 2vh + h^2$:

$$|D_\mu\phi|^2 = \frac{1}{2}(\partial_\mu h)^2 + \frac{e^2v^2}{2}A_\mu A^\mu + e^2vhA_\mu A^\mu + \frac{e^2h^2}{2}A_\mu A^\mu$$

Mass terms:
- Gauge boson: $m_A^2 = e^2v^2$, so $m_A = ev$
- Higgs: From potential $V(\phi) = \frac{1}{2}m_h^2 h^2 + \cdots$ with $m_h^2 = 2\lambda v^2$

Interactions:
- $hAA$ coupling: $g_{hAA} = e^2v$ (trilinear)
- $hhAA$ coupling: $g_{hhAA} = e^2/2$ (quartic)

The gauge field kinetic term $-\frac{1}{4}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}$ is unchanged—no mixing with Higgs derivatives.

**Answer:** Unitary gauge: Gauge boson mass $m_A = ev$, Higgs mass $m_h = \sqrt{2\lambda}v$, couplings $hAA$, $hhAA$ present.

The gauge choice matters for calculations but not physics. In $R_\xi$ gauges (generalizing Feynman gauge), the Goldstone appears explicitly as a would-be Goldstone with mass $\xi m_A^2$, mixing with the gauge field. Physical amplitudes are gauge-independent, but intermediate steps differ. Unitary gauge is simplest conceptually (no Goldstone), but has bad high-energy behavior (non-renormalizable propagators). Renormalizable gauges keep Goldstone with gauge parameter $\xi$.

### Example 2: Gauge Boson Propagator in Unitary Gauge

Derive the massive gauge boson propagator in unitary gauge and note its high-energy behavior.

**Solution:**

In unitary gauge, the gauge boson Lagrangian is:

$$\mathcal{L}_A = -\frac{1}{4}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu} + \frac{m_A^2}{2}A_\mu A^\mu$$

The equation of motion:

$$(\Box g_{\mu\nu} - \partial_\mu\partial_\nu)A^\nu + m_A^2 A_\mu = 0$$

Fourier transform: $(-q^2 g_{\mu\nu} + q_\mu q_\nu)A^\nu + m_A^2 A_\mu = 0$. The inverse (propagator):

$$D_{\mu\nu}(q) = \frac{-i}{q^2 - m_A^2}\left[g_{\mu\nu} - \frac{q_\mu q_\nu}{m_A^2}\right]$$

The term $-q_\mu q_\nu/m_A^2$ is the **longitudinal polarization**. At high energy $|q| \gg m_A$:

$$D_{\mu\nu} \sim \frac{-i}{q^2}g_{\mu\nu} + \frac{iq_\mu q_\nu}{m_A^2}$$

The longitudinal part grows with $q^2$! This violates power-counting and makes scattering amplitudes grow with energy (bad UV behavior). In renormalizable gauges, Goldstone contributions cancel this growth.

For physical processes, the growing terms cancel between diagrams due to gauge invariance (equivalence theorem), but intermediate calculations become cumbersome.

**Answer:** Unitary gauge propagator $D_{\mu\nu} = -i[g_{\mu\nu} - q_\mu q_\nu/m_A^2]/(q^2-m_A^2)$; longitudinal part $\sim q^2$ grows at high energy.

The physical picture: the Goldstone $\chi$ provided the third degree of freedom needed for a massive spin-1 particle. In the gauge-invariant formulation, $A_\mu$ and $\chi$ mix. Diagonalizing gives one massive vector (eating Goldstone) and no physical Goldstone. The Goldstone "reappears" at high energies through the equivalence theorem: longitudinal $W/Z$ bosons behave like Goldstones at $E \gg m_W, m_Z$.

### Example 3: Degree-of-Freedom Counting

Verify DOF conservation before and after Higgs mechanism.

**Solution:**

**Before SSB**: Complex scalar $\phi$ has 2 real components, U(1) gauge field $A_\mu$ massless has 2 polarizations (transverse).

Total DOF: $2 + 2 = 4$

**After SSB** (in unitary gauge):

Real Higgs $h$: 1 DOF

Massive gauge boson $A_\mu$ with mass $m_A$: 3 polarizations (2 transverse + 1 longitudinal)

Total DOF: $1 + 3 = 4$

Same number! The Goldstone became the longitudinal $A_\mu$ polarization.

Explicitly, massive vector satisfies:

$$p^\mu A_\mu = 0 \quad \text{(Lorentz condition)}$$

but for massive particle, this leaves 3 independent components (not 2). In rest frame $p^\mu = (m_A, 0, 0, 0)$:

$$m_A A^0 = 0 \quad \Rightarrow \quad A^0 = 0$$

Spatial $A^i$ ($i = 1,2,3$) are all independent → 3 DOF.

For massless photon, $A^0$ and one spatial component are eliminated by gauge + Lorentz → 2 DOF.

**Answer:** DOF conserved: $2 (\phi) + 2 (A_\mu \text{ massless}) = 1 (h) + 3 (A_\mu \text{ massive})$; Goldstone → longitudinal mode.

The superconductor provides a condensed matter realization of the Abelian Higgs mechanism. Cooper pairs condense with $\langle\psi\rangle \neq 0$, U(1) electromagnetic symmetry spontaneously broken, photon acquires effective mass inside superconductor (Meissner effect), and magnetic fields are expelled over penetration depth $\lambda_L \sim 1/m_\gamma$. The Anderson-Higgs mechanism in superconductivity preceded its particle physics application.

### Example 4: Meissner Effect from Higgs Mechanism

Show how the Higgs mechanism produces the Meissner effect (magnetic field expulsion) in superconductors.

**Solution:**

In a superconductor, Cooper pair condensate $\psi = |\psi|e^{i\theta}$ with charge $q = 2e$ couples to electromagnetic potential $A_\mu$:

$$\mathcal{L} = |(∂_\mu - iqA_\mu)\psi|^2 - V(|\psi|^2)$$

Below critical temperature, $\langle\psi\rangle = v/\sqrt{2} \neq 0$ (SSB). In unitary gauge $\psi = (v+h)/\sqrt{2}$:

$$|(∂_\mu - iqA_\mu)\psi|^2 \supset \frac{q^2v^2}{2}A_\mu A^\mu$$

The photon acquires effective mass:

$$m_\gamma^2 = q^2v^2 = 4e^2n_s$$

where $n_s = v^2/2$ is superfluid density.

For static magnetic field $\vec{B}$, Maxwell equation in superconductor becomes:

$$\nabla^2\vec{B} = m_\gamma^2\vec{B}$$

Exponential decay solution: $\vec{B}(\vec{r}) = \vec{B}(0)e^{-r/\lambda_L}$ where penetration depth:

$$\lambda_L = \frac{1}{m_\gamma} = \frac{1}{2e\sqrt{n_s}}$$

Typically $\lambda_L \sim 50$-$500$ nm. Magnetic flux expelled from superconductor bulk (Meissner effect)—manifestation of massive photon cannot propagate long distances.

Flux quantization: Phase $\theta$ winds around vortices in multiples of $2\pi$, giving flux quanta $\Phi_0 = h/(2e)$.

**Answer:** Cooper pair condensate $\langle\psi\rangle \neq 0$ gives photon mass $m_\gamma^2 = 4e^2n_s$; penetration depth $\lambda_L = 1/m_\gamma$, Meissner effect.

The generalization to non-Abelian gauge theories (electroweak theory) follows the same logic but with richer structure: SU(2)×U(1) symmetry, Higgs doublet, three gauge bosons gaining mass (W$^\pm$, Z), one photon remaining massless. The Abelian case captures all essential physics in simpler form.

### Example 5: Scalar-Gauge Boson Scattering

Calculate tree-level amplitude for $hh \to AA$ scattering in Abelian Higgs model.

**Solution:**

Relevant vertices from $\mathcal{L} = e^2vh A_\mu A^\mu + \frac{e^2h^2}{2}A_\mu A^\mu$:

$hAA$ vertex: $-ie^2v g_{\mu\nu}$

$hhAA$ vertex: $-ie^2 g_{\mu\nu}g_{\rho\sigma}$

For $h(p_1) + h(p_2) \to A(k_1) + A(k_2)$:

**Diagrams**:

1. Four-point contact: $hhAA$ vertex directly
2. $s$-channel $h$ exchange
3. $t$-channel $h$ exchange  
4. $u$-channel $h$ exchange

Contact diagram amplitude:

$$\mathcal{M}_{\text{contact}} = -ie^2 \epsilon_\mu^*(k_1)\epsilon_\nu^*(k_2)g^{\mu\nu} = -ie^2 \epsilon_1 \cdot \epsilon_2$$

$s$-channel ($h$ exchange):

$$\mathcal{M}_s = (-ie^2v)^2 \epsilon_\mu^*(k_1)\epsilon_\nu^*(k_2) g^{\mu\nu} \frac{i}{s - m_h^2}$$

where $s = (p_1+p_2)^2$. Similarly for $t$, $u$ channels.

Total amplitude combines all contributions. At high energy $s \gg m_h^2, m_A^2$:

$$\mathcal{M} \sim -ie^2[\epsilon_1 \cdot \epsilon_2] + \frac{-ie^4v^2[\epsilon_1 \cdot \epsilon_2]}{s} + \cdots$$

The contact term dominates, growing with $e^2$ but not with energy (good UV behavior).

For longitudinal polarizations ($\epsilon_L \sim k/m_A$), the equivalence theorem relates this to Goldstone scattering at high energy.

**Answer:** Tree-level $hh \to AA$ has contact $hhAA$ vertex plus $s,t,u$-channel $h$ exchanges; amplitude $\sim e^2$ at high energy.

The vacuum stability question arises: is the chosen vacuum truly stable, or could it decay? For $V(\phi) = -\mu^2|\phi|^2 + \lambda|\phi|^4$, stability requires $\lambda > 0$. In the Standard Model, loop corrections to the Higgs potential from top quark can drive $\lambda < 0$ at high scales if $m_h$ is too light, making the electroweak vacuum metastable. Measurements of $m_h = 125$ GeV and $m_t = 173$ GeV put us near the boundary—the vacuum is metastable with lifetime exceeding the age of the universe.

The **equivalence theorem** provides deep insight into the Higgs mechanism's high-energy behavior. At energies $E \gg m_A$, longitudinally polarized gauge bosons behave identically to the Goldstone bosons they absorbed. Scattering amplitudes involving longitudinal $A_L$ equal those with Goldstones $\chi$ up to corrections $\mathcal{O}(m_A/E)$:

$$\mathcal{M}(A_L A_L \to A_L A_L) = \mathcal{M}(\chi\chi \to \chi\chi) + \mathcal{O}(m_A^2/E^2)$$

This simplifies high-energy calculations enormously—instead of dealing with complicated gauge boson polarization sums, we compute with scalar Goldstones. For the Standard Model, $W_L W_L \to W_L W_L$ scattering at TeV energies equals Goldstone scattering, revealing why the Higgs was necessary: without it, this amplitude would violate unitarity around $\sqrt{s} \sim 1$ TeV.

The gauge choice freedom reflects an important principle: physics cannot depend on how we parametrize fields. Different gauges give different intermediate expressions but identical S-matrix elements. **Unitary gauge** ($\phi = (v+h)/\sqrt{2}$, no Goldstone) is physically transparent but non-renormalizable. **Feynman-'t Hooft gauge** ($R_\xi$ with $\xi = 1$) includes Goldstone with mass $m_\chi = m_A$, yielding nicer propagators. **Landau gauge** ($\xi = 0$) has $m_\chi = 0$ but mixed propagators. Modern calculations often use **background field gauge** preserving gauge invariance manifest.

The renormalization structure differs from global SSB. In Abelian Higgs, the gauge coupling $e$ and scalar coupling $\lambda$ both run with scale through renormalization group equations. Loop corrections generate anomalous dimensions for fields:

$$\gamma_\phi = \frac{1}{16\pi^2}\left[\frac{3e^2}{2}\right], \quad \gamma_A = -\frac{1}{16\pi^2}\left[\frac{e^2}{6}\right]$$

modifying the naive scaling dimensions. The beta function for $e$ receives contributions from both gauge and scalar loops:

$$\beta(e) = \frac{e^3}{12\pi^2}$$

similar to QED but modified by Higgs loops. For the Standard Model, these RG equations connect low-energy observables (masses, couplings) to high-energy parameters, enabling precision tests through radiative corrections.

### Example 6: Vacuum Stability Condition

Determine the condition for vacuum stability and discuss loop corrections.

**Solution:**

The tree-level potential:

$$V(\phi) = -\mu^2|\phi|^2 + \lambda|\phi|^4$$

For $\mu^2 > 0$, the origin is unstable, vacuum at $|\phi|^2 = v^2/2$. But what if $\lambda < 0$ at some scale? Then $V \to -\infty$ as $|\phi| \to \infty$—unbounded below!

**Stability condition**: $\lambda(\mu) > 0$ for all scales $\mu$ up to cutoff.

Loop corrections (renormalization group running):

$$\mu\frac{d\lambda}{d\mu} = \beta_\lambda = \frac{1}{16\pi^2}[12\lambda^2 - 12y_t^2\lambda + 6y_t^4 + \cdots]$$

where $y_t$ is top Yukawa coupling. The $-12y_t^2\lambda$ term is negative (top loop), driving $\lambda$ smaller at high energy.

For SM with $m_h = 125$ GeV, $m_t = 173$ GeV:

At $\mu = m_t$: $\lambda(m_t) \approx 0.13$ (positive)

At $\mu \sim 10^{10}$ GeV: $\lambda$ goes through zero

At $\mu > 10^{10}$ GeV: $\lambda < 0$ (unstable!)

However, the barrier height is large, and tunneling rate:

$$\Gamma \sim e^{-S_{\text{bounce}}} \sim e^{-10^{600}}$$

Vacuum lifetime $\tau \sim 10^{600}$ years $\gg$ age of universe $\sim 10^{10}$ years. We're safe, but just barely (**metastability**).

**Answer:** Stability requires $\lambda > 0$ at all scales; SM Higgs with $m_h = 125$ GeV gives metastable vacuum (lifetime $\gg t_{\text{universe}}$).

The Higgs mechanism's discovery at the LHC in 2012 confirmed this mechanism operates in nature. The measured properties—spin 0, couplings proportional to mass, decay branching ratios—all match Standard Model predictions based on the Abelian Higgs structure (generalized to SU(2)×U(1)). The Higgs boson is the first fundamental scalar field observed, opening questions about naturalness, hierarchy, and beyond-Standard-Model physics.

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: B** — Higgs mechanism: Goldstone boson "eaten" by gauge field, becoming longitudinal polarization. DOF: complex scalar (2) + massless gauge (2) = real Higgs (1) + massive gauge (3). In unitary gauge $\phi = (v+h)/\sqrt{2}$, Goldstone absent; in $R_\xi$ gauge, appears as unphysical mode.

**Problem 2: C** — From $|D_\mu\phi|^2 \supset (e^2v^2/2)A_\mu A^\mu$ with $\phi = v/\sqrt{2}$, mass term $m_A^2 = e^2v^2$, giving $m_A = ev$. Proportional to both coupling and VEV. In SM: $m_W = gv/2$, $m_Z = \sqrt{g^2+g'^2}v/2$ with $v = 246$ GeV.

**Problem 3: C** — DOF strictly conserved: Before SSB, complex scalar has 2 DOF, massless gauge 2 transverse polarizations → total 4. After SSB, real Higgs 1 DOF, massive gauge 3 polarizations (2 transverse + 1 longitudinal) → total 4. Goldstone becomes longitudinal mode.

**Problem 4: B** — Meissner effect: Cooper pair condensate $\langle\psi\rangle \neq 0$ breaks U(1)$_{\text{EM}}$, photon gets mass $m_\gamma = 2e\sqrt{n_s}$, magnetic field exponentially decays over penetration depth $\lambda_L = 1/m_\gamma \sim 100$ nm. First realization of Higgs mechanism (Anderson 1963), before particle physics application.

**Problem 5: C** — Unitary gauge propagator $D_{\mu\nu} = -i[g_{\mu\nu} - q_\mu q_\nu/m_A^2]/(q^2-m_A^2)$ has longitudinal term $\propto q^2$ at high energy (bad UV). Non-renormalizable propagator structure, but physical S-matrix gauge-independent. Renormalizable $R_\xi$ gauges avoid this, include Goldstone explicitly.
