# Quantum Field Theory Topic 4.1: Path Integral for Scalar Fields

The canonical quantization approach—promoting fields to operators, imposing commutation relations, constructing Fock space—provides the foundation for understanding quantum field theory. But there exists a complementary formulation that often proves more powerful: the **path integral**. Feynman's path integral expresses quantum amplitudes as sums over all possible field configurations, weighted by $e^{iS[\phi]}$ where $S[\phi]$ is the classical action. This formulation makes certain symmetries manifest, simplifies calculations involving loops and non-perturbative effects, and provides the natural framework for lattice field theory and non-Abelian gauge theories. Unlike canonical quantization which tracks operator time evolution, path integrals compute transition amplitudes directly by integrating over all possible field histories connecting initial and final states.

The **partition function** $Z[J] = \int \mathcal{D}\phi \, e^{i\int d^4x[\mathcal{L}(\phi) + J\phi]}$ is the fundamental object, generating all correlation functions through functional derivatives with respect to the source $J(x)$. For free fields, the path integral reduces to a **Gaussian integral**—exactly solvable, yielding the Feynman propagator. For interacting theories, the interaction Lagrangian $\mathcal{L}_{int}$ becomes a differential operator $\mathcal{L}_{int}(-i\delta/\delta J)$ acting on the free partition function, reproducing Wick's theorem and Feynman rules automatically. This elegant formulation reveals why the path integral isn't just an alternative method—it's often the most natural language for quantum field theory, particularly when dealing with gauge theories, instantons, and the connection to statistical mechanics.

This article develops the path integral formulation for scalar fields from its definition through Gaussian integration to generating functionals. We'll define the functional integral measure, derive the free field partition function, compute the propagator from path integrals, introduce source terms and generating functionals, derive correlation functions via functional derivatives, reproduce Feynman rules, understand the interaction picture in path integral language, and see how this formulation unifies quantum mechanics and statistical mechanics.

## Functional Integration and Partition Function

The path integral extends quantum mechanical path integration to field theory, summing over all possible field configurations.

### Example 1: Path Integral Definition

Define the path integral for a scalar field.

**Solution:**

**Quantum mechanical path integral**: For particle position $q(t)$:
$$\langle q_f, t_f|q_i, t_i\rangle = \int_{q(t_i)=q_i}^{q(t_f)=q_f} \mathcal{D}q(t) \, e^{iS[q]/\hbar}$$

where $S[q] = \int_{t_i}^{t_f} L(\dot{q}, q) dt$ and integration is over all paths.

**Field theory generalization**: Replace $q(t) \to \phi(x)$ (field configuration).

**Path integral**:
$$Z = \int \mathcal{D}\phi(x) \, e^{iS[\phi]/\hbar}$$

where $S[\phi] = \int d^4x \, \mathcal{L}(\phi, \partial_\mu\phi)$ (spacetime integral).

**Functional measure**: $\mathcal{D}\phi = \prod_{x \in \text{spacetime}} d\phi(x)$

Formally infinite-dimensional integral over all field configurations $\phi: \mathbb{R}^4 \to \mathbb{R}$.

**Weight factor**: $e^{iS[\phi]}$ where $S[\phi]$ is classical action.

**Interpretation**: Quantum amplitude = coherent sum over all classical and non-classical field histories, each weighted by phase determined by its action.

Natural units ($\hbar = 1$): $Z = \int \mathcal{D}\phi \, e^{iS[\phi]}$

**Answer:** Path integral $Z = \int \mathcal{D}\phi \, e^{iS[\phi]}$ sums over all field configurations weighted by $e^{iS}$.

### Example 2: Free Scalar Field Path Integral

Evaluate the path integral for a free Klein-Gordon field.

**Solution:**

**Free action**:
$$S_0[\phi] = \int d^4x \left[\frac{1}{2}\partial_\mu\phi\partial^\mu\phi - \frac{1}{2}m^2\phi^2\right]$$

$$= -\frac{1}{2}\int d^4x \, \phi(x)(\Box + m^2)\phi(x)$$

after integration by parts.

**Fourier transform**: $\phi(x) = \int \frac{d^4p}{(2\pi)^4}\tilde{\phi}(p)e^{-ip \cdot x}$

$$S_0 = -\frac{1}{2}\int\frac{d^4p}{(2\pi)^4}\tilde{\phi}(p)(p^2 - m^2)\tilde{\phi}(-p)$$

**Gaussian integral**: Path integral becomes product of Gaussian integrals in momentum space.

$$Z_0 = \int \mathcal{D}\phi \, e^{iS_0[\phi]} = \prod_p \int d\tilde{\phi}(p) \, e^{-\frac{i}{2}\tilde{\phi}(p)(p^2-m^2)\tilde{\phi}(-p)}$$

$$= \prod_p \sqrt{\frac{2\pi i}{p^2 - m^2}} = N \cdot \text{Det}^{-1/2}(-\Box - m^2 + i\epsilon)$$

where $N$ is infinite normalization constant (canceled in observables).

**Functional determinant**: Result expressed as determinant of differential operator.

For interacting theories, $Z = Z_0 \times (\text{perturbative expansion})$.

**Answer:** Free field: $Z_0 = \int \mathcal{D}\phi \, e^{iS_0} \propto \text{Det}^{-1/2}(-\Box-m^2)$ (Gaussian integral).

## Source Terms and Generating Functionals

Introducing source terms allows systematic generation of all correlation functions through functional derivatives.

### Example 3: Generating Functional

Define the generating functional with source term.

**Solution:**

Add **source term** $J(x)$ to action:
$$S[\phi, J] = S[\phi] + \int d^4x \, J(x)\phi(x)$$

**Generating functional**:
$$Z[J] = \int \mathcal{D}\phi \, e^{i\int d^4x[\mathcal{L}(\phi) + J(x)\phi(x)]}$$

**Physical meaning**: $J(x)$ is external source creating particles at spacetime point $x$.

**Free theory**:
$$Z_0[J] = \int \mathcal{D}\phi \, e^{i\int d^4x[\frac{1}{2}\partial_\mu\phi\partial^\mu\phi - \frac{1}{2}m^2\phi^2 + J\phi]}$$

Complete the square:
$$= Z_0[0] \cdot e^{-\frac{i}{2}\int d^4x d^4y \, J(x)D_F(x-y)J(y)}$$

where $D_F(x-y)$ is the Feynman propagator satisfying $(\Box_x + m^2)D_F(x-y) = -\delta^4(x-y)$.

**Momentum space**:
$$Z_0[J] = Z_0[0] \cdot e^{-\frac{i}{2}\int\frac{d^4p}{(2\pi)^4}\tilde{J}(p)\frac{i}{p^2-m^2+i\epsilon}\tilde{J}(-p)}$$

**Answer:** Generating functional $Z[J] = \int \mathcal{D}\phi \, e^{i\int[\mathcal{L}+J\phi]}$ generates correlation functions via derivatives.

### Example 4: Correlation Functions from Functional Derivatives

Derive correlation functions using functional derivatives.

**Solution:**

**Two-point function**:
$$\langle 0|T[\phi(x)\phi(y)]|0\rangle = \frac{1}{Z[0]}\frac{1}{i}\frac{\delta}{i\delta J(x)}\frac{1}{i}\frac{\delta}{i\delta J(y)}Z[J]\bigg|_{J=0}$$

$$= \frac{1}{i}\frac{\delta^2 Z[J]}{\delta J(x)\delta J(y)}\bigg|_{J=0}$$

For free theory:
$$= D_F(x - y) = \langle 0|T[\phi(x)\phi(y)]|0\rangle$$

Recovers Feynman propagator!

**n-point function**:
$$\langle 0|T[\phi(x_1)\cdots\phi(x_n)]|0\rangle = \frac{1}{i^n}\frac{\delta^n Z[J]}{\delta J(x_1)\cdots\delta J(x_n)}\bigg|_{J=0}$$

**Generating functional** $W[J] = -i\ln Z[J]$ generates **connected** correlation functions:
$$\langle 0|T[\phi(x_1)\cdots\phi(x_n)]|0\rangle_{\text{connected}} = \frac{\delta^n W[J]}{\delta J(x_1)\cdots\delta J(x_n)}\bigg|_{J=0}$$

**Physical interpretation**: Functional derivatives with respect to source $J$ "pull out" fields from path integral, computing correlation functions systematically.

**Feynman rules**: Emerge naturally from expanding $Z[J]$ perturbatively.

**Answer:** Correlation functions: $\langle T[\phi(x_1)\cdots\phi(x_n)]\rangle = \frac{1}{i^n}\frac{\delta^n Z[J]}{\delta J(x_1)\cdots\delta J(x_n)}\big|_{J=0}$ (functional derivatives).

## Interacting Theories and Feynman Rules

For interacting theories, the interaction Lagrangian becomes a functional differential operator acting on the free generating functional.

### Example 5: Interacting Theory Path Integral

Express interacting theory path integrals using functional derivatives.

**Solution:**

**Interacting action**: $S[\phi] = S_0[\phi] + S_{int}[\phi]$

For $\phi^4$ theory: $S_{int} = -\int d^4x \frac{\lambda}{4!}\phi^4(x)$

**Generating functional**:
$$Z[J] = \int \mathcal{D}\phi \, e^{i[S_0[\phi] + S_{int}[\phi] + \int J\phi]}$$

**Key trick**: Rewrite $\phi^4$ in terms of functional derivatives.

$$\phi(x)^4 \to \left(\frac{1}{i}\frac{\delta}{\delta J(x)}\right)^4$$

acting on $e^{i\int J\phi}$.

**Result**:
$$Z[J] = \exp\left[i\int d^4x \, \mathcal{L}_{int}\left(\frac{1}{i}\frac{\delta}{\delta J(x)}\right)\right] Z_0[J]$$

For $\phi^4$:
$$Z[J] = \exp\left[-\frac{i\lambda}{4!}\int d^4x \left(\frac{\delta}{\delta J(x)}\right)^4\right] Z_0[J]$$

**Perturbative expansion**: Expand exponential in powers of $\lambda$.
$$Z[J] = \left[1 - \frac{i\lambda}{4!}\int d^4x \left(\frac{\delta}{\delta J(x)}\right)^4 + \cdots\right]Z_0[J]$$

Each term generates Feynman diagrams:
- $Z_0[J]$: Free propagators
- $\frac{\delta}{\delta J}$: External source/particle
- Integration: Spacetime vertex

**Wick's theorem**: Automatically reproduced by evaluating functional derivatives.

**Answer:** Interacting: $Z[J] = e^{i\int \mathcal{L}_{int}(\frac{1}{i}\frac{\delta}{\delta J})}Z_0[J]$ (interaction as differential operator).

### Example 6: Feynman Rules from Path Integrals

Derive Feynman rules from the path integral formulation.

**Solution:**

Consider $Z[J]$ for $\phi^4$ theory to order $\lambda$.

**First order**:
$$Z^{(1)}[J] = -\frac{i\lambda}{4!}\int d^4x \left(\frac{\delta}{\delta J(x)}\right)^4 Z_0[J]$$

$$= -\frac{i\lambda}{4!}\int d^4x \left(\frac{\delta}{\delta J(x)}\right)^4 \exp\left[-\frac{i}{2}\int d^4y d^4z \, J(y)D_F(y-z)J(z)\right]$$

Each $\frac{\delta}{\delta J}$ brings down one propagator.

Four derivatives at same point $x$ → **four-point vertex** at $x$.

**Correlation function** (four external points):
$$\langle T[\phi(x_1)\phi(x_2)\phi(x_3)\phi(x_4)]\rangle = \frac{\delta^4 Z[J]}{\delta J(x_1)\cdots\delta J(x_4)}\bigg|_{J=0}$$

Contributes:
1. Disconnected diagrams (products of propagators)
2. Connected diagram: Four propagators meeting at vertex

**Momentum space**: Fourier transform gives:
- Propagators: $\frac{i}{p^2 - m^2 + i\epsilon}$
- Vertex: $-i\lambda$ with momentum conservation $\delta^4(\sum p_i)$

**Feynman rules** emerge automatically—no need for canonical quantization!

**Answer:** Path integral generates Feynman rules: functional derivatives → external lines, interaction terms → vertices, $Z_0$ → propagators.

### Example 7: Loop Diagrams in Path Integral

Understand loop diagrams in the path integral formulation.

**Solution:**

**Vacuum bubbles**: Diagrams with no external lines.

$$Z[0] = \int \mathcal{D}\phi \, e^{iS[\phi]}$$

**One-loop vacuum diagram** (order $\lambda^2$):

Two vertices connected by two propagators forming closed loop.

From path integral:
$$\sim \left(-\frac{i\lambda}{4!}\right)^2 \int d^4x d^4y \, D_F(x-y)D_F(y-x)$$

$$= \left(-\frac{i\lambda}{4!}\right)^2 \int\frac{d^4p}{(2\pi)^4}\left[\frac{i}{p^2-m^2+i\epsilon}\right]^2$$

**Loop integral**: Integration over internal momentum $p$ (loops correspond to unconstrained integration variables).

**Divergence**: Integral diverges as $p \to \infty$ (UV divergence).

Higher loops: More integration variables → higher-dimensional integrals, worse divergences.

**Normalization**: Vacuum bubbles factor out, cancel in observables (S-matrix elements).

$$\langle f|S|i\rangle = \frac{\langle f|Z|i\rangle}{Z[0]} = \frac{\text{connected diagrams}}{1 + \text{vacuum bubbles}}$$

**Answer:** Loops: Unconstrained momentum integrals in path integral, divergent at high momentum, cancel in physical observables.

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: B**
Path integral: Sum over all field configurations $\phi(x)$
Not just classical solutions (saddle points)
Includes quantum fluctuations around classical
Weight: $e^{iS[\phi]}$ (action determines phase)
Functional integral: $\int \mathcal{D}\phi$

**Problem 2: B**
Free fields: Action quadratic in $\phi$
Path integral: Gaussian functional integral
$$Z_0 = \int \mathcal{D}\phi \, e^{-\frac{i}{2}\int \phi(\Box+m^2)\phi}$$
Exactly solvable (functional determinant)
Gives propagator $D_F$ naturally

**Problem 3: B**
Generating functional: $Z[J] = \int \mathcal{D}\phi \, e^{i\int[\mathcal{L}+J\phi]}$
Correlation functions: $\langle T[\phi(x_1)\cdots\phi(x_n)]\rangle = \frac{\delta^n Z}{\delta J(x_1)\cdots\delta J(x_n)}\big|_{J=0}$
Systematic way to compute all correlators
Source $J(x)$ creates particles at $x$
Works for both free and interacting

**Problem 4: C**
Functional derivative: "Pulls out" field $\phi(x)$
$\frac{\delta}{\delta J(x)}Z[J] \sim \langle\phi(x)\rangle$ expectation value
Multiple derivatives: $\frac{\delta^n}{\delta J(x_1)\cdots\delta J(x_n)} \sim \langle T[\phi(x_1)\cdots\phi(x_n)]\rangle$
External lines in Feynman diagrams
Analogous to $\hat{a}^\dagger$ in operator formalism

**Problem 5: C**
Loops: Unconstrained internal momentum integrals
Each loop: $\int \frac{d^4p}{(2\pi)^4}$ over loop momentum
Not fixed by external kinematics
UV divergent (need renormalization)
Higher loops: More integrations, worse divergences
