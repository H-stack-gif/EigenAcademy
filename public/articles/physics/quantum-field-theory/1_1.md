# Quantum Field Theory Topic 1.1: Lagrangian Formalism and Noether's Theorem

Classical mechanics deals with particles described by coordinates $q_i(t)$. But fields—functions $\phi(\mathbf{x}, t)$ defined at every point in space—require a fundamentally different approach. The **Lagrangian formalism** extends from mechanics to field theory, treating spacetime coordinates on equal footing. Instead of a Lagrangian depending on discrete coordinates $L(q, \dot{q})$, we work with a **Lagrangian density** $\mathcal{L}(\phi, \partial_\mu\phi)$ integrated over spacetime. This framework isn't just mathematical elegance—it reveals deep connections between symmetries and conservation laws through **Noether's theorem**, arguably the most profound result in theoretical physics.

Noether's theorem states that every continuous symmetry of the action corresponds to a conserved current. Spacetime translation symmetry yields the energy-momentum tensor $T^{\mu\nu}$, encoding energy and momentum conservation. Internal symmetries—transformations mixing field components without touching spacetime—produce conserved charges like electric charge. This machinery is the foundation for understanding gauge theories, the Standard Model, and ultimately quantum field theory itself. Without Noether's theorem, we'd lack the conceptual tools to construct consistent interacting field theories.

This article develops the Lagrangian formalism from the action principle through the Euler-Lagrange equations to Noether's theorem. We'll derive field equations for scalars, spinors, and gauge fields, construct the energy-momentum tensor, explore internal symmetries, and see how symmetry principles dictate the form of physical theories.

## Action Principle for Fields

The action functional $S[\phi]$ is the integral of the Lagrangian density $\mathcal{L}$ over spacetime:

$$S[\phi] = \int d^4x \, \mathcal{L}(\phi, \partial_\mu\phi)$$

where $d^4x = dt \, d^3x$ and $\partial_\mu = (\partial_t, \nabla)$. The **principle of least action** states that physical field configurations extremize $S$.

### Example 1: Euler-Lagrange Equations for Fields

Derive the field equations from the action principle.

**Solution:**

Variation of the action under $\phi \to \phi + \delta\phi$:

$$\delta S = \int d^4x \left[\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\phi}\delta\phi + \frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi)}\delta(\partial_\mu\phi)\right]$$

Using $\delta(\partial_\mu\phi) = \partial_\mu(\delta\phi)$ and integrating by parts:

$$= \int d^4x \left[\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\phi} - \partial_\mu\left(\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi)}\right)\right]\delta\phi$$

For arbitrary $\delta\phi$ vanishing at boundaries, $\delta S = 0$ requires:

$$\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\phi} - \partial_\mu\left(\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi)}\right) = 0$$

This is the **Euler-Lagrange equation** for fields—the field theory analog of Newton's second law.

**Answer:** Field equations: $\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\phi} - \partial_\mu\left(\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi)}\right) = 0$ (extremizes action).

## Klein-Gordon and Dirac Lagrangians

### Example 2: Klein-Gordon Lagrangian

Construct the Lagrangian for a real scalar field and derive its equation of motion.

**Solution:**

For a real scalar field $\phi$, the simplest Lorentz-invariant Lagrangian with up to two derivatives:

$$\mathcal{L}_{KG} = \frac{1}{2}\partial_\mu\phi\partial^\mu\phi - \frac{1}{2}m^2\phi^2$$

where $\partial_\mu\phi\partial^\mu\phi = (\partial_t\phi)^2 - (\nabla\phi)^2$ (metric signature $+---$).

Applying Euler-Lagrange:
- $\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\phi} = -m^2\phi$
- $\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi)} = \partial^\mu\phi$

$$\partial_\mu\partial^\mu\phi + m^2\phi = 0$$

or using the d'Alembertian $\Box = \partial_\mu\partial^\mu$:

$$(\Box + m^2)\phi = 0$$

This is the **Klein-Gordon equation**, describing relativistic scalar particles.

**Answer:** Klein-Gordon Lagrangian $\mathcal{L} = \frac{1}{2}\partial_\mu\phi\partial^\mu\phi - \frac{1}{2}m^2\phi^2$ yields $(\Box + m^2)\phi = 0$.

### Example 3: Dirac Lagrangian

Write the Lagrangian for a spin-1/2 fermion field.

**Solution:**

For a Dirac spinor $\psi$ (four-component complex field), the Lagrangian is:

$$\mathcal{L}_D = \bar{\psi}(i\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu - m)\psi$$

where $\bar{\psi} = \psi^\dagger\gamma^0$ and $\gamma^\mu$ are the Dirac gamma matrices satisfying $\{\gamma^\mu, \gamma^\nu\} = 2g^{\mu\nu}$.

The Euler-Lagrange equation (treating $\psi$ and $\bar{\psi}$ as independent) gives:

$$(i\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu - m)\psi = 0$$

This is the **Dirac equation**, describing electrons, quarks, and all spin-1/2 particles. The form $\bar{\psi}(i\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu - m)\psi$ is required for Lorentz invariance—the Dirac matrices transform spinors under Lorentz boosts and rotations.

**Answer:** Dirac Lagrangian $\mathcal{L} = \bar{\psi}(i\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu - m)\psi$ yields $(i\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu - m)\psi = 0$.

## Noether's Theorem: Continuous Symmetries

Noether's theorem connects symmetries to conservation laws. If the action is invariant under a continuous transformation, there exists a conserved current.

### Example 4: Noether Current from Symmetry

Derive the general form of Noether's current.

**Solution:**

Consider an infinitesimal transformation:
$$x^\mu \to x'^\mu = x^\mu + \delta x^\mu, \quad \phi(x) \to \phi'(x') = \phi(x) + \delta\phi(x)$$

If the action is invariant ($\delta S = 0$), then the variation of the Lagrangian density must be a total derivative:

$$\delta\mathcal{L} = \partial_\mu K^\mu$$

The change in $\mathcal{L}$ can be written:

$$\delta\mathcal{L} = \frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\phi}\delta\phi + \frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi)}\partial_\mu(\delta\phi) + \mathcal{L}\partial_\mu(\delta x^\mu)$$

Using the Euler-Lagrange equations and rearranging:

$$\partial_\mu\left[\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi)}\delta\phi - K^\mu\right] = 0$$

Define the **Noether current**:

$$j^\mu = \frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi)}\delta\phi - K^\mu$$

This satisfies $\partial_\mu j^\mu = 0$ (continuity equation), implying the conserved charge:

$$Q = \int d^3x \, j^0$$

satisfies $dQ/dt = 0$.

**Answer:** Noether current $j^\mu = \frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi)}\delta\phi - K^\mu$ conserved for each continuous symmetry.

## Energy-Momentum Tensor

Spacetime translation symmetry $x^\mu \to x^\mu + a^\mu$ leads to the most important conserved current: the energy-momentum tensor.

### Example 5: Energy-Momentum Tensor

Derive the energy-momentum tensor from spacetime translation invariance.

**Solution:**

Under infinitesimal translation $x^\mu \to x^\mu + \epsilon^\mu$:
$$\delta\phi = -\epsilon^\mu\partial_\mu\phi$$

The Lagrangian transforms as:
$$\delta\mathcal{L} = -\epsilon^\mu\partial_\mu\mathcal{L}$$

Applying Noether's procedure with $K^\mu = \epsilon^\nu\mathcal{L}\delta^\mu_\nu$:

$$j^\mu = \frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi)}(-\epsilon^\nu\partial_\nu\phi) - \epsilon^\nu\mathcal{L}\delta^\mu_\nu$$

Since this holds for arbitrary $\epsilon^\nu$, define:

$$T^\mu_\nu = \frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi)}\partial_\nu\phi - \mathcal{L}\delta^\mu_\nu$$

This is the **canonical energy-momentum tensor**. Conservation $\partial_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$ implies:
- $T^{00}$ = energy density
- $T^{0i}$ = momentum density
- $T^{ij}$ = stress tensor

For the Klein-Gordon field:

$$T^{\mu\nu} = \partial^\mu\phi\partial^\nu\phi - g^{\mu\nu}\mathcal{L} = \partial^\mu\phi\partial^\nu\phi - g^{\mu\nu}\left(\frac{1}{2}\partial_\alpha\phi\partial^\alpha\phi - \frac{1}{2}m^2\phi^2\right)$$

**Answer:** Energy-momentum tensor $T^\mu_\nu = \frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi)}\partial_\nu\phi - \mathcal{L}\delta^\mu_\nu$, conserved: $\partial_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$.

## Internal Symmetries and Gauge Invariance

Internal symmetries transform field values without affecting spacetime coordinates. These lead to conserved charges like electric charge, baryon number, and color charge.

### Example 6: U(1) Symmetry and Charge Conservation

Derive charge conservation from U(1) global symmetry.

**Solution:**

Consider a complex scalar field $\phi$ with Lagrangian:

$$\mathcal{L} = \partial_\mu\phi^*\partial^\mu\phi - m^2|\phi|^2$$

This is invariant under the global U(1) transformation:
$$\phi \to e^{i\alpha}\phi, \quad \phi^* \to e^{-i\alpha}\phi^*$$

For infinitesimal $\alpha$: $\delta\phi = i\alpha\phi$, $\delta\phi^* = -i\alpha\phi^*$

Noether current:
$$j^\mu = \frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi)}\delta\phi + \frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi^*)}\delta\phi^*$$

$$= \partial^\mu\phi^* \cdot i\alpha\phi + \partial^\mu\phi \cdot (-i\alpha\phi^*)$$

$$j^\mu = i\alpha(\phi^*\partial^\mu\phi - \phi\partial^\mu\phi^*)$$

Dividing out $\alpha$:
$$j^\mu = i(\phi^*\partial^\mu\phi - \phi\partial^\mu\phi^*)$$

Conservation $\partial_\mu j^\mu = 0$ implies conserved charge:

$$Q = \int d^3x \, j^0 = i\int d^3x \, (\phi^*\dot{\phi} - \phi\dot{\phi}^*)$$

This is the **conserved electric charge** when coupled to electromagnetism.

**Answer:** U(1) symmetry $\phi \to e^{i\alpha}\phi$ yields conserved current $j^\mu = i(\phi^*\partial^\mu\phi - \phi\partial^\mu\phi^*)$.

### Example 7: Gauge Invariance and Minimal Coupling

Show how local gauge invariance requires introducing a gauge field.

**Solution:**

Attempt to make U(1) symmetry **local**: $\phi(x) \to e^{i\alpha(x)}\phi(x)$ with spacetime-dependent $\alpha(x)$.

Problem: The kinetic term is not invariant:
$$\partial_\mu\phi \to e^{i\alpha}(\partial_\mu\phi + i\phi\partial_\mu\alpha)$$

Solution: Introduce a gauge field $A_\mu$ transforming as:
$$A_\mu \to A_\mu - \frac{1}{e}\partial_\mu\alpha$$

Define the **covariant derivative**:
$$D_\mu\phi = (\partial_\mu + ieA_\mu)\phi$$

Now $D_\mu\phi \to e^{i\alpha}D_\mu\phi$ (transforms covariantly), so:

$$|D_\mu\phi|^2 = |(\partial_\mu + ieA_\mu)\phi|^2$$

is gauge invariant. Add kinetic term for $A_\mu$:

$$\mathcal{L} = -\frac{1}{4}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu} + |D_\mu\phi|^2 - m^2|\phi|^2$$

where $F_{\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu A_\nu - \partial_\nu A_\mu$ (field strength, gauge invariant).

This is **minimal coupling**—the gauge field $A_\mu$ (photon) must exist to maintain local gauge invariance.

**Answer:** Local U(1) symmetry requires gauge field $A_\mu$, minimal coupling $\partial_\mu \to D_\mu = \partial_\mu + ieA_\mu$.

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: C**
Euler-Lagrange for fields: $\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\phi} - \partial_\mu\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi)} = 0$
Rearranged: $\partial_\mu\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi)} = \frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\phi}$
Analog of $\frac{d}{dt}\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot{q}} = \frac{\partial L}{\partial q}$ for particles
Applies to scalar, vector, spinor fields
Foundation for all field equations

**Problem 2: B**
Noether's theorem: Continuous symmetry → conserved current
Each symmetry parameter gives one conserved charge
Translation: energy-momentum, Rotation: angular momentum
Internal symmetries: charge, isospin, color
Deep connection between symmetry and conservation

**Problem 3: B**
$T^{\mu\nu}$ conserved: $\partial_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$
From spacetime translation invariance $x^\mu \to x^\mu + a^\mu$
Four conserved quantities: $P^\mu = \int d^3x \, T^{0\mu}$
$P^0$ = energy, $P^i$ = momentum
Fundamental to special relativity

**Problem 4: C**
Complex scalar: $\mathcal{L} = \partial_\mu\phi^*\partial^\mu\phi - m^2|\phi|^2$
Global U(1): $\phi \to e^{i\alpha}\phi$
Conserved current: $j^\mu = i(\phi^*\partial^\mu\phi - \phi\partial^\mu\phi^*)$
Charge: $Q = \int d^3x \, j^0$
Foundation for electric charge conservation

**Problem 5: B**
Local gauge invariance: $\alpha = \alpha(x)$ spacetime-dependent
Kinetic term $\partial_\mu\phi$ not covariant
Requires gauge field $A_\mu$ with transformation $A_\mu \to A_\mu - \frac{1}{e}\partial_\mu\alpha$
Covariant derivative: $D_\mu = \partial_\mu + ieA_\mu$
Explains necessity of gauge bosons (photon, gluons, W/Z)
