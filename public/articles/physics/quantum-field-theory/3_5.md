# Quantum Field Theory Topic 3.5: Fock Space Applications

While most QED calculations focus on scattering amplitudes and cross sections, the **Fock space** structure of quantum field theory has profound applications beyond perturbative scattering. Fock space—the direct sum of zero-particle, one-particle, two-particle, ... subspaces—naturally describes systems where particle number varies, making it essential for understanding photon statistics, multi-particle states, and the quantum-classical transition. **Coherent states** $|\alpha\rangle$, eigenstates of the photon annihilation operator, describe classical electromagnetic fields and laser light. These minimum uncertainty states interpolate between quantum discreteness and classical continuity, with relative fluctuations vanishing in the large-photon-number limit. Beyond coherent states lie **squeezed states** with sub-vacuum uncertainty in one quadrature—crucial for gravitational wave detection and quantum metrology.

The number operator $\hat{N} = \sum_{\mathbf{k}}\hat{a}^\dagger_{\mathbf{k}}\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k}}$ counts total photon number, commuting with the free Hamiltonian but not with interactions. Multi-particle states require proper (anti)symmetrization: photons are bosons, obeying Bose-Einstein statistics, while electrons are fermions obeying Fermi-Dirac statistics with Pauli exclusion. **Thermal states** describe photons in equilibrium at temperature $T$, exhibiting Planck's blackbody distribution—the oldest quantum prediction, now understood as arising from bosonic statistics in Fock space. These applications bridge quantum field theory with statistical mechanics, quantum optics, and condensed matter physics, demonstrating that QFT describes not just scattering experiments but the fundamental quantum structure of matter and radiation.

This article explores Fock space applications from multi-particle state construction through coherent and squeezed states to thermal statistics. We'll build symmetric multi-photon states, construct and characterize coherent states, understand the classical limit, examine squeezed states and their applications, analyze the number operator and occupation statistics, explore thermal distributions, and see how quantum field theory naturally describes both particles and statistical ensembles.

## Multi-Particle States and Symmetrization

Fock space accommodates arbitrary particle numbers with proper quantum statistics encoded through (anti)symmetrization.

### Example 1: Multi-Photon Fock States

Construct and characterize multi-photon states.

**Solution:**

**Fock space basis**: $|n_1, n_2, n_3, \ldots\rangle$ where $n_i$ = photon number in mode $i$.

**Construction**:
$$|n_1, n_2, \ldots\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{n_1! n_2! \cdots}}(\hat{a}^\dagger_1)^{n_1}(\hat{a}^\dagger_2)^{n_2}\cdots|0\rangle$$

Normalization factor ensures $\langle n|n\rangle = 1$.

**Photons are bosons**: Creation operators commute $[\hat{a}^\dagger_i, \hat{a}^\dagger_j] = 0$.

**Symmetric states**: Exchange $i \leftrightarrow j$ leaves state unchanged.
$$|n_1, \ldots, n_i, \ldots, n_j, \ldots\rangle = |n_1, \ldots, n_j, \ldots, n_i, \ldots\rangle$$

**No restriction** on occupation number: $n_i = 0, 1, 2, 3, \ldots$ all allowed.

Example: Two photons in same mode.
$$|2_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\hat{a}^\dagger_{\mathbf{k},\lambda})^2|0\rangle$$

**Energy**: $E = \sum_i n_i\hbar\omega_i$ (additive).

**Momentum**: $\mathbf{P} = \sum_i n_i\hbar\mathbf{k}_i$ (additive).

**Contrast with fermions**: Electrons obey Pauli exclusion, $n_i = 0, 1$ only.

**Answer:** Multi-photon Fock states $|n_1, n_2, \ldots\rangle$, symmetric (bosons), arbitrary occupation $n_i \geq 0$.

### Example 2: Number Operator and Commutation

Analyze the photon number operator and its properties.

**Solution:**

**Total number operator**:
$$\hat{N} = \sum_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}\hat{N}_{\mathbf{k},\lambda} = \sum_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}\hat{a}^\dagger_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}$$

**Eigenvalue equation**:
$$\hat{N}|n_1, n_2, \ldots\rangle = \left(\sum_i n_i\right)|n_1, n_2, \ldots\rangle$$

Total particle number is sum over modes.

**Commutation with Hamiltonian**:

Free photons: $\hat{H}_0 = \sum \hbar\omega_k\hat{N}_{\mathbf{k}}$

$$[\hat{N}, \hat{H}_0] = 0$$

Photon number conserved in free theory.

**Interactions**: $[\hat{N}, \hat{H}_{int}] \neq 0$

Photon number not conserved (e.g., $e^+e^- \to \gamma\gamma$ changes photon number from 0 to 2).

**Application**: Intensity measurements count photons: $I \propto \langle\hat{N}\rangle$.

**Statistics**: $\Delta N = \sqrt{\langle\hat{N}^2\rangle - \langle\hat{N}\rangle^2}$ (quantum fluctuations).

**Answer:** Number operator $\hat{N} = \sum \hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a}$, eigenvalue = total photons, conserved for free fields only.

## Coherent States of Photons

Coherent states are the quantum states closest to classical electromagnetic waves, with well-defined phase and minimum uncertainty.

### Example 3: Coherent State Construction

Construct coherent states for the electromagnetic field.

**Solution:**

**Definition**: Eigenstate of annihilation operator.
$$\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}|\alpha_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}\rangle = \alpha_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}|\alpha_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}\rangle$$

where $\alpha_{\mathbf{k},\lambda} \in \mathbb{C}$ (complex eigenvalue).

**Expansion in Fock basis**:
$$|\alpha\rangle = e^{-|\alpha|^2/2}\sum_{n=0}^\infty\frac{\alpha^n}{\sqrt{n!}}|n\rangle$$

**Normalization**: $\langle\alpha|\alpha\rangle = 1$ ✓

**Mean photon number**:
$$\langle\hat{N}\rangle = \langle\alpha|\hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a}|\alpha\rangle = |\alpha|^2$$

Intensity proportional to $|\alpha|^2$.

**Photon statistics**: Poisson distribution.
$$P(n) = |\langle n|\alpha\rangle|^2 = e^{-|\alpha|^2}\frac{|\alpha|^{2n}}{n!}$$

**Variance**: $(\Delta N)^2 = \langle\hat{N}^2\rangle - \langle\hat{N}\rangle^2 = |\alpha|^2$

Standard deviation: $\Delta N = |\alpha| = \sqrt{\bar{n}}$ (characteristic of Poisson).

**Answer:** Coherent state $|\alpha\rangle = e^{-|\alpha|^2/2}\sum_n \frac{\alpha^n}{\sqrt{n!}}|n\rangle$, eigenstate of $\hat{a}$, Poisson statistics.

### Example 4: Classical Limit and Minimum Uncertainty

Show that coherent states are minimum uncertainty states and approach classical behavior.

**Solution:**

**Quadrature operators**:
$$\hat{X} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\hat{a} + \hat{a}^\dagger), \quad \hat{P} = \frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}(\hat{a}^\dagger - \hat{a})$$

with $[\hat{X}, \hat{P}] = i$.

**Expectation values** in $|\alpha\rangle$ with $\alpha = |\alpha|e^{i\phi}$:
$$\langle\hat{X}\rangle = \sqrt{2}|\alpha|\cos\phi, \quad \langle\hat{P}\rangle = \sqrt{2}|\alpha|\sin\phi$$

**Uncertainties**:
$$\Delta X = \Delta P = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$$

**Uncertainty product**:
$$\Delta X \Delta P = \frac{1}{2}$$

Saturates Heisenberg bound—**minimum uncertainty state**.

**Classical limit** ($|\alpha| \to \infty$):
$$\frac{\Delta X}{\langle\hat{X}\rangle} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2} \cdot \sqrt{2}|\alpha|} = \frac{1}{2|\alpha|} \to 0$$

Relative fluctuations vanish—classical oscillation emerges.

**Time evolution**: $|\alpha(t)\rangle = |\alpha_0 e^{-i\omega t}\rangle$ (coherent states remain coherent).

**Laser light**: Approximately coherent state with large $|\alpha|$ (well-defined phase, Poisson statistics).

**Answer:** Coherent states: Minimum uncertainty $\Delta X \Delta P = 1/2$, classical limit as $|\alpha| \to \infty$, laser $\approx$ coherent.

### Example 5: Displacement Operator

Express coherent states using the displacement operator.

**Solution:**

**Displacement operator**:
$$\hat{D}(\alpha) = e^{\alpha\hat{a}^\dagger - \alpha^*\hat{a}}$$

**Coherent state**:
$$|\alpha\rangle = \hat{D}(\alpha)|0\rangle$$

Displaces vacuum by complex amplitude $\alpha$.

**Properties**:
1. Unitary: $\hat{D}^\dagger(\alpha) = \hat{D}(-\alpha)$

2. Composition: $\hat{D}(\alpha)\hat{D}(\beta) = e^{i\text{Im}(\alpha\beta^*)}\hat{D}(\alpha + \beta)$

3. Displacement of operators:
$$\hat{D}^\dagger(\alpha)\hat{a}\hat{D}(\alpha) = \hat{a} + \alpha$$

**Overcomplete basis**: Coherent states satisfy resolution of identity.
$$\frac{1}{\pi}\int d^2\alpha \, |\alpha\rangle\langle\alpha| = \mathbb{1}$$

where $d^2\alpha = d(\text{Re}\alpha)d(\text{Im}\alpha)$.

Non-orthogonal: $\langle\beta|\alpha\rangle = e^{-|\alpha-\beta|^2/2}$ (overlap decays exponentially).

**Answer:** Displacement operator $\hat{D}(\alpha) = e^{\alpha\hat{a}^\dagger - \alpha^*\hat{a}}$ generates coherent states: $|\alpha\rangle = \hat{D}(\alpha)|0\rangle$.

## Squeezed States and Applications

### Example 6: Squeezed States

Define squeezed states and their uncertainty properties.

**Solution:**

**Squeezing operator**:
$$\hat{S}(r) = e^{\frac{r}{2}(\hat{a}^2 - (\hat{a}^\dagger)^2)}$$

where $r$ is the squeezing parameter (real).

**Squeezed vacuum**:
$$|0, r\rangle = \hat{S}(r)|0\rangle$$

**Uncertainties**:
$$\Delta X = e^{-r}/\sqrt{2}, \quad \Delta P = e^{r}/\sqrt{2}$$

Still minimum uncertainty: $\Delta X \Delta P = 1/2$ ✓

But: $\Delta X < 1/\sqrt{2}$ (below vacuum!) with $\Delta P > 1/\sqrt{2}$ (increased).

**Displaced squeezed state**:
$$|\alpha, r\rangle = \hat{D}(\alpha)\hat{S}(r)|0\rangle$$

Most general Gaussian pure state.

**Applications**:

1. **Gravitational wave detection** (LIGO): Inject squeezed vacuum to reduce shot noise in phase quadrature, improving sensitivity.

2. **Quantum metrology**: Beat standard quantum limit in interferometry.

3. **Quantum communication**: Continuous-variable quantum key distribution.

**Production**: Parametric down-conversion in nonlinear crystals, optical parametric amplifiers.

**Answer:** Squeezed states: $\Delta X < 1/\sqrt{2}$ (sub-vacuum) with $\Delta P > 1/\sqrt{2}$, used in LIGO for noise reduction.

## Thermal States and Statistics

### Example 7: Thermal Photon Distribution

Derive the thermal distribution for photons.

**Solution:**

**Thermal state** (density matrix):
$$\hat{\rho}_{\text{th}} = \frac{1}{Z}e^{-\beta\hat{H}}$$

where $\beta = 1/(k_B T)$, $Z = \text{Tr}[e^{-\beta\hat{H}}]$ (partition function).

For photons: $\hat{H} = \sum_{\mathbf{k}}\hbar\omega_k\hat{N}_{\mathbf{k}}$ (massless, $\omega_k = c|\mathbf{k}|$).

**Occupation number**:
$$\langle\hat{N}_{\mathbf{k}}\rangle = \text{Tr}[\hat{\rho}_{\text{th}}\hat{N}_{\mathbf{k}}] = \frac{\sum_n n e^{-\beta\hbar\omega_k n}}{\sum_n e^{-\beta\hbar\omega_k n}}$$

Geometric series:
$$= \frac{1}{e^{\beta\hbar\omega_k} - 1}$$

**Bose-Einstein distribution** (no chemical potential for photons).

**Planck's law**: Energy density per frequency.
$$u(\omega, T) = \frac{\hbar\omega^3}{\pi^2 c^3}\frac{1}{e^{\hbar\omega/k_B T} - 1}$$

Blackbody radiation spectrum.

**Stefan-Boltzmann**: Total energy density.
$$u_{tot} = \int_0^\infty u(\omega)d\omega = \frac{\pi^2}{15}\frac{(k_B T)^4}{\hbar^3 c^3} \propto T^4$$

**Answer:** Thermal photons: Bose-Einstein $\langle n\rangle = \frac{1}{e^{\beta\hbar\omega} - 1}$, Planck distribution, Stefan-Boltzmann $\propto T^4$.

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: B**
Photons: Bosons with integer spin $s = 1$
Creation operators commute: $[\hat{a}^\dagger, \hat{a}^\dagger] = 0$
Symmetric multi-particle states
No restriction on occupation: $n = 0, 1, 2, 3, \ldots$
Bose-Einstein statistics at finite temperature

**Problem 2: B**
Coherent states: $|\alpha\rangle$ eigenstate of $\hat{a}$
Photon number: Poisson distribution $P(n) = e^{-|\alpha|^2}\frac{|\alpha|^{2n}}{n!}$
Mean: $\bar{n} = |\alpha|^2$, variance: $(\Delta n)^2 = |\alpha|^2$
Not definite $n$ (superposition of Fock states)
Laser light $\approx$ coherent state

**Problem 3: C**
Coherent states: Minimum uncertainty
$\Delta X = \Delta P = 1/\sqrt{2}$
Product: $\Delta X \Delta P = 1/2$ (saturates Heisenberg)
Closest quantum analog to classical
Equal uncertainty in both quadratures

**Problem 4: B**
Squeezed states: Reduce uncertainty in one quadrature
$\Delta X < 1/\sqrt{2}$ below vacuum (with $\Delta P > 1/\sqrt{2}$)
Still saturate: $\Delta X \Delta P = 1/2$ (minimum)
LIGO application: Reduce shot noise
Cannot violate Heisenberg bound

**Problem 5: B**
Thermal photons: Bose-Einstein $\langle n\rangle = \frac{1}{e^{\beta\hbar\omega} - 1}$
Massless bosons, no chemical potential
Planck distribution for blackbody radiation
High $T$: Classical limit $\langle n\rangle \approx k_B T/\hbar\omega$
Low $T$: Exponentially suppressed
