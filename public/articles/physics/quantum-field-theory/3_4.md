# Quantum Field Theory Topic 3.4: One-Loop QED Corrections

Tree-level QED calculations agree spectacularly with experiment—but quantum field theory predicts more. **Loop corrections** arise from virtual particle fluctuations: electrons temporarily emit and reabsorb photons, photons split into virtual electron-positron pairs, vertex interactions receive quantum corrections. These effects modify physical observables at order $\alpha$ and beyond, leading to measurable deviations from the Dirac equation. The most famous is the **anomalous magnetic moment** of the electron: the gyromagnetic ratio is not exactly $g = 2$ as Dirac predicted, but $g = 2(1 + \alpha/(2\pi) + \cdots) \approx 2.00232$. Schwinger's 1948 calculation of the first correction $a_e = \alpha/(2\pi)$ was a triumph of QED, and modern measurements agree with theory to better than one part in a billion—the most precisely verified prediction in all of physics.

One-loop corrections manifest in three main types of diagrams: **vertex corrections** modify the electron-photon coupling, **electron self-energy** renormalizes the electron mass and field strength, and **vacuum polarization** describes virtual pairs screening electric charge. Each loop integral is **ultraviolet divergent**—infinite as integration momentum goes to infinity—requiring renormalization (the subject of Unit 5). But physical predictions are finite and unambiguous. Form factors $F_1(q^2)$ and $F_2(q^2)$ encode momentum-dependent corrections to the vertex, with $F_2(0) = \alpha/(2\pi)$ giving the anomalous moment. Vacuum polarization makes the coupling "run"—the effective charge increases at short distances. These quantum corrections are the smoking gun of virtual particle physics.

This article develops one-loop QED corrections from diagram topology through divergence structure to physical predictions. We'll identify one-loop diagrams, compute the vertex correction to the anomalous magnetic moment, understand self-energy corrections, examine vacuum polarization and charge screening, introduce form factors and the Gordon identity, preview renormalization requirements, and see how quantum corrections achieve unprecedented agreement with experiment.

## Anomalous Magnetic Moment

The magnetic moment of a Dirac electron is $\mu = (e/2m)\mathbf{S}$ with $g$-factor exactly 2. Quantum corrections modify this.

### Example 1: One-Loop Vertex Correction Diagram

Identify the Feynman diagram for the one-loop vertex correction.

**Solution:**

**Vertex correction**: One-loop diagram modifying electron-photon vertex.

```
   e⁻ in
    |
    |----⚬~~~γ~~~⚬----
    |    |       |
    ⚬~~~~γ~~~~~~~⚬
    |
   e⁻ out
```

Electron emits virtual photon, propagates, reabsorbs photon, then interacts with external photon.

**Amplitude** (schematic):
$$\Lambda^\mu(p', p) = (-ie)^3\int\frac{d^4k}{(2\pi)^4}\gamma^\nu\frac{i(\not\!p - \not\!k + m)}{(p-k)^2 - m^2}\gamma^\mu\frac{i(\not\!p' - \not\!k + m)}{(p'-k)^2 - m^2}\gamma_\nu\frac{-i}{k^2}$$

**Loop momentum** $k$ integrated over all values.

**Divergence**: Integral diverges as $k \to \infty$ (UV divergence).

Power counting: $\int d^4k/k^2 \sim \int dk \cdot k^3/k^2 = \int k dk$ (logarithmic divergence).

**Regularization**: Introduce cutoff or use dimensional regularization.

**Physical part**: Extract finite $q^2$-dependent form factors after renormalization.

**Answer:** One-loop vertex: Triangle diagram with internal electron and photon, logarithmically divergent, needs renormalization.

### Example 2: Schwinger's Result

Calculate the anomalous magnetic moment at one-loop order.

**Solution:**

**General vertex structure**:
$$\Gamma^\mu(p', p) = \gamma^\mu F_1(q^2) + \frac{i\sigma^{\mu\nu}q_\nu}{2m}F_2(q^2) + \text{higher order}$$

where $q = p' - p$ and $\sigma^{\mu\nu} = \frac{i}{2}[\gamma^\mu, \gamma^\nu]$.

**Tree level**: $F_1 = 1$, $F_2 = 0$ (Dirac vertex $\gamma^\mu$).

**One-loop correction**:

After lengthy calculation (Schwinger 1948):
$$F_2(0) = \frac{\alpha}{2\pi} + O(\alpha^2)$$

**Magnetic moment**: $\mathbf{\mu} = g\frac{e}{2m}\mathbf{S}$ where:
$$g = 2(1 + F_2(0)) = 2\left(1 + \frac{\alpha}{2\pi} + \cdots\right)$$

**Anomalous moment**:
$$a_e = \frac{g - 2}{2} = \frac{\alpha}{2\pi} \approx \frac{1}{2\pi \cdot 137} \approx 0.00116$$

**Experimental value** (2023): $a_e^{\text{exp}} = 0.00115965218073(28)$

**Theoretical value** (5 loops): $a_e^{\text{th}} = 0.00115965218161(23)$

Agreement to **12 significant figures**—most precise test of QED!

**Answer:** Schwinger result: $a_e = \frac{\alpha}{2\pi}$ (one-loop anomalous magnetic moment), confirmed to 12 digits.

### Example 3: Gordon Identity

Derive the Gordon identity relating $\gamma^\mu$ to $\sigma^{\mu\nu}$ terms.

**Solution:**

For on-shell spinors satisfying $(\not\!p' - m)\bar{u}(p') = 0$ and $(\not\!p - m)u(p) = 0$:

**Gordon identity**:
$$\bar{u}(p')\gamma^\mu u(p) = \bar{u}(p')\left[\frac{p'^\mu + p^\mu}{2m} + \frac{i\sigma^{\mu\nu}q_\nu}{2m}\right]u(p)$$

where $q = p' - p$.

**Proof**: Start with left side, insert $\mathbb{1} = (\not\!p' + m)/2m$ from left, $\mathbb{1} = (\not\!p + m)/2m$ from right (valid on-shell).

$$\bar{u}(p')\gamma^\mu u(p) = \frac{1}{4m^2}\bar{u}(p')(\not\!p' + m)\gamma^\mu(\not\!p + m)u(p)$$

Expand using $\{\gamma^\mu, \gamma^\nu\} = 2g^{\mu\nu}$:

$$= \frac{1}{2m}\bar{u}(p')[p'^\mu + p^\mu + i\sigma^{\mu\nu}q_\nu]u(p)$$

**Application**: Shows how Dirac vertex $\gamma^\mu$ contains both electric ($F_1$) and magnetic ($F_2$) couplings when form factors included.

**Answer:** Gordon identity: $\bar{u}(p')\gamma^\mu u(p) = \bar{u}(p')[\frac{p'^\mu + p^\mu}{2m} + \frac{i\sigma^{\mu\nu}q_\nu}{2m}]u(p)$ (on-shell).

## Electron Self-Energy and Vacuum Polarization

### Example 4: Electron Self-Energy Diagram

Analyze the one-loop electron self-energy correction.

**Solution:**

**Self-energy**: One-loop correction to electron propagator.

```
e⁻ ---⚬~~~γ~~~⚬--- e⁻
      |       |
      +-------+
       (loop)
```

Electron emits virtual photon, propagates, reabsorbs photon.

**Self-energy function** $\Sigma(p)$:
$$\Sigma(p) = (-ie)^2\int\frac{d^4k}{(2\pi)^4}\gamma^\mu\frac{i(\not\!p - \not\!k + m)}{(p-k)^2 - m^2}\gamma_\mu\frac{-i}{k^2}$$

**Divergence**: Quadratically divergent (worse than vertex!).

$$\Sigma(p) \sim \int\frac{d^4k}{k^2} \sim \int k^2 dk \to \infty$$

**Decomposition**:
$$\Sigma(p) = A + B\not\!p$$

where $A, B$ are divergent constants.

**Modified propagator**:
$$S_F(p) = \frac{i}{(\not\!p - m - \Sigma(p))} \approx \frac{i}{Z\not\!p - m_{\text{phys}}}$$

where $Z = 1/(1 - B)$ (field strength renormalization) and $m_{\text{phys}} = m + A$ (physical mass).

**Renormalization**: Absorb divergences into redefinition of $m$ and wavefunction.

**Answer:** Self-energy: Loop on external electron, quadratically divergent, renormalizes mass and field strength $Z$.

### Example 5: Vacuum Polarization

Describe vacuum polarization and charge screening.

**Solution:**

**Vacuum polarization**: Virtual $e^+e^-$ pair modifies photon propagator.

```
γ ~~~⚬---e⁺e⁻ loop---⚬~~~ γ
     |               |
     +---------------+
```

Photon splits into virtual pair, recombines.

**Polarization tensor** $\Pi^{\mu\nu}(q)$:
$$\Pi^{\mu\nu}(q) = (-ie)^2\int\frac{d^4k}{(2\pi)^4}\text{Tr}\left[\gamma^\mu\frac{i(\not\!k + m)}{k^2 - m^2}\gamma^\nu\frac{i(\not\!k - \not\!q + m)}{(k-q)^2 - m^2}\right]$$

**Structure** (Ward identity): $\Pi^{\mu\nu}(q) = (q^2 g^{\mu\nu} - q^\mu q^\nu)\Pi(q^2)$

**Modified propagator**:
$$D^{\mu\nu}_F(q) = \frac{-i}{q^2[1 - \Pi(q^2)]}\left[g^{\mu\nu} - \frac{q^\mu q^\nu}{q^2}\right]$$

**Running coupling**:
$$\alpha(q^2) = \frac{\alpha}{1 - \Pi(q^2)} \approx \alpha\left[1 + \frac{\alpha}{3\pi}\ln\frac{-q^2}{m^2}\right]$$

At large $|q^2|$ (short distances), $\alpha$ increases (anti-screening).

**Physical interpretation**: Virtual pairs "screen" electric charge at short distances—probe closer to bare charge.

**Answer:** Vacuum polarization: Virtual $e^+e^-$ loop, modifies photon propagator, running coupling $\alpha(q^2)$ increases at high $q^2$.

## Form Factors and Gordon Decomposition

### Example 6: Form Factor Interpretation

Explain the physical meaning of form factors $F_1$ and $F_2$.

**Solution:**

**Full vertex**:
$$\Gamma^\mu(p', p) = \gamma^\mu F_1(q^2) + \frac{i\sigma^{\mu\nu}q_\nu}{2m}F_2(q^2)$$

**Electric form factor** $F_1(q^2)$:
- $F_1(0) = 1$ (Ward identity, charge normalization)
- $F_1(q^2) - 1$ gives size/structure corrections
- Related to charge distribution $\rho(r)$ via Fourier transform

**Magnetic form factor** $F_2(q^2)$:
- $F_2(0) = a_e$ (anomalous magnetic moment)
- $F_2(q^2)$ describes magnetic moment distribution
- Non-zero from relativistic corrections (spin-orbit)

**Low $q^2$ expansion**:
$$F_1(q^2) \approx 1 - \frac{q^2}{6}\langle r^2_e\rangle + \cdots$$

defines mean square charge radius $\langle r^2_e\rangle$ (unmeasurably small for point-like electron).

**One-loop results**:
$$F_1(0) = 1, \quad F_2(0) = \frac{\alpha}{2\pi}$$

Higher orders: $(α/π)^2$, $(α/π)^3$, ... corrections known to 5 loops.

**Answer:** $F_1$: Electric form factor (charge distribution), $F_2$: Magnetic form factor (anomalous moment $a_e = F_2(0)$).

### Example 7: Precision Tests of QED

Summarize the experimental tests of one-loop QED corrections.

**Solution:**

**Anomalous magnetic moment**:
- Electron $a_e$: Measured to $\sim 10^{-13}$ precision
- Theory (5 loops): $a_e = \frac{\alpha}{2\pi} - 0.328\left(\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\right)^2 + \cdots$
- Agreement: 12 significant figures (best test of any physical theory)

**Muon $a_\mu$**:
- Similar structure, larger corrections (hadronic contributions important)
- Current tension between theory and experiment (~4σ)
- Active area of research, possible new physics

**Lamb shift**:
- Energy splitting in hydrogen: $2S_{1/2}$ vs $2P_{1/2}$
- Vacuum polarization + self-energy contributions
- Measured: 1057 MHz, agreement with theory

**Running coupling**:
- $\alpha(m_Z) \approx 1/128$ vs $\alpha(0) = 1/137$
- Evolution measured at colliders (LEP, LHC)
- Confirms vacuum polarization predictions

**Answer:** QED tested to unprecedented precision: $a_e$ agrees to $10^{-12}$, Lamb shift to 13 figures, running $\alpha$ measured at colliders.
