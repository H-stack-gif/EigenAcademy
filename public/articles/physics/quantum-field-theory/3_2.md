# Quantum Field Theory Topic 3.2: Tree-Level QED Processes

With the QED Feynman rules established, we can compute physical processes. **Tree-level** calculations—diagrams with no loops—give the classical limit of QED, corresponding to the first Born approximation in non-relativistic quantum mechanics. These processes are exact to order $\alpha = e^2/(4\pi) \approx 1/137$ (one power of the fine structure constant) and describe the primary electromagnetic interactions observed in nature: Compton scattering of photons off electrons, pair annihilation $e^+e^- \to \gamma\gamma$, and elastic scattering between charged particles. Computing these amplitudes requires careful manipulation of spinor algebra—contracting gamma matrices, applying completeness relations, and evaluating traces—but the results have spectacular agreement with experiment.

The key processes showcase different aspects of QED. **Electron-positron annihilation** to two photons demonstrates charge conjugation symmetry and exhibits the characteristic $\sin^2\theta$ angular distribution. **Compton scattering** $\gamma e^- \to \gamma e^-$ interpolates between the Thomson limit (low energy, classical scattering) and the Klein-Nishina formula (relativistic regime where quantum effects dominate). **Bhabha scattering** $e^+e^- \to e^+e^-$ involves both s-channel (annihilation) and t-channel (scattering) contributions that interfere, while **Møller scattering** $e^-e^- \to e^-e^-$ requires careful antisymmetrization of identical fermions. Each process illuminates different physics and tests QED in distinct kinematic regimes.

This article develops tree-level QED calculations from diagram construction through spinor algebra to cross section formulas. We'll compute annihilation amplitudes, derive the Klein-Nishina formula, understand channel decomposition in Bhabha scattering, handle identical particle statistics in Møller scattering, master trace evaluation techniques, and connect theoretical predictions to experimental measurements.

## Electron-Positron Annihilation

The process $e^+(p_1) + e^-(p_2) \to \gamma(k_1) + \gamma(k_2)$ proceeds through a single tree-level diagram where electron and positron annihilate at a vertex.

### Example 1: Annihilation Amplitude Calculation

Compute the invariant amplitude for $e^+e^- \to \gamma\gamma$.

**Solution:**

**Feynman diagram**: Two vertices connected by internal fermion propagator (s-channel).

```
e⁻(p₂) ---->-----⚬~~~~~ γ(k₁)
              |
e⁺(p₁) ---->-----⚬~~~~~ γ(k₂)
        (time flows up)
```

**Amplitude**:
$$i\mathcal{M} = \bar{v}(p_1)(-ie\gamma^\mu)\frac{i(\not\!p_2 - \not\!k_1 + m)}{(p_2-k_1)^2 - m^2}(-ie\gamma^\nu)u(p_2)\epsilon_\mu^*(k_1)\epsilon_\nu^*(k_2)$$

Plus diagram with $k_1 \leftrightarrow k_2$ (photon exchange).

**Simplification**: Using $(p_2 - k_1)^2 = m^2 - 2p_2 \cdot k_1$ and on-shell conditions:

$$\mathcal{M} = -ie^2\left[\bar{v}(p_1)\gamma^\mu\frac{\not\!p_2 - \not\!k_1 + m}{-2p_2 \cdot k_1}\gamma^\nu u(p_2)\epsilon_\mu^*(k_1)\epsilon_\nu^*(k_2) + (k_1 \leftrightarrow k_2)\right]$$

**Spin sum**: Using Casimir's trick:
$$\sum_{\text{spins}}|\mathcal{M}|^2 = \frac{e^4}{(p_2 \cdot k_1)^2}\text{Tr}[(\not\!p_1 - m)\gamma^\mu\not\!\epsilon_1(\not\!p_2 + m)\not\!\epsilon_2^*\gamma^\nu] + \text{exchange} + \text{interference}$$

After trace evaluation (lengthy but systematic):
$$\frac{1}{4}\sum_{\text{spins}}|\mathcal{M}|^2 = \frac{2e^4}{s}(1 + \cos^2\theta)$$

where $s = (p_1 + p_2)^2$ and $\theta$ is the scattering angle in CM frame.

**Answer:** Annihilation amplitude involves two diagrams (photon exchange), gives angular distribution $\propto (1 + \cos^2\theta)$.

### Example 2: Annihilation Cross Section

Derive the differential cross section for $e^+e^- \to \gamma\gamma$.

**Solution:**

From previous example: $\frac{1}{4}\sum_{\text{spins}}|\mathcal{M}|^2 = \frac{2e^4}{s}(1 + \cos^2\theta)$

**Differential cross section** (CM frame):
$$\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega} = \frac{1}{64\pi^2 s}\frac{|\mathbf{p}_f|}{|\mathbf{p}_i|}|\mathcal{M}|^2$$

For massless photons: $|\mathbf{p}_f| = \sqrt{s}/2$, and $|\mathbf{p}_i| \approx \sqrt{s}/2$ (high energy).

$$\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega} = \frac{1}{64\pi^2 s} \cdot \frac{2e^4}{s}(1 + \cos^2\theta) = \frac{e^4}{32\pi^2 s^2}(1 + \cos^2\theta)$$

Using $e^2 = 4\pi\alpha$:
$$\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega} = \frac{\alpha^2}{2s}(1 + \cos^2\theta)$$

**Total cross section**: Integrate over solid angle.
$$\sigma_{tot} = \int\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega}d\Omega = \frac{\alpha^2}{2s}\int_0^\pi(1 + \cos^2\theta)2\pi\sin\theta d\theta = \frac{4\pi\alpha^2}{3s}$$

High-energy limit ($s \to \infty$): Cross section falls as $1/s$ (characteristic of point-like scattering).

**Answer:** Differential cross section $\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega} = \frac{\alpha^2}{2s}(1 + \cos^2\theta)$, total $\sigma = \frac{4\pi\alpha^2}{3s}$.

## Compton Scattering

Compton scattering $\gamma(k) + e^-(p) \to \gamma(k') + e^-(p')$ has two tree-level diagrams: s-channel (photon absorbed then emitted) and u-channel (emission then absorption).

### Example 3: Klein-Nishina Formula

Derive the Klein-Nishina formula for Compton scattering.

**Solution:**

**Two diagrams**:

1. **s-channel**: Electron absorbs photon, then emits.
```
γ(k) ~~~⚬----- e(p+k)
         |
e(p) ----|
         |
         ⚬~~~~ γ(k')
```

2. **u-channel**: Electron emits photon, then absorbs.

**Amplitude**:
$$i\mathcal{M} = -ie^2\bar{u}(p')\left[\gamma^\mu\frac{\not\!p + \not\!k + m}{(p+k)^2 - m^2}\gamma^\nu + \gamma^\nu\frac{\not\!p - \not\!k' + m}{(p-k')^2 - m^2}\gamma^\mu\right]u(p)\epsilon_\mu(k)\epsilon_\nu^*(k')$$

Using $(p+k)^2 - m^2 = 2p \cdot k$ (on-shell electron):

After extensive spinor algebra and averaging/summing over spins:

**Klein-Nishina formula**:
$$\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega} = \frac{\alpha^2}{4m^2}\left(\frac{\omega'}{\omega}\right)^2\left[\frac{\omega}{\omega'} + \frac{\omega'}{\omega} - \sin^2\theta\right]$$

where $\omega$ and $\omega'$ are initial and final photon energies, related by:
$$\omega' = \frac{\omega}{1 + \frac{\omega}{m}(1 - \cos\theta)}$$

(Energy-momentum conservation constraint.)

**Thomson limit** ($\omega \ll m$, non-relativistic): $\omega' \approx \omega$

$$\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega} \approx \frac{\alpha^2}{m^2}(1 + \cos^2\theta) = r_0^2(1 + \cos^2\theta)$$

where $r_0 = \alpha/m \approx 2.8 \times 10^{-13}$ cm (classical electron radius).

Total Thomson cross section: $\sigma_T = \frac{8\pi\alpha^2}{3m^2}$ (classical limit, independent of photon energy).

**Answer:** Klein-Nishina: $\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega} = \frac{\alpha^2}{4m^2}(\frac{\omega'}{\omega})^2[\frac{\omega}{\omega'} + \frac{\omega'}{\omega} - \sin^2\theta]$, Thomson limit $\sigma_T = \frac{8\pi\alpha^2}{3m^2}$.

## Bhabha and Møller Scattering

Elastic scattering between electrons and positrons (Bhabha) or between two electrons (Møller) involves interference between multiple channels.

### Example 4: Bhabha Scattering Channels

Analyze the channel structure of $e^+e^- \to e^+e^-$ scattering.

**Solution:**

**Bhabha scattering**: $e^+(p_1) + e^-(p_2) \to e^+(p_3) + e^-(p_4)$

**Two channels**:

1. **s-channel** (annihilation): $e^+e^- \to \gamma^* \to e^+e^-$
```
e⁻(p₂) --->---⚬
             | (γ*)
e⁺(p₁) --->---⚬
             |
       ⚬---<--- e⁺(p₃)
       |
       ⚬---<--- e⁻(p₄)
```

Propagator: $\frac{-ig^{\mu\nu}}{s}$ where $s = (p_1 + p_2)^2$

2. **t-channel** (scattering): $e^+e^- \to e^+e^-$ via photon exchange
```
e⁻(p₂) --->---⚬~~~γ~~~⚬--->--- e⁻(p₄)
                               
e⁺(p₁) --->---------------<--- e⁺(p₃)
```

Propagator: $\frac{-ig^{\mu\nu}}{t}$ where $t = (p_2 - p_4)^2$

**Amplitude**:
$$i\mathcal{M} = \mathcal{M}_s + \mathcal{M}_t$$

$$= -ie^2\left[\frac{\bar{v}(p_1)\gamma^\mu u(p_2) \cdot \bar{u}(p_4)\gamma_\mu v(p_3)}{s} + \frac{\bar{v}(p_1)\gamma^\mu v(p_3) \cdot \bar{u}(p_4)\gamma_\mu u(p_2)}{t}\right]$$

**Interference**: $|\mathcal{M}|^2 = |\mathcal{M}_s|^2 + |\mathcal{M}_t|^2 + 2\text{Re}(\mathcal{M}_s^*\mathcal{M}_t)$

The interference term is crucial—negative in some regions, enhancing cross section in others.

**Answer:** Bhabha: s-channel (annihilation) + t-channel (exchange), interference between channels matters.

### Example 5: Møller Scattering and Antisymmetrization

Compute the amplitude for $e^-e^- \to e^-e^-$ including identical particle effects.

**Solution:**

**Møller scattering**: $e^-(p_1) + e^-(p_2) \to e^-(p_3) + e^-(p_4)$

**Two diagrams** (no annihilation channel for identical fermions):

1. **Direct t-channel**: Photon exchanged between 1→3 and 2→4
2. **Exchange u-channel**: Photon exchanged between 1→4 and 2→3

```
Direct:                    Exchange:
e⁻(p₁)-⚬~~~γ~~~⚬-e⁻(p₃)   e⁻(p₁)-⚬~~~γ~~~⚬-e⁻(p₄)
       |       |                  |       |
e⁻(p₂)--------⚬-e⁻(p₄)   e⁻(p₂)--------⚬-e⁻(p₃)
```

**Antisymmetrization**: Identical fermions require antisymmetric final state.

$$\mathcal{M} = \mathcal{M}_t - \mathcal{M}_u$$

(Minus sign from Fermi statistics!)

$$= -ie^2\left[\frac{\bar{u}(p_3)\gamma^\mu u(p_1) \cdot \bar{u}(p_4)\gamma_\mu u(p_2)}{t} - \frac{\bar{u}(p_4)\gamma^\mu u(p_1) \cdot \bar{u}(p_3)\gamma_\mu u(p_2)}{u}\right]$$

where $u = (p_1 - p_4)^2$.

**Spin average**: 
$$\frac{1}{4}\sum_{\text{spins}}|\mathcal{M}|^2 = \frac{e^4}{4}\left[\frac{\text{Tr}_1 \cdot \text{Tr}_2}{t^2} + \frac{\text{Tr}_1 \cdot \text{Tr}_2}{u^2} - 2\frac{\text{Tr}_{\text{int}}}{tu}\right]$$

Angular distribution differs from Bhabha due to antisymmetrization.

**Answer:** Møller: Direct t-channel minus exchange u-channel (antisymmetrization), no s-channel (identical electrons).

## Spinor Trace Techniques

### Example 6: Trace Identities

Apply trace identities to simplify QED amplitudes.

**Solution:**

**Key trace formulas**:

1. $\text{Tr}[\mathbb{1}] = 4$ (dimension of spinor space)

2. $\text{Tr}[\gamma^\mu] = 0$ (traceless)

3. $\text{Tr}[\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu] = 4g^{\mu\nu}$

4. $\text{Tr}[\text{odd number of } \gamma] = 0$

5. $\text{Tr}[\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu\gamma^\rho\gamma^\sigma] = 4(g^{\mu\nu}g^{\rho\sigma} - g^{\mu\rho}g^{\nu\sigma} + g^{\mu\sigma}g^{\nu\rho})$

6. $\text{Tr}[\not\!a\not\!b] = 4(a \cdot b)$

7. $\text{Tr}[\not\!a\not\!b\not\!c\not\!d] = 4[(a \cdot b)(c \cdot d) - (a \cdot c)(b \cdot d) + (a \cdot d)(b \cdot c)]$

**Example application** (from annihilation):
$$\text{Tr}[(\not\!p_1 - m)\gamma^\mu(\not\!p_2 + m)\gamma^\nu]$$

$$= \text{Tr}[\not\!p_1\gamma^\mu\not\!p_2\gamma^\nu] - m^2\text{Tr}[\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu]$$

$$= 4[p_1^\mu p_2^\nu + p_1^\nu p_2^\mu - (p_1 \cdot p_2)g^{\mu\nu}] - 4m^2 g^{\mu\nu}$$

Contract with photon polarizations and sum to get cross section.

**Computational strategy**: Expand in terms of $\not\!p$, use completeness, evaluate traces, contract indices.

**Answer:** Trace identities reduce spinor chains to scalar functions of momenta, systematic evaluation possible.

### Example 7: High-Energy Limit

Analyze QED processes in the ultra-relativistic limit $E \gg m$.

**Solution:**

**Ultra-relativistic electrons**: Ignore mass $m \to 0$ in numerator (not in denominators where it regulates IR divergences).

Spinors: $u(p) \approx \sqrt{2E}\begin{pmatrix}\chi \\ \chi\end{pmatrix}$ (helicity eigenstates)

**Simplification**: Many traces simplify dramatically.

$$\text{Tr}[\not\!p_1\gamma^\mu\not\!p_2\gamma^\nu] = 4[p_1^\mu p_2^\nu + p_1^\nu p_2^\mu - (p_1 \cdot p_2)g^{\mu\nu}]$$

**Example** (annihilation at high energy):
$$\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega} \approx \frac{\alpha^2}{2s}(1 + \cos^2\theta)$$

Independent of electron mass—purely kinematic.

**Physical interpretation**: At high energies, mass effects negligible, scattering determined by helicity (spin projection along momentum).

Collider experiments ($e^+e^- \to$ anything) operate in this regime, testing QED at highest precision.

**Answer:** High-energy limit: Massless spinor approximation, helicity basis, traces simplify, universal angular distributions.

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: B**
$e^+e^- \to \gamma\gamma$: Two tree-level diagrams
Photon 1 emitted first, then photon 2: One diagram
Photon 2 emitted first, then photon 1: Second diagram
Both contribute with interference
Total amplitude: $\mathcal{M} = \mathcal{M}_1 + \mathcal{M}_2$

**Problem 2: C**
Angular distribution: $\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega} \propto 1 + \cos^2\theta$
Characteristic of spin-1/2 → spin-1 + spin-1
Forward ($\theta = 0$): $1 + 1 = 2$
Sideways ($\theta = 90°$): $1 + 0 = 1$
Backward ($\theta = 180°$): $1 + 1 = 2$

**Problem 3: B**
Thomson limit: $\omega \ll m$ (non-relativistic)
Classical regime, $\omega' \approx \omega$
Cross section: $\sigma_T = \frac{8\pi\alpha^2}{3m^2}$ (constant)
Klein-Nishina: Full relativistic formula
High energy: Quantum effects important

**Problem 4: C**
Bhabha: Both channels
s-channel: Annihilation $e^+e^- \to \gamma^* \to e^+e^-$
t-channel: Scattering via photon exchange
Interference between channels crucial
Total: $\mathcal{M} = \mathcal{M}_s + \mathcal{M}_t$

**Problem 5: B**
Møller: Identical electrons require antisymmetrization
Direct (t-channel) minus exchange (u-channel)
$\mathcal{M} = \mathcal{M}_t - \mathcal{M}_u$ (minus sign!)
Fermi statistics for identical fermions
Different from Bhabha (distinguishable particles)
