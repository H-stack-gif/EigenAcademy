# Quantum Field Theory Topic 6.5: Deep Inelastic Scattering and Partons

Deep inelastic scattering revolutionized our understanding of hadron structure by revealing the proton's quark and gluon constituents through high-energy electron-proton collisions. In DIS, electrons scatter off protons via virtual photon exchange with large momentum transfer $Q^2 = -q^2 \gg 1$ GeV$^2$, probing distances $\sim 1/Q$ down to $0.01$ fm or less. At such scales, asymptotic freedom ensures weak coupling and the proton appears not as a simple bound state but as a roiling sea of quarks, antiquarks, and gluons—**partons**—each carrying a fraction $x$ (Bjorken variable) of the proton's momentum. The **structure functions** $F_1(x,Q^2)$ and $F_2(x,Q^2)$ measure how quarks share momentum, exhibiting approximate **Bjorken scaling** $F_2(x,Q^2) \approx F_2(x)$ at fixed $x$ for $Q^2 \gg \Lambda_{\text{QCD}}^2$, confirming the nearly point-like nature of partons. QCD corrections introduce logarithmic $Q^2$ dependence through **DGLAP evolution equations** governing how parton distributions evolve with resolution scale, tested precisely at HERA, the LHC, and other facilities.

Parton distribution functions $q_i(x,Q^2)$ for quarks of flavor $i$ and $g(x,Q^2)$ for gluons encode non-perturbative information about proton structure, determined from global fits to experimental data. At $Q^2 \sim 10$ GeV$^2$, the proton contains three **valence quarks** ($uud$) plus a sea of virtual $q\bar{q}$ pairs and gluons. The $x$ distribution shows that at small $x \sim 0.01$, the sea and gluons dominate—most of the proton's constituents carry tiny momentum fractions, with only the valence quarks peaking around $x \sim 0.2$. Sum rules like the momentum sum rule $\int_0^1 dx\,x[q(x)+\bar{q}(x)+g(x)] = 1$ ensure consistency, while the Gottfried sum rule and flavor asymmetries test our understanding of sea quark distributions.

### Example 1: Bjorken Scaling and Parton Model

Derive the Bjorken scaling relation for $F_2$ in the parton model.

**Solution:**

In DIS, electron scatters via virtual photon with four-momentum $q^\mu$:

$$Q^2 = -q^2, \quad \nu = P \cdot q / M, \quad x = \frac{Q^2}{2M\nu}$$

where $P^\mu$ is proton momentum, $M$ proton mass. Bjorken variable $x \in [0,1]$ represents momentum fraction.

**Parton model**: Proton composed of point-like partons (quarks) with momentum distribution $f_i(x)$ where $f_i(x)dx$ gives probability of finding quark $i$ with momentum fraction in $[x, x+dx]$.

Virtual photon scatters elastically off parton with charge $Q_i$:

$$\sigma_{e+\text{parton}} \propto Q_i^2 \delta(x - x_{\text{Bjorken}})$$

The delta function enforces kinematics: parton carries fraction $x$ of proton momentum.

Total cross section sums over partons:

$$\frac{d^2\sigma}{dx\,dQ^2} = \sum_i Q_i^2 f_i(x) \cdot \sigma_{\text{point}}(Q^2)$$

Structure function $F_2$ relates to cross section via:

$$F_2(x,Q^2) = \sum_i Q_i^2 x f_i(x)$$

In pure parton model (no QCD corrections), $f_i(x)$ is $Q^2$-independent, giving:

$$F_2(x,Q^2) \approx F_2(x)$$

**Bjorken scaling**: $F_2$ depends only on $x$, not separately on $Q^2$ or $\nu$.

For proton with $u,u,d$ valence quarks:

$$F_2^{ep}(x) = x\left[\frac{4}{9}(u(x)+\bar{u}(x)) + \frac{1}{9}(d(x)+\bar{d}(x)) + \frac{4}{9}(c(x)+\bar{c}(x)) + \cdots\right]$$

Experimentally, approximate scaling observed at SLAC in 1960s-70s, confirming partons.

**Answer:** Parton model gives $F_2(x,Q^2) = \sum_i Q_i^2 x f_i(x)$; $Q^2$-independence is Bjorken scaling, broken logarithmically by QCD.

The DGLAP (Dokshitzer-Gribov-Lipatov-Altarelli-Parisi) evolution equations describe how parton distributions change with $Q^2$ through gluon radiation and quark pair production. As we probe shorter distances (higher $Q^2$), we resolve more partons—a gluon can split into $q\bar{q}$ pairs, quarks radiate gluons, gluons split into gluon pairs. The evolution is governed by **splitting functions** $P_{ij}(z)$ giving the probability for parton $j$ to emit parton $i$ with momentum fraction $z$:

$$Q^2\frac{\partial q_i(x,Q^2)}{\partial Q^2} = \frac{\alpha_s(Q^2)}{2\pi}\int_x^1 \frac{dz}{z}\left[q_i(z,Q^2)P_{qq}(x/z) + g(z,Q^2)P_{qg}(x/z)\right]$$

with similar equation for gluon distribution. The splitting functions have singularities at $z \to 1$ (soft emission) and $z \to 0$ (collinear emission), regularized by $+$ prescription ensuring probability conservation.

### Example 2: DGLAP Evolution Equations

Write the DGLAP equation for quark distribution and explain physical meaning.

**Solution:**

The DGLAP equation for quark flavor $q_i$ at leading order:

$$\frac{\partial q_i(x,Q^2)}{\partial \ln Q^2} = \frac{\alpha_s(Q^2)}{2\pi}\int_x^1 \frac{dz}{z}\left[P_{qq}(x/z)q_i(z,Q^2) + P_{qg}(x/z)g(z,Q^2)\right]$$

where $z$ is momentum fraction of parent parton.

**Splitting functions** at leading order:

$$P_{qq}(z) = C_F\left[\frac{1+z^2}{(1-z)_+} + \frac{3}{2}\delta(1-z)\right]$$

$$P_{qg}(z) = T_F[z^2 + (1-z)^2]$$

where $C_F = 4/3$ (fundamental Casimir), $T_F = 1/2$, and $(1-z)_+$ is plus distribution.

**Physical interpretation**:

First term $P_{qq}(x/z)q_i(z)$: Quark at $z$ radiates gluon, leaving quark at $x = z - k$ where $k$ is gluon momentum fraction. Probability $\sim \alpha_s P_{qq}$.

Second term $P_{qg}(x/z)g(z)$: Gluon at $z$ splits into $q\bar{q}$ pair, creating quark at $x$. Probability $\sim \alpha_s P_{qg}$.

**$(1-z)_+$ prescription**: Regularizes soft singularity at $z \to 1$:

$$\int_0^1 f(z)\frac{1}{(1-z)_+}dz = \int_0^1 \frac{f(z)-f(1)}{1-z}dz$$

Ensures $\int P_{qq}(z)dz$ finite (probability conserved).

**Evolution**: At higher $Q^2$, probe shorter distances → see more radiated partons → distributions shift to smaller $x$ (sea quarks and gluons grow at small $x$).

Similarly for gluon:

$$\frac{\partial g(x,Q^2)}{\partial \ln Q^2} = \frac{\alpha_s(Q^2)}{2\pi}\int_x^1 \frac{dz}{z}\left[P_{gq}(x/z)\sum_i q_i(z,Q^2) + P_{gg}(x/z)g(z,Q^2)\right]$$

with $P_{gq}(z) = C_F\frac{1+(1-z)^2}{z}$ and $P_{gg}(z) = 2C_A\left[\frac{z}{(1-z)_+} + \frac{1-z}{z} + z(1-z)\right]$.

**Answer:** DGLAP $\partial q/\partial\ln Q^2 = (\alpha_s/2\pi)\int (P_{qq}q + P_{qg}g)dz/z$; describes $Q^2$ evolution via splitting.

Measurements at HERA (electron-proton collider at DESY) spanning $Q^2$ from $1$ to $10^5$ GeV$^2$ and $x$ from $10^{-5}$ to $0.75$ provide exquisite tests of DGLAP evolution. The rise of $F_2$ at small $x$ and the logarithmic $Q^2$ dependence match QCD predictions precisely. Combining data from fixed-target experiments (E665, NMC, BCDMS), HERA, and hadron collider processes (Tevatron, LHC) allows **global PDF fits** extracting parton distributions with percent-level uncertainties for accessible $x$ and $Q^2$ ranges.

### Example 3: Splitting Functions at Leading Order

Calculate $P_{qq}(z)$ at leading order from quark gluon emission.

**Solution:**

Consider quark emitting gluon: $q(p) \to q(p') + g(k)$ with $p' = zp$, $k = (1-z)p$ where $z$ is quark momentum fraction.

Amplitude squared (collinear limit):

$$|\mathcal{M}_{q\to qg}|^2 \propto g_s^2 C_F \frac{1+z^2}{1-z}$$

where $C_F = (N_c^2-1)/(2N_c) = 4/3$ for SU(3). The factor $1+z^2$ comes from fermion spin averaging, $(1-z)^{-1}$ from soft gluon enhancement.

**Probability** $dP = |\mathcal{M}|^2 \, d\Phi$ where $d\Phi$ is phase space. In $d=4-2\epsilon$ dimensions:

$$dP \propto \frac{\alpha_s}{2\pi}C_F\frac{1+z^2}{1-z}\frac{dz}{z}d\epsilon$$

The splitting function is coefficient:

$$P_{qq}(z) = C_F\frac{1+z^2}{1-z}$$

But $\int_0^1 P_{qq}(z)dz$ diverges at $z \to 1$ (infrared). Regularize with virtual corrections giving:

$$P_{qq}(z) = C_F\left[\frac{1+z^2}{(1-z)_+} + \frac{3}{2}\delta(1-z)\right]$$

The $\delta$-function ensures $\int_0^1 P_{qq}(z)dz = 0$ (probability conservation—total quark number unchanged by gluon emission).

For gluon splitting $g \to q\bar{q}$:

$$P_{qg}(z) = T_F[z^2 + (1-z)^2]$$

with $T_F = 1/2$. The $z^2 + (1-z)^2$ structure reflects vector boson (gluon) producing fermion pair.

Higher-order (NLO, NNLO) splitting functions computed to $\mathcal{O}(\alpha_s^2)$, $\mathcal{O}(\alpha_s^3)$ for precision.

**Answer:** $P_{qq}(z) = C_F[(1+z^2)/(1-z)_+ + (3/2)\delta(1-z)]$ from quark $\to$ quark + gluon collinear splitting.

Structure functions obey sum rules constraining parton distributions. The **momentum sum rule** $\int_0^1 x[q(x)+\bar{q}(x)+g(x)]dx = 1$ ensures partons carry all the proton's momentum. At $Q^2 \sim 10$ GeV$^2$, quarks carry about $40\%$, gluons $60\%$—the proton is mostly glue! The **Gottfried sum rule** relates proton and neutron structure functions, testing isospin symmetry and sea quark distributions. Violations of naive Gottfried sum observed at NMC indicated $\bar{d} \neq \bar{u}$ sea asymmetry, later confirmed in Drell-Yan processes.

### Example 4: Momentum Sum Rule

Derive the momentum sum rule and interpret physically.

**Solution:**

Proton four-momentum $P^\mu$ is carried by partons. Each parton type $i$ (quarks, antiquarks, gluons) has distribution $f_i(x,Q^2)$ where $xf_i(x)dx$ gives momentum fraction in $[x,x+dx]$.

Total momentum:

$$P^\mu = \sum_{\text{partons }i}\int_0^1 xf_i(x,Q^2)dx \cdot P^\mu$$

Conservation requires sum equals $1$:

$$\sum_i \int_0^1 xf_i(x,Q^2)dx = 1$$

Explicitly for $u,d,s$ quarks and gluons:

$$\int_0^1 x[u(x)+\bar{u}(x) + d(x)+\bar{d}(x) + s(x)+\bar{s}(x) + g(x)]dx = 1$$

**Measured values** at $Q^2 = 10$ GeV$^2$:

$$\langle x\rangle_{\text{quarks}} = \int x[q+\bar{q}]dx \approx 0.4$$

$$\langle x\rangle_{\text{gluons}} = \int xg(x)dx \approx 0.6$$

Gluons carry most of the momentum! This is striking—proton consists of three valence quarks by quantum numbers, but dynamically is $60\%$ glue.

**$Q^2$ dependence**: As $Q^2$ increases, gluon distributions grow at small $x$ (DGLAP evolution), but momentum sum rule holds exactly at all $Q^2$ (consequence of momentum conservation).

**Spin sum rule** (proton spin):

$$\frac{1}{2} = \frac{1}{2}\Delta\Sigma + L_q + \Delta G + L_g$$

where $\Delta\Sigma = \int [\Delta u + \Delta d + \Delta s]dx$ is quark spin contribution, $L_q$ quark orbital angular momentum, $\Delta G$ gluon polarization, $L_g$ gluon orbital. Measurements show quarks carry only $\sim 30\%$ of proton spin—the "proton spin crisis."

**Answer:** Momentum sum rule $\int_0^1 x[\text{all partons}]dx = 1$; quarks $\sim 40\%$, gluons $\sim 60\%$ of proton momentum.

The small-$x$ behavior of structure functions connects to QCD's gluon dynamics. At $x \to 0$, DGLAP evolution predicts $F_2 \sim x^{-\lambda}$ where $\lambda = 0.3$-$0.5$ comes from resumming leading $\ln(1/x)$ terms. HERA data show dramatic rise at small $x$, consistent with gluon-induced cascades populating low momentum fractions. The **BFKL** (Balitsky-Fadin-Kuraev-Lipatov) equation describes small-$x$ resummation beyond DGLAP, though saturation effects (parton recombination, Color Glass Condensate) may tame growth at extremely small $x < 10^{-4}$.

### Example 5: Quark Distributions from Valence and Sea

Decompose quark PDFs into valence and sea contributions and estimate at $Q^2 = 10$ GeV$^2$.

**Solution:**

Total quark distribution separates:

$$q_i(x) = q_i^{\text{val}}(x) + q_i^{\text{sea}}(x)$$

**Valence quarks**: Carry proton quantum numbers. For proton ($uud$):

$$u^{\text{val}}(x) = u(x) - \bar{u}(x), \quad d^{\text{val}}(x) = d(x) - \bar{d}(x)$$

Number sum rules:

$$\int_0^1 u^{\text{val}}(x)dx = 2, \quad \int_0^1 d^{\text{val}}(x)dx = 1$$

**Sea quarks**: Virtual $q\bar{q}$ pairs from gluon splitting, flavor-symmetric to first approximation:

$$u^{\text{sea}}(x) = \bar{u}(x), \quad d^{\text{sea}}(x) = \bar{d}(x)$$

In reality, $\bar{d} > \bar{u}$ (flavor asymmetry from meson cloud effects).

**Parametrization** at $Q_0^2 = 10$ GeV$^2$:

$$xu^{\text{val}}(x) \sim x^{0.5}(1-x)^3$$

$$xd^{\text{val}}(x) \sim x^{0.5}(1-x)^4$$

$$x[\bar{u}(x)+\bar{d}(x)] \sim (1-x)^7$$

$$xg(x) \sim x^{-0.3}(1-x)^5$$

Valence peaks at $x \sim 0.2$, sea and gluons dominate at small $x < 0.1$.

**Flavor ratios**:

At large $x \to 1$: $d/u \to 0$ (only $u$ quarks survive, proton $\to$ $uu$ diquark + $u$)

At $x \sim 0.3$: $d/u \approx 1/2$ (valence ratio $1:2$)

At $x \to 0$: $d/u \to 1$ (sea symmetric)

**Strangeness**: $s(x) = \bar{s}(x)$ from gluon splitting, $\int xs(x)dx \sim 0.05$ (small contribution).

**Gluon**: Dominates at small $x$, $g(x \sim 0.01) \gg q(x \sim 0.01)$.

**Answer:** Valence: $u^{\text{val}} = u - \bar{u}$ (2 quarks), $d^{\text{val}} = d - \bar{d}$ (1 quark); sea: $q^{\text{sea}} = \bar{q}$ from gluon splitting.

At the LHC, PDFs are essential input for predicting cross sections. Gluon-gluon fusion dominates Higgs production, while valence quark-antiquark annihilation produces Drell-Yan dileptons and W/Z bosons. PDF uncertainties propagate to theoretical predictions, with gluon PDF at large $x$ being particularly uncertain. Jet production, top quark pair production, and heavy flavor measurements constrain different regions of $(x,Q^2)$ space, with modern global fits combining hundreds of datasets.

### Example 6: PDF Applications at LHC

Estimate parton-parton luminosity for Higgs production at LHC.

**Solution:**

Higgs production via gluon fusion $gg \to H$ at LHC ($\sqrt{s} = 13$ TeV). Cross section:

$$\sigma(pp \to H) = \int dx_1 dx_2 \, g(x_1, Q^2) g(x_2, Q^2) \hat{\sigma}(gg \to H)$$

where $x_1, x_2$ are momentum fractions, $Q^2 \sim m_H^2$, $\hat{\sigma}$ is partonic cross section.

**Kinematics**: Higgs mass $m_H \approx 125$ GeV, rapidity $y \approx 0$ (central):

$$x_1 x_2 = \frac{m_H^2}{s} = \frac{(125)^2}{(13000)^2} \approx 9 \times 10^{-5}$$

For symmetric case $x_1 = x_2 \approx 0.01$ (typical).

**Gluon luminosity**: At $x \sim 0.01$, $Q^2 = m_H^2$:

$$xg(x,Q^2) \sim 5 \quad (\text{from PDF fits like CT18, NNPDF})$$

$$\frac{d\mathcal{L}_{gg}}{dx_1dx_2} = g(x_1)g(x_2) \sim 25$$

Integrating over phase space with gluon PDF peaked at small $x$:

$$\mathcal{L}_{gg} \sim 10^3 \text{ pb}^{-1}\text{GeV}^{-2}$$

Partonic cross section (loop-induced):

$$\hat{\sigma}(gg \to H) \sim \frac{\alpha_s^2(m_H)}{m_H^2}\cdot C \sim 0.5\text{ pb}$$

where $C$ includes loop factors, width.

Total: $\sigma(pp \to H) \sim 50$ pb at 13 TeV.

Measured: $\sigma \approx 55$ pb (excellent agreement!).

**PDF uncertainties**: Gluon PDF at $x \sim 0.01$ uncertain at $5$-$10\%$ level, propagates to Higgs cross section.

For high-mass resonances ($M \sim 1$ TeV), probe larger $x \sim M/\sqrt{s} \sim 0.1$ where PDFs better known.

**Answer:** Gluon luminosity at LHC peaks at small $x \sim 0.01$; drives Higgs, heavy flavor, jet production cross sections.

Extracting PDFs requires sophisticated global analyses combining diverse datasets with theoretical calculations at NLO or NNLO accuracy. Collaborations like CT (CTEQ-TEA), MMHT, NNPDF, and ABMP perform independent fits with different methodologies—parametric forms versus neural networks, different dataset selections, and varying treatments of systematic uncertainties. Comparison between groups provides robust uncertainty estimates essential for precision physics at current and future colliders.

### Example 7: DGLAP Evolution Numerical Example

Show numerically how quark PDF evolves from $Q_0^2 = 4$ GeV$^2$ to $Q^2 = 100$ GeV$^2$ using DGLAP.

**Solution:**

Start with $u(x, Q_0^2 = 4\text{ GeV}^2)$ from parametrization. Evolve to $Q^2 = 100$ GeV$^2$ using:

$$\frac{d u(x,Q^2)}{d\ln Q^2} = \frac{\alpha_s(Q^2)}{2\pi}\int_x^1 \frac{dz}{z}\left[P_{qq}(x/z)u(z,Q^2) + P_{qg}(x/z)g(z,Q^2)\right]$$

**Numerical implementation**: Discretize $x$ on grid ($50$-$100$ points logarithmically spaced), use Runge-Kutta or Euler method to evolve in $\ln Q^2$.

**Example at $x = 0.1$**:

At $Q_0^2 = 4$ GeV$^2$: $xu(0.1) \approx 0.4$, $xg(0.1) \approx 2$

Running coupling: $\alpha_s(4\text{ GeV}^2) \approx 0.3$, $\alpha_s(100\text{ GeV}^2) \approx 0.12$

Splitting function contributions:

$P_{qq}$ term: $\sim C_F \cdot 2 \approx 2.7$ (quark radiating gluon)

$P_{qg}$ term: $\sim T_F \cdot g(z) \approx 0.5 \cdot 2 = 1$ (gluon splitting)

Evolution step: $\Delta(\ln Q^2) = \ln(100/4) = 3.2$

$$u(0.1, 100\text{ GeV}^2) \approx u(0.1, 4\text{ GeV}^2) + \int d\ln Q^2 \frac{\alpha_s}{2\pi}[\cdots]$$

Approximate: Growth of $\sim 10$-$20\%$ at $x = 0.1$ due to gluon splitting dominating.

At small $x = 0.01$:

$xg(0.01, 4\text{ GeV}^2) \approx 5$, grows to $xg(0.01, 100\text{ GeV}^2) \approx 15$ (factor 3 increase!)

Gluon distribution rises rapidly at small $x$ with $Q^2$.

**Tools**: LHAPDF library provides interpolation of evolved PDFs, QCDNUM, APFEL for custom evolution.

**Answer:** DGLAP evolution: Quarks grow modestly ($\sim 10$-$20\%$), gluons grow rapidly (factor 2-3) from $Q_0^2 = 4$ to $100$ GeV$^2$ at small $x$.

Modern precision QCD at the LHC relies on NNLO (next-to-next-to-leading order) calculations combining hard scattering matrix elements with PDF evolution, parton showers, and hadronization models. Tools like MCFM, NNLOJET, and Matrix compute multi-leg processes at NNLO accuracy, while Monte Carlo event generators (Pythia, Herwig, Sherpa) provide exclusive event descriptions for detector simulations. The interplay between theory and experiment—refining PDFs through new measurements, testing QCD through precision observables, searching for beyond-Standard-Model physics—continues to drive collider physics forward.

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: B** — Bjorken $x = Q^2/(2M\nu) \in [0,1]$ is momentum fraction of struck parton in infinite-momentum frame. Parton model: proton composed of partons each carrying fraction $x$ of proton momentum. DIS measures $x$ distribution through structure functions.

**Problem 2: B** — DGLAP equations $\partial q/\partial\ln Q^2 = (\alpha_s/2\pi)\int P_{ij}dz/z$ describe $Q^2$ evolution via parton splitting: gluon $\to q\bar{q}$, quark $\to qg$. Higher $Q^2$ probes shorter distances, resolves more radiated partons. Logarithmic $Q^2$ dependence breaks Bjorken scaling.

**Problem 3: C** — Small $x$: Gluon distribution $xg(x) \sim x^{-0.3}$ dominates, sea quarks from $g \to q\bar{q}$ splitting also large. Valence quarks peaked at $x \sim 0.2$. At $x = 0.01$, gluons carry $\sim 80\%$ of PDF weight. HERA measured rise of $F_2$ at small $x$.

**Problem 4: B** — Momentum conservation: $\sum_i \int_0^1 x f_i(x)dx = 1$ where sum includes all quarks, antiquarks, gluons. Measured: quarks $\sim 40\%$, gluons $\sim 60\%$ at $Q^2 \sim 10$ GeV$^2$. Holds exactly at all $Q^2$ (DGLAP preserves).

**Problem 5: B** — $P_{qq}(z) = C_F[(1+z^2)/(1-z)_+ + \cdots]$ gives probability for quark to radiate gluon with momentum fraction $1-z$. Appears in DGLAP: $\partial q/\partial\ln Q^2 \sim P_{qq}q + P_{qg}g$. Singular at $z \to 1$ (soft gluon), regularized by virtual corrections.
