# Quantum Field Theory Topic 5.6: Effective Field Theory

Effective field theory (EFT) provides the conceptual and computational framework for systematically describing physics below some energy scale $\Lambda$ by integrating out high-energy degrees of freedom, yielding an effective Lagrangian containing all operators consistent with symmetries organized by their mass dimension. The Wilsonian approach implements this through functional integration: given a UV theory with fields $\phi$, separate modes into low-momentum $\phi_<$ (with $|p| < \Lambda$) and high-momentum $\phi_>$ (with $|p| > \Lambda$), then integrate out $\phi_>$ in the path integral to obtain $\mathcal{L}_{\text{eff}}[\phi_<] = \mathcal{L}_{\text{renorm}}[\phi_<] + \sum_i (c_i/\Lambda^{d_i-4})\mathcal{O}_i[\phi_<]$ where operators are classified by dimension: relevant ($d < 4$), marginal ($d = 4$), and irrelevant ($d > 4$), with suppression factor $(E/\Lambda)^{d-4}$ determining their importance at energy $E \ll \Lambda$. The Standard Model itself functions as an EFT below the Planck scale or any BSM physics scale, with the unique dimension-5 Weinberg operator $\mathcal{L}_5 = (c/\Lambda)(L\Phi)(L\Phi)$ generating Majorana neutrino masses after electroweak symmetry breaking as $m_\nu \sim v^2/\Lambda$, while dimension-6 operators like four-fermion interactions, dipole moments, and triple gauge couplings provide systematically organized parameterizations of beyond-Standard-Model physics constrained by precision measurements. The power of EFT lies in separating short-distance (unknown UV) from long-distance (measured low-energy) physics, enabling robust predictions independent of UV completion and organizing calculations through power counting in $E/\Lambda$.

The Wilsonian procedure for constructing effective theories begins with a partition function $Z = \int \mathcal{D}\phi\, e^{iS[\phi]}$ describing all degrees of freedom. Decompose fields by momentum scale:

$$\phi(x) = \phi_<(x) + \phi_>(x)$$

where $\phi_< = \int_{|p|<\Lambda} \tilde{\phi}(p)e^{ipx}$ contains low modes and $\phi_> = \int_{|p|>\Lambda} \tilde{\phi}(p)e^{ipx}$ high modes. The path integral factorizes:

$$Z = \int \mathcal{D}\phi_< \mathcal{D}\phi_>\, e^{iS[\phi_<,\phi_>]} = \int \mathcal{D}\phi_<\, e^{iS_{\text{eff}}[\phi_<]}$$

where:

$$e^{iS_{\text{eff}}[\phi_<]} = \int \mathcal{D}\phi_>\, e^{iS[\phi_<,\phi_>]}$$

Performing the functional integral over $\phi_>$ generates all possible local operators of $\phi_<$ allowed by symmetry. At tree level, this is matching: compute Green's functions in the full theory and effective theory, equate them, solve for coefficients $c_i$. At loop level, additional running and threshold corrections appear.

### Example 1: Integrating Out Heavy Scalar

Construct effective theory by integrating out heavy scalar field.

**Solution:**

Consider theory with light scalar $\phi$ and heavy scalar $\Phi$ with mass $M \gg m$:

$$\mathcal{L} = \frac{1}{2}(\partial\phi)^2 - \frac{1}{2}m^2\phi^2 + \frac{1}{2}(\partial\Phi)^2 - \frac{1}{2}M^2\Phi^2 - \frac{\lambda}{4!}\phi^4 - \frac{g}{2}\phi^2\Phi$$

Integrate out $\Phi$ in path integral. The equation of motion for $\Phi$:

$$(-\partial^2 + M^2)\Phi = g\phi^2$$

Solving (at tree level):

$$\Phi(x) = \int d^4y\, G(x-y)g\phi^2(y)$$

where $G(x-y) = \int d^4p/(2\pi)^4 e^{ip(x-y)}/(p^2 - M^2)$ is propagator. Substitute back:

$$S_{\text{eff}}[\phi] = S[\phi] + \frac{g}{2}\int d^4x\, \phi^2(x)\Phi(x)$$

$$= S[\phi] + \frac{g^2}{2}\int d^4x d^4y\, \phi^2(x)G(x-y)\phi^2(y)$$

For low momenta $p \ll M$, expand propagator:

$$G(p) = \frac{1}{p^2 - M^2} \approx -\frac{1}{M^2}\left(1 + \frac{p^2}{M^2} + \cdots\right)$$

The leading term:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{eff}} \approx \frac{1}{2}(\partial\phi)^2 - \frac{1}{2}m^2\phi^2 - \frac{\lambda_{\text{eff}}}{4!}\phi^4 + \cdots$$

where:

$$\lambda_{\text{eff}} = \lambda - \frac{6g^2}{M^2}$$

The heavy field $\Phi$ has been replaced by correction to $\phi^4$ coupling! Higher-derivative operators appear at order $1/M^4$:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{eff}} \supset -\frac{g^2}{M^4}\phi^2\partial^2\phi^2 + O(1/M^6)$$

These are dimension-6 operators suppressed by $(E/M)^2$ at low energy $E \ll M$.

**Answer:** Integrating out heavy scalar generates effective $\phi^4$ interaction $\delta\lambda = -6g^2/M^2$ plus higher-dimension operators $\sim \phi^2\partial^2\phi^2/M^4$.

The classification of operators by dimension determines their relevance at low energies. An operator $\mathcal{O}_i$ with mass dimension $d_i$ enters the effective Lagrangian as:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{eff}} \supset \frac{c_i}{\Lambda^{d_i-4}}\mathcal{O}_i$$

where $c_i$ is a dimensionless Wilson coefficient and $\Lambda$ is the scale of new physics. The contribution to observables at energy $E$:

$$\mathcal{A} \sim \frac{c_i}{\Lambda^{d_i-4}}E^{d_i}$$

scales as $(E/\Lambda)^{d_i-4}$. Operators are classified:
- **Relevant** ($d < 4$): Grow at low energy, dominate IR physics (masses, marginal couplings)
- **Marginal** ($d = 4$): Scale-independent, determine the theory (gauge couplings, Yukawas)
- **Irrelevant** ($d > 4$): Suppressed at low energy by powers of $E/\Lambda$

In the Standard Model, all renormalizable terms have $d \leq 4$. The effective Lagrangian includes non-renormalizable operators with $d > 4$ organized by dimension.

### Example 2: Dimension Counting for SM Effective Operators

List possible dimension-5 and dimension-6 operators in the Standard Model.

**Solution:**

**Dimension counting**: Fields have $[\phi] = 1$ (scalar), $[\psi] = 3/2$ (fermion), $[F_{\mu\nu}] = 2$ (field strength), $[\partial] = 1$. Operators must be gauge-invariant, Lorentz scalars.

**Dimension 5**:

Only one class (up to flavor structure):

$$\mathcal{O}_5 = (L_i\Phi)(L_j\Phi)$$

where $L$ is lepton doublet, $\Phi$ is Higgs doublet. Dimension: $3/2 + 1 + 3/2 + 1 = 5$. This is the **Weinberg operator** generating Majorana neutrino masses.

After EWSB with $\langle\Phi\rangle = v/\sqrt{2}$:

$$\mathcal{L}_5 = \frac{c_{ij}}{\Lambda}(\nu_i\nu_j)\frac{v^2}{2}$$

gives Majorana mass:

$$m_{\nu,ij} = \frac{c_{ij}v^2}{2\Lambda}$$

For $m_\nu \sim 0.1$ eV and $v = 246$ GeV:

$$\Lambda \sim \frac{v^2}{m_\nu} \sim \frac{(246)^2}{0.1}\text{ GeV} \sim 10^{15}\text{ GeV}$$

suggesting GUT-scale physics!

**Dimension 6**:

Many classes (84 operators in Warsaw basis after EOM and field redefinitions):
- Four-fermion: $(L\gamma^\mu L)(L\gamma_\mu L)$, $(Q\gamma^\mu Q)(\bar{u}\gamma_\mu\bar{u})$, etc.
- Dipole: $(\bar{Q}\sigma^{\mu\nu}u)F_{\mu\nu}\Phi$
- Triple gauge: $\Phi^\dagger\Phi W^{\mu\nu}W_{\mu\nu}$
- Modified Higgs: $(\Phi^\dagger D_\mu\Phi)^2$

Each suppressed by $1/\Lambda^2$. Current constraints from LHC, flavor physics, EWPT give $\Lambda \gtrsim 1-10$ TeV depending on operator.

**Answer:** Dimension-5: Weinberg operator $(L\Phi)(L\Phi)/\Lambda$ (unique, generates $m_\nu$). Dimension-6: 84 operators (Warsaw basis) including four-fermion, dipoles, triple gauge.

The Standard Model as an EFT organizes beyond-Standard-Model (BSM) physics model-independently. Any new physics at scale $\Lambda$ generates dimension-6 operators with coefficients encoding the UV theory:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{SM+EFT}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{SM}} + \frac{1}{\Lambda^2}\sum_i c_i^{(6)}\mathcal{O}_i^{(6)} + \frac{1}{\Lambda^3}\sum_j c_j^{(7)}\mathcal{O}_j^{(7)} + \cdots$$

The coefficients $c_i$ depend on the specific BSM model but observables are organized by $(v/\Lambda)^2$ where $v = 246$ GeV. Current LHC and precision measurements constrain $\Lambda \gtrsim$ few TeV for generic coefficients $c_i \sim 1$, while flavor physics (rare $B$ decays, $K$ mixing) push certain combinations to $\Lambda > 100$ TeV.

### Example 3: Four-Fermion Operators and Fermi Theory

Derive Fermi theory of weak interactions as EFT from integrating out $W$ boson.

**Solution:**

The SM weak interaction:

$$\mathcal{L} = g\bar{\psi}_L\gamma^\mu\tau^a\psi_L W_\mu^a + \text{h.c.}$$

where $W$ boson has mass $m_W = 80$ GeV. For processes at energy $E \ll m_W$, integrate out $W$:

$$W_\mu^a(p) = \frac{g}{p^2 - m_W^2}J_\mu^a(p)$$

where $J_\mu^a = \bar{\psi}_L\gamma_\mu\tau^a\psi_L$ is weak current. Substitute:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{eff}} = \frac{g^2}{2}\int d^4p\, J_\mu^a(p)\frac{1}{p^2 - m_W^2}J^{a\mu}(-p)$$

For $p^2 \ll m_W^2$:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{eff}} \approx -\frac{g^2}{2m_W^2}J_\mu^a J^{a\mu} = -\frac{G_F}{\sqrt{2}}(\bar{\psi}_L\gamma^\mu\psi_L)(\bar{\psi}_L\gamma_\mu\psi_L)$$

where Fermi constant:

$$G_F = \frac{\sqrt{2}g^2}{8m_W^2} = \frac{1}{2v^2} \approx 1.166 \times 10^{-5}\text{ GeV}^{-2}$$

This is **Fermi's four-fermion theory** with $[G_F] = -2$ (dimension-6 operator, non-renormalizable). Predicts beta decay, muon decay:

$$\Gamma(\mu^- \to e^-\bar{\nu}_e\nu_\mu) = \frac{G_F^2 m_\mu^5}{192\pi^3}(1 + \text{radiative corrections})$$

Matches experiment at percent level for $E \ll m_W$. At $E \sim m_W$, must use full SM with $W$ propagator (unitarity violation resolved).

**Matching**: Tree-level gives $c = g^2/(2m_W^2)$. One-loop corrections modify $c$ by $O(\alpha)$. This EFT approach systematizes weak interactions below electroweak scale.

**Answer:** Integrating out $W$ boson gives Fermi theory $\mathcal{L} = -G_F(\bar{\psi}\gamma^\mu\psi)^2$ with $G_F = \sqrt{2}g^2/(8m_W^2)$ (dimension-6, valid for $E \ll m_W$).

Matching coefficients at the cutoff scale $\Lambda$ requires computing amplitudes in both the full and effective theories and equating them. At tree level, this is straightforward: calculate the same process in UV and EFT, expand in $E/\Lambda$, match term by term. At loop level, additional subtleties arise: the EFT must reproduce not just tree-level amplitudes but also one-loop corrections up to terms $\sim E^2/\Lambda^2$. The matching scale $\mu_{\text{match}}$ is typically chosen as $\mu \sim \Lambda$ to minimize large logarithms, with running from $\Lambda$ down to physical scale $E$ via RG equations resumming logs.

### Example 4: Running of EFT Operators

Derive the RG equation for dimension-6 four-fermion operator.

**Solution:**

Consider four-fermion operator:

$$\mathcal{O}_6 = \frac{c}{\Lambda^2}(\bar{\psi}\gamma^\mu\psi)^2$$

The Wilson coefficient $c(\mu)$ runs with renormalization scale. At one-loop, the operator mixes under RG:

$$\mu\frac{dc}{d\mu} = \gamma_6(g) c$$

where $\gamma_6$ is the anomalous dimension. Computing one-loop diagrams with operator insertion:

$$\gamma_6 = \frac{g^2}{16\pi^2}(-6) + O(g^4)$$

The negative coefficient means $c$ decreases at high $\mu$ (asymptotic safety if $\gamma < 0$).

The RG equation solution:

$$c(\mu_2) = c(\mu_1)\exp\left[\int_{\mu_1}^{\mu_2}\frac{d\mu'}{\mu'}\gamma_6(g(\mu'))\right]$$

For QCD-like theory with $g$ running:

$$c(\mu) = c(\Lambda)\left(\frac{\alpha_s(\mu)}{\alpha_s(\Lambda)}\right)^{\gamma_6/\beta_0}$$

where $\beta_0$ is the one-loop beta function coefficient. If both $\beta_0 < 0$ (asymptotic freedom) and $\gamma_6 < 0$:

$$c(\mu) \sim \left[\ln\frac{\mu}{\Lambda}\right]^{-\gamma_6/\beta_0}$$

increases at low energy. The operator becomes more important in IR, potentially causing new physics at low scales.

**Operator mixing**: Multiple operators can mix under RG. The full equation:

$$\mu\frac{dc_i}{d\mu} = \sum_j \gamma_{ij} c_j$$

with mixing matrix $\gamma_{ij}$. Diagonalizing gives eigenbasis where operators run independently.

**Answer:** RG equation $\mu dc/d\mu = \gamma_6 c$ with $\gamma_6 = -6g^2/(16\pi^2)$ at one-loop; operator mixes, runs with coupling.

The hierarchy between scales determines the organization of effective theories. Consider the chain: Planck scale $M_{\text{Pl}} \sim 10^{19}$ GeV → GUT scale $M_{\text{GUT}} \sim 10^{16}$ GeV → SUSY scale $m_{\text{SUSY}} \sim 1$ TeV → Electroweak scale $v = 246$ GeV → QCD scale $\Lambda_{\text{QCD}} = 200$ MeV → Atomic scale $m_e = 0.5$ MeV. At each scale, we integrate out heavy particles, generating effective Lagrangians for lower energies. The Standard Model EFT operates between $\Lambda_{\text{BSM}}$ (TeV to $10^{19}$ GeV) and $v$, while low-energy hadron physics uses chiral perturbation theory below $\Lambda_{\text{QCD}}$.

### Example 5: Neutrino Mass from Weinberg Operator

Calculate neutrino mass from dimension-5 operator and determine $\Lambda$.

**Solution:**

The Weinberg operator:

$$\mathcal{L}_5 = \frac{c_{ij}}{2\Lambda}(L_i\Phi)(\Phi^\dagger L_j^c) + \text{h.c.}$$

where $L = (\nu, e)_L$, $\Phi = (\phi^+, \phi^0)$, and $L^c = C\bar{L}^T$ (charge conjugate). After EWSB $\langle\phi^0\rangle = v/\sqrt{2}$:

$$\mathcal{L}_5 \to \frac{c_{ij}v^2}{4\Lambda}\nu_i\nu_j + \text{h.c.}$$

This is a Majorana mass term. The mass matrix:

$$M_{\nu,ij} = \frac{c_{ij}v^2}{2\Lambda}$$

For diagonal $c_{ij} = c\delta_{ij}$ and typical neutrino mass $m_\nu \sim 0.05$ eV:

$$\Lambda = \frac{cv^2}{2m_\nu} \approx \frac{c \times (246\text{ GeV})^2}{2 \times 0.05\text{ eV}}$$

$$\approx c \times 6 \times 10^{14}\text{ GeV}$$

For $c \sim 1$ (natural size), $\Lambda \sim 10^{15}$ GeV, near GUT scale! This is the **see-saw mechanism**: tiny neutrino mass from huge ratio $v^2/\Lambda$.

**UV completion**: The operator arises from integrating out heavy right-handed neutrinos $N_R$ with Majorana mass $M_R \sim \Lambda$:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{UV}} = y_\nu L\Phi N_R + \frac{M_R}{2}N_R N_R + \text{h.c.}$$

Integrating out $N_R$ gives:

$$c = \frac{y_\nu^2}{M_R}$$

So $m_\nu = y_\nu^2 v^2/(2M_R)$. For $y_\nu \sim 1$, $M_R \sim 10^{15}$ GeV explains light neutrinos.

**Experimental**: Neutrino oscillation data determines $\Delta m^2$ and mixing angles but not absolute mass scale. Cosmological bounds $\sum m_\nu < 0.12$ eV imply $\Lambda > 5 \times 10^{14}$ GeV for $c \sim 1$.

**Answer:** Weinberg operator gives $m_\nu = cv^2/(2\Lambda)$; for $m_\nu \sim 0.05$ eV, $\Lambda \sim 10^{15}$ GeV (GUT scale, seesaw mechanism).

Precision tests constrain dimension-6 operators through global fits to electroweak precision data, Higgs measurements, flavor physics, and LHC searches. The strongest bounds come from processes that vanish or are highly suppressed in the SM, where dimension-6 contributions can dominate. Examples include triple gauge couplings measured at $e^+e^-$ colliders, CP-violating electric dipole moments ($d_e < 10^{-29}\,e\cdot$cm), and rare flavor-changing neutral currents. The interplay between different operators and observables requires global fits using Monte Carlo or analytic techniques to map allowed parameter space.

### Example 6: Constraints from Electric Dipole Moment

Derive constraint on CP-violating dimension-6 operator from electron EDM.

**Solution:**

The electron EDM arises from CP-violating dimension-6 operator:

$$\mathcal{O}_{\text{EDM}} = \frac{c_{\text{EDM}}}{\Lambda^2}\bar{e}\sigma^{\mu\nu}e F_{\mu\nu}$$

where $\sigma^{\mu\nu} = (i/2)[\gamma^\mu,\gamma^\nu]$. After EWSB, this gives:

$$\mathcal{L} = d_e\bar{e}\sigma^{\mu\nu}e F_{\mu\nu}$$

where:

$$d_e = \frac{c_{\text{EDM}}v}{\Lambda^2}\times O(1)$$

Current experimental bound:

$$|d_e| < 1.1 \times 10^{-29}\,e\cdot\text{cm}$$

With $v = 246$ GeV and $e\cdot\text{cm} = 1.95 \times 10^{-18}\text{ GeV}^{-1}$:

$$|d_e| < 2.1 \times 10^{-47}\text{ GeV}^{-1}$$

This gives:

$$\frac{c_{\text{EDM}}}{\Lambda^2} < \frac{2.1 \times 10^{-47}\text{ GeV}^{-1}}{246\text{ GeV}} \approx 8.5 \times 10^{-50}\text{ GeV}^{-2}$$

For $c_{\text{EDM}} \sim 1$:

$$\Lambda > \frac{1}{\sqrt{8.5 \times 10^{-50}}} \approx 3.4 \times 10^{24}\text{ GeV}$$

Ridiculously high! This is $10^5$ times the Planck scale. However, most BSM models have $c_{\text{EDM}} \ll 1$ due to CP-violating phases being small or suppressed by loops.

For $c_{\text{EDM}} \sim 10^{-4}$ (one-loop suppression):

$$\Lambda > 3.4 \times 10^{22}\text{ GeV}$$

Still far beyond any direct reach. EDM constraints are among the most stringent on CP-violating new physics.

**Interpretation**: Either $\Lambda \gg 10^{15}$ GeV, or BSM CP violation is highly suppressed (natural in many models), or both. The Standard Model itself generates $d_e \sim 10^{-38}\,e\cdot\text{cm}$ at three loops, many orders below current limit.

**Answer:** Electron EDM operator $(\bar{e}\sigma^{\mu\nu}e F_{\mu\nu})/\Lambda^2$ constrained by $|d_e| < 10^{-29}\,e\cdot$cm to $\Lambda > 10^{22}$ GeV for $c \sim 10^{-4}$ (extremely stringent).

### Example 7: EFT Power Counting in Chiral Perturbation Theory

Explain power counting in chiral perturbation theory for QCD at low energies.

**Solution:**

Below $\Lambda_{\text{QCD}} \sim 200$ MeV, quarks and gluons confine into hadrons. The light pseudoscalar mesons $\pi, K, \eta$ are approximate Goldstone bosons from spontaneous chiral symmetry breaking SU(3)$_L$ × SU(3)$_R$ → SU(3)$_V$. Chiral perturbation theory (ChPT) is the EFT for these Goldstone modes.

**Degrees of freedom**: Pion fields $\pi^a(x)$ (instead of quarks).

**Symmetries**: Chiral SU(3)$_L$ × SU(3)$_R$ → SU(3)$_V$ (broken by quark masses).

**Lagrangian**: Organized by derivatives and quark masses:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{ChPT}} = \mathcal{L}_2 + \mathcal{L}_4 + \mathcal{L}_6 + \cdots$$

where $\mathcal{L}_{2n}$ contains $2n$ derivatives or quark masses. Leading order:

$$\mathcal{L}_2 = \frac{f^2}{4}\text{Tr}[\partial_\mu U\partial^\mu U^\dagger + \chi U^\dagger + U\chi^\dagger]$$

where $U = e^{i\pi^a T^a/f}$ is the Goldstone matrix, $f \approx 93$ MeV pion decay constant, and $\chi$ contains quark masses.

**Power counting**: Momenta $p$, quark masses $m_q$, and $1/f$ all counted as $O(Q)$ where $Q \sim m_\pi \sim 140$ MeV. Expansion parameter:

$$\frac{Q}{\Lambda_{\chi}} \sim \frac{m_\pi}{1\text{ GeV}} \sim 0.15$$

Processes scale as $(Q/\Lambda_{\chi})^n$ at $\mathcal{O}(Q^n)$. Leading order ($\mathcal{L}_2$) gives tree-level $\pi\pi$ scattering. Next-to-leading order ($\mathcal{L}_4$) includes $\sim 10$ low-energy constants determined from experiment/lattice.

**Example**: Pion mass from quark mass:

$$m_\pi^2 = \frac{2B(m_u + m_d)}{1 + O(m_q)}$$

where $B \sim \Lambda_{\chi}$. ChPT predicts relationships between observables as expansions in $Q/\Lambda_{\chi}$, tested extensively.

**Modern applications**: ChPT extended to baryons (nucleons), photons, weak interactions. Essential for extracting quark masses, understanding nuclear forces, and low-energy precision tests.

**Answer:** ChPT power counts $\partial \sim m_q \sim m_\pi \sim Q$; expansion in $(Q/\Lambda_{\chi})^n$ with $\Lambda_{\chi} \sim 1$ GeV; organizes low-energy QCD systematically.

Effective field theory revolutionized how we think about quantum field theory and the Standard Model. Rather than viewing non-renormalizable interactions as pathological, EFT recognizes them as natural consequences of finite-resolution measurements—we simply cannot probe arbitrarily short distances with current experiments. The Standard Model itself is an effective theory valid up to some scale where new physics enters, whether supersymmetry, compositeness, extra dimensions, or quantum gravity. The systematic organization by operator dimension and symmetry allows robust predictions while parameterizing our ignorance of UV completion, turning the study of new physics into a well-defined program of measuring Wilson coefficients and comparing with theoretical expectations. From nuclear physics (chiral perturbation theory, pionless EFT, halo EFT) through the Standard Model (SMEFT) to quantum gravity (effective GR), effective field theory provides the unifying conceptual framework for separating scales and building on established low-energy physics.

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: B** — Operators classified by mass dimension $d$. **Relevant** ($d<4$): masses, $\phi^2$, $\phi^3$ → dominate at low $E$ (IR). **Marginal** ($d=4$): $\phi^4$, gauge interactions, Yukawa → scale-invariant. **Irrelevant** ($d>4$): $\phi^6/\Lambda^2$, $(\bar{\psi}\gamma^\mu\psi)^2/\Lambda^2$ → suppressed as $(E/\Lambda)^{d-4}$ at $E \ll \Lambda$. Power counting determines systematic EFT expansion. Higher-dimension = weaker at low energy.

**Problem 2: B** — Unique dimension-5: **Weinberg operator** $\mathcal{O}_5 = (L_i\Phi)(L_j\Phi)/\Lambda$ where $L$ = lepton doublet, $\Phi$ = Higgs. Dimension: $3/2+1+3/2+1=5$. After EWSB: $\langle\Phi\rangle = v/\sqrt{2}$ gives Majorana neutrino mass $m_{\nu,ij} = c_{ij}v^2/(2\Lambda)$. For $m_\nu \sim 0.05$ eV: $\Lambda \sim 10^{15}$ GeV (GUT scale). UV completion: seesaw with heavy $N_R$.

**Problem 3: B** — Fermi theory: **EFT below $m_W$**. Integrating out $W$ boson gives four-fermion $\mathcal{L} = -G_F/\sqrt{2}(\bar{\psi}_L\gamma^\mu\psi_L)^2$ with $G_F = \sqrt{2}g^2/(8m_W^2) \approx 1.166 \times 10^{-5}$ GeV$^{-2}$. Dimension-6 (non-renormalizable) but valid for $E \ll m_W \approx 80$ GeV. Predicts $\mu$ decay, $\beta$ decay to percent accuracy. Unitarity violation at $E \sim m_W$ resolved by full SM.

**Problem 4: B** — **Matching**: Determine Wilson coefficients by equating amplitudes in UV and EFT theories at scale $\mu \sim \Lambda$. Tree-level: calculate same process in both theories, expand in $E/\Lambda$, match coefficient. Loop-level: include quantum corrections up to precision desired. RG running then evolves $c_i(\mu)$ from $\Lambda$ down to physical scale. Minimizes large logs by choosing $\mu_{\text{match}} \sim \Lambda$.

**Problem 5: B** — Low energies $E \ll \Lambda$: dimension-4 operators dominate (renormalizable SM). Higher-dimension operators suppressed by $(E/\Lambda)^{d-4}$. Dimension-6: suppressed $\sim (E/\Lambda)^2 \sim 10^{-4}$ for $E \sim 100$ GeV, $\Lambda \sim 10$ TeV. Dimension-8: $\sim (E/\Lambda)^4 \sim 10^{-8}$. Systematic expansion: compute at given order in $E/\Lambda$, higher orders corrections. Breaks down at $E \sim \Lambda$ (need UV theory).
