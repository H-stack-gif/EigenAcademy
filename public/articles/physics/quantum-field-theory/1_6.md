# Quantum Field Theory Topic 1.6: Causality, Propagators, Fock Space

Quantum field theory must respect causality: spacelike-separated measurements cannot influence each other. In the operator formalism, this translates to (anti)commutation relations vanishing for spacelike separations—$[\hat{\phi}(\mathbf{x}, t), \hat{\phi}(\mathbf{y}, t')] = 0$ when $(x-y)^2 < 0$. This **microcausality** ensures QFT is compatible with special relativity. But understanding field propagation requires more than commutators—we need **propagators**, Green's functions encoding how disturbances propagate from point to point. The **Feynman propagator** $D_F(x-y) = \langle 0|T\hat{\phi}(x)\hat{\phi}(y)|0\rangle$ (time-ordered vacuum expectation) becomes the fundamental object for calculating scattering amplitudes in perturbation theory.

The quantum state space for field theory is **Fock space**, the direct sum of zero-particle, one-particle, two-particle, ... sectors. Unlike ordinary quantum mechanics where particle number is fixed, Fock space naturally accommodates particle creation and annihilation. This structure is essential for describing scattering processes where initial particles interact and produce different final states. The **Källén-Lehmann representation** further reveals that the field propagator encodes the complete particle spectrum, including bound states and resonances—the propagator's poles correspond to physical particles.

This article develops causality, propagators, and Fock space structure from first principles. We'll prove microcausality for scalar and Dirac fields, construct the Feynman propagator in momentum space, understand retarded and advanced Green's functions, explore the physical interpretation of propagators, build the complete Fock space structure, discuss coherent states for photons, and derive the Källén-Lehmann spectral representation.

## Causality and Commutation Relations

### Example 1: Microcausality for Scalar Fields

Prove that scalar fields commute at spacelike separations.

**Solution:**

Field commutator:
$$[\hat{\phi}(x), \hat{\phi}(y)] = \int\frac{d^3k}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{2\omega_k}[e^{-ik \cdot (x-y)} - e^{-ik \cdot (y-x)}]$$

$$= \int\frac{d^3k}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{2\omega_k}[e^{-ik \cdot (x-y)} - e^{+ik \cdot (x-y)}]$$

This depends only on $x - y$. For **spacelike separation** $(x-y)^2 < 0$:

By Lorentz invariance, can choose frame where $t = t'$ (simultaneous):
$$[\hat{\phi}(\mathbf{x}, t), \hat{\phi}(\mathbf{y}, t)] = \text{spatial commutator}$$

From equal-time canonical commutators with $\pi = \dot{\phi}$:
$$[\phi(\mathbf{x}), \phi(\mathbf{y})]_t = 0$$ ✓

More generally, even for $t \neq t'$ with $(x-y)^2 < 0$:

The integral oscillates and cancels (no frame makes $x$ and $y$ simultaneous consistently).

Result: $[\hat{\phi}(x), \hat{\phi}(y)] = 0$ for $(x-y)^2 < 0$ (**microcausality**).

**Answer:** Spacelike separated fields commute: $[\hat{\phi}(x), \hat{\phi}(y)] = 0$ for $(x-y)^2 < 0$ (causality preserved).

## Feynman Propagator

The time-ordered product $T\hat{\phi}(x)\hat{\phi}(y)$ defines the Feynman propagator, crucial for perturbation theory.

### Example 2: Feynman Propagator Definition

Define and calculate the Feynman propagator for scalar fields.

**Solution:**

**Time-ordering operator**:
$$T[\hat{\phi}(x)\hat{\phi}(y)] = \begin{cases}
\hat{\phi}(x)\hat{\phi}(y) & t_x > t_y \\
\hat{\phi}(y)\hat{\phi}(x) & t_y > t_x
\end{cases}$$

**Feynman propagator**:
$$D_F(x - y) = \langle 0|T\hat{\phi}(x)\hat{\phi}(y)|0\rangle$$

Substituting mode expansion:

$$D_F(x - y) = \int\frac{d^3k}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{2\omega_k}\left[\theta(t_x - t_y)e^{-ik \cdot (x-y)} + \theta(t_y - t_x)e^{-ik \cdot (y-x)}\right]$$

where $k^0 = \omega_k$ (on-shell).

**Momentum space** (Fourier transform):

$$\tilde{D}_F(p) = \int d^4x \, e^{ip \cdot x}D_F(x) = \frac{i}{p^2 - m^2 + i\epsilon}$$

where $i\epsilon$ ($\epsilon \to 0^+$) prescription shifts poles off real axis.

This form is fundamental for Feynman diagrams—internal propagators carry factor $i/(p^2 - m^2 + i\epsilon)$.

**Answer:** Feynman propagator $\tilde{D}_F(p) = \frac{i}{p^2 - m^2 + i\epsilon}$ (time-ordered Green's function).

### Example 3: Retarded and Advanced Propagators

Define retarded and advanced propagators and their physical interpretation.

**Solution:**

**Retarded propagator** (causal response):
$$D_R(x - y) = \theta(t_x - t_y)\langle 0|[\hat{\phi}(x), \hat{\phi}(y)]|0\rangle$$

Only propagates forward in time ($t_x > t_y$). Used for:
- Classical field response to sources
- Causal Green's functions
- Real-time evolution

**Advanced propagator**:
$$D_A(x - y) = -\theta(t_y - t_x)\langle 0|[\hat{\phi}(x), \hat{\phi}(y)]|0\rangle$$

Only propagates backward in time ($t_y > t_x$).

**Relationship**:
$$D_F = \theta(t_x - t_y)D_+ + \theta(t_y - t_x)D_-$$

where $D_\pm$ are positive/negative frequency Wightman functions.

**Physical interpretation**:
- $D_R$: Response at $x$ due to source at $y$ (classical causality)
- $D_F$: Propagation of virtual particles (quantum processes)
- $D_F$ allows both forward and backward time propagation (Feynman interpretation: antiparticles as particles going backward)

**Answer:** Retarded $D_R$ (forward time only), Feynman $D_F$ (both directions, virtual particles).

## Fock Space Structure

### Example 4: Complete Fock Space Construction

Build the full Fock space with creation and annihilation operators.

**Solution:**

**Fock space**: Direct sum over particle number sectors.

$$\mathcal{F} = \mathcal{F}_0 \oplus \mathcal{F}_1 \oplus \mathcal{F}_2 \oplus \cdots$$

**Zero-particle sector** $\mathcal{F}_0 = \mathbb{C}|0\rangle$:
- Vacuum state: $\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k}}|0\rangle = 0$ for all $\mathbf{k}$

**One-particle sector** $\mathcal{F}_1$:
- States: $|\mathbf{k}\rangle = \hat{a}^\dagger_{\mathbf{k}}|0\rangle$
- Hilbert space: $L^2(\mathbb{R}^3, d^3k)$ (square-integrable momentum wavefunctions)

**Two-particle sector** $\mathcal{F}_2$:
- States: $|\mathbf{k}_1, \mathbf{k}_2\rangle = \hat{a}^\dagger_{\mathbf{k}_1}\hat{a}^\dagger_{\mathbf{k}_2}|0\rangle$
- Bosons: Symmetric under exchange
- Fermions: Antisymmetric under exchange

**General n-particle sector**:
$$\mathcal{F}_n = \text{Sym}^n(\mathcal{H}_1) \quad \text{(bosons)}$$
$$\mathcal{F}_n = \text{Antisym}^n(\mathcal{H}_1) \quad \text{(fermions)}$$

**Completeness**: Any state can be expanded as:
$$|\psi\rangle = \sum_{n=0}^\infty\int d^3k_1 \cdots d^3k_n \, \psi_n(k_1, \ldots, k_n)|\mathbf{k}_1, \ldots, \mathbf{k}_n\rangle$$

**Answer:** Fock space $\mathcal{F} = \bigoplus_{n=0}^\infty \mathcal{F}_n$ (direct sum over all particle numbers).

### Example 5: Number Operator and Occupation

Define number operators and their properties.

**Solution:**

**Total number operator**:
$$\hat{N} = \sum_{\mathbf{k}}\hat{N}_{\mathbf{k}} = \sum_{\mathbf{k}}\hat{a}^\dagger_{\mathbf{k}}\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k}}$$

Properties:
- $\hat{N}|0\rangle = 0$
- $\hat{N}|\mathbf{k}_1, \ldots, \mathbf{k}_n\rangle = n|\mathbf{k}_1, \ldots, \mathbf{k}_n\rangle$
- $[\hat{N}, \hat{H}] = 0$ (number conserved in free theory)

**Occupation number**:
$$\langle n_{\mathbf{k}}\rangle = \langle\psi|\hat{N}_{\mathbf{k}}|\psi\rangle$$

For bosons: $n_{\mathbf{k}} = 0, 1, 2, 3, \ldots$ (unbounded).

For fermions: $n_{\mathbf{k}} = 0, 1$ only (Pauli exclusion).

**Statistics**:
- Bosons: $\langle n\rangle = \frac{1}{e^{\beta(\omega - \mu)} - 1}$ (Bose-Einstein at temperature $T = 1/\beta$)
- Fermions: $\langle n\rangle = \frac{1}{e^{\beta(\omega - \mu)} + 1}$ (Fermi-Dirac)

**Answer:** Number operator $\hat{N} = \sum_{\mathbf{k}}\hat{a}^\dagger_{\mathbf{k}}\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k}}$ counts total particles, eigenvalues $n = 0, 1, 2, \ldots$ (bosons) or $0, 1$ (fermions).

## Källén-Lehmann Representation

### Example 6: Spectral Representation of Propagator

Derive the Källén-Lehmann representation for the propagator.

**Solution:**

Insert complete set of energy eigenstates between field operators:

$$D_F(x - y) = \langle 0|T\hat{\phi}(x)\hat{\phi}(y)|0\rangle = \sum_n\langle 0|\hat{\phi}(x)|n\rangle\langle n|\hat{\phi}(y)|0\rangle\theta(t_x - t_y) + \cdots$$

In momentum space:

$$\tilde{D}_F(p) = \int_0^\infty d\mu^2 \frac{\rho(\mu^2)}{p^2 - \mu^2 + i\epsilon}$$

where **spectral density**:
$$\rho(\mu^2) = \sum_n(2\pi)\delta(p^2 - M_n^2)|\langle 0|\hat{\phi}(0)|n\rangle|^2$$

**Physical interpretation**:
- $\rho(\mu^2) \geq 0$ (positivity)
- Peaks at $\mu^2 = M_n^2$ correspond to physical particle masses
- Single-particle: $\rho(\mu^2) \approx Z\delta(\mu^2 - m^2) + \text{continuum}$
- Field strength: $Z = |\langle 0|\hat{\phi}|1p\rangle|^2$

Propagator poles → particle spectrum!

**Answer:** Källén-Lehmann: $\tilde{D}_F(p) = \int d\mu^2 \frac{\rho(\mu^2)}{p^2 - \mu^2 + i\epsilon}$ (spectral representation, $\rho \geq 0$).

### Example 7: Coherent States for Photons

Construct coherent states in Fock space.

**Solution:**

**Coherent state**: Eigenstate of annihilation operator.

$$\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k}, \lambda}|\alpha\rangle = \alpha_{\mathbf{k}, \lambda}|\alpha\rangle$$

where $\alpha_{\mathbf{k}, \lambda} \in \mathbb{C}$.

Explicit form:
$$|\alpha\rangle = \exp\left(\sum_{\mathbf{k}, \lambda}\alpha_{\mathbf{k}, \lambda}\hat{a}^\dagger_{\mathbf{k}, \lambda}\right)|0\rangle$$

$$= \exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\mathbf{k}, \lambda}|\alpha_{\mathbf{k}, \lambda}|^2\right)\prod_{\mathbf{k}, \lambda}\sum_{n=0}^\infty\frac{\alpha_{\mathbf{k}, \lambda}^n}{\sqrt{n!}}|n_{\mathbf{k}, \lambda}\rangle$$

Properties:
- Minimum uncertainty state (quantum-classical transition)
- Mean photon number: $\langle n_{\mathbf{k}, \lambda}\rangle = |\alpha_{\mathbf{k}, \lambda}|^2$
- Poisson distribution: $P(n) = e^{-|\alpha|^2}|\alpha|^{2n}/n!$
- Laser light ≈ coherent state (large $|\alpha|$)

Classical limit: $|\alpha| \to \infty$, relative fluctuations $\sim 1/|\alpha| \to 0$.

**Answer:** Coherent state $|\alpha\rangle = e^{\sum \alpha\hat{a}^\dagger}|0\rangle$ (eigenstate of $\hat{a}$, closest to classical).

Spacelike separated measurements don't interfere
Compatible with special relativity (no FTL signals)
Timelike: $[\phi, \phi] \neq 0$ (causally connected)
Essential for QFT consistency

Feynman propagator: $\tilde{D}_F(p) = \frac{i}{p^2 - m^2 + i\epsilon}$

Variable particle number (creation/annihilation)
Infinite-dimensional (unbounded from above)
Essential for relativistic QFT

Fundamental constraint on propagators

Closest quantum analog to classical oscillation
Laser light ≈ coherent state (large $|\alpha|$)
