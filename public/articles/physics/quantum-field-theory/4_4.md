# Quantum Field Theory Topic 4.4: Gauge Fixing and Faddeev-Popov Ghosts

Gauge theories—QED, QCD, the electroweak theory—possess **gauge redundancy**: infinitely many field configurations $A_\mu$ describe the same physical state, related by gauge transformations $A_\mu \to A_\mu + \partial_\mu\chi$. This redundancy creates a severe problem for path integrals: integrating over all gauge-equivalent configurations overcounts enormously, giving an infinite result for $Z$. The solution is **gauge fixing**—imposing a condition $G(A) = 0$ that picks out one representative from each gauge equivalence class. But naive gauge fixing breaks gauge invariance and ruins Ward identities. The **Faddeev-Popov procedure** provides the systematic solution: insert a clever identity $1 = \int \mathcal{D}\alpha \, \delta(G(A^\alpha))\det(\delta G/\delta\alpha)$ into the path integral, where $A^\alpha$ denotes the gauge-transformed field. The Jacobian determinant $\det(\delta G/\delta\alpha)$ becomes the source of **Faddeev-Popov ghosts**.

Ghosts are unphysical scalar fields obeying **Grassmann statistics**—they violate the spin-statistics theorem by being spin-0 fermions. In **Abelian theories** (QED), ghosts decouple from physical processes. But in **non-Abelian theories** (QCD, electroweak), ghosts couple to gauge bosons and are essential for maintaining unitarity. Ghost loops cancel unphysical polarizations of gauge bosons, ensuring that only transverse photon states contribute to S-matrix elements. Without ghosts, non-Abelian gauge theories would give nonsensical predictions—negative probabilities, violation of unitarity. The Faddeev-Popov determinant, reexpressed as a Grassmann path integral over ghost fields, becomes part of the Lagrangian with its own Feynman rules. This elegant formalism makes gauge fixing systematic and preserves gauge invariance in physical observables.

This article develops the Faddeev-Popov procedure from gauge redundancy through determinant computation to ghost Feynman rules. We'll understand gauge redundancy in path integrals, implement gauge fixing conditions, derive the Faddeev-Popov determinant, express it as a ghost path integral, examine ghosts in QED (decoupling), understand ghosts in QCD (essential coupling), write ghost Feynman rules, and see how the BRST symmetry elegantly encodes gauge invariance with ghosts.

## Gauge Redundancy and Gauge Fixing

Path integrals over gauge fields overcount due to gauge equivalence—we must fix the gauge to obtain sensible results.

### Example 1: Gauge Redundancy Problem

Explain why gauge theories need gauge fixing in path integrals.

**Solution:**

**Gauge transformation**: $A_\mu(x) \to A_\mu^\alpha(x) = A_\mu(x) + \frac{1}{e}\partial_\mu\alpha(x)$

Different $A_\mu$ related by gauge transformation describe **same physical state**.

**Path integral**:
$$Z = \int \mathcal{D}A_\mu \, e^{iS[A]}$$

**Problem**: Integrate over all $A_\mu$, including gauge-equivalent configurations.

Volume of gauge group = $\int \mathcal{D}\alpha$ (infinite!).

$$Z = \int \mathcal{D}A \, e^{iS[A]} = \underbrace{\int \mathcal{D}\alpha}_{\infty} \times \int \mathcal{D}A_{\text{phys}} \, e^{iS[A_{\text{phys}}]}$$

**Result**: $Z = \infty$ (not well-defined).

**Solution**: **Gauge fixing**—impose condition $G(A) = 0$ selecting one representative per equivalence class.

Examples:
- **Lorenz gauge**: $\partial^\mu A_\mu = 0$
- **Coulomb gauge**: $\nabla \cdot \mathbf{A} = 0$
- **Axial gauge**: $A^3 = 0$

**Goal**: Modify path integral to integrate only over inequivalent configurations.

$$Z_{\text{fixed}} = \int_{\substack{\mathcal{D}A \\ G(A)=0}} e^{iS[A]}$$

But implementing this carefully requires Faddeev-Popov.

**Answer:** Gauge redundancy: Infinitely many $A_\mu$ describe same physics, path integral overcounts, needs gauge fixing.

### Example 2: Faddeev-Popov Identity

Derive the Faddeev-Popov identity.

**Solution:**

**Goal**: Insert $1$ into path integral in clever way.

For any gauge transformation $A \to A^\alpha$:
$$1 = \int \mathcal{D}\alpha \, \delta(G(A^\alpha))$$

where $\delta(G(A^\alpha))$ is functional delta function.

**Jacobian**: Change of variables requires Jacobian.
$$\delta(G(A^\alpha)) = \frac{1}{\det M}\delta(G(A))$$

where **Faddeev-Popov operator**:
$$M(x, y) = \frac{\delta G(A^\alpha)(x)}{\delta\alpha(y)}\bigg|_{\alpha=0}$$

**Faddeev-Popov identity**:
$$1 = \Delta_{\text{FP}}[A]\int \mathcal{D}\alpha \, \delta(G(A^\alpha))$$

where $\Delta_{\text{FP}}[A] = \det M$ (Faddeev-Popov determinant).

**Path integral**:
$$Z = \int \mathcal{D}A \, e^{iS[A]}$$

$$= \int \mathcal{D}A \, \Delta_{\text{FP}}[A]\left[\int \mathcal{D}\alpha \, \delta(G(A^\alpha))\right] e^{iS[A]}$$

Change variables: $A \to A^\alpha$ inside integral.
$$= \int \mathcal{D}\alpha \int \mathcal{D}A \, \Delta_{\text{FP}}[A]\delta(G(A))e^{iS[A]}$$

**Gauge invariance**: $S[A^\alpha] = S[A]$ and $\Delta_{\text{FP}}[A^\alpha] = \Delta_{\text{FP}}[A]$ (can show).

$$= \underbrace{\int \mathcal{D}\alpha}_{\text{Vol(gauge group)}} \times \int \mathcal{D}A \, \Delta_{\text{FP}}[A]\delta(G(A))e^{iS[A]}$$

**Gauge-fixed path integral**:
$$Z = \text{Vol(gauge group)} \times \int \mathcal{D}A \, \Delta_{\text{FP}}[A]\delta(G(A))e^{iS[A]}$$

Factor out infinite volume (cancels in observables).

**Answer:** Faddeev-Popov: $Z = \int \mathcal{D}A \, \Delta_{\text{FP}}[A]\delta(G(A))e^{iS}$ with $\Delta_{\text{FP}} = \det M$.

## Faddeev-Popov Determinant and Ghosts

The Faddeev-Popov determinant, when expressed as a Grassmann path integral, introduces ghost fields.

### Example 3: Ghost Field Path Integral

Express the Faddeev-Popov determinant as a ghost integral.

**Solution:**

**Determinant**: $\Delta_{\text{FP}} = \det M$ where $M$ is differential operator.

**Grassmann trick**: For Grassmann variables $c, \bar{c}$:
$$\det M = \int \mathcal{D}\bar{c}\mathcal{D}c \, e^{i\int \bar{c}(x)M(x,y)c(y)}$$

(Gaussian Grassmann integral gives determinant, not inverse!)

**Ghost action**:
$$S_{\text{ghost}} = \int d^4x \, \bar{c}(x)M(x)c(x)$$

where $M = \frac{\delta G(A^\alpha)}{\delta\alpha}\big|_{\alpha=0}$ (Faddeev-Popov operator).

**Full path integral**:
$$Z = \int \mathcal{D}A\mathcal{D}\bar{c}\mathcal{D}c \, \delta(G(A))e^{i[S[A] + S_{\text{ghost}}]}$$

**Ghost fields** $c(x), \bar{c}(x)$:
- Grassmann (anticommuting) despite being scalars
- Violate spin-statistics theorem (unphysical)
- Only appear in loops (internal lines)
- Cancel unphysical gauge boson polarizations

**Gauge fixing term**: Replace $\delta(G(A))$ with Gaussian:
$$\delta(G(A)) \to e^{-\frac{i}{2\xi}\int[G(A)]^2}$$

(Gaussian average over gauge-fixing condition, parameter $\xi$.)

**Answer:** Ghost path integral: $\Delta_{\text{FP}} = \int \mathcal{D}\bar{c}\mathcal{D}c \, e^{iS_{\text{ghost}}}$ (Grassmann, unphysical scalars).

### Example 4: QED Ghosts Decouple

Show that ghosts decouple in Abelian theories like QED.

**Solution:**

**QED gauge transformation**: $A_\mu \to A_\mu + \partial_\mu\alpha$

**Lorenz gauge**: $G(A) = \partial^\mu A_\mu = 0$

**Faddeev-Popov operator**:
$$M = \frac{\delta G(A^\alpha)}{\delta\alpha} = \frac{\delta(\partial^\mu(A_\mu + \partial_\mu\alpha))}{\delta\alpha} = \partial^\mu\partial_\mu = \Box$$

Independent of $A_\mu$! (Abelian structure.)

**Ghost action**:
$$S_{\text{ghost}} = \int d^4x \, \bar{c}(x)\Box c(x)$$

**No coupling to photons**—ghosts are free!

**Ghost propagator**:
$$D_c(p) = \frac{i}{p^2 + i\epsilon}$$

(Scalar propagator.)

**Physical observables**: Ghosts never appear in external lines (unphysical), and in QED they don't couple to photons, so they don't contribute to any diagrams.

**Result**: Ghosts decouple in QED—can be ignored!

**Contrast with non-Abelian**: In QCD, ghosts couple to gluons (essential).

**Answer:** QED ghosts: Faddeev-Popov operator $M = \Box$ independent of $A_\mu$, ghosts decouple (free, don't interact).

### Example 5: Non-Abelian Ghosts

Analyze ghost coupling in non-Abelian gauge theories.

**Solution:**

**Yang-Mills theory**: $SU(N)$ gauge group.

**Field strength**: $F_{\mu\nu}^a = \partial_\mu A_\nu^a - \partial_\nu A_\mu^a + gf^{abc}A_\mu^b A_\nu^c$

(Non-linear in $A$ due to $f^{abc}$ structure constants.)

**Gauge transformation**: $A_\mu^a \to A_\mu^a + D_\mu^{ab}\alpha^b$

where $D_\mu^{ab} = \delta^{ab}\partial_\mu + gf^{abc}A_\mu^c$ (covariant derivative).

**Gauge condition** (Lorenz): $G^a(A) = \partial^\mu A_\mu^a = 0$

**Faddeev-Popov operator**:
$$M^{ab} = \frac{\delta G^a(A^{\alpha})}{\delta\alpha^b} = \partial^\mu D_\mu^{ab} = \delta^{ab}\Box + gf^{abc}\partial^\mu A_\mu^c$$

**Depends on $A_\mu$**—ghosts couple to gluons!

**Ghost action**:
$$S_{\text{ghost}} = \int d^4x \, \bar{c}^a(\partial^\mu D_\mu^{ab})c^b$$

$$= \int d^4x \, \bar{c}^a[\delta^{ab}\Box + gf^{abc}\partial^\mu A_\mu^c]c^b$$

**Ghost-gluon vertex**: $gf^{abc}\partial^\mu$ (momentum-dependent three-point vertex).

**Essential for unitarity**: Ghost loops cancel unphysical gluon polarizations, ensuring S-matrix unitarity.

Without ghosts: QCD would give nonsense (negative probabilities).

**Answer:** Non-Abelian ghosts: Couple to gauge bosons via $gf^{abc}A_\mu^c$ term, essential for unitarity.

## Ghost Feynman Rules and BRST Symmetry

### Example 6: Ghost Feynman Rules

Write the Feynman rules for ghosts in Yang-Mills theory.

**Solution:**

**Ghost propagator** (Lorenz gauge):
$$D_c^{ab}(p) = \frac{i\delta^{ab}}{p^2 + i\epsilon}$$

(Scalar propagator in adjoint representation.)

**Ghost-gluon vertex**:

Vertex: $\bar{c}^a$, $A_\mu^b$, $c^c$ meeting.

Factor: $gf^{abc}p^\mu$ where $p$ is ghost momentum flowing into vertex.

Graphically:
```
  c (dotted)
   |
   |  gf^abc p^μ
   •
  /|\
 / | \
A_μ (wavy)
```

**Ghost-ghost-gluon coupling**: Purely non-Abelian (from $f^{abc}$).

**In diagrams**:
- Ghosts only on internal lines (unphysical, can't be produced)
- Ghost loops contribute $(-1)$ (Grassmann statistics)
- Cancel unphysical gauge boson polarizations in loops

**Practical use**: Compute ghost contributions when calculating gluon self-energy, vertex corrections, etc.

**Answer:** Ghost rules: Propagator $\frac{i\delta^{ab}}{p^2}$, vertex $gf^{abc}p^\mu$ (ghost-ghost-gluon), internal lines only.

### Example 7: BRST Symmetry

Introduce BRST symmetry as the underlying gauge invariance with ghosts.

**Solution:**

**Problem**: Gauge fixing breaks explicit gauge invariance.

**Solution**: Gauge invariance survives as **BRST symmetry** (Becchi-Rouet-Stora-Tyutin).

**BRST transformation** (infinitesimal parameter $\epsilon$, Grassmann):

$$\delta_{\text{BRST}}A_\mu^a = \epsilon D_\mu^{ab}c^b$$

$$\delta_{\text{BRST}}c^a = -\frac{g}{2}\epsilon f^{abc}c^b c^c$$

$$\delta_{\text{BRST}}\bar{c}^a = \epsilon \frac{1}{\xi}(\partial^\mu A_\mu^a)$$

(Ghosts transform non-linearly.)

**Key property**: $\delta_{\text{BRST}}^2 = 0$ (nilpotent).

**Full Lagrangian**:
$$\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{YM}} + \mathcal{L}_{\text{gauge-fix}} + \mathcal{L}_{\text{ghost}}$$

is **BRST invariant**: $\delta_{\text{BRST}}\mathcal{L} = 0$

**Physical states**: BRST cohomology (states annihilated by BRST charge, modulo exact states).

Physical observables: BRST-invariant operators.

**Significance**:
- Elegant formulation of gauge theories with gauge fixing
- Ghosts arise naturally from BRST transformations
- Ward identities → BRST Ward identities
- Foundation for string field theory, topological field theories

**Answer:** BRST symmetry: Nilpotent $\delta_{\text{BRST}}^2 = 0$, encodes gauge invariance with ghosts, physical states = BRST cohomology.
