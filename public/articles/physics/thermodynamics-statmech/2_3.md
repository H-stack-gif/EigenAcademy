# Article 2.3: Thermodynamic Processes for Ideal Gases

Ideal gases provide the simplest arena for applying the first law. With the equation of state $PV = nRT$ and internal energy depending only on temperature, we can calculate exact values for work, heat, and energy changes in various processes. These idealized processes—isothermal, adiabatic, isobaric, isochoric—serve as building blocks for understanding real thermodynamic cycles like those in engines and refrigerators.

Each process type imposes a different constraint, leading to different mathematical descriptions and physical behaviors. Isothermal processes maintain constant temperature through continuous heat exchange. Adiabatic processes occur without heat transfer, with all work changing internal energy directly. Isobaric and isochoric processes maintain constant pressure or volume, respectively. Understanding these processes requires integrating the first law with appropriate constraints.

This article systematically develops work and heat calculations for each process type, exploring both the mathematical techniques (integration with constraints, coupled differential equations) and physical insights. We'll also examine free expansion—a highly irreversible process where a gas expands into vacuum, doing no work despite changing state dramatically.

## Isothermal Processes ($T = \text{const}$)

For **isothermal** process with ideal gas, temperature is constant.

**Key relations**:

From ideal gas law at constant $T$:

$$PV = nRT = \text{const} \implies P_1 V_1 = P_2 V_2$$

**Internal energy change**:

For ideal gas, $U = U(T)$ only (Joule's law):

$$\Delta U = 0$$

**First law**:

$$\Delta U = Q - W = 0 \implies Q = W$$

All heat absorbed converts to work.

**Work calculation**:

$$W = \int_{V_1}^{V_2} P dV = \int_{V_1}^{V_2} \frac{nRT}{V} dV = nRT \ln\left(\frac{V_2}{V_1}\right)$$

Or equivalently:

$$W = nRT \ln\left(\frac{P_1}{P_2}\right) = -nRT \ln\left(\frac{P_2}{P_1}\right)$$

**Heat transferred**:

$$Q = W = nRT \ln\left(\frac{V_2}{V_1}\right)$$

**Physical interpretation**: System must be in thermal contact with reservoir at temperature $T$. As gas expands and cools slightly, heat flows in to maintain constant temperature.

## Adiabatic Processes ($Q = 0$)

**Adiabatic** process has no heat transfer: $Q = 0$. System is thermally isolated.

**Key relations**:

From first law with $Q = 0$:

$$dU = -P dV$$

For ideal gas: $dU = nC_V dT$ and $P = nRT/V$:

$$nC_V dT = -\frac{nRT}{V} dV$$

$$\frac{dT}{T} = -\frac{R}{C_V} \frac{dV}{V} = -(\gamma - 1)\frac{dV}{V}$$

where $\gamma = C_P/C_V$ and we used $C_P - C_V = nR$.

Integrating:

$$\ln T = -(\gamma - 1) \ln V + \text{const}$$

$$TV^{\gamma-1} = \text{const}$$

Using $T = PV/(nR)$:

$$PV^\gamma = \text{const}$$

Also: $T^\gamma P^{1-\gamma} = \text{const}$

**Work calculation**:

$$W = -\Delta U = -nC_V(T_2 - T_1) = nC_V(T_1 - T_2)$$

Or using $PV^\gamma = P_1 V_1^\gamma = P_2 V_2^\gamma$:

$$W = \frac{P_1 V_1 - P_2 V_2}{\gamma - 1} = \frac{nR(T_1 - T_2)}{\gamma - 1}$$

**Physical interpretation**: Expanding gas does work by decreasing internal energy—temperature drops. Compressing gas increases temperature. All energy exchange is through work.

## Isobaric Processes ($P = \text{const}$)

**Isobaric** process maintains constant pressure.

**Key relations**:

From ideal gas at constant $P$:

$$\frac{V}{T} = \frac{nR}{P} = \text{const} \implies \frac{V_1}{T_1} = \frac{V_2}{T_2}$$

**Work**:

$$W = P(V_2 - V_1) = P\Delta V = nR(T_2 - T_1) = nR\Delta T$$

**Heat transferred**:

$$Q = nC_P \Delta T$$

**Internal energy change**:

$$\Delta U = nC_V \Delta T$$

From first law: $Q = \Delta U + W$ gives $nC_P \Delta T = nC_V \Delta T + nR\Delta T$, confirming $C_P = C_V + nR$.

**Physical interpretation**: Heating at constant pressure causes expansion. Energy increases temperature and does expansion work.

## Isochoric Processes ($V = \text{const}$)

**Isochoric** (or isovolumetric) maintains constant volume.

**Key relations**:

From ideal gas at constant $V$:

$$\frac{P}{T} = \frac{nR}{V} = \text{const} \implies \frac{P_1}{T_1} = \frac{P_2}{T_2}$$

**Work**:

$$W = \int P dV = 0$$

No work since $dV = 0$.

**Heat transferred**:

$$Q = nC_V \Delta T$$

**Internal energy change**:

$$\Delta U = Q = nC_V \Delta T$$

From first law: all heat increases internal energy.

**Physical interpretation**: Rigid container prevents expansion. All heat increases molecular kinetic energy (temperature).

## Free Expansion (Joule Expansion)

**Free expansion**: Gas expands into evacuated chamber ($P_{ext} = 0$).

**Characteristics**:
- Adiabatic: $Q = 0$ (isolated)
- No work: $W = 0$ (expanding into vacuum)
- First law: $\Delta U = 0$

For **ideal gas**: $U = U(T)$ only, so $\Delta T = 0$. Temperature doesn't change!

**Physical interpretation**: Highly irreversible process. Entropy increases significantly despite no energy transfer. Real gases cool slightly (Joule-Thomson effect) due to intermolecular forces.

## Comparison of Processes

| Process | Constraint | $\Delta U$ | $W$ | $Q$ |
|---------|-----------|-----------|-----|-----|
| Isothermal | $T = \text{const}$ | $0$ | $nRT\ln(V_2/V_1)$ | $W$ |
| Adiabatic | $Q = 0$ | $nC_V\Delta T$ | $-\Delta U$ | $0$ |
| Isobaric | $P = \text{const}$ | $nC_V\Delta T$ | $nR\Delta T$ | $nC_P\Delta T$ |
| Isochoric | $V = \text{const}$ | $nC_V\Delta T$ | $0$ | $\Delta U$ |

## Example 1: Isothermal Process Calculation

Two moles of ideal gas at $T = 350$ K expand isothermally from $P_1 = 5.0$ atm to $P_2 = 2.0$ atm. Calculate $Q$, $W$, and $\Delta U$.

**Solution:**

Isothermal ideal gas: $\Delta U = 0$.

Work:

$$W = nRT \ln\left(\frac{P_1}{P_2}\right) = (2)(8.314)(350) \ln\left(\frac{5.0}{2.0}\right)$$

$$= 5820 \times \ln(2.5) = 5820 \times 0.916 = 5331 \text{ J} = 5.33 \text{ kJ}$$

Heat:

$$Q = W = 5.33 \text{ kJ}$$

**Answer:** $Q = 5.33$ kJ, $W = 5.33$ kJ, $\Delta U = 0$

## Example 2: Adiabatic Expansion

One mole of helium ($\gamma = 5/3$, $C_V = \frac{3}{2}R$) expands adiabatically from $(P_1, V_1, T_1) = (3.0 \text{ atm}, 10 \text{ L}, 400 \text{ K})$ to $V_2 = 20$ L. Find:
(a) $T_2$
(b) $P_2$
(c) $W$

**Solution:**

(a) From $TV^{\gamma-1} = \text{const}$:

$$T_2 = T_1\left(\frac{V_1}{V_2}\right)^{\gamma-1} = 400\left(\frac{10}{20}\right)^{2/3} = 400(0.5)^{0.667}$$

$$= 400 \times 0.630 = 252 \text{ K}$$

(b) From $PV^\gamma = \text{const}$:

$$P_2 = P_1\left(\frac{V_1}{V_2}\right)^\gamma = 3.0\left(\frac{10}{20}\right)^{5/3} = 3.0(0.5)^{1.667}$$

$$= 3.0 \times 0.315 = 0.945 \text{ atm}$$

(c) Work:

$$W = nC_V(T_1 - T_2) = (1)\left(\frac{3}{2} \times 8.314\right)(400 - 252)$$

$$= 12.471 \times 148 = 1846 \text{ J} = 1.85 \text{ kJ}$$

**Physical check**: Expanding gas does positive work, cooling from 400 K to 252 K.

**Answer:** (a) 252 K, (b) 0.945 atm, (c) 1.85 kJ

## Example 3: Isobaric vs. Isochoric Heating

Two moles of nitrogen ($C_V = \frac{5}{2}R$, $C_P = \frac{7}{2}R$) are heated from $300$ K to $400$ K. Compare:
(a) Isobaric process
(b) Isochoric process

**Solution:**

**(a) Isobaric**:

$$Q = nC_P\Delta T = (2)\left(\frac{7}{2} \times 8.314\right)(100) = 5820 \text{ J}$$

$$W = nR\Delta T = (2)(8.314)(100) = 1663 \text{ J}$$

$$\Delta U = nC_V\Delta T = (2)\left(\frac{5}{2} \times 8.314\right)(100) = 4157 \text{ J}$$

Check: $Q = \Delta U + W = 4157 + 1663 = 5820$ ✓

**(b) Isochoric**:

$$Q = nC_V\Delta T = 4157 \text{ J}$$

$$W = 0$$

$$\Delta U = 4157 \text{ J}$$

**Comparison**: Same $\Delta U$ (state function), but isobaric requires more heat (5820 vs 4157 J) because some energy does expansion work.

**Answer:** (a) $Q = 5.82$ kJ, $W = 1.66$ kJ; (b) $Q = 4.16$ kJ, $W = 0$

## Example 4: Free Expansion

One mole of ideal gas at $T_1 = 300$ K occupies $V_1 = 5.0$ L. It expands freely into evacuated chamber reaching $V_2 = 15.0$ L. Find all thermodynamic quantities.

**Solution:**

Free expansion: $P_{ext} = 0$ and adiabatic ($Q = 0$).

**Work**:

$$W = \int P_{ext} dV = 0$$

**Heat**:

$$Q = 0$$

**Internal energy**:

$$\Delta U = Q - W = 0$$

**Temperature**: For ideal gas with $\Delta U = 0$:

$$T_2 = T_1 = 300 \text{ K}$$

**Pressure**: From ideal gas law:

$$P_2 = \frac{nRT_2}{V_2} = \frac{RT}{V_2} = \frac{RT}{V_1} \cdot \frac{V_1}{V_2} = P_1 \cdot \frac{5.0}{15.0} = \frac{P_1}{3}$$

**Physical interpretation**: Highly irreversible—gas distributes throughout available space with no energy change but significant entropy increase.

**Answer:** $Q = 0$, $W = 0$, $\Delta U = 0$, $T_2 = 300$ K, $P_2 = P_1/3$

## Example 5: Diesel Cycle Step (Adiabatic Compression)

Air ($\gamma = 1.4$) is compressed adiabatically from $(T_1, P_1) = (300 \text{ K}, 1.0 \text{ atm})$ with compression ratio $V_1/V_2 = 15$. Find $T_2$ and $P_2$.

**Solution:**

**Temperature**:

$$T_2 = T_1\left(\frac{V_1}{V_2}\right)^{\gamma-1} = 300(15)^{0.4} = 300 \times 2.954 = 886 \text{ K}$$

**Pressure**:

$$P_2 = P_1\left(\frac{V_1}{V_2}\right)^\gamma = 1.0(15)^{1.4} = 1.0 \times 44.3 = 44.3 \text{ atm}$$

**Physical interpretation**: Diesel engines use high compression ratios (15:1 or higher) to achieve high temperatures ($886$ K $= 613°\text{C}$) that ignite fuel without spark plugs.

**Answer:** $T_2 = 886$ K, $P_2 = 44.3$ atm

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: C** - Isothermal ideal gas: $\Delta U = 0$ (and $Q = W \neq 0$).

**Problem 2: C** - Adiabatic: $PV^\gamma = \text{const}$ (also $TV^{\gamma-1} = \text{const}$).

**Problem 3: D** - Isobaric: $W = P\Delta V = nR\Delta T$.

**Problem 4: B** - Isochoric: $W = 0$ (constant volume, no expansion).

**Problem 5: C** - Free expansion ideal gas: $\Delta U = 0 \Rightarrow T$ unchanged.
