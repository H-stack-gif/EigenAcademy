# Article 3.4: Entropy Changes in Various Processes

Understanding entropy abstractly is one thing—calculating it for real processes is another. How does entropy change during isothermal expansion? Adiabatic compression? Phase transitions? Mixing of gases? Each process type has characteristic entropy behavior determined by the interplay of heat transfer, temperature changes, and volume changes.

For ideal gases, the entropy formula $\Delta S = nC_V\ln(T_f/T_i) + nR\ln(V_f/V_i)$ provides a starting point, but we must adapt it to specific constraints. Isothermal processes involve only volume changes. Reversible adiabatic processes have zero entropy change. Irreversible processes like free expansion increase entropy despite no heat transfer. Phase transitions have discontinuous entropy jumps at constant temperature.

This article systematically calculates entropy changes for all major process types, explores mixing entropy, examines phase transitions, and analyzes entropy generation in heat transfer. The mathematics involves logarithmic integration, multivariable entropy functions, and careful application of state function properties.

## Entropy Change for Ideal Gas Processes

General formula for ideal gas with $dU = nC_V dT$ and $PV = nRT$:

$$dS = \frac{dU + P dV}{T} = nC_V\frac{dT}{T} + nR\frac{dV}{V}$$

Integrating:

$$\boxed{\Delta S = nC_V\ln\frac{T_f}{T_i} + nR\ln\frac{V_f}{V_i}}$$

**Alternative forms**:

Using $P$ and $V$:
$$\Delta S = nC_V\ln\frac{P_f V_f}{P_i V_i} + nR\ln\frac{V_f}{V_i} = n\left(C_V\ln\frac{P_f}{P_i} + C_P\ln\frac{V_f}{V_i}\right)$$

Using $T$ and $P$:
$$\Delta S = nC_P\ln\frac{T_f}{T_i} - nR\ln\frac{P_f}{P_i}$$

## Isothermal Process ($T = \text{const}$)

For isothermal ideal gas expansion/compression:

$$\Delta S = nR\ln\frac{V_f}{V_i} = -nR\ln\frac{P_f}{P_i}$$

**For expansion** ($V_f > V_i$): $\Delta S > 0$ (entropy increases)

**For compression** ($V_f < V_i$): $\Delta S < 0$ (entropy decreases locally, but total including surroundings increases if irreversible)

Heat transferred:

$$Q = nRT\ln\frac{V_f}{V_i}$$

Check: $\Delta S = Q/T$ ✓

## Adiabatic Reversible Process ($Q = 0$, reversible)

For reversible adiabatic process:

$$dS = \frac{\delta Q_{rev}}{T} = \frac{0}{T} = 0$$

$$\boxed{\Delta S = 0}$$

**Isentropic process**: constant entropy.

This is why reversible adiabatic curves on $PV$ diagram are also constant-entropy curves.

Relations: $PV^\gamma = \text{const}$, $TV^{\gamma-1} = \text{const}$

## Free Expansion (Joule Expansion)

**Free expansion**: gas expands into vacuum. Irreversible, adiabatic ($Q = 0$), no work ($W = 0$).

For ideal gas: $\Delta U = 0 \Rightarrow \Delta T = 0$ (temperature unchanged).

Initial state: $(T, V_i)$
Final state: $(T, V_f)$ with $V_f > V_i$

Entropy change (using reversible isothermal path):

$$\boxed{\Delta S = nR\ln\frac{V_f}{V_i} > 0}$$

**Key point**: Entropy increases despite $Q = 0$! This is irreversible process in isolated system—entropy must increase.

## Mixing of Gases

Two ideal gases at same $T$ and $P$ but different volumes $V_1$ and $V_2$ separated by partition. Remove partition—gases mix.

**Entropy of mixing**:

Each gas expands from its volume to total volume $V_{total} = V_1 + V_2$:

$$\Delta S_1 = n_1 R\ln\frac{V_{total}}{V_1}$$

$$\Delta S_2 = n_2 R\ln\frac{V_{total}}{V_2}$$

$$\Delta S_{mix} = \Delta S_1 + \Delta S_2 = n_1 R\ln\frac{V_1 + V_2}{V_1} + n_2 R\ln\frac{V_1 + V_2}{V_2}$$

Using mole fractions $x_1 = n_1/(n_1 + n_2)$, $x_2 = n_2/(n_1 + n_2)$:

$$\boxed{\Delta S_{mix} = -nR(x_1\ln x_1 + x_2\ln x_2)}$$

where $n = n_1 + n_2$.

Since $0 < x_i < 1$: $\ln x_i < 0$, so $\Delta S_{mix} > 0$ always.

**General form** for $k$ gases:

$$\Delta S_{mix} = -nR\sum_{i=1}^k x_i\ln x_i$$

## Phase Transitions

During phase change at constant $T$ and $P$:

$$\Delta S = \frac{Q}{T} = \frac{nL}{T}$$

where $L$ is latent heat (fusion or vaporization).

**Melting** (solid → liquid):

$$\Delta S_{fus} = \frac{nL_{fus}}{T_m}$$

where $T_m$ is melting temperature.

**Vaporization** (liquid → gas):

$$\Delta S_{vap} = \frac{nL_{vap}}{T_b}$$

where $T_b$ is boiling temperature.

**Physical interpretation**: Phase transitions increase disorder:
- Solid → liquid: molecules gain translational freedom
- Liquid → gas: molecules gain complete freedom

Therefore: $\Delta S_{fus} > 0$ and $\Delta S_{vap} > 0$ always.

## Heat Transfer and Entropy Generation

When heat $Q$ transfers from hot object at $T_H$ to cold object at $T_C$:

$$\Delta S_{hot} = -\frac{Q}{T_H}$$

$$\Delta S_{cold} = +\frac{Q}{T_C}$$

$$\Delta S_{total} = Q\left(\frac{1}{T_C} - \frac{1}{T_H}\right) = Q\frac{T_H - T_C}{T_H T_C} > 0$$

Since $T_H > T_C$: entropy increases for irreversible heat transfer.

**Entropy generation**:

$$S_{gen} = Q\frac{T_H - T_C}{T_H T_C}$$

Larger $\Delta T$ produces more entropy generation (more irreversible).

## Example 1: Isothermal Expansion Entropy

Two moles of ideal gas expand isothermally at $T = 350$ K from $V_i = 15$ L to $V_f = 40$ L. Find $\Delta S$.

**Solution:**

Isothermal ideal gas:

$$\Delta S = nR\ln\frac{V_f}{V_i} = (2)(8.314)\ln\frac{40}{15}$$

$$= 16.628 \times \ln(2.667) = 16.628 \times 0.981 = 16.3 \text{ J/K}$$

**Physical interpretation**: Expansion increases gas disorder. Positive entropy change.

**Answer:** $\Delta S = 16.3$ J/K

---

## Example 2: Adiabatic Reversible Entropy

One mole helium ($\gamma = 5/3$) expands adiabatically and reversibly from $(T_i, V_i) = (400 \text{ K}, 10 \text{ L})$ to $(T_f, V_f) = (252 \text{ K}, 20 \text{ L})$. Find $\Delta S$.

**Solution:**

For reversible adiabatic process:

$$\Delta S = 0$$

(isentropic—constant entropy)

**Verification**: Check using general formula:

$$\Delta S = nC_V\ln\frac{T_f}{T_i} + nR\ln\frac{V_f}{V_i}$$

$$= (1)\left(\frac{3}{2} \times 8.314\right)\ln\frac{252}{400} + (1)(8.314)\ln\frac{20}{10}$$

$$= 12.471 \times \ln(0.63) + 8.314 \times \ln(2)$$

$$= 12.471 \times (-0.462) + 8.314 \times 0.693$$

$$= -5.76 + 5.76 = 0$$ ✓

**Answer:** $\Delta S = 0$

---

## Example 3: Free Expansion Entropy

One mole nitrogen at $T = 300$ K expands freely from $V_i = 8$ L to $V_f = 24$ L. Calculate:
(a) $\Delta T$
(b) $\Delta S$
(c) Interpret result

**Solution:**

(a) Free expansion of ideal gas: $\Delta U = 0 \Rightarrow \Delta T = 0$

$$T_f = T_i = 300 \text{ K}$$

(b) Entropy change (using isothermal reversible path):

$$\Delta S = nR\ln\frac{V_f}{V_i} = (1)(8.314)\ln\frac{24}{8}$$

$$= 8.314 \times \ln(3) = 8.314 \times 1.099 = 9.14 \text{ J/K}$$

(c) **Interpretation**: Despite no heat transfer ($Q = 0$) and no temperature change, entropy increases! This is irreversible expansion in isolated system—second law requires $\Delta S > 0$.

**Answer:** (a) $\Delta T = 0$, (b) $\Delta S = 9.14$ J/K, (c) Irreversible increases entropy

## Example 4: Entropy of Mixing

Two containers each hold 1 mole of ideal gas at same $T = 300$ K and $P = 1$ atm. Container 1 has nitrogen (volume $V_1 = 24.6$ L), container 2 has oxygen (volume $V_2 = 24.6$ L). Partition is removed—gases mix. Find $\Delta S_{mix}$.

**Solution:**

Each gas expands to total volume:

$$V_{total} = V_1 + V_2 = 49.2 \text{ L}$$

Nitrogen:

$$\Delta S_1 = n_1 R\ln\frac{V_{total}}{V_1} = (1)(8.314)\ln\frac{49.2}{24.6}$$

$$= 8.314 \times \ln(2) = 8.314 \times 0.693 = 5.76 \text{ J/K}$$

Oxygen:

$$\Delta S_2 = n_2 R\ln\frac{V_{total}}{V_2} = 5.76 \text{ J/K}$$

(same because equal moles and volumes)

**Total mixing entropy**:

$$\Delta S_{mix} = \Delta S_1 + \Delta S_2 = 5.76 + 5.76 = 11.52 \text{ J/K}$$

Using mole fractions ($x_1 = x_2 = 0.5$):

$$\Delta S_{mix} = -nR(x_1\ln x_1 + x_2\ln x_2)$$

$$= -(2)(8.314)(0.5\ln 0.5 + 0.5\ln 0.5)$$

$$= -16.628 \times 2(0.5 \times (-0.693)) = -16.628 \times (-0.693) = 11.52$$ ✓

**Answer:** $\Delta S_{mix} = 11.52$ J/K

## Example 5: Phase Transition Entropy

One mole of ice at $T_m = 273$ K melts to water at 273 K. Latent heat of fusion $L_{fus} = 6.01$ kJ/mol. Find $\Delta S$.

**Solution:**

Phase transition at constant temperature:

$$\Delta S = \frac{nL_{fus}}{T_m} = \frac{(1)(6010)}{273} = 22.0 \text{ J/K}$$

**Physical interpretation**: Melting increases disorder as molecules gain translational freedom. Positive entropy change. This entropy increase drives melting above 0°C (becomes thermodynamically favorable).

**Answer:** $\Delta S = 22.0$ J/K

A) Zero
B) Negative

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: C** - Isothermal expansion: $\Delta S = nR\ln(V_f/V_i) > 0$ (entropy **increases**).

**Problem 2: C** - Reversible adiabatic: $\Delta S = 0$ (isentropic process).

**Problem 3: C** - Free expansion: $\Delta S > 0$ **despite** $Q = 0$ (irreversible in isolated system).

**Problem 4: C** - Mixing entropy: $\Delta S_{mix} > 0$ always (**positive**).

**Problem 5: C** - Melting: $\Delta S = L/T > 0$ (disorder increases, entropy **positive**).
