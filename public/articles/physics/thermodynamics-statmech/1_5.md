# Article 1.5: Work and Thermodynamic Processes

Work is energy transfer through mechanical displacement against force. In thermodynamics, we consider **expansion work**—work when volume changes against external pressure. A gas expanding pushes a piston outward (positive work). A compressed gas has work done on it. Unlike heat (temperature-driven), work involves organized macroscopic forces.

The crucial feature: **path-dependence**. Work from A to B depends on the entire process, not just endpoints. Expand isothermally? Different work than adiabatically. This path-dependence is why work isn't a state function and we write $\delta W$ (inexact) not $dW$.

This article explores work calculations for various processes: isothermal, isobaric, isochoric, adiabatic, polytropic. Understanding idealized processes is essential for analyzing engines, refrigerators, and thermodynamic systems. We'll distinguish reversible (idealized, maximum work) from irreversible (real, less efficient) processes.

## Definition of Work

For gas in piston, work done **by** system:
$$\delta W = P_{ext} dV$$

where $P_{ext}$ is external pressure, $dV$ is volume change.

Sign convention:
- $dV > 0$ (expansion): positive work by system
- $dV < 0$ (compression): negative work by system (work done on)

Finite process:
$$W = \int_1^2 P_{ext} dV$$

**Key**: Work depends on external pressure during process, which may differ from system pressure if irreversible.

## Reversible vs. Irreversible Work

**Reversible process**:
1. Proceeds infinitely slowly through equilibrium states
2. Can be reversed by infinitesimal change
3. Produces maximum work (expansion) or requires minimum work (compression)

For reversible: $P_{ext} \approx P$ (system pressure):
$$\delta W_{rev} = P dV$$

**Irreversible process**: finite rate, non-equilibrium. Examples:
- Free expansion: vacuum ($P_{ext} = 0$, $W = 0$)
- Sudden expansion: constant low external pressure
- Any friction/dissipation

Fundamental result:
$$|W_{rev}| > |W_{irrev}|$$

## Indicator Diagrams

**PV diagram** plots $P$ vs $V$. Area under curve = work:
$$W = \int P dV = \text{(area under curve)}$$

Different processes have different shapes, different work.

Cyclic process: net work = enclosed area:
$$W_{cycle} = \oint P dV$$

## Isothermal Processes ($T = \text{const}$)

For ideal gas with $PV = nRT$:
$$W = \int_{V_1}^{V_2} \frac{nRT}{V} dV = nRT \ln\left(\frac{V_2}{V_1}\right)$$

Or:
$$W = nRT \ln\left(\frac{P_1}{P_2}\right)$$

Physical: For ideal gas $\Delta U = 0$, so $Q = W$ (all heat → work).

## Isobaric Processes ($P = \text{const}$)

$$W = P(V_2 - V_1) = P \Delta V$$

For ideal gas:
$$W = nR(T_2 - T_1) = nR \Delta T$$

Physical: Heating at constant pressure causes expansion. Some heat → temperature, some → work.

## Isochoric Processes ($V = \text{const}$)

Since $dV = 0$:
$$W = 0$$

No work. All energy transfer is heat: $Q = \Delta U$.

## Adiabatic Processes ($Q = 0$)

No heat transfer. By first law: $\Delta U = -W$.

**Adiabatic relations for ideal gas**:
$$PV^\gamma = \text{const}$$
$$TV^{\gamma-1} = \text{const}$$

where $\gamma = C_P/C_V$ (adiabatic index).

**Derivation**: From $dU = -PdV$ and $dU = nC_V dT$:
$$nC_V dT = -\frac{nRT}{V} dV$$
$$\frac{dT}{T} = -\frac{R}{C_V} \frac{dV}{V} = -(\gamma - 1)\frac{dV}{V}$$

Integrating:
$$TV^{\gamma-1} = \text{const}$$

Work:
$$W = nC_V(T_1 - T_2) = \frac{P_1 V_1 - P_2 V_2}{\gamma - 1}$$

## Polytropic Processes

General power law:
$$PV^n = \text{const}$$

where $n$ is polytropic index:
- $n = 0$: isobaric
- $n = 1$: isothermal (ideal gas)
- $n = \gamma$: adiabatic
- $n \to \infty$: isochoric

Work ($n \neq 1$):
$$W = \frac{P_1 V_1 - P_2 V_2}{n - 1}$$

## Example 1: Isothermal Expansion

$n = 1.5$ moles ideal gas expand isothermally at $T = 400$ K from $V_1 = 8.0$ L to $V_2 = 20.0$ L. Find work.

**Solution:**

$$W = nRT \ln\left(\frac{V_2}{V_1}\right) = (1.5)(8.314)(400) \ln(2.5)$$
$$= 4988.4 \times 0.916 = 4.57 \text{ kJ}$$

**Answer:** $W = 4.57$ kJ

---

## Example 2: Adiabatic Compression

One mole helium ($\gamma = 5/3$) at $T_1 = 300$ K, $V_1 = 25$ L compressed adiabatically to $V_2 = 10$ L. Find:
(a) Final temperature (b) Work done on gas

**Solution:**

(a) From $TV^{\gamma-1} = \text{const}$:
$$T_2 = T_1\left(\frac{V_1}{V_2}\right)^{\gamma-1} = 300(2.5)^{2/3} = 300(1.842) = 553 \text{ K}$$

(b) Work:
$$W = nC_V(T_1 - T_2) = (1.0)(12.47)(300 - 553) = -3150 \text{ J}$$

Work on gas: $+3150$ J = $3.15$ kJ

**Answer:** (a) 553 K, (b) 3.15 kJ on gas

---

## Example 3: Isobaric vs. Isochoric

$n = 2.0$ moles ideal gas heated from 300 K to 450 K.
(a) Isobaric at $P = 1.5 \times 10^5$ Pa
(b) Isochoric

**Solution:**

(a) Isobaric:
$$W = nR\Delta T = (2.0)(8.314)(150) = 2.49 \text{ kJ}$$

(b) Isochoric:
$$W = 0$$

**Answer:** (a) 2.49 kJ, (b) 0

---

## Example 4: Free Expansion

$n = 1.0$ mole ideal gas at $T = 350$ K, $V_1 = 10$ L expands freely to $V_2 = 30$ L. Find:
(a) Work (b) Final temperature (c) $\Delta U$

**Solution:**

(a) Free expansion: $P_{ext} = 0$, so $W = 0$.

(b) Adiabatic ($Q = 0$) with $W = 0$: $\Delta U = 0$.
For ideal gas: $\Delta T = 0$, so $T_2 = 350$ K.

(c) $\Delta U = 0$

**Answer:** (a) 0, (b) 350 K, (c) 0

---

## Example 5: Reversible vs. Irreversible

Gas at $P_1 = 8.0$ atm, $V_1 = 5.0$ L expands to $V_2 = 15.0$ L.
(a) Reversible isothermal
(b) Irreversible against $P_{ext} = 2.0$ atm

**Solution:**

(a) Reversible isothermal:
$$W_{rev} = P_1 V_1 \ln(3.0) = (8.0)(5.0)(1.099) = 43.96 \text{ L} \cdot text {atm} = 4.45 \text{ kJ}$$

(b) Irreversible:
$$W_{irrev} = P_{ext}\Delta V = (2.0)(10.0) = 20.0 \text{ L} \cdot text {atm} = 2.03 \text{ kJ}$$

Reversible extracts more than twice the work!

**Answer:** (a) 4.45 kJ, (b) 2.03 kJ
