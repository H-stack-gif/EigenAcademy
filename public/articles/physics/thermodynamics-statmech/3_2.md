# Article 3.2: Carnot Cycle and Thermodynamic Temperature

What is the most efficient possible heat engine? This question was answered by Sadi Carnot in 1824, long before the modern formulation of thermodynamics. Carnot showed that the maximum efficiency depends only on the operating temperatures, not on the working substance or engine design. This remarkable result—the **Carnot theorem**—establishes fundamental limits on energy conversion.

The **Carnot cycle** consists of four reversible processes: two isothermal (heat exchange at constant temperature) and two adiabatic (no heat exchange). Its elegant simplicity allows exact calculation of efficiency, yielding $\eta_C = 1 - T_C/T_H$. This formula does more than give maximum efficiency—it provides an absolute thermodynamic temperature scale independent of any particular substance's properties.

This article develops the Carnot cycle step-by-step, calculates its efficiency, proves Carnot's theorem, establishes the thermodynamic temperature scale, and explores implications for absolute zero and real engines. The mathematics involves cyclic integrals, PV diagram analysis, and logical proof of universal efficiency bounds.

## The Carnot Cycle

The **Carnot cycle** operates between two thermal reservoirs at $T_H$ (hot) and $T_C$ (cold) through four reversible steps:

**Step 1**: Isothermal expansion at $T_H$
- System at temperature $T_H$
- Absorbs heat $Q_H$ from hot reservoir
- Expands from $(P_1, V_1)$ to $(P_2, V_2)$
- Work done: $W_1 = nRT_H\ln(V_2/V_1)$

**Step 2**: Adiabatic expansion
- No heat transfer ($Q = 0$)
- Temperature drops from $T_H$ to $T_C$
- Expands from $(P_2, V_2)$ to $(P_3, V_3)$
- Work done: $W_2 = nC_V(T_H - T_C)$

**Step 3**: Isothermal compression at $T_C$
- System at temperature $T_C$
- Rejects heat $Q_C$ to cold reservoir
- Compresses from $(P_3, V_3)$ to $(P_4, V_4)$
- Work done: $W_3 = nRT_C\ln(V_4/V_3) < 0$ (negative—work done on system)

**Step 4**: Adiabatic compression
- No heat transfer
- Temperature rises from $T_C$ to $T_H$
- Compresses from $(P_4, V_4)$ to $(P_1, V_1)$
- Work done: $W_4 = nC_V(T_C - T_H) < 0$

The cycle closes, returning to initial state.

## Carnot Efficiency Calculation

**Total work per cycle**:

$$W = W_1 + W_2 + W_3 + W_4 = Q_H - Q_C$$

(from first law for cyclic process with $\Delta U = 0$)

For isothermal steps:

$$Q_H = nRT_H\ln(V_2/V_1)$$

$$Q_C = nRT_C\ln(V_3/V_4) = -nRT_C\ln(V_4/V_3)$$

From adiabatic relations $T_H V_2^{\gamma-1} = T_C V_3^{\gamma-1}$ and $T_H V_1^{\gamma-1} = T_C V_4^{\gamma-1}$:

Dividing these:

$$\frac{V_2^{\gamma-1}}{V_1^{\gamma-1}} = \frac{V_3^{\gamma-1}}{V_4^{\gamma-1}}$$

$$\frac{V_2}{V_1} = \frac{V_3}{V_4}$$

Therefore:

$$\ln(V_2/V_1) = \ln(V_3/V_4)$$

Heat ratio:

$$\frac{Q_C}{Q_H} = \frac{nRT_C\ln(V_3/V_4)}{nRT_H\ln(V_2/V_1)} = \frac{T_C}{T_H}$$

**Carnot efficiency**:

$$\boxed{\eta_C = 1 - \frac{Q_C}{Q_H} = 1 - \frac{T_C}{T_H}}$$

This depends only on temperatures, not on working substance!

## Carnot's Theorem

**Carnot's theorem** (1824):

1. All **reversible** heat engines operating between the same two temperatures have the same efficiency
2. No **irreversible** engine can be more efficient than a reversible one

**Proof** (part 1):

Suppose two reversible engines R₁ and R₂ operate between same $T_H$ and $T_C$, but $\eta_1 > \eta_2$.

Run R₁ forward as engine and R₂ backward as refrigerator, with same heat $Q_H$ from hot reservoir.

R₁ produces more work ($W_1 > W_2$) because $\eta_1 > \eta_2$.

Use work $W_2$ to run R₂ as refrigerator. Excess work $W_1 - W_2$ is available.

Net effect: Extract heat from hot reservoir and convert entirely to work—**violates Kelvin-Planck**!

Therefore, all reversible engines must have same efficiency.

**Proof** (part 2): Similar argument shows irreversible engine cannot exceed reversible efficiency.

## Thermodynamic Temperature Scale

Carnot efficiency provides absolute temperature definition:

$$\frac{Q_C}{Q_H} = \frac{T_C}{T_H} \quad \text{(reversible engine)}$$

This defines temperature **independent of any substance properties**!

Choose standard: water triple point at $T_{tp} = 273.16$ K.

For any other temperature:

$$T = 273.16 \times \frac{Q}{Q_{tp}}$$

where $Q$ and $Q_{tp}$ are heats absorbed at $T$ and $T_{tp}$ by reversible engine.

This is the **thermodynamic temperature scale**—fundamental and universal.

## Absolute Zero

From Carnot efficiency:

$$\eta_C = 1 - \frac{T_C}{T_H}$$

For $\eta_C = 1$ (perfect engine), need $T_C = 0$.

This defines **absolute zero**: the temperature where Carnot engine would have 100% efficiency (all heat converts to work).

From second law: cannot reach absolute zero in finite steps (**third law**).

Physical meaning: At $T = 0$, molecular motion ceases (classically), entropy reaches minimum.

## Reversible vs. Irreversible Cycles

**Reversible cycles**:
- Maximum efficiency $\eta_{rev} = 1 - T_C/T_H$
- No entropy generation
- Infinitely slow (idealization)

**Irreversible cycles**:
- Lower efficiency $\eta_{irr} < \eta_{rev}$
- Entropy generation
- Finite rate (all real cycles)

Sources of irreversibility:
- Friction
- Heat transfer across finite $\Delta T$
- Turbulence
- Unrestrained expansion

**Second law efficiency**:

$$\eta_{II} = \frac{\eta_{actual}}{\eta_{Carnot}}$$

Measures how close real engine approaches ideal.

## Example 1: Carnot Efficiency

Calculate maximum efficiency for heat engine operating between $T_H = 500$ K and $T_C = 300$ K.

**Solution:**

Carnot efficiency:

$$\eta_C = 1 - \frac{T_C}{T_H} = 1 - \frac{300}{500} = 1 - 0.6 = 0.4 = 40\%$$

**Physical interpretation**: With these temperatures, maximum possible efficiency is 40%. Real engines will be less efficient due to irreversibilities.

**Answer:** $\eta_C = 40\%$

## Example 2: Carnot Cycle Work Calculation

A Carnot engine absorbs $Q_H = 1000$ J at $T_H = 600$ K and rejects heat at $T_C = 300$ K. Find:
(a) Heat rejected $Q_C$
(b) Work done $W$
(c) Efficiency $\eta$

**Solution:**

(a) For Carnot engine:

$$\frac{Q_C}{Q_H} = \frac{T_C}{T_H}$$

$$Q_C = Q_H \times \frac{T_C}{T_H} = 1000 \times \frac{300}{600} = 500 \text{ J}$$

(b) Work:

$$W = Q_H - Q_C = 1000 - 500 = 500 \text{ J}$$

(c) Efficiency:

$$\eta = \frac{W}{Q_H} = \frac{500}{1000} = 0.5 = 50\%$$

Check: $\eta_C = 1 - 300/600 = 0.5$ ✓

**Answer:** (a) $Q_C = 500$ J, (b) $W = 500$ J, (c) $\eta = 50\%$

## Example 3: Comparing Real Engine to Carnot

A real steam engine operates between $T_H = 500°\text{C} = 773$ K and $T_C = 100°\text{C} = 373$ K with actual efficiency $\eta_{actual} = 35\%$. Find:
(a) Carnot efficiency
(b) Second law efficiency

**Solution:**

(a) Maximum (Carnot) efficiency:

$$\eta_C = 1 - \frac{T_C}{T_H} = 1 - \frac{373}{773} = 1 - 0.483 = 0.517 = 51.7\%$$

(b) Second law efficiency:

$$\eta_{II} = \frac{\eta_{actual}}{\eta_C} = \frac{0.35}{0.517} = 0.677 = 67.7\%$$

**Physical interpretation**: Real engine achieves 67.7% of theoretically possible efficiency. Remaining 32.3% lost to irreversibilities (friction, heat losses, etc.).

**Answer:** (a) $\eta_C = 51.7\%$, (b) $\eta_{II} = 67.7\%$

## Example 4: Required Temperature for Target Efficiency

A Carnot engine operates with cold reservoir at $T_C = 300$ K. What hot reservoir temperature gives $\eta = 60\%$?

**Solution:**

From Carnot efficiency:

$$\eta_C = 1 - \frac{T_C}{T_H}$$

$$0.60 = 1 - \frac{300}{T_H}$$

$$\frac{300}{T_H} = 0.40$$

$$T_H = \frac{300}{0.40} = 750 \text{ K} = 477°\text{C}$$

**Physical interpretation**: Need very high temperature (477°C) to achieve 60% efficiency with room-temperature cold reservoir. Modern coal plants use superheated steam around 540-600°C.

**Answer:** $T_H = 750$ K

---

## Example 5: Carnot Refrigerator

A Carnot refrigerator operates between $T_H = 300$ K (room) and $T_C = 250$ K (interior). Find maximum coefficient of performance.

**Solution:**

For Carnot refrigerator:

$$K_{Carnot} = \frac{Q_C}{W} = \frac{Q_C}{Q_H - Q_C}$$

Using $Q_C/Q_H = T_C/T_H$:

$$\frac{Q_C}{Q_H - Q_C} = \frac{T_C}{T_H - T_C}$$

$$K_{Carnot} = \frac{T_C}{T_H - T_C} = \frac{250}{300 - 250} = \frac{250}{50} = 5.0$$

**Physical interpretation**: Ideally, 1 joule of work removes 5 joules of heat. Small temperature difference (50 K) gives high COP. Real refrigerators: $K \approx 2$-$3$.

**Answer:** $K_{Carnot} = 5.0$
