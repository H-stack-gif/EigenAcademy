# Article 5.2: Microcanonical Ensemble

The microcanonical ensemble describes the simplest statistical situation: an **isolated system** with fixed energy, volume, and particle number $(E, V, N)$. No energy or particles cross the boundary—the system is completely cut off from surroundings. According to the fundamental postulate, all microstates with energy $E$ are equally probable. The number of such microstates, $\Omega(E)$, is the **density of states**, and Boltzmann's formula $S = k_B \ln \Omega$ connects it directly to thermodynamic entropy.

From the microcanonical entropy $S(E, V, N)$, we can derive all thermodynamic relations. Temperature emerges from $1/T = (\partial S/\partial E)_{V,N}$—a system with many accessible states near energy $E$ has high temperature. Pressure emerges from $P/T = (\partial S/\partial V)_{E,N}$—entropy's sensitivity to volume determines pressure. The microcanonical ensemble is the foundation from which other ensembles (canonical, grand canonical) are derived as special cases.

This article develops the microcanonical ensemble systematically, calculates density of states for simple systems, derives thermodynamic quantities from $S(E, V, N)$, applies the formalism to ideal gases, explores two-level systems, and demonstrates how thermodynamics emerges from counting microstates. The mathematics involves combinatorics, multivariable calculus with entropy as primary function, surface integrals in phase space, Stirling's approximation, and partial derivative relations.

## Isolated System with Fixed $(E, V, N)$

**Microcanonical ensemble**: Collection of systems with:
- Fixed energy $E$ (or within narrow range $E$ to $E + \delta E$)
- Fixed volume $V$
- Fixed particle number $N$

All accessible microstates equally probable:

$$P_i = \frac{1}{\Omega(E, V, N)}$$

where $\Omega(E, V, N)$ is number of microstates.

**Constraints**: System is isolated—no energy or particle exchange with surroundings.

## Density of States

**Density of states** $\Omega(E)$ counts microstates with energy between $E$ and $E + \delta E$:

$$\Omega(E) \, \delta E = \frac{1}{h^{3N}} \int_{E < H(\mathbf{q}, \mathbf{p}) < E + \delta E} d\Gamma$$

For continuous systems:

$$\Omega(E) = \frac{1}{h^{3N}} \int \delta(H(\mathbf{q}, \mathbf{p}) - E) \, d\Gamma$$

where $\delta$ is Dirac delta function.

**Surface integral**: $\Omega(E)$ is proportional to surface area of constant-energy hypersurface in phase space.

For many systems, $\Omega(E)$ grows extremely rapidly with $E$:

$$\Omega(E) \sim E^{\alpha N}$$

where $\alpha$ is system-dependent constant. This rapid growth is crucial for thermodynamics.

## Entropy in Microcanonical Ensemble

**Boltzmann's formula**:

$$\boxed{S = k_B \ln \Omega(E, V, N)}$$

This is the **fundamental relation** of microcanonical ensemble.

**Properties**:
- $S$ is extensive: $S(\lambda E, \lambda V, \lambda N) = \lambda S(E, V, N) + k_B \ln \lambda^{\lambda N}$
- $S$ is maximized at equilibrium
- $S$ increases for spontaneous processes (second law)

**Connection to thermodynamics**: $S(E, V, N)$ contains all thermodynamic information. All other quantities derived from it.

## Temperature from Entropy

Define **thermodynamic temperature**:

$$\boxed{\frac{1}{T} = \left(\frac{\partial S}{\partial E}\right)_{V,N}}$$

Or equivalently:

$$\frac{1}{k_B T} = \frac{\partial \ln \Omega}{\partial E}\bigg|_{V,N}$$

**Physical interpretation**: Temperature measures how rapidly number of accessible states grows with energy.

- High $T$: $\Omega(E)$ grows slowly with $E$ (many states already accessible)
- Low $T$: $\Omega(E)$ grows rapidly with $E$ (each energy quantum opens many new states)

**Example**: For $\Omega(E) \propto E^{\alpha N}$:

$$S = k_B \alpha N \ln E + \text{const}$$

$$\frac{1}{T} = \frac{\partial S}{\partial E} = \frac{k_B \alpha N}{E}$$

$$E = k_B \alpha N T$$

Average energy per particle: $\bar{\epsilon} = E/N = k_B \alpha T$ (equipartition-like).

## Pressure from Entropy

Define **pressure**:

$$\boxed{\frac{P}{T} = \left(\frac{\partial S}{\partial V}\right)_{E,N}}$$

Or:

$$P = T\left(\frac{\partial S}{\partial V}\right)_{E,N}$$

**Physical interpretation**: Pressure measures how entropy changes with volume at fixed energy.

- High $P$: Entropy very sensitive to volume (system wants to expand)
- Low $P$: Entropy insensitive to volume

**Ideal gas**: For $\Omega \propto V^N$:

$$S = Nk_B \ln V + f(E, N)$$

$$\frac{P}{T} = \frac{\partial S}{\partial V} = \frac{Nk_B}{V}$$

$$PV = Nk_B T$$ (ideal gas law!)

## Chemical Potential from Entropy

Define **chemical potential**:

$$\boxed{\frac{\mu}{T} = -\left(\frac{\partial S}{\partial N}\right)_{E,V}}$$

Or:

$$\mu = -T\left(\frac{\partial S}{\partial N}\right)_{E,V}$$

**Physical interpretation**: Chemical potential measures entropy cost of adding particle at fixed energy and volume.

## Ideal Gas in Microcanonical Ensemble

For **monatomic ideal gas** of $N$ particles in volume $V$ with total energy $E$:

$$H = \sum_{i=1}^N \frac{p_i^2}{2m}$$

Phase space volume (classical):

$$\Omega(E) \propto \frac{V^N}{N! h^{3N}} \int \delta\left(\sum_i \frac{p_i^2}{2m} - E\right) d^{3N}p$$

The momentum integral is surface of $(3N)$-dimensional sphere with radius $\sqrt{2mE}$:

$$\Omega(E, V, N) \propto \frac{V^N E^{3N/2}}{N!}$$

Using Stirling's approximation $\ln N! \approx N\ln N - N$:

$$S = k_B \ln \Omega = Nk_B\left[\ln V + \frac{3}{2}\ln E + \text{const}\right]$$

**Temperature**:

$$\frac{1}{T} = \frac{\partial S}{\partial E} = \frac{3Nk_B}{2E}$$

$$E = \frac{3}{2}Nk_B T$$

This is the **ideal gas energy**: average $3k_B T/2$ per particle (equipartition for 3 translational degrees of freedom).

**Pressure**:

$$P = T\frac{\partial S}{\partial V} = T \cdot \frac{Nk_B}{V} = \frac{Nk_B T}{V}$$

Ideal gas law: $PV = Nk_B T$ ✓

## Two-Level System

Consider **two-level system**: each particle in ground state (energy 0) or excited state (energy $\epsilon$).

Total energy: $E = n\epsilon$ where $n$ is number of excited particles.

Number of microstates:

$$\Omega(E, N) = \binom{N}{n} = \frac{N!}{n!(N-n)!}$$

Entropy:

$$S = k_B \ln\binom{N}{n}$$

Using Stirling for large $N$:

$$S \approx k_B[N\ln N - n\ln n - (N-n)\ln(N-n)]$$

**Temperature**:

$$\frac{1}{T} = \frac{\partial S}{\partial E} = \frac{1}{\epsilon}\frac{\partial S}{\partial n}$$

$$= \frac{k_B}{\epsilon}\left[\ln(N-n) - \ln n\right] = \frac{k_B}{\epsilon}\ln\frac{N-n}{n}$$

Solving for $n$:

$$\frac{n}{N-n} = e^{-\epsilon/(k_B T)}$$

$$n = \frac{N}{1 + e^{\epsilon/(k_B T)}}$$

This is the **Fermi-Dirac** form (but applied to distinguishable classical particles).

## Example 1: Calculating $\Omega$ for Two Particles

Two distinguishable particles, each can be in state A (energy 0) or state B (energy $\epsilon$). Total energy $E = \epsilon$. Find $\Omega$.

**Solution:**

Total energy $E = \epsilon$ means exactly one particle excited.

**Possible microstates**:
1. Particle 1 in B, particle 2 in A: (B, A)
2. Particle 1 in A, particle 2 in B: (A, B)

Therefore: $\Omega = 2$

**Entropy**:

$$S = k_B \ln 2 \approx 0.693 k_B$$

**Verification**: Using formula $\Omega = \binom{N}{n}$ with $N = 2$, $n = 1$:

$$\Omega = \binom{2}{1} = \frac{2!}{1! \cdot 1!} = 2$$ ✓

**Answer:** $\Omega = 2$ microstates

## Example 2: Temperature of Ideal Gas

Ideal gas: $E = \frac{3}{2}Nk_B T$. Express temperature from microcanonical relation.

**Solution:**

From microcanonical ensemble:

$$\frac{1}{T} = \left(\frac{\partial S}{\partial E}\right)_{V,N}$$

For ideal gas: $S = Nk_B[\ln V + \frac{3}{2}\ln E + \text{const}]$

$$\frac{1}{T} = \frac{\partial}{\partial E}\left[\frac{3}{2}Nk_B \ln E + \text{const}\right] = \frac{3Nk_B}{2E}$$

Inverting:

$$T = \frac{2E}{3Nk_B}$$

Or:

$$E = \frac{3}{2}Nk_B T$$

This matches kinetic theory result! Each translational degree of freedom contributes $\frac{1}{2}k_B T$ (equipartition).

**Answer:** $T = 2E/(3Nk_B)$ or $E = \frac{3}{2}Nk_B T$

## Example 3: Pressure Derivation

For ideal gas with $S = Nk_B[\ln V + \frac{3}{2}\ln E]$ (ignoring constants), find pressure.

**Solution:**

Microcanonical pressure:

$$P = T\left(\frac{\partial S}{\partial V}\right)_{E,N}$$

$$\frac{\partial S}{\partial V} = Nk_B \frac{\partial}{\partial V}(\ln V) = \frac{Nk_B}{V}$$

$$P = T \cdot \frac{Nk_B}{V} = \frac{Nk_B T}{V}$$

Ideal gas law: $PV = Nk_B T$ ✓

**Physical interpretation**: Entropy increases logarithmically with volume. Large volume means more available position states, higher entropy.

**Answer:** $P = Nk_B T/V$ (ideal gas law)

## Example 4: Entropy Change with Energy

System has $\Omega(E) = CE^{2N}$ where $C$ is constant. Find $S(E)$ and temperature.

**Solution:**

**Entropy**:

$$S = k_B \ln \Omega = k_B \ln(CE^{2N}) = k_B(\ln C + 2N\ln E)$$

$$= 2Nk_B \ln E + k_B \ln C$$

**Temperature**:

$$\frac{1}{T} = \frac{\partial S}{\partial E} = 2Nk_B \frac{\partial}{\partial E}(\ln E) = \frac{2Nk_B}{E}$$

$$T = \frac{E}{2Nk_B}$$

Or:

$$E = 2Nk_B T$$

**Physical interpretation**: Average energy per particle is $2k_B T$—consistent with equipartition if each particle has 4 quadratic degrees of freedom (e.g., 2D harmonic oscillator: 2 kinetic + 2 potential).

**Answer:** $S = 2Nk_B \ln E + \text{const}$, $T = E/(2Nk_B)$

## Example 5: Two-Level System Temperature

System of $N = 100$ two-level particles ($\epsilon = 0.1$ eV) with $n = 30$ particles excited. Find temperature.

**Solution:**

From two-level system analysis:

$$\frac{1}{T} = \frac{k_B}{\epsilon}\ln\frac{N-n}{n}$$

$$T = \frac{\epsilon}{k_B \ln[(N-n)/n]}$$

With $N = 100$, $n = 30$:

$$T = \frac{0.1 \text{ eV}}{k_B \ln(70/30)} = \frac{0.1}{k_B \ln(2.333)}$$

Using $k_B = 8.617 \times 10^{-5}$ eV/K:

$$T = \frac{0.1}{(8.617 \times 10^{-5}) \times 0.847} = \frac{0.1}{7.30 \times 10^{-5}} = 1370 \text{ K}$$

**Physical interpretation**: At this temperature, about 30% of particles are excited. Higher temperature would excite more particles.

**Answer:** $T \approx 1370$ K

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: B** - Microcanonical: fixed **$(E, V, N)$** (isolated system).

**Problem 2: C** - Boltzmann: $S = k_B \ln \Omega$ (entropy from state counting).

**Problem 3: B** - Temperature: $1/T = (\partial S/\partial E)_{V,N}$ (definition in microcanonical).

**Problem 4: B** - Ideal gas: $E \propto T$ (specifically $E = \frac{3}{2}Nk_B T$ for monatomic).

**Problem 5: C** - Density of states: **increases rapidly** with energy (exponentially or power-law).
