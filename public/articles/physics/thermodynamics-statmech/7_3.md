# Article 7.3: Collisions and Mean Free Path

Gas molecules don't travel in straight lines indefinitely—they collide with each other. Between collisions, molecules move freely at constant velocity; collisions redirect their motion randomly. The average distance traveled between collisions is the **mean free path** $\lambda$, a fundamental length scale in kinetic theory. The **collision frequency** $\nu$ determines how often molecules collide, setting timescales for momentum and energy transfer. These quantities depend on molecular size (collision cross section), number density, and average speed.

The mean free path bridges microscopic and macroscopic physics. At **high pressure** (high density), $\lambda$ is small—molecules collide frequently, making the gas behave as a continuous medium. At **low pressure** (low density), $\lambda$ becomes large, eventually exceeding container dimensions—the **molecular flow regime** where molecules collide with walls more often than each other. Transport phenomena—diffusion, viscosity, thermal conductivity—all depend fundamentally on $\lambda$ and collision dynamics.

This article derives the collision cross section for hard spheres, calculates mean free path from geometric arguments, introduces collision frequency, derives the distribution of free path lengths as exponential, connects these concepts to transport coefficients, and explores the transition between collision-dominated and free-molecular flow. The mathematics involves statistical geometry, exponential distributions, probability per unit length, dimensional analysis, and density scaling.

## Collision Cross Section

Model molecules as **hard spheres** with diameter $d$.

**Collision occurs** when centers approach within distance $d$.

**Collision cross section**: Effective target area for collision:

$$\boxed{\sigma = \pi d^2}$$

**Physical interpretation**: If molecule sweeps out area $\sigma$ as it moves, it will collide with any molecule whose center lies in that area.

**Typical values**:
- Helium: $d \approx 2.2 \times 10^{-10}$ m → $\sigma = 1.5 \times 10^{-19}$ m²
- Nitrogen: $d \approx 3.7 \times 10^{-10}$ m → $\sigma = 4.3 \times 10^{-19}$ m²
- Larger molecules have larger cross sections

**Relative motion**: When both molecules move, effective collision uses **relative velocity**. For random motion, this introduces factor $\sqrt{2}$.

## Mean Free Path

Consider molecule moving through gas with number density $n$.

**Volume swept per unit distance**: $\sigma$ (cross-sectional area)

**Number of molecules encountered per unit distance**: $n\sigma$

**Mean free path** $\lambda$: Average distance between collisions.

**Naive estimate**: $\lambda = 1/(n\sigma)$

**Correction for relative motion**: Other molecules also move, so effective collision rate higher by $\sqrt{2}$.

$$\boxed{\lambda = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2} n \sigma}}$$

**Alternative forms**:

Using $\sigma = \pi d^2$:

$$\lambda = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2} \pi n d^2}$$

Using ideal gas law $n = P/(k_B T)$:

$$\lambda = \frac{k_B T}{\sqrt{2} \pi d^2 P}$$

**Key property**: $\lambda \propto 1/P$ at fixed temperature—mean free path **increases** as pressure decreases.

**Typical values** at STP ($P = 10^5$ Pa, $T = 273$ K):
- Air: $\lambda \approx 68$ nm
- At $P = 1$ Pa (rough vacuum): $\lambda \approx 6.8$ mm
- At $P = 10^{-6}$ Pa (high vacuum): $\lambda \approx 68$ m

## Collision Frequency

**Collision frequency** $\nu$: Number of collisions per unit time.

$$\boxed{\nu = \frac{\langle v \rangle}{\lambda}}$$

where $\langle v \rangle$ is mean speed.

**Physical meaning**: Time to travel one mean free path is $\lambda/\langle v \rangle$, so collision rate is inverse.

Substituting $\lambda$ and $\langle v \rangle = \sqrt{8k_B T/(\pi m)}$:

$$\nu = \sqrt{2} n \sigma \langle v \rangle = \sqrt{2} \pi d^2 n \sqrt{\frac{8k_B T}{\pi m}}$$

$$\boxed{\nu = 4nd^2\sqrt{\frac{\pi k_B T}{m}}}$$

**Properties**:
- $\nu \propto n$ (higher density → more collisions)
- $\nu \propto \sqrt{T}$ (higher temperature → faster motion → more collisions)
- $\nu \propto d^2$ (larger molecules → more collisions)

**Typical values** at STP for air:
- $\nu \approx 10^{10}$ collisions/second (10 billion per second!)

Molecules collide extremely frequently.

## Mean Time Between Collisions

**Mean collision time**:

$$\boxed{\tau = \frac{1}{\nu} = \frac{\lambda}{\langle v \rangle}}$$

**Typical value** at STP:

$$\tau = \frac{68 \times 10^{-9}}{500} = 1.4 \times 10^{-10} \text{ s} = 0.14 \text{ ns}$$

Extremely short! Molecules are constantly colliding.

**Collision time vs. free flight time**:
- Time of collision: $\tau_c \sim d/\langle v \rangle \sim 10^{-13}$ s
- Time between collisions: $\tau \sim 10^{-10}$ s

Ratio: $\tau_c/\tau \sim 10^{-3}$

Molecules spend only ~0.1% of time actually colliding—rest is free flight. This justifies ideal gas assumption of negligible interaction time.

## Distribution of Free Paths

**Question**: What is probability molecule travels distance $\ell$ without collision?

**Probability of collision in distance $d\ell$**: $d\ell/\lambda$ (fraction of mean free path)

**Survival probability** $P(\ell)$ satisfies:

$$P(\ell + d\ell) = P(\ell) \cdot \left(1 - \frac{d\ell}{\lambda}\right)$$

$$\frac{dP}{d\ell} = -\frac{P}{\lambda}$$

**Solution**: $P(\ell) = e^{-\ell/\lambda}$

**Free path distribution** (probability density):

$$\boxed{f(\ell) = \frac{1}{\lambda} e^{-\ell/\lambda}}$$

This is **exponential distribution** with mean $\lambda$.

**Verification**:

$$\langle \ell \rangle = \int_0^\infty \ell f(\ell) d\ell = \int_0^\infty \frac{\ell}{\lambda} e^{-\ell/\lambda} d\ell = \lambda$$ ✓

**Physical interpretation**: Most molecules have free path $< \lambda$, but rare molecules travel much farther (exponential tail).

## Transport Processes Overview

Mean free path determines **transport coefficients** (detailed in next two articles):

**Diffusion coefficient**:

$$D \sim \frac{1}{3}\lambda \langle v \rangle$$

**Viscosity**:

$$\eta \sim \frac{1}{3}nm\lambda \langle v \rangle$$

**Thermal conductivity**:

$$\kappa \sim \frac{1}{3}nC_V \lambda \langle v \rangle$$

All have form: (transport coefficient) $\sim$ (density factor) × $\lambda$ × $\langle v \rangle$

**Physical mechanism**: Transport by random walk—molecules carry properties (particles, momentum, energy) distance $\sim \lambda$ between collisions.

**Pressure dependence**:
- $\lambda \propto 1/P$
- $\langle v \rangle$ independent of $P$
- $D \propto 1/P$ (diffusion slower at high pressure)
- $\eta$ independent of $P$ (surprising!—viscosity pressure-independent)
- $\kappa$ independent of $P$

## Example 1: Mean Free Path in Air

Air at STP: $P = 1.01 \times 10^5$ Pa, $T = 273$ K, $d \approx 3.7 \times 10^{-10}$ m. Calculate $\lambda$.

**Solution:**

$$\lambda = \frac{k_B T}{\sqrt{2}\pi d^2 P}$$

$$= \frac{(1.381 \times 10^{-23})(273)}{\sqrt{2}\pi (3.7 \times 10^{-10})^2 (1.01 \times 10^5)}$$

$$= \frac{3.77 \times 10^{-21}}{(1.414)(3.14)(1.37 \times 10^{-19})(1.01 \times 10^5)}$$

$$= \frac{3.77 \times 10^{-21}}{6.15 \times 10^{-14}} = 6.13 \times 10^{-8} \text{ m}$$

$$= 61.3 \text{ nm}$$

**Comparison**: Molecular diameter $d = 0.37$ nm, so $\lambda/d \approx 165$—molecule travels ~165 diameters between collisions.

**Answer:** $\lambda = 61$ nm

## Example 2: Collision Frequency

For air at STP with $\lambda = 68$ nm and $\langle v \rangle = 470$ m/s, find collision frequency.

**Solution:**

$$\nu = \frac{\langle v \rangle}{\lambda} = \frac{470}{68 \times 10^{-9}}$$

$$= 6.91 \times 10^9 \text{ s}^{-1}$$

About **7 billion collisions per second**!

**Mean time between collisions**:

$$\tau = \frac{1}{\nu} = 1.45 \times 10^{-10} \text{ s} = 0.145 \text{ ns}$$

Very short time—molecule's trajectory constantly redirected.

**Answer:** $\nu = 6.9 \times 10^9$ Hz, $\tau = 0.14$ ns

## Example 3: Vacuum System

Vacuum chamber at $P = 10^{-3}$ Pa, $T = 300$ K with $d = 3.7 \times 10^{-10}$ m. Is it in molecular flow regime if chamber size $L = 10$ cm?

**Solution:**

Calculate mean free path:

$$\lambda = \frac{k_B T}{\sqrt{2}\pi d^2 P} = \frac{(1.381 \times 10^{-23})(300)}{\sqrt{2}\pi (3.7 \times 10^{-10})^2 (10^{-3})}$$

$$= \frac{4.14 \times 10^{-21}}{6.09 \times 10^{-23}} = 6.8 \times 10^1 \text{ m} = 68 \text{ m}$$

**Knudsen number**: $\text{Kn} = \lambda/L = 68/0.1 = 680$

Since $\text{Kn} \gg 1$: **Molecular flow regime**

Molecules collide with walls more than with each other—gas dynamics qualitatively different from normal pressures.

**Answer:** Yes, molecular flow regime ($\lambda = 68$ m $\gg L = 10$ cm)

---

## Example 4: Free Path Probability

What fraction of molecules travel at least $2\lambda$ without collision?

**Solution:**

Survival probability:

$$P(\ell \geq 2\lambda) = \int_{2\lambda}^\infty \frac{1}{\lambda} e^{-\ell/\lambda} d\ell$$

$$= e^{-\ell/\lambda}\bigg|_{2\lambda}^\infty = 0 - e^{-2} = e^{-2}$$

$$= 0.135 = 13.5\%$$

About 1 in 7 molecules travels at least twice the mean free path before colliding.

**Exponential tail**: Small but non-negligible probability of long free paths.

**Answer:** 13.5% travel at least $2\lambda$

---

## Example 5: Pressure Scaling

Mean free path at 1 atm is 68 nm. What is it at 0.001 atm (same temperature)?

**Solution:**

$$\lambda \propto \frac{1}{P}$$

$$\frac{\lambda_2}{\lambda_1} = \frac{P_1}{P_2} = \frac{1}{0.001} = 1000$$

$$\lambda_2 = 1000 \times 68 \text{ nm} = 68 \times 10^{-6} \text{ m} = 68 \text{ μm}$$

At lower pressure, mean free path dramatically increases.

**Application**: This is why vacuum systems need careful design—at low pressure, molecules don't "sense" nearby surfaces through collisions; they directly hit walls.

**Answer:** $\lambda = 68$ μm

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: B** - Cross section: $\sigma = \pi d^2$ (circle of radius $d$).

**Problem 2: D** - Mean free path: $\lambda \propto 1/P$ (inverse proportional).

**Problem 3: B** - Collision frequency: $\nu = \langle v \rangle/\lambda$ (speed over path length).

**Problem 4: C** - Free path: **exponential distribution** $f(\ell) = (1/\lambda)e^{-\ell/\lambda}$.

**Problem 5: C** - Lower pressure: $\lambda$ **increases** (fewer molecules → longer paths).
