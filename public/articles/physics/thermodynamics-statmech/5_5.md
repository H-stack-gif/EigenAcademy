# Article 5.5: Grand Canonical Ensemble

The canonical ensemble allows energy fluctuations while holding particle number fixed. But many systems exchange both energy and particles with surroundings: gas molecules adsorbing on surfaces, electrons moving between conductors, atoms in chemical reactions. The **grand canonical ensemble** describes systems in contact with a reservoir that supplies both energy and particles, maintaining fixed temperature $T$ and chemical potential $\mu$.

The **grand partition function** $\mathcal{Z} = \sum_{N,i} e^{\beta(\mu N - E_i)}$ sums over all particle numbers and all energy states, weighted by the grand canonical Boltzmann factor. The **grand potential** $\Phi = -k_B T \ln \mathcal{Z}$ plays the same role as Helmholtz free energy in canonical ensemble. Remarkably, $\Phi = -PV$, connecting grand canonical formalism directly to pressure. The fugacity $z = e^{\beta \mu}$ provides an alternative control parameter, particularly useful for quantum gases.

This article develops the grand canonical ensemble from first principles, derives the grand partition function, introduces the grand potential and its properties, calculates average particle number and fluctuations, explores the connection to pressure, introduces fugacity, and demonstrates applications to open systems. The mathematics involves double summations over particle number and states, derivatives with respect to chemical potential, fluctuation-dissipation relations, and connections between different ensembles.

## System in Contact with Particle and Energy Reservoir

**Setup**: Small system can exchange:
- Energy with large reservoir
- Particles with large reservoir

**Controlled variables**: Temperature $T$ and chemical potential $\mu$ (fixed by reservoir)

**Fluctuating variables**: Energy $E$ and particle number $N$ (can vary)

This is the most general equilibrium ensemble—encompasses both canonical (fixed $N$) and microcanonical (fixed $E$, $N$) as special cases.

## Deriving the Grand Canonical Distribution

Combined system + reservoir with total $N_{total}$ particles at energy $E_{total}$.

Probability system has $N$ particles with energy $E$:

$$P(N, E) \propto \Omega_r(N_{total} - N, E_{total} - E)$$

where $\Omega_r$ is reservoir density of states.

Taylor expanding $\ln \Omega_r$:

$$\ln \Omega_r \approx \ln \Omega_r(N_{total}, E_{total}) - \beta E + \beta \mu N$$

where:
- $\beta = (\partial \ln \Omega_r/\partial E)_{N_{total}} = 1/(k_B T)$
- $\mu = -T(\partial \ln \Omega_r/\partial N)_{E_{total}}$ = chemical potential

Therefore:

$$\boxed{P(N, i) = \frac{e^{\beta(\mu N - E_i)}}{\mathcal{Z}}}$$

where $i$ labels microstates with $N$ particles.

## Grand Partition Function

**Grand partition function**:

$$\boxed{\mathcal{Z} = \sum_{N=0}^\infty \sum_i e^{\beta(\mu N - E_i)} = \sum_{N=0}^\infty e^{\beta \mu N} Z_N(T, V)}$$

where $Z_N$ is canonical partition function for $N$ particles.

**Alternative form** using fugacity $z = e^{\beta \mu}$:

$$\mathcal{Z} = \sum_{N=0}^\infty z^N Z_N$$

**Properties**:
- $\mathcal{Z}(T, V, \mu)$ is the generating function
- Sum over all possible particle numbers
- Weights by chemical potential (favors large $N$ if $\mu > 0$, small $N$ if $\mu < 0$)

## Grand Potential

**Grand potential** (or Landau free energy):

$$\boxed{\Phi = -k_B T \ln \mathcal{Z}}$$

This is the characteristic thermodynamic potential for grand canonical ensemble.

**Relation to other potentials**:

$$\Phi = F - \mu N = U - TS - \mu N$$

where $F$ is Helmholtz free energy.

**Fundamental relation**:

$$d\Phi = -S dT - P dV - N d\mu$$

Compare with Gibbs free energy $dG = -S dT + V dP + \mu dN$:

$$\Phi = -PV$$

So:

$$\boxed{PV = k_B T \ln \mathcal{Z}}$$

This directly connects pressure to grand partition function!

## Average Particle Number

**Average particle number**:

$$\langle N \rangle = \sum_{N,i} N \cdot P(N,i) = \frac{1}{\mathcal{Z}} \sum_{N,i} N e^{\beta(\mu N - E_i)}$$

**Derivative formula**:

$$\boxed{\langle N \rangle = \frac{1}{\beta}\frac{\partial \ln \mathcal{Z}}{\partial \mu}\bigg|_{T,V}}$$

**Derivation**:

$$\frac{\partial \ln \mathcal{Z}}{\partial \mu} = \frac{1}{\mathcal{Z}}\frac{\partial \mathcal{Z}}{\partial \mu} = \frac{1}{\mathcal{Z}}\sum_{N,i} \beta N e^{\beta(\mu N - E_i)} = \beta \langle N \rangle$$

**Alternative**: From $\Phi = -PV$:

$$\langle N \rangle = -\left(\frac{\partial \Phi}{\partial \mu}\right)_{T,V} = -\frac{\partial(-PV)}{\partial \mu} = V\frac{\partial P}{\partial \mu}$$

## Particle Number Fluctuations

**Variance of particle number**:

$$\langle (\Delta N)^2 \rangle = \langle N^2 \rangle - \langle N \rangle^2$$

Using derivatives of $\mathcal{Z}$:

$$\boxed{\langle (\Delta N)^2 \rangle = k_B T \left(\frac{\partial \langle N \rangle}{\partial \mu}\right)_{T,V}}$$

**Relative fluctuations**:

$$\frac{\sigma_N}{\langle N \rangle} = \frac{\sqrt{\langle (\Delta N)^2 \rangle}}{\langle N \rangle} \propto \frac{1}{\sqrt{\langle N \rangle}}$$

For macroscopic systems ($\langle N \rangle \sim 10^{23}$), relative fluctuations are tiny—canonical and grand canonical give essentially identical results.

## Fugacity

**Fugacity** $z$ is defined:

$$z = e^{\beta \mu}$$

**Physical meaning**: Fugacity is the "effective concentration" controlling particle number.

**Grand partition function** in terms of fugacity:

$$\mathcal{Z} = \sum_{N=0}^\infty z^N Z_N(T, V)$$

**Average particle number**:

$$\langle N \rangle = z\frac{\partial \ln \mathcal{Z}}{\partial z}\bigg|_{T,V}$$

**Low density limit** (classical ideal gas):

$$z \ll 1 \implies \mu \ll k_B T$$ (negative, large magnitude)

**High density**: $z$ can become order unity or larger.

## Connection Between Ensembles

**Microcanonical** $(E, V, N)$ → **Canonical** $(T, V, N)$ → **Grand canonical** $(T, V, \mu)$

Each successive ensemble allows more fluctuations:
- Microcanonical: No fluctuations (all fixed)
- Canonical: Energy fluctuates
- Grand canonical: Energy and particle number fluctuate

**Thermodynamic limit** ($N \to \infty$, $V \to \infty$, $N/V$ fixed):

All ensembles give identical thermodynamic results—fluctuations become negligible.

**Choice of ensemble**: Use whichever has fixed variables matching experimental control.

## Example 1: Grand Partition Function for Single Site

Single adsorption site: can be empty ($N = 0$, $E = 0$) or occupied ($N = 1$, $E = -\epsilon$). Find $\mathcal{Z}$ and $\langle N \rangle$.

**Solution:**

$$\mathcal{Z} = \sum_{N=0}^1 e^{\beta(\mu N - E_N)}$$

$$= e^0 + e^{\beta(\mu - (-\epsilon))} = 1 + e^{\beta(\mu + \epsilon)}$$

**Average occupancy**:

$$\langle N \rangle = \frac{1}{\beta}\frac{\partial \ln \mathcal{Z}}{\partial \mu}$$

$$= \frac{1}{\beta} \cdot \frac{1}{1 + e^{\beta(\mu + \epsilon)}} \cdot \beta e^{\beta(\mu + \epsilon)}$$

$$= \frac{e^{\beta(\mu + \epsilon)}}{1 + e^{\beta(\mu + \epsilon)}} = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-\beta(\mu + \epsilon)}}$$

**Physical interpretation**: This is Fermi-Dirac form! Site occupation probability depends on chemical potential and binding energy.

**Answer:** $\mathcal{Z} = 1 + e^{\beta(\mu + \epsilon)}$, $\langle N \rangle = 1/(1 + e^{-\beta(\mu + \epsilon)})$

## Example 2: Grand Potential Calculation

System with $\mathcal{Z} = 1 + 2e^{\beta \mu}$ at $T = 300$ K. Find grand potential $\Phi$.

**Solution:**

$$\Phi = -k_B T \ln \mathcal{Z} = -k_B T \ln(1 + 2e^{\beta \mu})$$

At specific $\mu$: suppose $\beta \mu = 1$ (so $\mu = k_B T$):

$$\mathcal{Z} = 1 + 2e^1 = 1 + 2(2.718) = 6.436$$

$$\Phi = -(1.381 \times 10^{-23})(300)\ln(6.436)$$

$$= -4.14 \times 10^{-21} \times 1.862 = -7.71 \times 10^{-21} \text{ J}$$

**Connection to pressure**: $\Phi = -PV$, so:

$$P = -\frac{\Phi}{V}$$

**Answer:** $\Phi = -k_B T \ln(1 + 2e^{\beta \mu})$

## Example 3: Particle Number from Derivative

For $\mathcal{Z} = e^{\alpha z}$ where $z = e^{\beta \mu}$ and $\alpha$ is constant, find $\langle N \rangle$.

**Solution:**

$$\ln \mathcal{Z} = \alpha z = \alpha e^{\beta \mu}$$

$$\langle N \rangle = \frac{1}{\beta}\frac{\partial \ln \mathcal{Z}}{\partial \mu} = \frac{1}{\beta}\frac{\partial}{\partial \mu}(\alpha e^{\beta \mu})$$

$$= \frac{\alpha}{\beta} \cdot \beta e^{\beta \mu} = \alpha e^{\beta \mu} = \alpha z$$

**Physical interpretation**: Average particle number proportional to fugacity—higher chemical potential drives more particles into system.

**Answer:** $\langle N \rangle = \alpha e^{\beta \mu}$

## Example 4: Fluctuations in Ideal Gas

Ideal gas grand canonical: $\langle N \rangle = PV/(k_B T)$. Find particle number fluctuations.

**Solution:**

Fluctuation relation:

$$\langle (\Delta N)^2 \rangle = k_B T \left(\frac{\partial \langle N \rangle}{\partial \mu}\right)_{T,V}$$

For ideal gas: $\langle N \rangle = PV/(k_B T)$

From $P = nk_B T/V = \langle N \rangle k_B T/V$:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial \mu} = \frac{k_B T}{V}\frac{\partial \langle N \rangle}{\partial \mu}$$

Using thermodynamic relation: $\partial P/\partial \mu = n/V$ for ideal gas (where $n = \langle N \rangle$):

$$\frac{\partial \langle N \rangle}{\partial \mu} = \frac{\langle N \rangle}{k_B T}$$

Therefore:

$$\langle (\Delta N)^2 \rangle = k_B T \cdot \frac{\langle N \rangle}{k_B T} = \langle N \rangle$$

**Relative fluctuation**:

$$\frac{\sigma_N}{\langle N \rangle} = \frac{\sqrt{\langle N \rangle}}{\langle N \rangle} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\langle N \rangle}}$$

For $\langle N \rangle = 10^{23}$: $\sigma_N/\langle N \rangle = 10^{-11.5}$ (tiny!)

**Answer:** $\langle (\Delta N)^2 \rangle = \langle N \rangle$, relative fluctuation $\propto 1/\sqrt{\langle N \rangle}$

## Example 5: Pressure from Grand Partition Function

System with $\mathcal{Z} = (1 + ze^{-\beta \epsilon})^M$ where $M$ is number of sites. Find pressure.

**Solution:**

$$\Phi = -k_B T \ln \mathcal{Z} = -k_B T \cdot M \ln(1 + ze^{-\beta \epsilon})$$

$$= -Mk_B T \ln(1 + e^{\beta(\mu - \epsilon)})$$

Pressure:

$$P = -\frac{\Phi}{V}$$

If system has $M$ sites in volume $V$:

$$P = \frac{Mk_B T}{V}\ln(1 + e^{\beta(\mu - \epsilon)})$$

**Physical interpretation**: Pressure increases with site density $M/V$, temperature, and fugacity (controlled by $\mu$).

**Answer:** $P = (Mk_B T/V)\ln(1 + e^{\beta(\mu - \epsilon)})$

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: C** - Grand canonical: fixed **$(T, V, \mu)$** (exchanges energy and particles).

**Problem 2: B** - Grand partition function: $\mathcal{Z} = \sum_N e^{\beta \mu N} Z_N$ (sum over all $N$).

**Problem 3: B** - Grand potential: $\Phi = -PV$ (connects to pressure).

**Problem 4: C** - Average number: $\langle N \rangle = (1/\beta)(\partial \ln \mathcal{Z}/\partial \mu)$ (derivative w.r.t. $\mu$).

**Problem 5: D** - Fugacity: $z = e^{\beta \mu}$ (effective concentration parameter).
