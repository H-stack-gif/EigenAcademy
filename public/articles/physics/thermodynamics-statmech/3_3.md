# Article 3.3: Entropy and the Clausius Theorem

The Carnot cycle revealed that $Q/T$ has special significance—for reversible cycles, heat divided by temperature relates to efficiency in a fundamental way. Rudolf Clausius (1850s) generalized this observation: for any cyclic process, the integral $\oint \delta Q/T$ is not zero for irreversible cycles. This led to the discovery of a new state function: **entropy**.

Entropy is arguably the most profound concept in thermodynamics. Unlike energy (conserved), entropy increases in isolated systems—it's the quantitative measure of irreversibility. The **Clausius inequality** $\oint \delta Q/T \leq 0$ distinguishes reversible from irreversible processes. For reversible processes, equality holds, allowing us to define entropy through $dS = \delta Q_{rev}/T$. The resulting **principle of entropy increase** gives precise mathematical form to the second law.

This article develops the Clausius inequality, defines entropy as a state function, calculates entropy changes for various processes, explores irreversible entropy generation, and establishes the principle of entropy increase. The mathematics centers on line integrals, exact differentials, integrating factors, and inequality analysis.

## The Clausius Inequality

Consider any cyclic process with heat transfers at various temperatures. The **Clausius inequality** states:

$$\oint \frac{\delta Q}{T} \leq 0$$

with equality for **reversible** cycles:

$$\oint \frac{\delta Q}{T} = 0 \quad \text{(reversible)}$$

**Proof sketch**: Divide arbitrary cycle into infinitesimal Carnot cycles. For each:

$$\frac{\delta Q_H}{T_H} - \frac{\delta Q_C}{T_C} \leq 0$$

(equality for reversible). Summing over all infinitesimal cycles gives Clausius inequality.

**Physical meaning**: $\delta Q/T$ integrated around any cycle is negative (irreversible) or zero (reversible)—never positive.

## Entropy as State Function

The Clausius inequality for reversible cycles:

$$\oint \frac{\delta Q_{rev}}{T} = 0$$

means $\delta Q_{rev}/T$ is an **exact differential** of a state function. We call this **entropy** $S$:

$$\boxed{dS = \frac{\delta Q_{rev}}{T}}$$

This defines entropy change:

$$\Delta S = \int_i^f \frac{\delta Q_{rev}}{T}$$

Since $S$ is a state function:
- $\Delta S$ depends only on initial and final states
- $\oint dS = 0$ for any cycle
- We can calculate $\Delta S$ using any reversible path connecting states

**Units**: J/K (joules per kelvin)

## Temperature as Integrating Factor

Recall from Unit 1: heat $\delta Q$ is an inexact differential:

$$\oint \delta Q \neq 0$$

But temperature $T$ serves as **integrating factor**:

$$\oint \frac{\delta Q_{rev}}{T} = \oint dS = 0$$

This transforms inexact differential $\delta Q$ into exact differential $dS$. Temperature is the unique integrating factor (up to multiplicative constant).

## Second Law: Entropy Formulation

For **any** process (reversible or irreversible):

$$dS \geq \frac{\delta Q}{T}$$

with equality for reversible processes.

For **isolated system** ($\delta Q = 0$):

$$dS \geq 0$$

**Principle of entropy increase**: Entropy of isolated system never decreases.

- Reversible process: $\Delta S = 0$ (constant entropy)
- Irreversible process: $\Delta S > 0$ (entropy increases)

This is the **most general statement of second law**.

## Entropy Change Calculations

To calculate $\Delta S$ between states A and B:

1. Find any reversible path from A to B
2. Integrate: $\Delta S = \int_A^B \delta Q_{rev}/T$

Different reversible paths give same $\Delta S$ (state function property).

**For ideal gas** with $dU = nC_V dT$ and $\delta Q = dU + P dV$:

$$dS = \frac{dU + P dV}{T} = nC_V\frac{dT}{T} + nR\frac{dV}{V}$$

Integrating:

$$\Delta S = nC_V\ln\frac{T_f}{T_i} + nR\ln\frac{V_f}{V_i}$$

Alternative forms using $P$ and $V$ or $P$ and $T$.

## Entropy Change for Irreversible Processes

For **irreversible** process, we cannot use $\Delta S = \int \delta Q/T$ directly (this formula requires reversible path).

Instead:
1. Identify initial and final states
2. Find any reversible path between them
3. Calculate $\Delta S$ along reversible path
4. Result applies to irreversible process (same endpoints)

**Key point**: $\Delta S$ is same for reversible and irreversible processes with same endpoints (state function!), but paths differ.

For irreversible process in isolated system:

$$\Delta S_{total} = \Delta S_{system} + \Delta S_{surroundings} > 0$$

## Entropy Generation

Define **entropy generation** $S_{gen}$:

$$\Delta S = \int \frac{\delta Q}{T} + S_{gen}$$

where $S_{gen} \geq 0$ (zero for reversible, positive for irreversible).

Entropy generation quantifies irreversibility:
- $S_{gen} = 0$: reversible process
- $S_{gen} > 0$: irreversible process (larger value = more irreversible)

## Example 1: Entropy Change for Reversible Isothermal Process

One mole of ideal gas expands isothermally and reversibly at $T = 400$ K from $V_i = 10$ L to $V_f = 25$ L. Find $\Delta S$.

**Solution:**

For isothermal process: $dT = 0$

$$dS = nC_V\frac{dT}{T} + nR\frac{dV}{V} = 0 + nR\frac{dV}{V}$$

Integrating:

$$\Delta S = nR\ln\frac{V_f}{V_i} = (1)(8.314)\ln\frac{25}{10}$$

$$= 8.314 \times \ln(2.5) = 8.314 \times 0.916 = 7.62 \text{ J/K}$$

**Physical interpretation**: Gas becomes more disordered as it expands. Entropy increases even though process is reversible—heat $Q$ is absorbed from reservoir, increasing system entropy while decreasing reservoir entropy by equal amount.

**Answer:** $\Delta S = 7.62$ J/K

## Example 2: Entropy Change for Heating at Constant Volume

Two moles of ideal gas ($C_V = \frac{5}{2}R$) heat from $T_i = 300$ K to $T_f = 400$ K at constant volume. Find $\Delta S$.

**Solution:**

At constant volume: $dV = 0$

$$dS = nC_V\frac{dT}{T}$$

Integrating:

$$\Delta S = nC_V\ln\frac{T_f}{T_i} = (2)\left(\frac{5}{2} \times 8.314\right)\ln\frac{400}{300}$$

$$= 20.785 \times \ln(1.333) = 20.785 \times 0.2877 = 5.98 \text{ J/K}$$

**Physical interpretation**: Heating increases molecular kinetic energy and disorder, increasing entropy.

**Answer:** $\Delta S = 5.98$ J/K

## Example 3: Clausius Inequality for Carnot Cycle

Verify Clausius inequality $\oint \delta Q/T = 0$ for reversible Carnot cycle.

**Solution:**

Carnot cycle has four steps:

1. **Isothermal expansion** at $T_H$: absorbs $Q_H$
   $$\int_1 \frac{\delta Q}{T} = \frac{Q_H}{T_H}$$

2. **Adiabatic expansion**: $Q = 0$
   $$\int_2 \frac{\delta Q}{T} = 0$$

3. **Isothermal compression** at $T_C$: rejects $Q_C$
   $$\int_3 \frac{\delta Q}{T} = -\frac{Q_C}{T_C}$$

4. **Adiabatic compression**: $Q = 0$
   $$\int_4 \frac{\delta Q}{T} = 0$$

**Total**:

$$\oint \frac{\delta Q}{T} = \frac{Q_H}{T_H} - \frac{Q_C}{T_C}$$

For reversible Carnot cycle: $Q_C/Q_H = T_C/T_H$, so:

$$\oint \frac{\delta Q}{T} = \frac{Q_H}{T_H} - \frac{Q_H T_C/T_H}{T_C} = \frac{Q_H}{T_H} - \frac{Q_H}{T_H} = 0$$ ✓

**Answer:** Verified: $\oint \delta Q/T = 0$ for reversible Carnot cycle

## Example 4: Entropy Generation in Irreversible Process

A gas expands freely (Joule expansion) from $V_i = 5$ L to $V_f = 15$ L at constant $T = 300$ K. Calculate:
(a) $\Delta S$ for the gas
(b) Entropy generation $S_{gen}$

**Solution:**

(a) Free expansion is irreversible, but entropy is state function. Use reversible isothermal path with same endpoints:

$$\Delta S = nR\ln\frac{V_f}{V_i}$$

Assuming 1 mole:

$$\Delta S = (1)(8.314)\ln\frac{15}{5} = 8.314 \times \ln(3) = 8.314 \times 1.099 = 9.14 \text{ J/K}$$

(b) For free expansion: $Q = 0$ (isolated), $W = 0$ (vacuum).

Entropy generation:

$$S_{gen} = \Delta S - \int\frac{\delta Q}{T} = \Delta S - 0 = 9.14 \text{ J/K}$$

**Physical interpretation**: Highly irreversible process generates significant entropy. System becomes more disordered without energy exchange.

**Answer:** (a) $\Delta S = 9.14$ J/K, (b) $S_{gen} = 9.14$ J/K

## Example 5: Total Entropy Change in Heat Transfer

A hot object at $T_H = 400$ K transfers $Q = 1000$ J to cold object at $T_C = 300$ K. Calculate:
(a) $\Delta S_{hot}$
(b) $\Delta S_{cold}$
(c) $\Delta S_{total}$

**Solution:**

(a) Hot object loses heat:

$$\Delta S_{hot} = -\frac{Q}{T_H} = -\frac{1000}{400} = -2.5 \text{ J/K}$$

(b) Cold object gains heat:

$$\Delta S_{cold} = +\frac{Q}{T_C} = \frac{1000}{300} = 3.33 \text{ J/K}$$

(c) Total (universe):

$$\Delta S_{total} = \Delta S_{hot} + \Delta S_{cold} = -2.5 + 3.33 = 0.83 \text{ J/K}$$

**Physical interpretation**: $\Delta S_{total} > 0$ confirms irreversible process (spontaneous heat flow). Universe entropy increases despite local entropy decrease in hot object.

**Answer:** (a) $-2.5$ J/K, (b) $+3.33$ J/K, (c) $+0.83$ J/K
