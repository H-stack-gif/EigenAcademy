# Article 2.5: Differential Equations in Thermodynamics

Thermodynamic processes unfold in time. A cup of coffee cools gradually toward room temperature. Heat diffuses through a metal rod from hot end to cold. A gas compressed adiabatically heats up along a specific trajectory in state space. Understanding these time-dependent and spatially-varying phenomena requires **differential equations**—mathematical descriptions of how rates of change relate to current states.

Two classes of differential equations appear in thermodynamics: **ordinary differential equations (ODEs)** describing processes depending on a single variable (usually time), and **partial differential equations (PDEs)** describing spatially-distributed systems where quantities vary in both space and time. The heat equation—a prototypical parabolic PDE—governs thermal diffusion and appears throughout physics and engineering.

This article develops the ODE and PDE techniques essential for thermodynamics: first-order separable equations, exponential decay solutions, Newton's law of cooling, and the one-dimensional heat diffusion equation with separation of variables. These mathematical tools enable us to predict temperature evolution, analyze thermal relaxation, and solve boundary value problems in heat transfer.

## First-Order ODEs in Thermodynamic Processes

Many thermodynamic processes are described by **first-order ordinary differential equations** relating rates to state variables.

**General form**:

$$\frac{dy}{dx} = f(x, y)$$

**Separable equations** (variables separate):

$$\frac{dy}{dx} = g(x)h(y)$$

can be solved by separating:

$$\frac{dy}{h(y)} = g(x) dx$$

Integrating both sides:

$$\int \frac{dy}{h(y)} = \int g(x) dx + C$$

## Coupled Differential Equations

Thermodynamic processes often involve **coupled** differential equations. For example, adiabatic process:

$$dU = -P dV$$

with $U = nC_V T$ and $P = nRT/V$ gives:

$$nC_V dT = -\frac{nRT}{V} dV$$

These couple $T$ and $V$:

$$\frac{dT}{T} = -\frac{R}{C_V} \frac{dV}{V}$$

Separable! Integrating gives $TV^{\gamma-1} = \text{const}$.

## Newton's Law of Cooling

**Newton's law of cooling** states that cooling rate is proportional to temperature difference with environment:

$$\frac{dT}{dt} = -k(T - T_{env})$$

where $k > 0$ is the cooling constant (units: s$^{-1}$).

This is a **first-order linear ODE**. Rearranging:

$$\frac{dT}{T - T_{env}} = -k dt$$

Integrating:

$$\ln|T - T_{env}| = -kt + C$$

$$T - T_{env} = Ae^{-kt}$$

Using initial condition $T(0) = T_0$:

$$A = T_0 - T_{env}$$

**Solution**:

$$T(t) = T_{env} + (T_0 - T_{env})e^{-kt}$$

**Physical interpretation**:
- **Exponential decay** toward environment temperature
- Time constant: $\tau = 1/k$ (time to decay by factor $e$)
- After time $t = \tau$: $T - T_{env} = (T_0 - T_{env})/e \approx 0.37(T_0 - T_{env})$
- After $t = 5\tau$: practically at equilibrium ($< 1\%$ difference)

## Heat Diffusion Equation (1D)

The **heat equation** governs temperature distribution in materials:

$$\frac{\partial T}{\partial t} = \alpha \frac{\partial^2 T}{\partial x^2}$$

where $\alpha = k/(\rho c)$ is **thermal diffusivity** (m²/s):
- $k$ = thermal conductivity
- $\rho$ = density
- $c$ = specific heat

This is a **parabolic PDE**—temperature at each point evolves based on curvature of temperature profile.

**Physical interpretation**:
- $\partial^2 T/\partial x^2 > 0$: concave up, temperature increases
- $\partial^2 T/\partial x^2 < 0$: concave down, temperature decreases
- $\partial^2 T/\partial x^2 = 0$: linear profile, no change (steady state)

## Separation of Variables for PDEs

To solve heat equation, use **separation of variables**. Assume:

$$T(x, t) = X(x) \Theta(t)$$

Substituting into heat equation:

$$X \frac{d\Theta}{dt} = \alpha \Theta \frac{d^2 X}{dx^2}$$

Dividing by $X\Theta$:

$$\frac{1}{\Theta} \frac{d\Theta}{dt} = \frac{\alpha}{X} \frac{d^2 X}{dx^2}$$

Left side depends only on $t$, right only on $x$—both must equal constant $-\lambda$:

$$\frac{1}{\Theta} \frac{d\Theta}{dt} = -\lambda$$

$$\frac{\alpha}{X} \frac{d^2 X}{dx^2} = -\lambda$$

This separates into two ODEs:

$$\frac{d\Theta}{dt} = -\lambda \Theta \implies \Theta(t) = Ce^{-\lambda t}$$

$$\frac{d^2 X}{dx^2} = -\frac{\lambda}{\alpha} X$$

The spatial equation requires boundary conditions to determine $\lambda$ and $X(x)$.

## Steady-State Solutions

**Steady state**: temperature distribution doesn't change with time ($\partial T/\partial t = 0$).

Heat equation reduces to **Laplace equation**:

$$\frac{\partial^2 T}{\partial x^2} = 0$$

General solution:

$$T(x) = Ax + B$$

Linear temperature profile! Boundaries determine $A$ and $B$.

For rod with ends at $T_1$ and $T_2$:

$$T(x) = T_1 + \frac{T_2 - T_1}{L}x$$

## Boundary and Initial Conditions

Solving heat equation requires:

**Initial condition**: $T(x, 0) = f(x)$ (temperature distribution at $t = 0$)

**Boundary conditions** (two for second-order spatial derivative):
- **Dirichlet**: $T(0, t) = T_1$, $T(L, t) = T_2$ (fixed temperature at boundaries)
- **Neumann**: $\partial T/\partial x|_{x=0} = 0$ (insulated boundary, no heat flux)
- **Robin**: Mixed condition involving $T$ and $\partial T/\partial x$

## Example 1: Newton's Law of Cooling

Coffee at $T_0 = 90°\text{C}$ cools in room at $T_{env} = 20°\text{C}$. After $t = 5$ min, temperature is $70°\text{C}$. Find:
(a) Cooling constant $k$
(b) Temperature after $15$ min
(c) Time to reach $30°\text{C}$

**Solution:**

Newton's law: $T(t) = T_{env} + (T_0 - T_{env})e^{-kt}$

(a) At $t = 5$ min:

$$70 = 20 + (90 - 20)e^{-5k}$$
$$50 = 70e^{-5k}$$
$$e^{-5k} = \frac{50}{70} = \frac{5}{7}$$
$$-5k = \ln(5/7) = -0.336$$
$$k = 0.0672 \text{ min}^{-1}$$

(b) At $t = 15$ min:

$$T(15) = 20 + 70e^{-0.0672 \times 15} = 20 + 70e^{-1.008}$$
$$= 20 + 70(0.365) = 20 + 25.6 = 45.6°\text{C}$$

(c) For $T = 30°\text{C}$:

$$30 = 20 + 70e^{-0.0672t}$$
$$10 = 70e^{-0.0672t}$$
$$e^{-0.0672t} = 1/7$$
$$-0.0672t = \ln(1/7) = -1.946$$
$$t = 29.0 \text{ min}$$

**Answer:** (a) $k = 0.067$ min$^{-1}$, (b) $45.6°\text{C}$, (c) $29$ min

## Example 2: Adiabatic Process ODE

Derive the relation $TV^{\gamma-1} = \text{const}$ for adiabatic ideal gas process by solving the differential equation.

**Solution:**

For adiabatic process: $dU = -P dV$

With $U = nC_V T$ and $P = nRT/V$:

$$nC_V dT = -\frac{nRT}{V} dV$$

$$C_V \frac{dT}{T} = -R \frac{dV}{V}$$

This is **separable**:

$$\frac{dT}{T} = -\frac{R}{C_V} \frac{dV}{V}$$

Integrate both sides:

$$\int \frac{dT}{T} = -\frac{R}{C_V} \int \frac{dV}{V}$$

$$\ln T = -\frac{R}{C_V} \ln V + \text{const}$$

Using $C_P - C_V = nR$ and $\gamma = C_P/C_V$:

$$\frac{R}{C_V} = \frac{C_P - C_V}{C_V} = \gamma - 1$$

Therefore:

$$\ln T = -(\gamma - 1) \ln V + \ln C$$

$$\ln T + (\gamma - 1) \ln V = \ln C$$

$$\ln(TV^{\gamma-1}) = \ln C$$

$$TV^{\gamma-1} = C = \text{const}$$

**Answer:** Derived $TV^{\gamma-1} = \text{const}$ from ODE

## Example 3: Exponential Decay Time Constant

A system cools according to $T(t) = 20 + 50e^{-0.1t}$ where $t$ is in minutes and $T$ in °C. Find:
(a) Time constant
(b) Time for temperature to drop to half its initial excess

**Solution:**

Comparing with $T(t) = T_{env} + (T_0 - T_{env})e^{-t/\tau}$:

- $T_{env} = 20°\text{C}$
- $T_0 - T_{env} = 50°\text{C}$, so $T_0 = 70°\text{C}$
- $k = 0.1$ min$^{-1}$

(a) **Time constant**:

$$\tau = \frac{1}{k} = \frac{1}{0.1} = 10 \text{ min}$$

(b) **Half-life**: When excess temperature drops to half:

$$T - T_{env} = \frac{1}{2}(T_0 - T_{env})$$

$$50e^{-0.1t} = 25$$

$$e^{-0.1t} = 0.5$$

$$-0.1t = \ln(0.5) = -0.693$$

$$t = 6.93 \text{ min}$$

**Relation**: Half-life $t_{1/2} = \tau \ln 2 \approx 0.693\tau$

**Answer:** (a) $\tau = 10$ min, (b) $t_{1/2} = 6.9$ min

## Example 4: Steady-State Temperature Distribution

Metal rod of length $L = 1.0$ m has left end at $T_1 = 100°\text{C}$ and right end at $T_2 = 20°\text{C}$. Find steady-state temperature at $x = 0.4$ m.

**Solution:**

Steady state satisfies $d^2T/dx^2 = 0$, giving linear profile:

$$T(x) = T_1 + \frac{T_2 - T_1}{L}x$$

At $x = 0.4$ m:

$$T(0.4) = 100 + \frac{20 - 100}{1.0}(0.4) = 100 + (-80)(0.4)$$

$$= 100 - 32 = 68°\text{C}$$

**Physical interpretation**: Linear temperature gradient from hot to cold end. Heat flux is constant throughout rod.

**Answer:** $T = 68°\text{C}$

## Example 5: Heat Equation with Separation of Variables

For heat equation $\partial T/\partial t = \alpha \partial^2 T/\partial x^2$ with boundary conditions $T(0, t) = T(L, t) = 0$, find the separated spatial equation and its general form.

**Solution:**

Using $T(x, t) = X(x)\Theta(t)$ and separation constant $-\lambda$:

Spatial equation:

$$\frac{d^2 X}{dx^2} = -\frac{\lambda}{\alpha} X$$

Define $\omega^2 = \lambda/\alpha$:

$$\frac{d^2 X}{dx^2} + \omega^2 X = 0$$

General solution:

$$X(x) = A\cos(\omega x) + B\sin(\omega x)$$

**Boundary conditions**:

$X(0) = 0 \Rightarrow A = 0$

$X(L) = 0 \Rightarrow B\sin(\omega L) = 0$

Non-trivial solution requires $\sin(\omega L) = 0$:

$$\omega_n L = n\pi, \quad n = 1, 2, 3, ...$$

$$\omega_n = \frac{n\pi}{L}$$

**Eigenfunction**:

$$X_n(x) = \sin\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right)$$

**Answer:** Spatial equation gives $X_n(x) = \sin(n\pi x/L)$
