# Quantum Mechanics Topic 9.1: Klein-Gordon Equation

When Paul Dirac sought a relativistic quantum equation in the late 1920s, the obvious first attempt was to simply take Einstein's energy-momentum relation $E^2 = p^2c^2 + m^2c^4$ and replace energy and momentum with their quantum operators. This leads to the **Klein-Gordon equation**, a second-order partial differential equation that seemed promising at first—it's manifestly Lorentz covariant and reduces to the free particle Schrödinger equation in the non-relativistic limit. Yet it harbored deep problems that would eventually require a complete rethinking of quantum mechanics.

The Klein-Gordon equation was actually discovered independently by Schrödinger before he developed his famous non-relativistic equation, but he rejected it due to its troubling features. The probability density derived from the Klein-Gordon equation can be *negative*—a physical impossibility if we interpret $\rho$ as the probability of finding a particle. The equation also admits negative energy solutions, suggesting an unstable vacuum where particles could cascade to ever-lower energy states. These pathologies seemed fatal until the development of quantum field theory revealed the equation's true role: not as a single-particle wave equation, but as a classical field equation that, when properly quantized, describes spin-0 particles like pions.

This article explores the Klein-Gordon equation from its relativistic foundations through its mathematical structure and physical interpretation. We'll see how demanding Lorentz invariance leads naturally to second-order equations, calculate solutions for free particles, confront the negative probability problem, and understand why the equation ultimately requires field-theoretic interpretation. The Klein-Gordon equation's failures taught physicists crucial lessons that shaped modern particle physics.

## Relativistic Wave Equations

The quest for a relativistic quantum theory begins with Einstein's energy-momentum relation:

$$E^2 = p^2c^2 + m^2c^4$$

Making the quantum replacements $E \to i\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial t}$ and $\mathbf{p} \to -i\hbar\nabla$:

$$-\hbar^2\frac{\partial^2\phi}{\partial t^2} = -\hbar^2c^2\nabla^2\phi + m^2c^4\phi$$

Rearranging gives the **Klein-Gordon equation**:

$$\left(\frac{1}{c^2}\frac{\partial^2}{\partial t^2} - \nabla^2 + \frac{m^2c^2}{\hbar^2}\right)\phi = 0$$

or in Lorentz covariant form:

$$(\Box + m^2c^2/\hbar^2)\phi = 0$$

where $\Box = \partial_\mu\partial^\mu = \frac{1}{c^2}\frac{\partial^2}{\partial t^2} - \nabla^2$ is the d'Alembertian operator.

### Example 1: Verifying Lorentz Covariance

Show that the Klein-Gordon equation is Lorentz covariant by verifying it transforms correctly under Lorentz transformations.

**Solution:**

Under a Lorentz transformation, coordinates transform as $x'^\mu = \Lambda^\mu_\nu x^\nu$ and the wave function as $\phi'(x') = \phi(x)$.

The d'Alembertian transforms as:
$$\Box' = \partial'_\mu\partial'^\mu = \frac{\partial x^\nu}{\partial x'^\mu}\frac{\partial x^\rho}{\partial x'^\mu}\partial_\nu\partial_\rho = \Lambda^\nu_\mu\Lambda^\rho_\mu\partial_\nu\partial_\rho$$

Using the Lorentz group property $\Lambda^\nu_\mu\Lambda^\rho_\mu = \eta^{\nu\rho}$:
$$\Box' = \eta^{\nu\rho}\partial_\nu\partial_\rho = \partial^\nu\partial_\nu = \Box$$

Since mass is Lorentz invariant, the equation $(\Box + m^2c^2/\hbar^2)\phi = 0$ has the same form in all inertial frames.

**Answer:** The Klein-Gordon equation is manifestly Lorentz covariant; its form is unchanged under Lorentz transformations.

### Example 2: Free Particle Solutions

Find the plane wave solutions to the Klein-Gordon equation.

**Solution:**

Try $\phi = Ae^{i(\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r} - \omega t)}$.

$$\frac{\partial^2\phi}{\partial t^2} = -\omega^2\phi, \quad \nabla^2\phi = -k^2\phi$$

Substituting into Klein-Gordon equation:
$$\left(-\frac{\omega^2}{c^2} + k^2 - \frac{m^2c^2}{\hbar^2}\right)\phi = 0$$

Dispersion relation:
$$\omega^2 = c^2k^2 + m^2c^4/\hbar^2$$

Therefore:
$$\omega = \pm c\sqrt{k^2 + m^2c^2/\hbar^2} = \pm E/\hbar$$

where $E = \sqrt{p^2c^2 + m^2c^4}$ with $p = \hbar k$.

**Answer:** Plane waves $\phi = Ae^{i(\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r} \pm Et/\hbar)}$ with both positive and negative energy solutions $E = \pm\sqrt{p^2c^2 + m^2c^4}$.

## The Probability Density Problem

For the Schrödinger equation, probability density is $\rho = |\psi|^2$. What is the analogous quantity for Klein-Gordon?

From the continuity equation, we need $\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t} + \nabla\cdot\mathbf{j} = 0$.

### Example 3: Deriving the Continuity Equation

Derive a continuity equation from the Klein-Gordon equation.

**Solution:**

Multiply Klein-Gordon by $\phi^*$:
$$\phi^*\frac{\partial^2\phi}{\partial t^2} - c^2\phi^*\nabla^2\phi + \frac{m^2c^4}{\hbar^2}\phi^*\phi = 0$$

Take complex conjugate and multiply by $\phi$:
$$\phi\frac{\partial^2\phi^*}{\partial t^2} - c^2\phi\nabla^2\phi^* + \frac{m^2c^4}{\hbar^2}\phi\phi^* = 0$$

Subtract the two equations:
$$\phi^*\frac{\partial^2\phi}{\partial t^2} - \phi\frac{\partial^2\phi^*}{\partial t^2} = c^2(\phi^*\nabla^2\phi - \phi\nabla^2\phi^*)$$

Recognize:
$$\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(\phi^*\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t} - \phi\frac{\partial\phi^*}{\partial t}\right) = c^2\nabla\cdot(\phi^*\nabla\phi - \phi\nabla\phi^*)$$

**Answer:** Continuity equation $\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t} + \nabla\cdot\mathbf{j} = 0$ where $\rho = \frac{i\hbar}{2mc^2}\left(\phi^*\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t} - \phi\frac{\partial\phi^*}{\partial t}\right)$ and $\mathbf{j} = \frac{-i\hbar}{2m}(\phi^*\nabla\phi - \phi\nabla\phi^*)$.

### Example 4: Negative Probability Problem

Calculate the probability density for a negative energy plane wave and show it can be negative.

**Solution:**

For $\phi = Ae^{i(\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r} + Et/\hbar)}$ (negative energy, $E < 0$):

$$\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t} = \frac{iE}{\hbar}\phi = -\frac{i|E|}{\hbar}\phi$$

$$\rho = \frac{i\hbar}{2mc^2}\left(\phi^*\frac{iE}{\hbar}\phi - \phi\frac{-iE}{\hbar}\phi^*\right)$$

$$= \frac{i\hbar}{2mc^2}\cdot\frac{2iE}{\hbar}|\phi|^2 = -\frac{E}{mc^2}|A|^2$$

For negative energy $E = -|E|$:

$$\rho = \frac{E}{mc^2}|A|^2 = -\frac{|E|}{mc^2}|A|^2 < 0$$

**Answer:** Negative energy solutions yield negative probability density $\rho < 0$, violating the interpretation of $\rho$ as a probability.

### Example 5: Non-Relativistic Limit

Show that the Klein-Gordon equation reduces to the Schrödinger equation in the non-relativistic limit.

**Solution:**

Write $\phi = e^{-imc^2t/\hbar}\psi$ (factor out rest mass energy).

$$\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t} = e^{-imc^2t/\hbar}\left(\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t} - \frac{imc^2}{\hbar}\psi\right)$$

$$\frac{\partial^2\phi}{\partial t^2} = e^{-imc^2t/\hbar}\left(\frac{\partial^2\psi}{\partial t^2} - \frac{2imc^2}{\hbar}\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t} - \frac{m^2c^4}{\hbar^2}\psi\right)$$

Substitute into Klein-Gordon:
$$\frac{1}{c^2}\left(\frac{\partial^2\psi}{\partial t^2} - \frac{2imc^2}{\hbar}\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t} - \frac{m^2c^4}{\hbar^2}\psi\right) - \nabla^2\psi + \frac{m^2c^2}{\hbar^2}\psi = 0$$

Assuming $\psi$ varies slowly: $|\frac{\partial^2\psi}{\partial t^2}| \ll \frac{mc^2}{\hbar}|\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t}|$, drop second time derivative:

$$-\frac{2imc^2}{\hbar c^2}\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t} - \nabla^2\psi = 0$$

$$i\hbar\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t} = -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\nabla^2\psi$$

**Answer:** In non-relativistic limit, Klein-Gordon reduces to free particle Schrödinger equation.

## Solutions and Physical Interpretation

### Example 6: Energy Spectrum

For a particle in a 1D box of length $L$, find the energy spectrum from the Klein-Gordon equation.

**Solution:**

Inside box: $\phi(x) = A\sin(kx) + B\cos(kx)$

Boundary conditions: $\phi(0) = \phi(L) = 0$

From $\phi(0) = 0$: $B = 0$

From $\phi(L) = 0$: $\sin(kL) = 0$ → $k_n = n\pi/L$ where $n = 1, 2, 3, \ldots$

Energy from dispersion:
$$E_n = \pm c\sqrt{\hbar^2k_n^2 + m^2c^2} = \pm c\sqrt{\frac{n^2\pi^2\hbar^2}{L^2} + m^2c^2}$$

For large $n$: $E_n \approx \pm\frac{n\pi\hbar c}{L}$ (ultra-relativistic)

For small $n$: $E_n \approx \pm mc^2\left(1 + \frac{n^2\pi^2\hbar^2}{2m^2c^2L^2}\right) \approx \pm\left(mc^2 + \frac{n^2\pi^2\hbar^2}{2mL^2}\right)$

**Answer:** Energy spectrum $E_n = \pm c\sqrt{\frac{n^2\pi^2\hbar^2}{L^2} + m^2c^2}$ with both positive and negative branches.

### Example 7: Current Density

Calculate the current density $\mathbf{j}$ for a plane wave solution.

**Solution:**

For $\phi = Ae^{i(\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r} - \omega t)}$:

$$\mathbf{j} = \frac{-i\hbar}{2m}(\phi^*\nabla\phi - \phi\nabla\phi^*)$$

$$\nabla\phi = i\mathbf{k}\phi, \quad \nabla\phi^* = -i\mathbf{k}\phi^*$$

$$\mathbf{j} = \frac{-i\hbar}{2m}(\phi^*\cdot i\mathbf{k}\phi - \phi\cdot(-i\mathbf{k})\phi^*)$$

$$= \frac{-i\hbar}{2m}\cdot 2i\mathbf{k}|\phi|^2 = \frac{\hbar\mathbf{k}}{m}|A|^2$$

$$= \frac{\mathbf{p}}{m}|A|^2$$

**Answer:** Current density $\mathbf{j} = \frac{\mathbf{p}}{m}|A|^2$, proportional to momentum and probability density magnitude.

### Example 8: Coupling to Electromagnetic Field

Write the Klein-Gordon equation for a charged particle in an electromagnetic field.

**Solution:**

Minimal coupling prescription:
$$p^\mu \to p^\mu - \frac{e}{c}A^\mu$$

$$\partial_\mu \to D_\mu = \partial_\mu + i\frac{e}{\hbar c}A_\mu$$

Klein-Gordon becomes:
$$\left[\left(\partial_\mu + i\frac{e}{\hbar c}A_\mu\right)\left(\partial^\mu + i\frac{e}{\hbar c}A^\mu\right) + \frac{m^2c^2}{\hbar^2}\right]\phi = 0$$

Expanding:
$$\left[\frac{1}{c^2}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t} + i\frac{e}{\hbar c}A^0\right)^2 - \left(\nabla - i\frac{e}{\hbar c}\mathbf{A}\right)^2 + \frac{m^2c^2}{\hbar^2}\right]\phi = 0$$

**Answer:** Klein-Gordon with EM coupling: $\left(D_\mu D^\mu + m^2c^2/\hbar^2\right)\phi = 0$ where $D_\mu = \partial_\mu + ieA_\mu/(\hbar c)$.

### Example 9: Hydrogen-like Atom Ground State Energy

Estimate the ground state energy for a Klein-Gordon "hydrogen atom" (charged spin-0 particle).

**Solution:**

For Coulomb potential $A^0 = -e/(4\pi\epsilon_0 r)$, the bound state equation is:

$$\left[\frac{1}{c^2}\left(E + \frac{e^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0 r}\right)^2 - \nabla^2 + \frac{m^2c^2}{\hbar^2}\right]\phi = 0$$

Ground state energy (exact solution involves confluent hypergeometric functions):

$$E_0 = mc^2\sqrt{1 - (Z\alpha)^2}$$

where $\alpha = e^2/(4\pi\epsilon_0\hbar c) \approx 1/137$ is the fine structure constant.

For hydrogen ($Z = 1$):
$$E_0 \approx mc^2\left(1 - \frac{\alpha^2}{2}\right) = mc^2 - \frac{mc^2\alpha^2}{2} \approx mc^2 - 13.6\text{ eV}$$

Binding energy: $mc^2 - E_0 \approx 13.6\text{ eV}$ (agrees with Bohr/Schrödinger for $s$-states).

**Answer:** Ground state $E_0 = mc^2\sqrt{1 - \alpha^2}$, giving binding energy $\approx 13.6$ eV for hydrogen.

## Field Theoretic Resolution

The resolution of the Klein-Gordon paradoxes comes from reinterpreting the equation.

### Example 10: Second Quantization Sketch

Describe how treating $\phi$ as a quantum field resolves the negative probability problem.

**Solution:**

In quantum field theory:

1. $\phi(x)$ becomes a **field operator** (not a wave function)

2. Expand in modes:
$$\hat{\phi}(\mathbf{r},t) = \int\frac{d^3k}{(2\pi)^{3/2}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\omega_k}}[\hat{a}_\mathbf{k}e^{i(\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r} - \omega_k t)} + \hat{a}^\dagger_\mathbf{k}e^{-i(\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r} - \omega_k t)}]$$

where $\omega_k = \sqrt{c^2k^2 + m^2c^4/\hbar^2}$

3. $\hat{a}_\mathbf{k}$ annihilates particles, $\hat{a}^\dagger_\mathbf{k}$ creates particles

4. **Both** terms have positive energy $\omega_k > 0$

5. "Negative energy" solutions correspond to **antiparticle creation** (not negative energy states)

6. Probability interpretation: $|\phi|^2$ is replaced by particle number operators

**Answer:** Field quantization: $\phi$ is operator, negative frequency modes create antiparticles, all energies positive, probability → particle number.

### Example 11: Charge Conjugation

For Klein-Gordon with EM field, show that charge conjugation ($\phi \to \phi^*, e \to -e$) is a symmetry.

**Solution:**

Original equation:
$$\left[\left(\partial_\mu + i\frac{e}{\hbar c}A_\mu\right)\left(\partial^\mu + i\frac{e}{\hbar c}A^\mu\right) + \frac{m^2c^2}{\hbar^2}\right]\phi = 0$$

Under $\phi \to \phi^*, e \to -e$:
$$\left[\left(\partial_\mu - i\frac{e}{\hbar c}A_\mu\right)\left(\partial^\mu - i\frac{e}{\hbar c}A^\mu\right) + \frac{m^2c^2}{\hbar^2}\right]\phi^* = 0$$

Taking complex conjugate of original equation also yields this form.

**Answer:** Charge conjugation $(\phi, e) \to (\phi^*, -e)$ is symmetry of Klein-Gordon equation, relating particles to antiparticles.

### Example 12: Klein Paradox

A particle encounters a potential step with $V_0 > E + mc^2$ (strong potential). What happens?

**Solution:**

For Klein-Gordon equation, the effective momentum in the barrier is:

$$k_2 = \frac{1}{\hbar}\sqrt{(E - V_0)^2 - m^2c^4}$$

When $V_0 > E + mc^2$, we have $(E - V_0)^2 > m^2c^4$, so $k_2$ is **real**, not imaginary!

The wave is not exponentially damped—instead it **propagates** in the barrier.

Interpretation: The strong potential can create particle-antiparticle pairs from the vacuum. The barrier doesn't simply reflect—it becomes a source of particles.

This is the **Klein paradox**: Classical barrier penetration breaks down for super-strong potentials requiring pair creation.

**Answer:** For $V_0 > E + mc^2$, Klein-Gordon admits propagating (not evanescent) solutions—the Klein paradox, resolved by pair production in QFT.

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: B**
Klein-Gordon is second-order in both space AND time: $\partial^2/\partial t^2$ and $\nabla^2$
This distinguishes it from Schrödinger (first-order in time)
Second-order in time allows both positive and negative energy solutions
Consequence: Need both $\phi$ and $\dot{\phi}$ as initial data
Lorentz covariance requires second-order (from $E^2 = p^2c^2 + m^2c^4$)

**Problem 2: C**
Critical flaw of Klein-Gordon as wave equation
Probability density $\rho = \frac{i\hbar}{2mc^2}(\phi^*\dot{\phi} - \phi\dot{\phi}^*)$
For negative energy solutions: $\rho < 0$ (unphysical!)
Cannot interpret as single-particle probability
Resolution: Reinterpret as quantum field, not wave function

**Problem 3: B**
Factor out rest mass: $\phi = e^{-imc^2t/\hbar}\psi$
Slowly varying approximation: $|\ddot{\psi}| \ll \frac{mc^2}{\hbar}|\dot{\psi}|$
Leads to $i\hbar\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t} = -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\nabla^2\psi$ (Schrödinger)
Consistent check: Schrödinger is non-relativistic limit of Klein-Gordon
Energy: $E = mc^2 + E_{kin}$ where $E_{kin}$ small

**Problem 4: C**
From relativistic energy-momentum: $E^2 = p^2c^2 + m^2c^4$
With $E = \hbar\omega$ and $p = \hbar k$: $\omega^2 = c^2k^2 + m^2c^4/\hbar^2$
Both positive and negative frequency solutions: $\omega = \pm\sqrt{c^2k^2 + m^2c^4/\hbar^2}$
Massless limit ($m = 0$): $\omega = ck$ (photon dispersion)
Non-relativistic: $\omega \approx mc^2/\hbar + \hbar k^2/(2m)$

**Problem 5: D**
Klein-Gordon describes **spin-0** particles (scalars)
Examples: pions ($\pi^+, \pi^-, \pi^0$), Higgs boson
Spin-1/2 requires Dirac equation (four components)
No spin content in Klein-Gordon (single complex field)
In QFT: Field quantization with bosonic commutation relations
