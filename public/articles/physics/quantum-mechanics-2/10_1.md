# Quantum Mechanics Topic 10.1: Field Quantization and Photons

In classical physics, light is an electromagnetic wave—a smooth, continuous field oscillating through space. But the photoelectric effect and blackbody radiation forced physicists to accept that light also comes in discrete packets: **photons**. How can the same entity be both a wave (described by Maxwell's equations) and a particle (absorbed one quantum at a time)? The answer lies in **field quantization**, the process of promoting the classical electromagnetic field to a quantum operator. Each mode of the field becomes a quantum harmonic oscillator, and the excitation quanta of these oscillators are photons.

This quantization procedure transforms our understanding fundamentally. The classical field amplitude $\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{r}, t)$ becomes an operator $\hat{\mathbf{A}}(\mathbf{r}, t)$ built from creation and annihilation operators $\hat{a}^\dagger_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}$ and $\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}$ that add or remove photons from mode $(\mathbf{k}, \lambda)$. The energy is no longer continuous—it's quantized in units of $\hbar\omega$ for each mode. Even the vacuum (no photons present) has energy: the famous **zero-point energy** that leads to observable effects like the Casimir force. This framework doesn't just explain photons—it's the foundation for quantum electrodynamics and ultimately all quantum field theories.

This article develops the quantization of the electromagnetic field from Maxwell's equations through the harmonic oscillator analogy to the full operator formalism. We'll expand the field in modes, impose commutation relations, construct the photon number states (Fock states), and see how classical electromagnetic waves emerge from quantum coherent states. The journey from classical fields to quantum photons reveals the deep connection between waves and particles.

## Classical Electromagnetic Field

Maxwell's equations in vacuum (no sources):

$$\nabla \cdot \mathbf{E} = 0, \quad \nabla \times \mathbf{E} = -\frac{\partial \mathbf{B}}{\partial t}$$

$$\nabla \cdot \mathbf{B} = 0, \quad \nabla \times \mathbf{B} = \frac{1}{c^2}\frac{\partial \mathbf{E}}{\partial t}$$

Using vector potential $\mathbf{A}$ with Coulomb gauge $\nabla \cdot \mathbf{A} = 0$:

$$\mathbf{B} = \nabla \times \mathbf{A}, \quad \mathbf{E} = -\frac{\partial \mathbf{A}}{\partial t}$$

Wave equation: $\nabla^2\mathbf{A} - \frac{1}{c^2}\frac{\partial^2\mathbf{A}}{\partial t^2} = 0$

### Example 1: Plane Wave Solutions

Find the plane wave solutions to the classical field equations.

**Solution:**

For plane waves in box of volume $V = L^3$ with periodic boundary conditions:

$$\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{r}, t) = \sum_{\mathbf{k},\lambda} \mathbf{A}_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}(t) e^{i\mathbf{k} \cdot \mathbf{r}}$$

where $\mathbf{k} = \frac{2\pi}{L}(n_x, n_y, n_z)$ with integer $n_i$.

For each mode: $\mathbf{A}_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}(t) = A_{\mathbf{k},\lambda} \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\mathbf{k},\lambda} e^{-i\omega_k t}$

where $\omega_k = c|\mathbf{k}|$ (dispersion relation).

Polarization vectors $\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}$ (two transverse polarizations $\lambda = 1, 2$):
- Perpendicular to $\mathbf{k}$: $\mathbf{k} \cdot \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\mathbf{k},\lambda} = 0$
- Orthonormal: $\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\mathbf{k},\lambda} \cdot \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\mathbf{k},\lambda'} = \delta_{\lambda\lambda'}$

**Answer:** Plane waves $\mathbf{A} \sim \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}e^{i(\mathbf{k} \cdot \mathbf{r} - \omega_k t)}$ with $\omega_k = c|\mathbf{k}|$ and two transverse polarizations.

### Example 2: Field Energy and Hamiltonian

Calculate the energy of the electromagnetic field.

**Solution:**

Energy density: $u = \frac{1}{2}(\epsilon_0 E^2 + \frac{1}{\mu_0}B^2)$

Using $\mathbf{E} = -\dot{\mathbf{A}}$ and $\mathbf{B} = \nabla \times \mathbf{A}$:

Total energy:
$$H = \int_V d^3r \left[\frac{\epsilon_0}{2}|\dot{\mathbf{A}}|^2 + \frac{1}{2\mu_0}|\nabla \times \mathbf{A}|^2\right]$$

For mode expansion with $A_{\mathbf{k}} = A_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}$:

$$H = \sum_{\mathbf{k},\lambda} \frac{V\epsilon_0}{2}[|\dot{A}_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}|^2 + \omega_k^2|A_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}|^2]$$

This is exactly the Hamiltonian for a collection of **independent harmonic oscillators**, one for each mode $(\mathbf{k}, \lambda)$.

**Answer:** Field energy $H = \sum_{\mathbf{k},\lambda} \frac{V\epsilon_0}{2}(\dot{A}_{\mathbf{k}}^2 + \omega_k^2 A_{\mathbf{k}}^2)$ (sum of harmonic oscillators).

## Quantization Procedure

To quantize, promote classical variables to operators and impose commutation relations.

### Example 3: Canonical Quantization

Apply canonical quantization to the electromagnetic field.

**Solution:**

Classical field amplitude: $A_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}(t)$ (complex number)

Conjugate momentum: $\Pi_{\mathbf{k},\lambda} = \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \dot{A}_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}} = V\epsilon_0 \dot{A}_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}$

**Quantization**: Replace with operators satisfying:
$$[\hat{A}_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}, \hat{\Pi}_{\mathbf{k}',\lambda'}] = i\hbar \delta_{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{k}'}\delta_{\lambda\lambda'}$$

Define creation and annihilation operators:
$$\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k},\lambda} = \sqrt{\frac{V\epsilon_0\omega_k}{2\hbar}}\left(\hat{A}_{\mathbf{k},\lambda} + \frac{i}{\omega_k}\hat{\Pi}_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}\right)$$

$$\hat{a}^\dagger_{\mathbf{k},\lambda} = \sqrt{\frac{V\epsilon_0\omega_k}{2\hbar}}\left(\hat{A}_{\mathbf{k},\lambda} - \frac{i}{\omega_k}\hat{\Pi}_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}\right)$$

Inverting:
$$\hat{A}_{\mathbf{k},\lambda} = \sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2V\epsilon_0\omega_k}}(\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k},\lambda} + \hat{a}^\dagger_{\mathbf{k},\lambda})$$

**Answer:** Quantization: $\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}$, $\hat{a}^\dagger_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}$ satisfy bosonic commutation relations.

### Example 4: Commutation Relations

Verify the commutation relations for creation/annihilation operators.

**Solution:**

From canonical commutator $[\hat{A}_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}, \hat{\Pi}_{\mathbf{k}',\lambda'}] = i\hbar \delta_{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{k}'}\delta_{\lambda\lambda'}$:

$$[\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}, \hat{a}^\dagger_{\mathbf{k}',\lambda'}] = \frac{V\epsilon_0\omega_k}{2\hbar}\left[\hat{A}_{\mathbf{k},\lambda} + \frac{i}{\omega_k}\hat{\Pi}_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}, \hat{A}_{\mathbf{k}',\lambda'} - \frac{i}{\omega_{k'}}\hat{\Pi}_{\mathbf{k}',\lambda'}\right]$$

$$= \frac{V\epsilon_0\omega_k}{2\hbar}\left[\frac{i}{\omega_k}[\hat{\Pi}_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}, \hat{A}_{\mathbf{k}',\lambda'}] - \frac{i}{\omega_{k'}}[\hat{A}_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}, \hat{\Pi}_{\mathbf{k}',\lambda'}]\right]$$

$$= \frac{V\epsilon_0\omega_k}{2\hbar}\left(-\frac{i}{\omega_k}(-i\hbar) - \frac{i}{\omega_k}i\hbar\right)\delta_{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{k}'}\delta_{\lambda\lambda'}$$

$$= \delta_{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{k}'}\delta_{\lambda\lambda'}$$

Similarly: $[\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}, \hat{a}_{\mathbf{k}',\lambda'}] = 0$ and $[\hat{a}^\dagger_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}, \hat{a}^\dagger_{\mathbf{k}',\lambda'}] = 0$

**Answer:** Bosonic commutation relations: $[\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}, \hat{a}^\dagger_{\mathbf{k}',\lambda'}] = \delta_{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{k}'}\delta_{\lambda\lambda'}$, others zero.

### Example 5: Quantized Hamiltonian

Express the Hamiltonian in terms of creation/annihilation operators.

**Solution:**

Classical: $H = \sum_{\mathbf{k},\lambda} \frac{V\epsilon_0}{2}(\dot{A}_{\mathbf{k}}^2 + \omega_k^2 A_{\mathbf{k}}^2)$

Substituting operator expressions:
$$\hat{H} = \sum_{\mathbf{k},\lambda} \frac{\hbar\omega_k}{2}[(\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k},\lambda} - \hat{a}^\dagger_{\mathbf{k},\lambda})(\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k},\lambda} - \hat{a}^\dagger_{\mathbf{k},\lambda})^{\dagger} + (\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k},\lambda} + \hat{a}^\dagger_{\mathbf{k},\lambda})^2]$$

Expanding and using commutation relations:
$$\hat{H} = \sum_{\mathbf{k},\lambda} \hbar\omega_k\left(\hat{a}^\dagger_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k},\lambda} + \frac{1}{2}\right)$$

This is exactly the Hamiltonian for **independent quantum harmonic oscillators**.

**Answer:** $\hat{H} = \sum_{\mathbf{k},\lambda} \hbar\omega_k(\hat{N}_{\mathbf{k},\lambda} + \frac{1}{2})$ where $\hat{N}_{\mathbf{k},\lambda} = \hat{a}^\dagger_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}$ is photon number operator.

## Fock Space and Photon States

### Example 6: Vacuum State

Define the vacuum state and calculate its energy.

**Solution:**

**Vacuum**: State with no photons in any mode.

$$\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}|0\rangle = 0 \quad \text{for all } \mathbf{k}, \lambda$$

Vacuum energy:
$$E_0 = \langle 0|\hat{H}|0\rangle = \sum_{\mathbf{k},\lambda} \frac{\hbar\omega_k}{2}$$

This is **infinite** (sum over all modes)!

Physical interpretation:
- Zero-point energy of each oscillator mode
- Observable: Casimir effect (boundary conditions modify sum)
- Usually subtracted (normal ordering) or measured relative to vacuum

**Answer:** Vacuum $|0\rangle$: no photons, but infinite zero-point energy $E_0 = \sum_{\mathbf{k},\lambda} \frac{\hbar\omega_k}{2}$.

### Example 7: Single-Photon States

Construct and characterize single-photon states.

**Solution:**

Single photon in mode $(\mathbf{k}, \lambda)$:
$$|1_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}\rangle = \hat{a}^\dagger_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}|0\rangle$$

Properties:
- $\hat{N}_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}|1_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}\rangle = \hat{a}^\dagger_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}\hat{a}^\dagger_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}|0\rangle = \hat{a}^\dagger_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}([\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}, \hat{a}^\dagger_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}] + \hat{a}^\dagger_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k},\lambda})|0\rangle = |1_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}\rangle$

- Energy: $E = \hbar\omega_k + \sum \frac{\hbar\omega}{2}$ (one quantum plus vacuum)

- Normalized: $\langle 1_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}|1_{\mathbf{k}',\lambda'}\rangle = \delta_{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{k}'}\delta_{\lambda\lambda'}$

- Momentum: $\hbar\mathbf{k}$ (photon carries momentum)

- Polarization: $\lambda$ (two helicity states for massless spin-1)

**Answer:** $|1_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}\rangle = \hat{a}^\dagger_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}|0\rangle$ with energy $\hbar\omega_k$, momentum $\hbar\mathbf{k}$, polarization $\lambda$.

### Example 8: Multi-Photon Fock States

Construct general Fock states with multiple photons.

**Solution:**

General Fock state: $|n_1, n_2, n_3, \ldots\rangle$ where $n_i$ photons in mode $i$.

$$|n_1, n_2, \ldots\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{n_1! n_2! \cdots}} (\hat{a}^\dagger_1)^{n_1}(\hat{a}^\dagger_2)^{n_2}\cdots |0\rangle$$

Properties:
- $\hat{N}_i|n_1, n_2, \ldots\rangle = n_i|n_1, n_2, \ldots\rangle$ (definite photon number)

- Energy: $E = \sum_i n_i\hbar\omega_i + E_0$

- Ladder operators:
  - $\hat{a}^\dagger_i|n_1, \ldots, n_i, \ldots\rangle = \sqrt{n_i + 1}|n_1, \ldots, n_i+1, \ldots\rangle$
  - $\hat{a}_i|n_1, \ldots, n_i, \ldots\rangle = \sqrt{n_i}|n_1, \ldots, n_i-1, \ldots\rangle$

- Orthonormal: $\langle n_1, n_2, \ldots | n'_1, n'_2, \ldots \rangle = \prod_i \delta_{n_i n'_i}$

**Answer:** Fock states $|n_1, n_2, \ldots\rangle$ with definite photon numbers, form complete orthonormal basis.

### Example 9: Field Operator Form

Write the field operator $\hat{\mathbf{A}}(\mathbf{r}, t)$ in terms of creation/annihilation operators.

**Solution:**

Combining all modes (including complex conjugate for reality):

$$\hat{\mathbf{A}}(\mathbf{r}, t) = \sum_{\mathbf{k},\lambda} \sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2\epsilon_0\omega_k V}}\left[\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}e^{i(\mathbf{k} \cdot \mathbf{r} - \omega_k t)} + \hat{a}^\dagger_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}\boldsymbol{\epsilon}^*_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}e^{-i(\mathbf{k} \cdot \mathbf{r} - \omega_k t)}\right]$$

This is Hermitian: $\hat{\mathbf{A}}^\dagger = \hat{\mathbf{A}}$ (observable).

Electric field:
$$\hat{\mathbf{E}}(\mathbf{r}, t) = -\frac{\partial\hat{\mathbf{A}}}{\partial t} = i\sum_{\mathbf{k},\lambda} \sqrt{\frac{\hbar\omega_k}{2\epsilon_0 V}}\left[\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}e^{i(\mathbf{k} \cdot \mathbf{r} - \omega_k t)} - \hat{a}^\dagger_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}\boldsymbol{\epsilon}^*_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}e^{-i(\mathbf{k} \cdot \mathbf{r} - \omega_k t)}\right]$$

**Answer:** $\hat{\mathbf{A}} = \sum_{\mathbf{k},\lambda} \sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2\epsilon_0\omega_k V}}[\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}e^{i\mathbf{k} \cdot \mathbf{r}} e^{-i\omega_k t} + \text{h.c.}]$

## Physical Observables and Vacuum Effects

### Example 10: Photon Number Fluctuations

Calculate the photon number fluctuations in vacuum.

**Solution:**

Vacuum state: $|0\rangle$ with $\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}|0\rangle = 0$

Mean photon number:
$$\langle 0|\hat{N}_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}|0\rangle = \langle 0|\hat{a}^\dagger_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}|0\rangle = 0$$

Mean square:
$$\langle 0|\hat{N}_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}^2|0\rangle = \langle 0|(\hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a})^2|0\rangle = 0$$

But electric field has fluctuations:
$$\langle 0|\hat{E}^2|0\rangle = \sum_{\mathbf{k},\lambda} \frac{\hbar\omega_k}{2\epsilon_0 V} \neq 0$$

**Vacuum fluctuations**: Zero mean but non-zero variance.

These fluctuations:
- Cause spontaneous emission (atoms decay in vacuum)
- Produce Casimir force
- Lead to Lamb shift

**Answer:** Vacuum: $\langle N \rangle = 0$ but $\langle E^2 \rangle \neq 0$ (vacuum fluctuations observable).

### Example 11: Casimir Effect

Estimate the Casimir force between parallel conducting plates.

**Solution:**

Two parallel plates at distance $a$ modify allowed modes (boundary conditions).

Without plates: All $\mathbf{k}$ allowed, vacuum energy diverges.

With plates: Only $k_\perp = n\pi/a$ allowed (quantized perpendicular component).

Energy difference (plates vs. infinite separation):
$$E_{Casimir} = \sum_{\text{allowed}} \frac{\hbar\omega_k}{2} - \sum_{\text{free space}} \frac{\hbar\omega_k}{2}$$

After careful regularization:
$$E_{Casimir} = -\frac{\pi^2\hbar c}{720 a^3} \cdot A$$

where $A$ is plate area.

Force per unit area (attractive):
$$F/A = -\frac{\partial E}{\partial a}/A = -\frac{\pi^2\hbar c}{240 a^4}$$

For $a = 1$ μm: $F/A \approx 0.013$ Pa (measurable!).

**Answer:** Casimir force $F/A = -\frac{\pi^2\hbar c}{240 a^4}$ (attractive, from vacuum energy modification).

### Example 12: Photon Momentum and Radiation Pressure

Calculate the momentum carried by photons.

**Solution:**

Classical momentum density: $\mathbf{g} = \epsilon_0(\mathbf{E} \times \mathbf{B}) = \frac{1}{c^2}\mathbf{S}$ (Poynting vector)

Total momentum:
$$\mathbf{P} = \int d^3r \, \mathbf{g} = \sum_{\mathbf{k},\lambda} \hbar\mathbf{k} \, n_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}$$

Momentum operator:
$$\hat{\mathbf{P}} = \sum_{\mathbf{k},\lambda} \hbar\mathbf{k} \, \hat{a}^\dagger_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}$$

Each photon carries momentum $\hbar\mathbf{k}$ in direction of propagation.

Radiation pressure on mirror:
$$P = \frac{2I}{c}$$

where $I$ is intensity (factor 2 from reflection).

**Answer:** Photon momentum $\hbar\mathbf{k}$ per photon; radiation pressure $P = 2I/c$ on perfect reflector.

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: B**
EM field: Collection of independent harmonic oscillators
One oscillator per mode $(\mathbf{k}, \lambda)$
Each mode: frequency $\omega_k = c|\mathbf{k}|$
Creation/annihilation operators: $\hat{a}^\dagger_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}$, $\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}$
Energy: $\hbar\omega_k(n + 1/2)$ per mode

**Problem 2: A**
Bosonic commutation: $[\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k},\lambda}, \hat{a}^\dagger_{\mathbf{k}',\lambda'}] = \delta_{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{k}'}\delta_{\lambda\lambda'}$
All other commutators: $[\hat{a}, \hat{a}] = [\hat{a}^\dagger, \hat{a}^\dagger] = 0$
NOT anticommutators (photons are bosons)
No $\hbar$ (dimensionless operators after normalization)
Same as harmonic oscillator algebra

**Problem 3: B**
Vacuum: $E_0 = \sum_{\mathbf{k},\lambda} \frac{\hbar\omega_k}{2}$ (infinite!)
Sum over all modes $\to \infty$
Zero-point energy per mode: $\hbar\omega_k/2$
Observable: Casimir effect (boundary modifies sum)
Usually: Normal order or subtract (define relative to vacuum)

**Problem 4: B**
Casimir force: Attractive $F \propto -1/a^4$
From vacuum energy modification between plates
Boundary conditions change allowed modes
Quantum effect (needs $\hbar$, vanishes as $\hbar \to 0$)
Measured experimentally (agrees with QFT prediction)

**Problem 5: B**
Photon momentum: $p = \hbar k = \hbar|\mathbf{k}|$
Direction: Along $\mathbf{k}$ (propagation direction)
Massless: $p = E/c = \hbar\omega/c = \hbar k$ (using $\omega = ck$)
Observable: Radiation pressure, Compton scattering
Each photon: discrete momentum quantum
