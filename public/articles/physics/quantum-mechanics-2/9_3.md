# Quantum Mechanics Topic 9.3: Hydrogen Atom with Dirac Equation

The hydrogen atom stands as quantum mechanics' greatest triumph—exact solutions, perfect agreement with spectroscopy, foundation for understanding all atoms. But the non-relativistic Schrödinger treatment leaves mysteries. Why do the $2S_{1/2}$ and $2P_{1/2}$ states have slightly different energies (the fine structure)? Why does the electron have a magnetic moment exactly twice what orbital motion alone would give? These questions find their answer in the Dirac equation, which treats the electron and proton's Coulomb interaction relativistically.

Solving the Dirac equation for hydrogen is considerably more challenging than Schrödinger's version. The radial equations become coupled differential equations for the large and small components. Yet the problem remains exactly solvable, yielding energy levels that depend on the total angular momentum $j$ and principal quantum number $n$, but NOT separately on orbital angular momentum $l$. This accidental degeneracy between states with the same $n$ and $j$ but different $l$ (like $2S_{1/2}$ and $2P_{1/2}$) is exact in the Dirac theory. Experimentally, we observe a tiny splitting—the famous **Lamb shift**—which requires quantum electrodynamics to explain.

This article develops the Dirac hydrogen atom from the coupling to the Coulomb field through the exact solution. We'll separate variables in a way that respects both spherical symmetry and spin-orbit coupling, find the radial equations, solve them using power series, and extract the energy spectrum. The fine structure emerges automatically, spin-orbit coupling is built-in, and we'll see why the Dirac equation represents the deepest pre-QED understanding of atomic structure.

## Dirac Equation in Central Potential

### Example 1: Coupling to Coulomb Field

Write the Dirac equation for an electron in the Coulomb potential of a proton.

**Solution:**

Coulomb potential: $V(r) = -\frac{e^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0 r} = -\frac{Ze^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0 r}$ (using $Z = 1$ for hydrogen)

Minimal coupling: $\mathbf{p} \to \mathbf{p} - e\mathbf{A}$, $E \to E - eV$

For static Coulomb: $\mathbf{A} = 0$, $A^0 = V(r)$

Dirac equation:
$$[c\boldsymbol{\alpha}\cdot\mathbf{p} + \beta mc^2 + V(r)]\psi = E\psi$$

or
$$E\psi = \left[c\boldsymbol{\alpha}\cdot\mathbf{p} + \beta mc^2 - \frac{Ze^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0 r}\right]\psi$$

**Answer:** Dirac-Coulomb equation: $(c\boldsymbol{\alpha}\cdot\mathbf{p} + \beta mc^2 + V(r))\psi = E\psi$ where $V(r) = -Ze^2/(4\pi\epsilon_0 r)$.

### Example 2: Conserved Quantum Numbers

What are the constants of motion for Dirac hydrogen?

**Solution:**

1. **Energy** $E$ (time-independent Hamiltonian)

2. **Total angular momentum** $\hat{\mathbf{J}} = \hat{\mathbf{L}} + \hat{\mathbf{S}}$ where $\hat{\mathbf{S}} = \frac{\hbar}{2}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}$

   Check: $[\hat{H}, \hat{\mathbf{J}}] = 0$ (spherical symmetry)

3. **Parity** $\hat{\Pi} = \beta\left(\begin{smallmatrix} 0 & I \\ I & 0 \end{smallmatrix}\right)$ (spatial inversion)

4. **$J_z$** component of total angular momentum

Orbital $\hat{\mathbf{L}}$ and spin $\hat{\mathbf{S}}$ are NOT separately conserved (spin-orbit coupling).

Quantum numbers: $(n, j, m_j, \pi)$ where $\pi = \pm 1$ (parity)

For hydrogen, can also label by $l$ (orbital from non-relativistic limit).

**Answer:** Constants of motion: $E$, $\mathbf{J}^2$, $J_z$, parity. Quantum numbers: $(n, j, m_j)$ plus parity/orbital labels.

### Example 3: Spinor Angular Momentum Eigenstates

Construct simultaneous eigenstates of $\mathbf{J}^2$, $J_z$, and parity.

**Solution:**

Define **spinor spherical harmonics**:
$$\Omega_{jm}^l(\theta,\phi) = \sum_{m_l, m_s} \langle l, \tfrac{1}{2}; m_l, m_s | j, m \rangle Y_l^{m_l}(\theta,\phi)\chi_{m_s}$$

where $\chi_{1/2} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}$, $\chi_{-1/2} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}$ are spin-1/2 states.

Parity of $Y_l^m$ is $(-1)^l$, so states come in pairs:
- $j = l + 1/2$: $\Omega_{jm}^l$ with parity $(-1)^l$
- $j = l - 1/2$: $\Omega_{jm}^{l-1}$ with parity $(-1)^{l-1} = -(-1)^l$

For given $j$, two possible $l$ values: $l = j \pm 1/2$ (except $j = 1/2$ has only $l = 0$ or $l = 1$).

**Answer:** Spinor spherical harmonics $\Omega_{jm}^l$ combine orbital and spin angular momentum; for each $j$ (except $j=1/2$), two values $l = j \pm 1/2$.

## Separation of Variables and Radial Equations

### Example 4: Ansatz for Separated Solution

Write the form of the Dirac spinor using separation of variables.

**Solution:**

Four-component spinor:
$$\psi(\mathbf{r}) = \begin{pmatrix} \psi_A \\ \psi_B \end{pmatrix}$$

where $\psi_A$, $\psi_B$ are two-component spinors (large and small components).

Separation ansatz:
$$\psi = \begin{pmatrix} \frac{1}{r}F(r)\Omega_{jm}^l \\ \frac{i}{r}G(r)\Omega_{jm}^{\tilde{l}} \end{pmatrix}$$

where $F(r)$, $G(r)$ are radial functions.

For parity to be definite, need $\tilde{l} = 2j - l$ (so upper and lower have opposite parity).

Define quantum number $\kappa$:
$$\kappa = \begin{cases} -(j + 1/2) = -l - 1 & \text{for } j = l + 1/2 \\ +(j + 1/2) = l & \text{for } j = l - 1/2 \end{cases}$$

Then $\tilde{l} = |\kappa| - 1$.

**Answer:** Separated form $\psi = \begin{pmatrix} F(r)\Omega_{jm}^l/r \\ iG(r)\Omega_{jm}^{\tilde{l}}/r \end{pmatrix}$ where $\kappa$ labels angular momentum state.

### Example 5: Radial Dirac Equations

Derive the coupled radial equations for $F(r)$ and $G(r)$.

**Solution:**

Substituting ansatz into Dirac equation and using:
$$\boldsymbol{\sigma}\cdot\mathbf{r}\Omega_{jm}^l = \Omega_{jm}^{\tilde{l}}, \quad \boldsymbol{\sigma}\cdot\mathbf{r}\Omega_{jm}^{\tilde{l}} = \Omega_{jm}^l$$

(property of spinor spherical harmonics)

After manipulation:
$$\frac{dF}{dr} - \frac{\kappa}{r}F = \frac{1}{\hbar c}\left(E - mc^2 - V(r)\right)G$$

$$\frac{dG}{dr} + \frac{\kappa}{r}G = -\frac{1}{\hbar c}\left(E + mc^2 - V(r)\right)F$$

These are coupled first-order radial equations.

**Answer:** Coupled equations:
$F' - \frac{\kappa}{r}F = \frac{1}{\hbar c}(E - mc^2 - V)G$,
$G' + \frac{\kappa}{r}G = -\frac{1}{\hbar c}(E + mc^2 - V)F$.

### Example 6: Asymptotic Behavior

Find the asymptotic behavior of $F$ and $G$ as $r \to 0$ and $r \to \infty$.

**Solution:**

**As $r \to 0$**: Coulomb potential diverges, but less singular than $1/r^2$.

Near origin, try $F \sim r^s$, $G \sim r^s$:

From radial equations: $s = \pm\sqrt{\kappa^2 - (Z\alpha)^2}$

For bound states (regular at origin): $s = +\sqrt{\kappa^2 - (Z\alpha)^2} \equiv \gamma$

**As $r \to \infty$**: For bound states, $E < mc^2$:

$$F, G \sim e^{-\lambda r}$$

where $\lambda = \sqrt{m^2c^2 - E^2}/(\hbar c)$ (positive for bound states).

**Answer:** $r \to 0$: $F, G \sim r^\gamma$ where $\gamma = \sqrt{\kappa^2 - (Z\alpha)^2}$; $r \to \infty$: $F, G \sim e^{-\lambda r}$.

## Exact Energy Spectrum

### Example 7: Energy Level Formula

State the exact energy eigenvalues for Dirac hydrogen.

**Solution:**

Principal quantum number $n$ counts radial excitations.

Exact result (derived from power series solution):

$$E_{nj} = mc^2\left[1 + \left(\frac{Z\alpha}{n - (j + 1/2) + \sqrt{(j+1/2)^2 - (Z\alpha)^2}}\right)^2\right]^{-1/2}$$

where $\alpha = e^2/(4\pi\epsilon_0\hbar c) \approx 1/137$ is fine structure constant.

For small $Z\alpha$ (hydrogen), expand:

$$E_{nj} \approx mc^2 - \frac{mc^2(Z\alpha)^2}{2n^2}\left[1 + \frac{(Z\alpha)^2}{n^2}\left(\frac{n}{j+1/2} - \frac{3}{4}\right)\right]$$

First term: rest mass
Second term: Bohr/Schrödinger energy
Third term: fine structure correction

**Answer:** $E_{nj} = mc^2[1 + (Z\alpha)^2/(n - (j+1/2) + \sqrt{(j+1/2)^2 - (Z\alpha)^2})^2]^{-1/2}$ (exact).

### Example 8: Fine Structure Splitting

Calculate the fine structure splitting for $n = 2$ states.

**Solution:**

For $n = 2$:
- $2S_{1/2}$: $l = 0$, $j = 1/2$
- $2P_{1/2}$: $l = 1$, $j = 1/2$
- $2P_{3/2}$: $l = 1$, $j = 3/2$

From approximate formula:
$$E_{nj} \approx -\frac{13.6\text{ eV}}{n^2}\left[1 + \frac{\alpha^2}{n^2}\left(\frac{n}{j+1/2} - \frac{3}{4}\right)\right]$$

For $n = 2$, $j = 1/2$:
$$E_{2,1/2} = -3.4\text{ eV}\left[1 + \frac{\alpha^2}{4}(2 - 0.75)\right] = -3.4\text{ eV}[1 + 1.25\alpha^2/4]$$

For $n = 2$, $j = 3/2$:
$$E_{2,3/2} = -3.4\text{ eV}\left[1 + \frac{\alpha^2}{4}(1 - 0.75)\right] = -3.4\text{ eV}[1 + 0.25\alpha^2/4]$$

Splitting:
$$\Delta E = E_{2,3/2} - E_{2,1/2} \approx 3.4\text{ eV} \cdot \frac{\alpha^2}{4}(1.25 - 0.25) = 3.4\text{ eV} \cdot \frac{\alpha^2}{4}$$

With $\alpha = 1/137$: $\Delta E \approx 4.5 \times 10^{-5}$ eV

**Answer:** Fine structure splitting $\Delta E_{2P_{3/2} - 2P_{1/2}} \approx 4.5 \times 10^{-5}$ eV $\approx 11$ GHz.

### Example 9: Degeneracy Pattern

What is the degeneracy of states with principal quantum number $n$?

**Solution:**

For each $n$: $j = 1/2, 3/2, \ldots, n - 1/2$

For each $j$: degeneracy $(2j + 1)$ from $m_j$ values

For hydrogen, states labeled by $(n, l, j, m_j)$:

For given $n$: $l = 0, 1, \ldots, n-1$

For each $l$: $j = l \pm 1/2$ ( $l = 0$ gives only $j = 1/2$)

Total $= 2n^2$ (same as Schrödinger if we include spin).

**Answer:** Degeneracy $g_n = 2n^2$ (same as non-relativistic with spin), but split by fine structure.

### Example 10: Accidental Degeneracy

Why do $2S_{1/2}$ and $2P_{1/2}$ have the same energy in Dirac theory?

**Solution:**

Both have $n = 2$, $j = 1/2$.

Energy depends on $n$ and $j$ only (not on $l$ separately).

This is **accidental degeneracy** from special properties of Coulomb potential.

Dirac formula:
$$E_{2,1/2} = mc^2[1 + (Z\alpha)^2/(2 - 1 + \sqrt{1 - (Z\alpha)^2})^2]^{-1/2}$$

Same for both $2S_{1/2}$ ($l=0$) and $2P_{1/2}$ ($l=1$).

Experimentally: Tiny splitting (Lamb shift $\sim 1057$ MHz) lifts degeneracy—requires QED.

**Answer:** States with same $(n,j)$ degenerate in Dirac theory; Coulomb potential's special symmetry. Lamb shift breaks this.

## Physical Interpretation and Comparisons

### Example 11: Comparison with Perturbation Theory

Show that Dirac energies match first-order relativistic perturbation theory.

**Solution:**

Non-relativistic perturbation for fine structure includes:

1. **Relativistic kinetic energy**: $\hat{H}_1 = -\frac{p^4}{8m^3c^2}$

2. **Spin-orbit coupling**: $\hat{H}_{SO} = \frac{1}{2m^2c^2}\frac{1}{r}\frac{dV}{dr}\mathbf{L}\cdot\mathbf{S}$

Combined first-order correction:
$$\Delta E = \frac{mc^2\alpha^4}{2n^3}\left[\frac{n}{j+1/2} - \frac{3}{4}\right]$$

Expanding Dirac formula to first order in $\alpha^2$:
$$E_{nj} - mc^2 \approx -\frac{mc^2\alpha^2}{2n^2} - \frac{mc^2\alpha^4}{2n^4}\left[\frac{n}{j+1/2} - \frac{3}{4}\right]$$

**Exact agreement** with perturbation theory to $O(\alpha^4)$.

Higher orders in Dirac are exact; perturbation is approximate.

**Answer:** Dirac energies agree with relativistic perturbation theory to $O(\alpha^4)$; Dirac is exact to all orders in $\alpha$.

### Example 12: Lamb Shift

What is the Lamb shift and why isn't it in the Dirac equation?

**Solution:**

**Lamb shift**: $2S_{1/2} - 2P_{1/2}$ splitting in hydrogen $\approx 1057$ MHz.

Dirac predicts these degenerate (same $n,j$).

**Origin**: Quantum electrodynamics effects:
1. **Vacuum polarization**: Virtual $e^+e^-$ pairs screen nuclear charge
2. **Self-energy**: Electron interacts with its own radiation field

These corrections:
$$\Delta E_{Lamb} \sim \frac{\alpha^5 mc^2}{n^3}\ln\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}\right)$$

Higher order in $\alpha$ than fine structure ($\alpha^4$).

Different for $s$-states vs. $p$-states because $|\psi(0)|^2$ differs.

Measured by Lamb and Retherford (1947), led to modern QED.

**Answer:** Lamb shift $\sim 1057$ MHz from QED corrections (vacuum polarization, self-energy); requires QFT, not in Dirac equation.

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: B**
Dirac energies: $E_{nj}$ depend on $n$ (principal) and $j$ (total angular momentum)
NOT separately on $l$ (orbital)—accidental degeneracy
$2S_{1/2}$ and $2P_{1/2}$: same energy (both $n=2, j=1/2$)
$m_j$ just changes orientation, not energy (spherical symmetry)
Contrast with non-relativistic: depends on $n$ only (not $l$ or $j$)

**Problem 2: B**
Degenerate in Dirac theory (same $n=2$, $j=1/2$)
Fine structure splits by $j$, not by $l$ within same $j$
Lamb shift (QED) breaks this degeneracy: $2S_{1/2}$ higher by $\sim 1057$ MHz
Experimentally discovered 1947 (Lamb and Retherford)
Parity: $2S_{1/2}$ even (+), $2P_{1/2}$ odd (-), but energy same in Dirac

**Problem 3: C**
Fine structure constant: $\alpha = \frac{e^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0\hbar c} \approx \frac{1}{137.036}$
Dimensionless combination of fundamental constants
Measures strength of EM interaction
Fine structure corrections $\sim \alpha^2 \approx 5 \times 10^{-5}$
QED: Perturbation series in powers of $\alpha$

**Problem 4: B**
Spin-orbit emerges automatically from Dirac equation
No need to add by hand (unlike non-relativistic theory)
From coupling of spin $\mathbf{S} = \frac{\hbar}{2}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}$ to orbital motion
Built into $\boldsymbol{\alpha}\cdot\mathbf{p}$ term
Correct magnitude and sign

**Problem 5: B**
Lamb shift: QED correction ($\sim \alpha^5$)
Vacuum polarization + self-energy
Not in Dirac equation (purely relativistic QM)
$2S_{1/2}$ raised $\sim 1057$ MHz above $2P_{1/2}$
Historic: Led to development of modern QED
