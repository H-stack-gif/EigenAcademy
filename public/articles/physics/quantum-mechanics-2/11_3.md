# Quantum Mechanics 2 Topic 11.3: Entanglement, Decoherence, and Foundations

In 1935, Einstein, Podolsky, and Rosen (EPR) challenged the completeness of quantum mechanics. They described a thought experiment where measuring one particle instantly determines the state of another, arbitrarily far away—"spooky action at a distance." EPR argued this proved quantum mechanics incomplete, that hidden variables must exist. But in 1964, John Bell showed that any local hidden variable theory must satisfy certain inequalities. Quantum mechanics **violates** Bell's inequalities. Experiments confirmed: quantum correlations are stronger than any classical theory allows. This is **entanglement**—the most distinctly quantum phenomenon.

Entanglement is not just a curiosity. It's the resource powering quantum computing, quantum cryptography, and quantum teleportation. Yet it poses deep questions: How do entangled states collapse during measurement? Why does the quantum world appear classical at macroscopic scales? **Decoherence** provides an answer: interaction with the environment causes apparent wavefunction collapse by destroying coherence. The measurement problem—how and when collapse occurs—remains one of quantum mechanics' deepest unsolved questions, connected to interpretations ranging from Copenhagen to many-worlds.

This article explores quantum foundations. We'll understand entanglement and EPR correlations, derive and analyze Bell's inequalities, see how decoherence explains the quantum-classical transition, examine the measurement problem and its interpretations, and explore how reduced density matrices describe subsystems. These topics probe the conceptual heart of quantum mechanics, where physics meets philosophy.

## Entangled States and EPR Correlations

An entangled state cannot be written as a product of individual states: $|\psi\rangle \neq |\psi_A\rangle \otimes |\psi_B\rangle$.

### Example 1: Singlet State

The spin singlet state for two spin-1/2 particles is:

$$|\psi\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|↑\rangle_A|↓\rangle_B - |↓\rangle_A|↑\rangle_B)$$

Show this is entangled.

**Solution:**

Suppose $|\psi\rangle = |\psi_A\rangle \otimes |\psi_B\rangle$ where $|\psi_A\rangle = \alpha|↑\rangle_A + \beta|↓\rangle_A$ and $|\psi_B\rangle = \gamma|↑\rangle_B + \delta|↓\rangle_B$.

Then:
$$|\psi\rangle = \alpha\gamma|↑↑\rangle + \alpha\delta|↑↓\rangle + \beta\gamma|↓↑\rangle + \beta\delta|↓↓\rangle$$

For this to equal singlet: $\alpha\gamma = 0$, $\alpha\delta = 1/\sqrt{2}$, $\beta\gamma = -1/\sqrt{2}$, $\beta\delta = 0$

From first equation: $\alpha = 0$ or $\gamma = 0$

If $\alpha = 0$: Then $\alpha\delta = 0 \neq 1/\sqrt{2}$ (contradiction!)

If $\gamma = 0$: Then $\beta\gamma = 0 \neq -1/\sqrt{2}$ (contradiction!)

**Answer:** Singlet cannot be factorized; it is **entangled**

### Example 2: EPR Correlation

For the singlet state, if Alice measures $\sigma_z$ on particle A and finds $↑$, what is Bob's state?

**Solution:**

Before measurement: $|\psi\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|↑\rangle_A|↓\rangle_B - |↓\rangle_A|↑\rangle_B)$

Alice measures $\sigma_z$ on A and gets $|↑\rangle_A$.

Projection: State collapses to component with $|↑\rangle_A$:

$$|\psi\rangle \to |↑\rangle_A|↓\rangle_B$$

Bob's particle is now **definitely** in state $|↓\rangle_B$!

**Key point**: Alice's measurement **instantly** determines Bob's state, regardless of separation.

**Answer:** Bob's particle collapses to $|↓\rangle_B$ (perfect anti-correlation)

### Example 3: Measurement in Different Basis

Alice measures $\sigma_x$ on her particle instead. What are the correlations?

**Solution:**

Express singlet in $\sigma_x$ basis: $|±\rangle_x = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|↑\rangle \pm |↓\rangle)$

$$|↑\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|+\rangle_x + |-\rangle_x)$$
$$|↓\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|+\rangle_x - |-\rangle_x)$$

Singlet becomes:
$$|\psi\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|+\rangle_{Ax}|-\rangle_{Bx} - |-\rangle_{Ax}|+\rangle_{Bx})$$

Same form! If Alice measures $|+\rangle_{Ax}$, Bob has $|-\rangle_{Bx}$.

**Answer:** Perfect anti-correlation in ANY basis (rotationally invariant)

## Bell's Inequality and Local Realism

Bell's theorem: No local hidden variable theory can reproduce all quantum predictions.

### Example 4: CHSH Inequality

Consider measurements of $\mathbf{a} \cdot \boldsymbol{\sigma}$ and $\mathbf{b} \cdot \boldsymbol{\sigma}$ on A, $\mathbf{c} \cdot \boldsymbol{\sigma}$ and $\mathbf{d} \cdot \boldsymbol{\sigma}$ on B. Define correlation:

$$E(\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{c}) = \langle\psi|(\mathbf{a} \cdot \boldsymbol{\sigma}_A)(\mathbf{c} \cdot \boldsymbol{\sigma}_B)|\psi\rangle$$

Show that local hidden variables require $|S| \leq 2$ where:

$$S = E(\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{c}) - E(\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{d}) + E(\mathbf{b}, \mathbf{c}) + E(\mathbf{b}, \mathbf{d})$$

**Solution:**

Local hidden variables: Outcomes predetermined by $\lambda$

$$A(\mathbf{a}, \lambda) = \pm 1, \quad B(\mathbf{c}, \lambda) = \pm 1$$

Correlation: $E(\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{c}) = \int d\lambda \rho(\lambda) A(\mathbf{a}, \lambda) B(\mathbf{c}, \lambda)$

Compute:
$$S = \int d\lambda \rho(\lambda) [A(\mathbf{a})(B(\mathbf{c}) - B(\mathbf{d})) + A(\mathbf{b})(B(\mathbf{c}) + B(\mathbf{d}))]$$

Since $B(\mathbf{c}), B(\mathbf{d}) = \pm 1$: Either $B(\mathbf{c}) - B(\mathbf{d}) = 0$ and $B(\mathbf{c}) + B(\mathbf{d}) = \pm 2$

Or: $B(\mathbf{c}) + B(\mathbf{d}) = 0$ and $B(\mathbf{c}) - B(\mathbf{d}) = \pm 2$

Either way: $|A(\mathbf{a})(B(\mathbf{c}) - B(\mathbf{d})) + A(\mathbf{b})(B(\mathbf{c}) + B(\mathbf{d}))| = 2$

Therefore: $|S| \leq 2$

**Answer:** CHSH inequality $|S| \leq 2$ from local realism

### Example 5: Quantum Violation

Calculate $S$ for the singlet state with $\mathbf{a} = \hat{\mathbf{x}}$, $\mathbf{b} = \hat{\mathbf{z}}$, $\mathbf{c} = (\hat{\mathbf{x}} + \hat{\mathbf{z}})/\sqrt{2}$, $\mathbf{d} = (\hat{\mathbf{x}} - \hat{\mathbf{z}})/\sqrt{2}$.

**Solution:**

For singlet: $E(\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{c}) = -\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{c}$

Calculate each term:
$E(\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{c}) = -\hat{\mathbf{x}} \cdot \frac{(\hat{\mathbf{x}} + \hat{\mathbf{z}})}{\sqrt{2}} = -\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$

$E(\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{d}) = -\hat{\mathbf{x}} \cdot \frac{(\hat{\mathbf{x}} - \hat{\mathbf{z}})}{\sqrt{2}} = -\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$

$E(\mathbf{b}, \mathbf{c}) = -\hat{\mathbf{z}} \cdot \frac{(\hat{\mathbf{x}} + \hat{\mathbf{z}})}{\sqrt{2}} = -\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$

$E(\mathbf{b}, \mathbf{d}) = -\hat{\mathbf{z}} \cdot \frac{(\hat{\mathbf{x}} - \hat{\mathbf{z}})}{\sqrt{2}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$

For optimal violation: Choose angles $0°, 45°, 22.5°, 67.5°$

Result: $S = 2\sqrt{2} \approx 2.828$

**Answer:** $|S| = 2\sqrt{2} > 2$ (violates Bell inequality! Quantum mechanics wins)

### Example 6: Experimental Tests

What do experiments show about Bell's inequality?

**Solution:**

Aspect experiments (1982): Measured correlations with entangled photons

Results consistently show: $|S| = 2.7 \pm 0.05$ (close to quantum prediction $2\sqrt{2}$)

Clear violation of $|S| \leq 2$ (local realism bound)

Loopholes closed over decades:
- Locality loophole: Space-like separation (2015)
- Detection loophole: High-efficiency detectors (2013)
- Freedom-of-choice loophole: Cosmic photons choose settings (2018)

All tests confirm quantum mechanics, rule out local hidden variables.

**Answer:** Experiments decisively confirm quantum nonlocality; Bell inequality violated

## Decoherence and the Quantum-Classical Transition

Why don't we see macroscopic superpositions? **Decoherence** provides the answer.

### Example 7: Reduced Density Matrix

A system $S$ is entangled with environment $E$. Define the reduced density matrix.

**Solution:**

Total state: $|\Psi\rangle_{SE}$ (generally entangled)

Full density matrix: $\hat{\rho}_{SE} = |\Psi\rangle\langle\Psi|$

Reduced density matrix for system:
$\hat{\rho}_S = \text{Tr}_E(\hat{\rho}_{SE})$

In basis $\{|i\rangle_E\}$:
$\hat{\rho}_S = \sum_i \langle i|_E \hat{\rho}_{SE} |i\rangle_E$

Key property: If $|\Psi\rangle$ is entangled, then $\hat{\rho}_S$ is **mixed** (not pure).

Purity: $\text{Tr}(\hat{\rho}_S^2) < 1$ for mixed state

**Answer:** Tracing out environment converts pure entangled state to mixed state for subsystem

### Example 8: Decoherence of Superposition

A qubit in state $|\psi\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|0\rangle + |1\rangle)$ interacts with environment. Show coherence decays.

**Solution:**

Initially: $|\psi\rangle_S \otimes |E_0\rangle$

After interaction: $|\Psi\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|0\rangle|E_0\rangle + |1\rangle|E_1\rangle)$

where $|E_0\rangle$ and $|E_1\rangle$ are environment states (approximately orthogonal for macroscopic environment).

Reduced density matrix:
$\hat{\rho}_S = \frac{1}{2}(|0\rangle\langle 0| + |1\rangle\langle 1| + |0\rangle\langle 1|\langle E_1|E_0\rangle + |1\rangle\langle 0|\langle E_0|E_1\rangle)$

As $\langle E_1|E_0\rangle \to 0$:
$\hat{\rho}_S \to \frac{1}{2}(|0\rangle\langle 0| + |1\rangle\langle 1|)$

Off-diagonal terms (coherences) vanish! System becomes **effectively classical**.

**Answer:** Environment entanglement destroys coherence; superposition → mixture

### Example 9: Pointer States

What states are robust against decoherence?

**Solution:**

**Pointer states**: States that remain (nearly) unentangled with environment.

For position-based decoherence: Localized position eigenstates $|x\rangle$ are pointer states.

Why? Scattering from environment measures position:
$|x\rangle|E_0\rangle \to |x\rangle|E_x\rangle$

Different positions couple to distinguishable environment states.

But superpositions decohere:
$\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|x_1\rangle + |x_2\rangle)|E_0\rangle \to \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|x_1\rangle|E_1\rangle + |x_2\rangle|E_2\rangle)$

Reduced density matrix: Coherence between $|x_1\rangle$ and $|x_2\rangle$ suppressed by $\langle E_1|E_2\rangle \approx 0$.

**Answer:** Pointer states = eigenstates of interaction with environment (classically observable quantities)

## The Measurement Problem

The measurement problem: How and when does wavefunction collapse occur?

### Example 10: von Neumann Measurement Scheme

Describe the ideal quantum measurement process.

**Solution:**

**Stage 1: Pre-measurement**

System: $|\psi\rangle_S = \sum_n c_n|n\rangle$

Apparatus: $|A_0\rangle$ (ready state)

Combined: $|\psi\rangle_S \otimes |A_0\rangle$

**Stage 2: Unitary evolution (Schrödinger)**

Interaction Hamiltonian couples system to apparatus:

$\sum_n c_n|n\rangle|A_0\rangle \to \sum_n c_n|n\rangle|A_n\rangle$

where $|A_n\rangle$ are distinguishable apparatus states (pointer positions).

System and apparatus now **entangled**!

**Stage 3: Collapse (von Neumann)**

"Mysteriously," state becomes: $|n_k\rangle|A_k\rangle$ with probability $|c_k|^2$.

**Problem**: Stage 3 is **non-unitary**, violates Schrödinger equation. When and how does it occur?

**Answer:** Measurement creates entanglement (unitary), then collapse (non-unitary, mysterious)

### Example 11: Interpretations of Quantum Mechanics

What are major interpretations addressing the measurement problem?

**Solution:**

**Copenhagen Interpretation**: 
- Wavefunction collapse is fundamental
- Occurs during measurement (undefined when exactly)
- Classical-quantum divide

**Many-Worlds Interpretation**:
- No collapse; all outcomes occur
- Universe splits into branches: $\sum_n c_n|n\rangle|A_n\rangle|O_n\rangle$ where $|O_n\rangle$ includes observer
- Each branch experiences one outcome

**Decoherence/Consistent Histories**:
- Collapse is apparent, not fundamental
- Environment entanglement makes interference unobservable
- Effectively classical behavior emerges

**Bohmian Mechanics**:
- Pilot wave guides particles
- Deterministic, nonlocal
- Hidden variables (particle positions)

**Answer:** Different interpretations provide different ontologies; same predictions

### Example 12: Schrödinger's Cat

Explain the Schrödinger's cat paradox.

**Solution:**

Setup:
- Quantum system (radioactive atom): $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|\text{decayed}\rangle + |\text{intact}\rangle)$
- Coupled to detector → poison → cat

Evolution:
$\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|\text{decayed}\rangle|\text{dead cat}\rangle + |\text{intact}\rangle|\text{alive cat}\rangle)$

**Paradox**: Schrödinger equation predicts superposition of dead and alive cat!

Never observed macroscopically. Why?

**Resolution (decoherence)**: 
- Cat has $\sim 10^{26}$ molecules constantly interacting with environment
- Decoherence time: $\tau \sim 10^{-40}$ seconds
- Superposition destroyed instantly; cat is either dead OR alive (mixture), not AND (superposition)

**Answer:** Macroscopic superpositions decohere instantly; cat appears classical

### Example 13: Quantum Erasure

In a double-slit experiment with "which-path" information stored in environment, can you recover interference?

**Solution:**

**Stage 1**: Particle + environment entangled:
$|\Psi\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|1\rangle|E_1\rangle + |2\rangle|E_2\rangle)$

No interference (environment "measured" path).

**Stage 2**: Perform measurement on environment in basis:
$|E_\pm\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|E_1\rangle \pm |E_2\rangle)$

If outcome is $|E_+\rangle$:
$|\psi\rangle_S = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|1\rangle + |2\rangle)$

Interference restored! ("Which-path" information erased)

If outcome is $|E_-\rangle$:
$|\psi\rangle_S = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|1\rangle - |2\rangle)$

Also shows interference (opposite phase).

**Answer:** Quantum erasure: Erasing which-path info restores interference (delayed choice possible)

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: D**
Entanglement: Cannot write as $|\psi\rangle = |\psi_A\rangle \otimes |\psi_B\rangle$
Example: Singlet $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|↑↓\rangle - |↓↑\rangle)$
Can be written as sum: $\sum_i c_i|\phi_i^A\rangle|\phi_i^B\rangle$ but not single product
Measurement on A instantly affects B
Non-local correlations

**Problem 2: B**
Bell's theorem: Local hidden variables cannot reproduce QM
Experiments violate $|S| \leq 2$, confirm $|S| = 2\sqrt{2}$
Rules out Einstein's local realism
Does NOT allow faster-than-light communication (no-signaling theorem)
Nature is fundamentally nonlocal

**Problem 3: B**
Decoherence: Environment entanglement destroys phase coherence
Reduced density matrix: Off-diagonal terms $\to 0$
Pure state $\to$ mixed state (for subsystem)
Superposition becomes mixture (appears classical)
NOT true collapse (no-collapse interpretations exist)

**Problem 4: C**
Pointer states: Eigenstates of system-environment interaction
Robust (don't entangle much with environment)
Emerge naturally from decoherence dynamics
Usually correspond to classical observables
Explains preferred basis problem

**Problem 5: B**
Measurement problem: When/how does $\sum c_n|n\rangle \to |n_k\rangle$?
Schrödinger equation is unitary (no collapse)
von Neumann measurement scheme adds non-unitary collapse
Different interpretations resolve differently:
- Copenhagen: Fundamental collapse
- Many-worlds: No collapse, branching
- Decoherence: Apparent collapse
