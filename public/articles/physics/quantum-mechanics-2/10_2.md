# Quantum Mechanics Topic 10.2: Coherent States and Photon Statistics

Quantum harmonic oscillators typically live in energy eigenstates $|n\rangle$ with definite photon number. But classical electromagnetic waves don't have definite photon number—a laser beam's intensity fluctuates around a mean value. What quantum state best represents classical light? The answer is **coherent states**, discovered by Schrödinger in 1926 and later recognized as the quantum states that most closely resemble classical oscillations. These remarkable states are eigenstates of the annihilation operator itself: $\hat{a}|\alpha\rangle = \alpha|\alpha\rangle$, with eigenvalue $\alpha$ (a complex number, not quantized).

Coherent states have extraordinary properties. They minimize the uncertainty product $\Delta E \Delta \phi$, saturating the Heisenberg bound—they're as "classical" as quantum mechanics allows. Their photon number distribution follows Poisson statistics, not the fixed values of Fock states. Under time evolution, coherent states remain coherent, just with a rotating phase—mimicking classical oscillation perfectly. A laser operates by producing coherent states with large $|\alpha|$, where quantum fluctuations become negligible compared to the mean. Beyond coherent states lie **squeezed states**, which reduce uncertainty in one quadrature below the vacuum level at the expense of increased uncertainty in the conjugate quadrature—crucial for gravitational wave detection.

This article explores coherent and squeezed states from their definition through their statistical properties and physical realizations. We'll construct coherent states, prove they're minimum uncertainty states, calculate photon number distributions, understand the displacement operator, and see how coherent states bridge quantum and classical optics. The mathematics reveals why lasers work and how quantum mechanics permits states arbitrarily close to classical behavior.

## Defining Coherent States

### Example 1: Eigenstate of Annihilation Operator

Define coherent states and find their explicit form.

**Solution:**

**Coherent state**: Eigenstate of $\hat{a}$:
$$\hat{a}|\alpha\rangle = \alpha|\alpha\rangle$$

where $\alpha \in \mathbb{C}$ (complex eigenvalue).

Expand in Fock basis: $|\alpha\rangle = \sum_{n=0}^\infty c_n|n\rangle$

$$\hat{a}|\alpha\rangle = \sum_n c_n\sqrt{n}|n-1\rangle = \alpha\sum_n c_n|n\rangle$$

Matching coefficients:
$$c_n\sqrt{n} = \alpha c_{n-1}$$

$$c_n = \frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{n}}c_{n-1} = \frac{\alpha^n}{\sqrt{n!}}c_0$$

Normalization: $\sum_n |c_n|^2 = |c_0|^2\sum_n \frac{|\alpha|^{2n}}{n!} = |c_0|^2 e^{|\alpha|^2} = 1$

Therefore: $c_0 = e^{-|\alpha|^2/2}$

**Answer:** $|\alpha\rangle = e^{-|\alpha|^2/2}\sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{\alpha^n}{\sqrt{n!}}|n\rangle$ (coherent state).

### Example 2: Normalization and Orthogonality

Show that coherent states are normalized but not orthogonal.

**Solution:**

**Normalization**:
$$\langle\alpha|\alpha\rangle = e^{-|\alpha|^2}\sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{|\alpha|^{2n}}{n!} = e^{-|\alpha|^2} e^{|\alpha|^2} = 1$$ ✓

**Inner product**:
$$\langle\beta|\alpha\rangle = e^{-|\alpha|^2/2}e^{-|\beta|^2/2}\sum_n \frac{(\beta^*)^n\alpha^n}{n!} = e^{-|\alpha|^2/2}e^{-|\beta|^2/2}e^{\beta^*\alpha}$$

$$= e^{-|\alpha|^2/2 - |\beta|^2/2 + \beta^*\alpha}$$

For $\alpha \neq \beta$: $\langle\beta|\alpha\rangle \neq 0$ (not orthogonal!)

However: $|\langle\beta|\alpha\rangle|^2 = e^{-|\alpha - \beta|^2}$ → vanishes as $|\alpha - \beta| \to \infty$

**Answer:** Normalized: $\langle\alpha|\alpha\rangle = 1$; not orthogonal: $\langle\beta|\alpha\rangle = e^{-|\alpha|^2/2 - |\beta|^2/2 + \beta^*\alpha}$.

### Example 3: Displacement Operator

Express coherent states using the displacement operator.

**Solution:**

Define **displacement operator**:
$$\hat{D}(\alpha) = e^{\alpha\hat{a}^\dagger - \alpha^*\hat{a}}$$

Claim: $|\alpha\rangle = \hat{D}(\alpha)|0\rangle$

Proof using Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff:
$$e^{\alpha\hat{a}^\dagger - \alpha^*\hat{a}} = e^{-|\alpha|^2/2}e^{\alpha\hat{a}^\dagger}e^{-\alpha^*\hat{a}}$$

Acting on vacuum:
$$\hat{D}(\alpha)|0\rangle = e^{-|\alpha|^2/2}e^{\alpha\hat{a}^\dagger}|0\rangle = e^{-|\alpha|^2/2}\sum_n \frac{\alpha^n}{n!}(\hat{a}^\dagger)^n|0\rangle$$

$$= e^{-|\alpha|^2/2}\sum_n \frac{\alpha^n}{\sqrt{n!}}|n\rangle = |\alpha\rangle$$ ✓

Properties:
- Unitary: $\hat{D}^\dagger(\alpha) = \hat{D}(-\alpha)$
- Composition: $\hat{D}(\alpha)\hat{D}(\beta) = e^{i\text{Im}(\alpha\beta^*)}\hat{D}(\alpha + \beta)$

**Answer:** $|\alpha\rangle = \hat{D}(\alpha)|0\rangle$ where $\hat{D}(\alpha) = e^{\alpha\hat{a}^\dagger - \alpha^*\hat{a}}$ (displacement from vacuum).

### Example 4: Mean Photon Number

Calculate the mean photon number in a coherent state.

**Solution:**

Need $\hat{a}^\dagger$ acting on bra:
$$\langle\alpha|\hat{a}^\dagger = (\hat{a}|\alpha\rangle)^\dagger = (\alpha|\alpha\rangle)^\dagger = \alpha^*\langle\alpha|$$

Therefore:
$$\langle n \rangle = \langle\alpha|\hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a}|\alpha\rangle = \alpha^*\alpha = |\alpha|^2$$

Alternative: Direct calculation:
$$\langle n \rangle = e^{-|\alpha|^2}\sum_n n\frac{|\alpha|^{2n}}{n!} = |\alpha|^2 e^{-|\alpha|^2}\sum_n \frac{|\alpha|^{2(n-1)}}{(n-1)!} = |\alpha|^2$$

**Answer:** Mean photon number $\langle n \rangle = |\alpha|^2$ (intensity proportional to $|\alpha|^2$).

## Photon Statistics and Uncertainty

### Example 5: Poisson Distribution

Show that photon number follows Poisson statistics.

**Solution:**

Probability of $n$ photons:
$$P(n) = |\langle n|\alpha\rangle|^2 = e^{-|\alpha|^2}\frac{|\alpha|^{2n}}{n!}$$

This is **Poisson distribution** with parameter $\bar{n} = |\alpha|^2$.

Variance:
$$(\Delta n)^2 = \langle n^2 \rangle - \langle n \rangle^2$$

$$\langle n^2 \rangle = \langle\alpha|(\hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a})^2|\alpha\rangle = \langle\alpha|\hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a}\hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a}|\alpha\rangle$$

Using $\hat{a}\hat{a}^\dagger = \hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a} + 1$:
$$= \langle\alpha|\hat{a}^\dagger(\hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a} + 1)\hat{a}|\alpha\rangle = |\alpha|^4 + |\alpha|^2$$

$$(\Delta n)^2 = |\alpha|^4 + |\alpha|^2 - |\alpha|^4 = |\alpha|^2 = \bar{n}$$

**Poisson property**: $(\Delta n)^2 = \bar{n}$ (variance equals mean).

**Answer:** $P(n) = e^{-|\alpha|^2}\frac{|\alpha|^{2n}}{n!}$ (Poisson), variance $(\Delta n)^2 = |\alpha|^2 = \bar{n}$.

### Example 6: Minimum Uncertainty

Prove coherent states are minimum uncertainty states.

**Solution:**

For harmonic oscillator, define quadratures:
$$\hat{X} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\hat{a} + \hat{a}^\dagger), \quad \hat{P} = \frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}(\hat{a}^\dagger - \hat{a})$$

with $[\hat{X}, \hat{P}] = i$.

For coherent state $|\alpha\rangle$ with $\alpha = |\alpha|e^{i\theta}$:

$$\langle X \rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\langle\alpha|(\hat{a} + \hat{a}^\dagger)|\alpha\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\alpha + \alpha^*) = \sqrt{2}|\alpha|\cos\theta$$

$$\langle X^2 \rangle = \frac{1}{2}\langle\alpha|(\hat{a} + \hat{a}^\dagger)^2|\alpha\rangle = \frac{1}{2}(\alpha^2 + (\alpha^*)^2 + 2|\alpha|^2 + 1)$$

$$(\Delta X)^2 = \langle X^2 \rangle - \langle X \rangle^2 = \frac{1}{2}$$

Similarly: $(\Delta P)^2 = \frac{1}{2}$

Therefore: $\Delta X \Delta P = \frac{1}{2}$ (saturates Heisenberg bound!)

**Answer:** Coherent states minimize uncertainty: $\Delta X \Delta P = \frac{1}{2}$ (equal uncertainty in both quadratures).

### Example 7: Time Evolution

Find how coherent states evolve in time.

**Solution:**

Hamiltonian: $\hat{H} = \hbar\omega(\hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a} + \frac{1}{2})$

At $t = 0$: $|\psi(0)\rangle = |\alpha\rangle$

$$|\psi(t)\rangle = e^{-i\hat{H}t/\hbar}|\alpha\rangle = e^{-i\omega t/2}e^{-i\omega t\hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a}}|\alpha\rangle$$

$$= e^{-i\omega t/2}\sum_n e^{-|\alpha|^2/2}\frac{\alpha^n}{\sqrt{n!}}e^{-in\omega t}|n\rangle$$

$$= e^{-i\omega t/2}e^{-|\alpha e^{-i\omega t}|^2/2}\sum_n \frac{(\alpha e^{-i\omega t})^n}{\sqrt{n!}}|n\rangle$$

$$= e^{-i\omega t/2}|\alpha e^{-i\omega t}\rangle$$

**Coherent states remain coherent**, just with rotating phase!

**Answer:** $|\psi(t)\rangle = e^{-i\omega t/2}|\alpha e^{-i\omega t}\rangle$ (coherent state with rotating eigenvalue).

## Classical Limit and Lasers

### Example 8: Classical Limit

Show that coherent states approach classical oscillation for large $|\alpha|$.

**Solution:**

Expectation value of position (in harmonic oscillator):
$$\langle\hat{x}(t)\rangle = \sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}}\langle\alpha(t)|(\hat{a} + \hat{a}^\dagger)|\alpha(t)\rangle$$

where $\alpha(t) = \alpha_0 e^{-i\omega t}$.

$$= \sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}}(\alpha_0 e^{-i\omega t} + \alpha_0^* e^{i\omega t}) = \sqrt{\frac{2\hbar}{m\omega}}|\alpha_0|\cos(\omega t - \phi)$$

Amplitude: $A = \sqrt{\frac{2\hbar}{m\omega}}|\alpha_0|$

Uncertainty: $\Delta x = \sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}}$ (constant, independent of $|\alpha|$)

Relative uncertainty:
$$\frac{\Delta x}{\langle x \rangle_{max}} = \frac{1}{2|\alpha_0|}$$

For large $|\alpha_0|$: Relative fluctuations vanish → classical oscillation.

**Answer:** Large $|\alpha|$: $\langle x(t) \rangle \approx A\cos(\omega t)$ classical, with $\Delta x/A \propto 1/|\alpha| \to 0$.

### Example 9: Laser Light

Describe the quantum state of laser light.

**Solution:**

**Ideal laser**: Produces coherent state $|\alpha\rangle$ with large $|\alpha|$.

Properties:
1. **Well-defined phase**: $\alpha = |\alpha|e^{i\phi}$ (coherent superposition)

2. **Poisson photon statistics**: $\Delta n/\bar{n} = 1/\sqrt{|\alpha|^2} \to 0$ for large $|\alpha|$

3. **Minimum uncertainty**: $\Delta E \Delta \phi = 1/2$ (can't do better)

4. **First-order coherence**: $g^{(1)}(\tau) = e^{-i\omega\tau}$ (perfect correlation)

Contrast with:
- **Thermal light**: Mixed state with Bose-Einstein statistics, $\Delta n > \sqrt{\bar{n}}$
- **Fock state**: Fixed photon number, completely undefined phase

**Real lasers**: Approximately coherent with small technical noise.

**Answer:** Laser ≈ coherent state with large $|\alpha|$ (well-defined phase, Poisson statistics, minimum uncertainty).

### Example 10: Coherent State Completeness

Show coherent states form an overcomplete basis.

**Solution:**

**Resolution of identity**: Can expand any state in coherent states.

$$\frac{1}{\pi}\int d^2\alpha \, |\alpha\rangle\langle\alpha| = \mathbb{1}$$

where $d^2\alpha = d(\text{Re}\alpha) \, d(\text{Im}\alpha)$ (integration over complex plane).

Proof (apply to Fock state):
$$\frac{1}{\pi}\int d^2\alpha \, |\alpha\rangle\langle\alpha|n\rangle = \frac{1}{\pi}\int d^2\alpha \, e^{-|\alpha|^2/2}\frac{(\alpha^*)^n}{\sqrt{n!}}|\alpha\rangle$$

Using polar coordinates $\alpha = re^{i\theta}$:
$$= \frac{1}{\pi}\int_0^\infty dr \int_0^{2\pi} d\theta \, r e^{-r^2/2}\frac{r^n e^{-in\theta}}{\sqrt{n!}}|re^{i\theta}\rangle$$

The $\theta$ integral picks out $|n\rangle$ component, giving $|n\rangle$ back.

**Overcomplete**: Not orthogonal ($\langle\beta|\alpha\rangle \neq 0$ for $\beta \neq \alpha$).

**Answer:** Completeness: $\frac{1}{\pi}\int d^2\alpha \, |\alpha\rangle\langle\alpha| = \mathbb{1}$ (overcomplete, non-orthogonal basis).

## Squeezed States

### Example 11: Squeezed State Definition

Define squeezed states and compare to coherent states.

**Solution:**

**Squeezed state**: Reduces uncertainty in one quadrature below vacuum level.

Squeezing operator:
$$\hat{S}(r) = e^{\frac{r}{2}(\hat{a}^2 - (\hat{a}^\dagger)^2)}$$

where $r$ is squeezing parameter (real).

Squeezed vacuum: $|0, r\rangle = \hat{S}(r)|0\rangle$

Uncertainties:
$$\Delta X = e^{-r}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}, \quad \Delta P = e^{r}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$$

Still saturates uncertainty: $\Delta X \Delta P = \frac{1}{2}$

But: $\Delta X < \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$ (below vacuum!) at expense of $\Delta P > \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$.

**Displaced squeezed state**: $|\alpha, r\rangle = \hat{D}(\alpha)\hat{S}(r)|0\rangle$

Most general Gaussian pure state.

**Answer:** Squeezed states: $\Delta X < 1/\sqrt{2}$ (sub-vacuum in one quadrature), $\Delta P > 1/\sqrt{2}$ (super-vacuum in other).

### Example 12: Applications of Squeezing

Describe applications of squeezed light.

**Solution:**

**Gravitational wave detection** (LIGO):
- Measure tiny displacement $\Delta L \sim 10^{-18}$ m
- Shot noise: $\Delta L \propto \Delta N/\sqrt{N} = 1/\sqrt{N}$
- Squeezed light: Reduce shot noise in phase quadrature
- LIGO uses squeezed vacuum to improve sensitivity by factor $\sim 3$ dB

**Quantum cryptography**:
- Squeezed states for continuous-variable QKD
- Security from Heisenberg uncertainty

**Quantum metrology**:
- Beat standard quantum limit $\Delta\phi \propto 1/\sqrt{N}$
- Approach Heisenberg limit $\Delta\phi \propto 1/N$

**Quantum computing**:
- Continuous-variable quantum computing
- Squeezed states as resource

**Answer:** Squeezing applications: LIGO (noise reduction), QKD, metrology beyond SQL, CV quantum computing.

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: B**
Coherent states: $\hat{a}|\alpha\rangle = \alpha|\alpha\rangle$
Eigenstates of annihilation operator
Eigenvalue $\alpha \in \mathbb{C}$ (complex, not quantized)
NOT eigenstates of $\hat{N}$, $\hat{H}$, or $\hat{a}^\dagger$
Closest quantum analog to classical oscillation

**Problem 2: C**
Mean photon number: $\langle n \rangle = |\alpha|^2$
Intensity $I \propto |\alpha|^2$
Variance: $(\Delta n)^2 = |\alpha|^2$ (Poisson)
Large $|\alpha|$: Classical limit
$\alpha = 0$: Vacuum state

**Problem 3: B**
Photon statistics: Poisson $P(n) = e^{-|\alpha|^2}\frac{|\alpha|^{2n}}{n!}$
Superposition of all Fock states
Variance equals mean: $(\Delta n)^2 = \bar{n}$
Different from thermal (super-Poissonian)
Different from Fock (zero variance)

**Problem 4: C**
Overcomplete basis: $\frac{1}{\pi}\int d^2\alpha |\alpha\rangle\langle\alpha| = \mathbb{1}$
NOT orthogonal: $\langle\beta|\alpha\rangle = e^{-|\alpha-\beta|^2/2} \neq 0$
Normalized: $\langle\alpha|\alpha\rangle = 1$
Form complete (overcomplete) set
Can expand any state

**Problem 5: C**
Squeezed: Reduce uncertainty in one quadrature
$\Delta X < 1/\sqrt{2}$ below vacuum (with $\Delta P > 1/\sqrt{2}$)
Still saturate: $\Delta X \Delta P = 1/2$ (Heisenberg limit)
Cannot have $\Delta X = 0$ (would need $\Delta P = \infty$)
Applications: LIGO, quantum metrology
