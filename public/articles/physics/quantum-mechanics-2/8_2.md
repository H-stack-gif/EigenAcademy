# Quantum Mechanics Topic 8.2: WKB Approximation and Tunneling

When quantum systems have slowly-varying potentials compared to the de Broglie wavelength, they behave **semi-classically**—almost like classical particles but with quantum corrections. The **WKB approximation** (Wentzel-Kramers-Brillouin, independently discovered by Jeffreys) exploits this regime, expressing wavefunctions as exponentials of action integrals. Instead of solving differential equations, we compute classical trajectories and phase accumulations. The method works beautifully for high quantum numbers (correspondence principle), slowly varying potentials, and away from turning points where classical motion reverses.

WKB's power appears most dramatically in **tunneling**—quantum penetration through classically forbidden regions. A particle with energy $E$ encountering barrier of height $V > E$ would classically reflect completely. Quantum mechanically, the wavefunction decays exponentially through the barrier with characteristic length $\sim \hbar/\sqrt{2m(V-E)}$. If the barrier is thin enough, significant amplitude emerges on the far side—the particle has tunneled through. The transmission probability is $T \sim \exp(-2\gamma)$ where $\gamma$ is the **Gamow factor**, an action integral through the forbidden region. Tunneling enables radioactive alpha decay, quantum transport in semiconductors, and scanning tunneling microscopy.

This article develops WKB approximation systematically. We'll derive the semi-classical waveforms in allowed and forbidden regions, understand connection formulas that match solutions across turning points, apply Bohr-Sommerfeld quantization to find energy levels, compute tunneling rates through barriers, and see applications from alpha decay to field emission. WKB bridges classical and quantum mechanics, revealing how classical behavior emerges as $\hbar \to 0$ while capturing purely quantum phenomena like tunneling.

## Semi-Classical Wavefunctions

In classical mechanics, Hamilton-Jacobi theory expresses motion through action function $S(x)$ with $p = dS/dx$. WKB adapts this to quantum mechanics.

### Example 1: WKB Ansatz

For slowly varying potential, assume wavefunction:

$$\psi(x) = A(x)e^{iS(x)/\hbar}$$

Insert into Schrödinger equation $-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\psi'' + V\psi = E\psi$ and derive WKB conditions.

**Solution:**

Derivatives:

$$\psi' = \left(A' + \frac{iA S'}{\hbar}\right)e^{iS/\hbar}$$

$$\psi'' = \left(A'' + \frac{2iA'S'}{\hbar} - \frac{AS'^2}{\hbar^2} + \frac{iAS''}{\hbar}\right)e^{iS/\hbar}$$

Schrödinger equation becomes:

$$-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\left(A'' + \frac{2iA'S'}{\hbar} - \frac{AS'^2}{\hbar^2} + \frac{iAS''}{\hbar}\right) + VA = EA$$

Collecting powers of $\hbar$:

**$\hbar^0$ (leading):** $\frac{S'^2}{2m} = E - V$ → $S' = \pm\sqrt{2m(E-V)} \equiv \pm p(x)$

**$\hbar^1$ (first correction):** $\frac{A''}{A} + \frac{S''}{p} + 2\frac{A'}{A}\frac{p}{p} = 0$

Second equation simplifies (slowly varying): $A \propto 1/\sqrt{p}$

**WKB solution:**

$$\psi(x) = \frac{C}{\sqrt{p(x)}}\exp\left[\pm\frac{i}{\hbar}\int p(x)dx\right]$$

**Validity:** $|\psi''/\psi| \ll k^2$ → $\lambda_{dB} \ll L$ (wavelength $\ll$ potential scale)

**Answer:** WKB gives $\psi \propto \frac{1}{\sqrt{p}}\exp[\pm i\int p dx/\hbar]$ for slowly varying $V$

### Example 2: Classically Allowed Region

In region where $E > V(x)$, momentum $p(x) = \sqrt{2m(E-V)}$ is real. Write general WKB solution.

**Solution:**

$$\psi(x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{p(x)}}\left[C_+\exp\left(\frac{i}{\hbar}\int_{x_0}^x p(x')dx'\right) + C_-\exp\left(-\frac{i}{\hbar}\int_{x_0}^x p(x')dx'\right)\right]$$

**Physical interpretation:**
- First term: wave traveling right (positive group velocity)
- Second term: wave traveling left
- Amplitude $\propto 1/\sqrt{p}$: conservation of probability current $j = \frac{p}{m}|\psi|^2$

As $p$ increases (particle speeds up): amplitude decreases to conserve flux.

**Classical limit:** Wavefunctions concentrate near classical trajectory as $\hbar \to 0$.

**Answer:** $\psi = \frac{C_+}{\sqrt{p}}e^{i\phi_+} + \frac{C_-}{\sqrt{p}}e^{i\phi_-}$ where $\phi_\pm = \pm\frac{1}{\hbar}\int p dx$

The factor $1/\sqrt{p}$ ensures that fast-moving particles have smaller amplitude, consistent with probability conservation.

### Example 3: Classically Forbidden Region

For $E < V(x)$, momentum $p = i\kappa$ where $\kappa(x) = \sqrt{2m(V-E)}$ is real.

Write WKB solution.

**Solution:**

With $p = i\kappa$:

$$\psi(x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\kappa(x)}}\left[D_+\exp\left(-\frac{1}{\hbar}\int_{x_0}^x\kappa(x')dx'\right) + D_-\exp\left(+\frac{1}{\hbar}\int_{x_0}^x\kappa(x')dx'\right)\right]$$

**Physical interpretation:**
- First term: exponentially decaying (penetration into barrier)
- Second term: exponentially growing (unphysical if unbounded)

For barrier: choose $D_- = 0$ to avoid divergence.

$$\psi(x) \sim \frac{1}{\sqrt{\kappa}}e^{-\int\kappa dx/\hbar}$$

**Decay length:** $\lambda \sim \hbar/\kappa = \hbar/\sqrt{2m(V-E)}$

Higher barriers or heavier particles → faster decay.

**Answer:** $\psi \sim \frac{1}{\sqrt{\kappa}}\exp[-\int\kappa dx/\hbar]$ (exponential decay in forbidden region)

## Connection Formulas

At classical turning points where $E = V(x)$, momentum $p \to 0$ and WKB fails ($1/\sqrt{p} \to \infty$). **Connection formulas** match solutions across turning points using exact solutions (Airy functions) in transition region.

### Example 4: Connection Formula Derivation Sketch

Near turning point $x_1$ where $E = V(x_1)$, linearize potential: $V(x) \approx E + F(x-x_1)$ where $F = V'(x_1)$.

Show Schrödinger equation reduces to Airy equation.

**Solution:**

In region near $x_1$: $E - V \approx -F(x-x_1)$

Define $\xi = \left(\frac{2mF}{\hbar^2}\right)^{1/3}(x-x_1)$

Schrödinger equation: $\frac{d^2\psi}{d\xi^2} = \xi\psi$ (Airy equation)

**Solutions:** Airy functions $\text{Ai}(\xi)$ and $\text{Bi}(\xi)$

Asymptotic behavior:
- $\xi \to +\infty$ (forbidden): $\text{Ai}(\xi) \sim \frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi}\xi^{1/4}}e^{-2\xi^{3/2}/3}$ (exponential decay)
- $\xi \to -\infty$ (allowed): $\text{Ai}(\xi) \sim \frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}|\xi|^{1/4}}\sin(2|\xi|^{3/2}/3 + \pi/4)$ (oscillatory)

Matching WKB solutions to Airy asymptotics gives **connection formulas**.

**Answer:** Airy function interpolates between oscillatory (allowed) and exponential (forbidden) regions

### Example 5: Left Turning Point Connection

For turning point at $x = a$ with allowed region $x < a$ and forbidden region $x > a$, the connection formula is:

$$\frac{1}{\sqrt{|p|}}\cos\left(\frac{1}{\hbar}\int_x^a|p|dx' - \frac{\pi}{4}\right) \xrightarrow{x=a} \frac{1}{\sqrt{\kappa}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{\hbar}\int_a^x\kappa dx'\right)$$

Explain the $\pi/4$ phase shift.

**Solution:**

The $\pi/4$ arises from asymptotic phase of Airy function.

**Physical meaning:** Reflection phase shift at turning point.

Without $\pi/4$: incorrect interference in bound state problems.

**Critical:** Connection formulas are **not symmetric**—depends on direction of approach.

From forbidden to allowed (opposite direction) has different form:

$$\frac{2}{\sqrt{|p|}}\cos\left(\frac{1}{\hbar}\int_x^a|p|dx' - \frac{\pi}{4}\right) \xleftarrow{x=a} \frac{1}{\sqrt{\kappa}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{\hbar}\int_a^x\kappa dx'\right)$$

**Factor 2 difference:** One-way matching (decay → oscillation gives factor 2; oscillation → decay gives 1).

**Answer:** $\pi/4$ phase shift from Airy function; factor depends on matching direction

## Bohr-Sommerfeld Quantization

For bound states in potential well with turning points $x_1$ and $x_2$, applying connection formulas at both ends yields quantization condition.

### Example 6: Deriving Bohr-Sommerfeld Formula

For particle in potential well, require wavefunction consistent after one round trip. Show this gives:

$$\oint p dx = 2\pi\hbar\left(n + \frac{1}{2}\right)$$

**Solution:**

Wave travels from $x_1$ to $x_2$ and back. Phase accumulated:

$$\phi = \frac{2}{\hbar}\int_{x_1}^{x_2}p dx$$

Connection formulas at both turning points contribute $-\pi/4$ each (total $-\pi/2$).

For constructive interference: $\phi - \pi/2 = 2\pi n$

$$\frac{2}{\hbar}\int_{x_1}^{x_2}p dx = 2\pi n + \frac{\pi}{2} = \pi\left(2n + \frac{1}{2}\right)$$

$$\int_{x_1}^{x_2}p dx = \pi\hbar\left(n + \frac{1}{2}\right)$$

For full cycle (factor 2): $\oint p dx = 2\pi\hbar(n + 1/2)$

**Old quantum theory:** Bohr's semiclassical quantization recovered!

**Answer:** $\int_{x_1}^{x_2} p dx = \pi\hbar(n + 1/2)$ (Bohr-Sommerfeld quantization)

### Example 7: WKB for Harmonic Oscillator

Apply Bohr-Sommerfeld to harmonic oscillator $V = \frac{1}{2}m\omega^2 x^2$. Derive energy levels.

**Solution:**

Turning points: $\frac{1}{2}m\omega^2x^2 = E$ → $x = \pm\sqrt{2E/(m\omega^2)}$

Momentum: $p = \sqrt{2m(E - \frac{1}{2}m\omega^2x^2)}$

Integral (using substitution $x = \sqrt{2E/(m\omega^2)}\sin\theta$):

$$\int_{-x_1}^{x_1}p dx = \int_{-x_1}^{x_1}\sqrt{2mE - m^2\omega^2x^2}dx$$

$$= 2\sqrt{2mE}\int_0^{x_1}\sqrt{1 - \frac{m\omega^2x^2}{2E}}dx = \frac{\pi E}{\omega}$$

Bohr-Sommerfeld: $\frac{\pi E}{\omega} = \pi\hbar(n + 1/2)$

$$E_n = \hbar\omega\left(n + \frac{1}{2}\right)$$

**Exact result!** WKB gives correct energies for harmonic oscillator.

This is special—usually WKB approximate, but harmonic oscillator special because uniform force.

**Answer:** $E_n = \hbar\omega(n+1/2)$ (exact from WKB for harmonic oscillator)

## Tunneling Through Barriers

For barrier of height $V > E$ from $x = a$ to $x = b$, transmission probability involves exponential of action through forbidden region.

### Example 8: Tunneling Probability

For rectangular barrier $V_0$ from $x=0$ to $x=a$, particle with $E < V_0$ has transmission:

$$T \approx \exp\left[-\frac{2}{\hbar}\int_0^a\sqrt{2m(V_0-E)}dx\right]$$

Evaluate for constant barrier.

**Solution:**

$$\int_0^a\sqrt{2m(V_0-E)}dx = a\sqrt{2m(V_0-E)} \equiv a\kappa$$

Transmission:

$$T = \exp(-2\kappa a) = \exp\left[-\frac{2a}{\hbar}\sqrt{2m(V_0-E)}\right]$$

**Thin barrier** ($\kappa a \ll 1$): $T \approx 1 - 2\kappa a$ (nearly transparent)

**Thick barrier** ($\kappa a \gg 1$): $T \ll 1$ (exponentially suppressed)

**Energy dependence:** $T$ increases rapidly as $E \to V_0$ (exponential in $\sqrt{V_0-E}$)

**Answer:** $T = \exp[-2a\sqrt{2m(V_0-E)}/\hbar]$ for rectangular barrier

### Example 9: Alpha Decay

Radioactive nucleus emits alpha particle through Coulomb barrier. Model as:

$$V(r) = \begin{cases} V_0 & r < R \\ \frac{2Ze^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0 r} & r > R \end{cases}$$

Alpha has energy $E$ inside nucleus. Estimate decay rate.

**Solution:**

**Turning points:** Inside at $r = R$, outside where $E = \frac{2Ze^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0 r_2}$

$$r_2 = \frac{2Ze^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0 E}$$

**Gamow factor:**

$$\gamma = \frac{1}{\hbar}\int_R^{r_2}\sqrt{2m\left(\frac{2Ze^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0 r} - E\right)}dr$$

After integration (non-trivial):

$$\gamma \approx \frac{2Z}{\hbar}\sqrt{\frac{me^2}{2\pi\epsilon_0}}\left[\arccos\sqrt{\frac{R}{r_2}} - \sqrt{\frac{R}{r_2}\left(1-\frac{R}{r_2}\right)}\right]$$

For $R \ll r_2$ (usual case):

$$\gamma \approx \frac{\pi Ze^2}{\hbar v}\sqrt{\frac{m}{2E}}$$

**Decay rate:** $\Gamma \sim \nu e^{-2\gamma}$ where $\nu \sim$ attempt frequency

Exponential sensitivity to $Z$ and $E$ explains wide range of alpha half-lives ($\mu$s to $10^{10}$ years).

**Answer:** $\Gamma \propto \exp[-2\pi Z e^2\sqrt{m/(2E)}/\hbar v]$ (Gamow formula)

### Example 10: Field Emission

Electrons in metal face potential barrier:

$$V(x) = \begin{cases} -W & x < 0 \\ -W + eEx & x > 0 \end{cases}$$

where $W$ is work function, $E$ is electric field. Compute tunneling current.

**Solution:**

**Barrier:** Triangular shape from linear electric potential

Electron at Fermi level $E_F$ sees barrier from $x=0$ to turning point $x_t = W/(eE)$

**Tunneling probability:**

$$T \approx \exp\left[-\frac{2}{\hbar}\int_0^{W/(eE)}\sqrt{2m(W - eEx)}dx\right]$$

Integral: $\int_0^{x_t}\sqrt{W - eEx}dx = \frac{2W^{3/2}}{3eE}$

$$T \sim \exp\left[-\frac{4\sqrt{2mW^3}}{3\hbar eE}\right]$$

**Current density:** $j \propto E^2 T$ (Fowler-Nordheim equation)

Exponential field dependence enables field emission microscopy: apply large $E$ → electrons tunnel out.

**Answer:** $j \propto E^2\exp[-\text{const}/E]$ (field emission current)

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: B**
WKB valid when **wavelength $\ll$ potential scale**: $\lambda_{dB} = h/p \ll L$ where $L$ is distance over which $V$ changes significantly. Equivalently: $|V'|/(V-E) \ll p/\hbar$. Slowly varying potential allows semi-classical approximation. **Fails** at turning points ($p \to 0$, $\lambda \to \infty$), requires connection formulas. Also fails for rapid oscillations, sharp features. High quantum numbers satisfy WKB (correspondence principle): large $n$ → small $\lambda/L$. Deep wells better than shallow.

**Problem 2: C**
Forbidden region ($E < V$): momentum imaginary, $p = i\kappa$ where $\kappa = \sqrt{2m(V-E)}$ real. WKB: $\psi \sim \frac{1}{\sqrt{\kappa}}\exp[-\int\kappa dx/\hbar]$ decays exponentially. Physical: quantum penetration into classically forbidden region. Decay length $\sim \hbar/\kappa$—higher barriers or heavier mass → faster decay. Tunneling exploits: if barrier thin enough, significant amplitude on far side. Impossible classically. Growth ($\exp[+\int\kappa dx]$) unphysical in infinite barrier.

**Problem 3: B**
Connection formulas at turning point introduce $\pm\pi/4$ phase shift from Airy function asymptotics. Critical for Bohr-Sommerfeld: bound state has two turning points → total $-\pi/2$ → quantization $\oint p dx = 2\pi\hbar(n+1/2)$. Without $\pi/4$: wrong energies. Direction-dependent: forbidden → allowed different from allowed → forbidden (factor 2 amplitude difference). Physically: reflection phase at classical turning point. Semi-classical quantum correction to classical orbit quantization.

**Problem 4: C**
Bohr-Sommerfeld: $\oint p dx = 2\pi\hbar(n+1/2)$ where integral over full classical orbit. Equivalent (one-way): $\int_{x_1}^{x_2}p dx = \pi\hbar(n+1/2)$. **Not** $2\pi\hbar n$—the 1/2 crucial! Comes from connection formula phases. Old quantum theory (Bohr): $\oint p dx = nh$; WKB corrects to $(n+1/2)$. Matches exact quantum: harmonic oscillator $E_n = \hbar\omega(n+1/2)$, hydrogen also correct. Semi-classical quantization from wave interference.

**Problem 5: C**
Tunneling: $T = \exp[-2\gamma]$ where **Gamow factor** $\gamma = \frac{1}{\hbar}\int_{x_1}^{x_2}\kappa dx$ through barrier. For rectangular barrier: $\gamma = a\sqrt{2m(V_0-E)}/\hbar$. Exponential dependence on: (1) barrier width $a$, (2) barrier height $(V_0-E)$, (3) particle mass $m$. Extremely sensitive: small changes in $V_0, a$ dramatically affect $T$. Alpha decay: explains $10^{20}$ range in lifetimes. STM: exponential distance dependence enables atomic resolution. Not linear or power-law—exponential suppression.
