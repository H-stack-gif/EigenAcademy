# Article 7.4: Small Oscillations

Systems near stable equilibrium undergo small oscillations describable by linear differential equations. The Lagrangian expanded to second order yields kinetic energy as a quadratic form $T = \frac{1}{2}\dot{\mathbf{q}}^T\mathbf{M}\dot{\mathbf{q}}$ and potential energy as $V = \frac{1}{2}\mathbf{q}^T\mathbf{K}\mathbf{q}$ (measuring from equilibrium). The mass matrix $\mathbf{M}$ and stiffness matrix $\mathbf{K}$ are symmetric, enabling simultaneous diagonalization that decouples the equations into independent normal modes. Each mode oscillates at its characteristic frequency determined by the generalized eigenvalue problem $\mathbf{K}\mathbf{a} = \omega^2\mathbf{M}\mathbf{a}$.

This matrix formulation unifies coupled oscillator theory: normal modes are eigenvectors, frequencies are eigenvalues, and orthogonality follows from matrix symmetry. The transformation to normal coordinates diagonalizes both $\mathbf{M}$ and $\mathbf{K}$ simultaneously, revealing the underlying independent oscillator structure. This framework applies to molecules, crystals, electromagnetic cavities, and any system with multiple coupled degrees of freedom near equilibrium.

This article develops small oscillations systematically: expanding energy about equilibrium, expressing kinetic and potential energy as quadratic forms, deriving the matrix eigenvalue problem, finding normal modes through diagonalization, and establishing orthogonality relations. The mathematics emphasizes linear algebra—symmetric matrices, eigenvalues/eigenvectors, quadratic forms, simultaneous diagonalization. Applications span molecular vibrations, coupled pendulums, and crystal lattices.

## Energy Expansion Near Equilibrium

For system with generalized coordinates $\mathbf{q} = (q_1, ..., q_n)$, expand Lagrangian about stable equilibrium $\mathbf{q}_0$:

$$L(\mathbf{q}, \dot{\mathbf{q}}) = L(\mathbf{q}_0, \mathbf{0}) + \frac{\partial L}{\partial \mathbf{q}}\Big|_0 \cdot \delta\mathbf{q} + \frac{1}{2}\delta\mathbf{q}^T \frac{\partial^2 L}{\partial \mathbf{q}^2}\Big|_0 \delta\mathbf{q} + ...$$

**At equilibrium:** $\partial V/\partial \mathbf{q} = 0$ and $\mathbf{q}_0 = 0$ (shift coordinates).

**To second order:**

**Kinetic energy (quadratic form in $\dot{\mathbf{q}}$):**
$$T = \frac{1}{2}\sum_{ij} M_{ij}\dot{q}_i\dot{q}_j = \frac{1}{2}\dot{\mathbf{q}}^T\mathbf{M}\dot{\mathbf{q}}$$

where **mass matrix** $\mathbf{M}$ is symmetric and positive definite.

**Potential energy (quadratic form in $\mathbf{q}$):**
$$V = V_0 + \frac{1}{2}\sum_{ij} K_{ij}q_iq_j = V_0 + \frac{1}{2}\mathbf{q}^T\mathbf{K}\mathbf{q}$$

where **stiffness matrix** $\mathbf{K}$ is symmetric. For stable equilibrium, $\mathbf{K}$ is positive definite.

**Lagrangian:**
$$L = \frac{1}{2}\dot{\mathbf{q}}^T\mathbf{M}\dot{\mathbf{q}} - \frac{1}{2}\mathbf{q}^T\mathbf{K}\mathbf{q}$$

(dropping constant $V_0$)

### Example 1: Double Pendulum Small Oscillations

Two pendulums (lengths $\ell$, masses $m$) coupled by spring ($k$) at distance $d$ below pivots. Find mass and stiffness matrices for small angles $\theta_1$, $\theta_2$.

**Solution:**

**Kinetic energy:** Each pendulum has $T_i = \frac{1}{2}m\ell^2\dot{\theta}_i^2$:
$$T = \frac{1}{2}m\ell^2(\dot{\theta}_1^2 + \dot{\theta}_2^2)$$

**Mass matrix:**
$$\mathbf{M} = m\ell^2\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$$

**Potential energy:**
- Gravity: $V_g = mg\ell(2 - \cos\theta_1 - \cos\theta_2) \approx mg\ell(\theta_1^2/2 + \theta_2^2/2)$
- Spring: extension $\approx d(\theta_1 - \theta_2)$, so $V_s = \frac{1}{2}kd^2(\theta_1 - \theta_2)^2$

$$V = \frac{1}{2}mg\ell(\theta_1^2 + \theta_2^2) + \frac{1}{2}kd^2(\theta_1 - \theta_2)^2$$

$$= \frac{1}{2}[(mg\ell + kd^2)\theta_1^2 + (mg\ell + kd^2)\theta_2^2 - 2kd^2\theta_1\theta_2]$$

**Stiffness matrix:**
$$\mathbf{K} = \begin{pmatrix} mg\ell + kd^2 & -kd^2 \\ -kd^2 & mg\ell + kd^2 \end{pmatrix}$$

**Answer:** $\mathbf{M} = m\ell^2\mathbf{I}$, $\mathbf{K} = \begin{pmatrix} mg\ell + kd^2 & -kd^2 \\ -kd^2 & mg\ell + kd^2 \end{pmatrix}$

## Matrix Equations of Motion

**Euler-Lagrange equations:**
$$\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot{q}_i}\right) - \frac{\partial L}{\partial q_i} = 0$$

From $L = \frac{1}{2}\dot{\mathbf{q}}^T\mathbf{M}\dot{\mathbf{q}} - \frac{1}{2}\mathbf{q}^T\mathbf{K}\mathbf{q}$:

$$\frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot{q}_i} = \sum_j M_{ij}\dot{q}_j, \quad \frac{\partial L}{\partial q_i} = -\sum_j K_{ij}q_j$$

**Matrix form:**
$$\mathbf{M}\ddot{\mathbf{q}} + \mathbf{K}\mathbf{q} = \mathbf{0}$$

This is a system of $n$ coupled second-order ODEs.

**Normal mode ansatz:** Seek solutions $\mathbf{q}(t) = \mathbf{a}e^{i\omega t}$:

$$-\omega^2\mathbf{M}\mathbf{a}e^{i\omega t} + \mathbf{K}\mathbf{a}e^{i\omega t} = \mathbf{0}$$

$$\mathbf{K}\mathbf{a} = \omega^2\mathbf{M}\mathbf{a}$$

This is a **generalized eigenvalue problem**.

### Example 2: Solving Eigenvalue Problem

For $\mathbf{M} = m\mathbf{I}$, $\mathbf{K} = k\begin{pmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ -1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$, find normal mode frequencies.

**Solution:**

Eigenvalue equation: $\mathbf{K}\mathbf{a} = \omega^2 m\mathbf{a}$

$$k\begin{pmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ -1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} a_1 \\ a_2 \end{pmatrix} = \omega^2 m\begin{pmatrix} a_1 \\ a_2 \end{pmatrix}$$

**Characteristic equation:**
$$\det(\mathbf{K} - \omega^2 m\mathbf{I}) = 0$$

$$\det\begin{pmatrix} 2k - \omega^2 m & -k \\ -k & 2k - \omega^2 m \end{pmatrix} = 0$$

$$(2k - \omega^2 m)^2 - k^2 = 0$$

$$(2k - \omega^2 m) = \pm k$$

**Mode 1:** $2k - \omega_1^2 m = k \implies \omega_1^2 = k/m$, so $\omega_1 = \sqrt{k/m}$

**Mode 2:** $2k - \omega_2^2 m = -k \implies \omega_2^2 = 3k/m$, so $\omega_2 = \sqrt{3k/m}$

**Answer:** $\omega_1 = \sqrt{k/m}$, $\omega_2 = \sqrt{3k/m}$

## Normal Mode Eigenvectors

For each eigenvalue $\omega_i^2$, find eigenvector $\mathbf{a}^{(i)}$ from:
$$(\mathbf{K} - \omega_i^2\mathbf{M})\mathbf{a}^{(i)} = \mathbf{0}$$

### Example 3: Finding Eigenvectors

For system in Example 2, find normal mode eigenvectors.

**Solution:**

**Mode 1** ($\omega_1^2 = k/m$):
$$\begin{pmatrix} 2k - k & -k \\ -k & 2k - k \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} a_1 \\ a_2 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} k & -k \\ -k & k \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} a_1 \\ a_2 \end{pmatrix} = \mathbf{0}$$

First row: $ka_1 - ka_2 = 0 \implies a_1 = a_2$

Normalized: $\mathbf{a}^{(1)} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}$ (in-phase mode)

**Mode 2** ($\omega_2^2 = 3k/m$):
$$\begin{pmatrix} 2k - 3k & -k \\ -k & 2k - 3k \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} a_1 \\ a_2 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -k & -k \\ -k & -k \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} a_1 \\ a_2 \end{pmatrix} = \mathbf{0}$$

First row: $-ka_1 - ka_2 = 0 \implies a_1 = -a_2$

Normalized: $\mathbf{a}^{(2)} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix}$ (out-of-phase mode)

**Answer:** $\mathbf{a}^{(1)} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}$, $\mathbf{a}^{(2)} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix}$

## Orthogonality Relations

Normal mode eigenvectors satisfy **orthogonality with respect to $\mathbf{M}$ and $\mathbf{K}$:**

$$(\mathbf{a}^{(i)})^T\mathbf{M}\mathbf{a}^{(j)} = \mu_i\delta_{ij}$$

$$(\mathbf{a}^{(i)})^T\mathbf{K}\mathbf{a}^{(j)} = \mu_i\omega_i^2\delta_{ij}$$

**Proof:** From $\mathbf{K}\mathbf{a}^{(i)} = \omega_i^2\mathbf{M}\mathbf{a}^{(i)}$ and $\mathbf{K}\mathbf{a}^{(j)} = \omega_j^2\mathbf{M}\mathbf{a}^{(j)}$:

Multiply first by $(\mathbf{a}^{(j)})^T$:
$$(\mathbf{a}^{(j)})^T\mathbf{K}\mathbf{a}^{(i)} = \omega_i^2(\mathbf{a}^{(j)})^T\mathbf{M}\mathbf{a}^{(i)}$$

Multiply second by $(\mathbf{a}^{(i)})^T$:
$$(\mathbf{a}^{(i)})^T\mathbf{K}\mathbf{a}^{(j)} = \omega_j^2(\mathbf{a}^{(i)})^T\mathbf{M}\mathbf{a}^{(j)}$$

Since $\mathbf{K}$ and $\mathbf{M}$ are symmetric, left sides are equal. Subtract:
$$0 = (\omega_i^2 - \omega_j^2)(\mathbf{a}^{(j)})^T\mathbf{M}\mathbf{a}^{(i)}$$

For $\omega_i \neq \omega_j$: $(\mathbf{a}^{(j)})^T\mathbf{M}\mathbf{a}^{(i)} = 0$ ∎

### Example 4: Verifying Orthogonality

Verify orthogonality for modes in Example 3 with $\mathbf{M} = m\mathbf{I}$.

**Solution:**

$$(\mathbf{a}^{(1)})^T\mathbf{M}\mathbf{a}^{(2)} = \frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix} m\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix}$$

$$= \frac{m}{2}\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix} = \frac{m}{2}(1 - 1) = 0$$ ✓

**Answer:** Modes are orthogonal

## Normal Coordinates

Form **modal matrix** $\mathbf{A} = [\mathbf{a}^{(1)} | ... | \mathbf{a}^{(n)}]$ with eigenvectors as columns.

**Coordinate transformation:**
$$\mathbf{q} = \mathbf{A}\boldsymbol{\eta}$$

where $\boldsymbol{\eta} = (\eta_1, ..., \eta_n)^T$ are **normal coordinates**.

**Transformed kinetic energy:**
$$T = \frac{1}{2}\dot{\mathbf{q}}^T\mathbf{M}\dot{\mathbf{q}} = \frac{1}{2}\dot{\boldsymbol{\eta}}^T\mathbf{A}^T\mathbf{M}\mathbf{A}\dot{\boldsymbol{\eta}}$$

By orthogonality: $\mathbf{A}^T\mathbf{M}\mathbf{A} = \text{diag}(\mu_1, ..., \mu_n)$

**Transformed potential energy:**
$$V = \frac{1}{2}\mathbf{q}^T\mathbf{K}\mathbf{q} = \frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{\eta}^T\mathbf{A}^T\mathbf{K}\mathbf{A}\boldsymbol{\eta}$$

By eigenvalue equation: $\mathbf{A}^T\mathbf{K}\mathbf{A} = \text{diag}(\mu_1\omega_1^2, ..., \mu_n\omega_n^2)$

**Decoupled equations:**
$$\mu_i\ddot{\eta}_i + \mu_i\omega_i^2\eta_i = 0$$

$$\ddot{\eta}_i + \omega_i^2\eta_i = 0$$

Each normal coordinate oscillates independently!

### Example 5: General Solution

For system in Examples 2-3, write general solution.

**Solution:**

**Normal coordinates:**
$$\eta_1(t) = A_1\cos(\omega_1 t + \phi_1) = A_1\cos(\sqrt{k/m}\, t + \phi_1)$$

$$\eta_2(t) = A_2\cos(\omega_2 t + \phi_2) = A_2\cos(\sqrt{3k/m}\, t + \phi_2)$$

**Transform back:**
$$\mathbf{q} = \mathbf{A}\boldsymbol{\eta} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & -1 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} \eta_1 \\ \eta_2 \end{pmatrix}$$

$$q_1 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\eta_1 + \eta_2)$$

$$q_2 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\eta_1 - \eta_2)$$

Constants $A_1, A_2, \phi_1, \phi_2$ determined by initial conditions.

**Answer:** Superposition of two normal modes at frequencies $\omega_1, \omega_2$
