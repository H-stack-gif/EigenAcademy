# Article 7.2: Lagrangian Mechanics

Lagrangian mechanics reformulates Newtonian mechanics through energy rather than forces. The Lagrangian $L = T - V$ (kinetic minus potential energy) completely determines dynamics through the Euler-Lagrange equations. This approach excels for systems with constraints, complex coordinate systems, or many degrees of freedom. Generalized coordinates—any set of independent variables specifying configuration—replace Cartesian coordinates, automatically satisfying constraints and simplifying equations of motion.

The power of Lagrangian mechanics lies in coordinate independence and manifest conservation laws. Noether's theorem connects symmetries to conserved quantities: time-translation invariance yields energy conservation, spatial translation gives momentum conservation, rotational symmetry implies angular momentum conservation. This framework extends naturally to field theory, relativistic mechanics, and quantum mechanics, making it fundamental to modern physics.

This article develops Lagrangian mechanics from Hamilton's principle, introduces generalized coordinates and velocities, derives the Euler-Lagrange equations for mechanical systems, establishes conservation laws, and handles constrained systems. The mathematics employs calculus of variations, differential equations, and coordinate transformations. Applications span pendulums, coupled systems, rotating frames, and continuous systems.

## Hamilton's Principle

**Hamilton's Principle (Principle of Least Action):** The actual path taken by a system between times $t_1$ and $t_2$ extremizes the **action**:

$$S = \int_{t_1}^{t_2} L(q, \dot{q}, t) dt$$

where $L(q, \dot{q}, t) = T - V$ is the **Lagrangian** (kinetic minus potential energy).

**Applying Euler-Lagrange:** For system with generalized coordinates $q_1, ..., q_n$:

$$\frac{\partial L}{\partial q_i} - \frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot{q}_i}\right) = 0, \quad i = 1, ..., n$$

These are the **Euler-Lagrange equations** (or **Lagrange's equations**).

### Example 1: Simple Harmonic Oscillator

Derive equation of motion for mass $m$ on spring with constant $k$ using Lagrangian.

**Solution:**

Generalized coordinate: $x$ (displacement from equilibrium)

**Kinetic energy:** $T = \frac{1}{2}m\dot{x}^2$

**Potential energy:** $V = \frac{1}{2}kx^2$

**Lagrangian:**
$$L = T - V = \frac{1}{2}m\dot{x}^2 - \frac{1}{2}kx^2$$

**Euler-Lagrange equation:**
$$\frac{\partial L}{\partial x} = -kx$$

$$\frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot{x}} = m\dot{x}$$

$$\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot{x}}\right) = m\ddot{x}$$

$$-kx - m\ddot{x} = 0$$

$$m\ddot{x} + kx = 0$$ ✓

**Answer:** $\ddot{x} + \omega_0^2 x = 0$ where $\omega_0 = \sqrt{k/m}$

## Generalized Coordinates

**Generalized coordinates** $q_i$ are any set of independent variables that uniquely specify the system configuration.

**Degrees of freedom:** Number of independent generalized coordinates needed.

**Examples:**
- Particle in 3D: $(x, y, z)$ or $(r, \theta, \phi)$
- Pendulum: $\theta$ (angle)
- Two-body problem: $(\mathbf{r}_1, \mathbf{r}_2)$ or $(\mathbf{R}_{CM}, \mathbf{r}_{rel})$

**Generalized velocities:** $\dot{q}_i = dq_i/dt$

**Generalized momenta:** $p_i = \frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot{q}_i}$

### Example 2: Pendulum in Lagrangian Formulation

Derive equation of motion for simple pendulum (length $\ell$, mass $m$) using angle $\theta$ from vertical.

**Solution:**

**Generalized coordinate:** $\theta$

**Position:** $x = \ell\sin\theta$, $y = -\ell\cos\theta$ (taking $y = 0$ at pivot)

**Velocity:** $\dot{x} = \ell\cos\theta \cdot \dot{\theta}$, $\dot{y} = \ell\sin\theta \cdot \dot{\theta}$

$$v^2 = \dot{x}^2 + \dot{y}^2 = \ell^2\dot{\theta}^2(\cos^2\theta + \sin^2\theta) = \ell^2\dot{\theta}^2$$

**Kinetic energy:** $T = \frac{1}{2}m\ell^2\dot{\theta}^2$

**Potential energy:** $V = mgy = -mg\ell\cos\theta$ (taking $V = 0$ at pivot)

**Lagrangian:**
$$L = \frac{1}{2}m\ell^2\dot{\theta}^2 + mg\ell\cos\theta$$

**Euler-Lagrange:**
$$\frac{\partial L}{\partial \theta} = -mg\ell\sin\theta$$

$$\frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot{\theta}} = m\ell^2\dot{\theta}$$

$$-mg\ell\sin\theta - \frac{d}{dt}(m\ell^2\dot{\theta}) = 0$$

$$m\ell^2\ddot{\theta} + mg\ell\sin\theta = 0$$

$$\ddot{\theta} + \frac{g}{\ell}\sin\theta = 0$$

**Answer:** $\ddot{\theta} + \frac{g}{\ell}\sin\theta = 0$ (nonlinear pendulum equation)

## Conservation Laws from Symmetries

**Cyclic (ignorable) coordinates:** If $L$ doesn't depend on $q_i$ (but may depend on $\dot{q}_i$):

$$\frac{\partial L}{\partial q_i} = 0$$

Then Euler-Lagrange gives:
$$\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot{q}_i}\right) = 0$$

So $p_i = \frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot{q}_i} = $ constant (conserved generalized momentum).

**Energy conservation:** If $L$ doesn't depend explicitly on time ($\partial L/\partial t = 0$), then the **Hamiltonian** (total energy for natural systems) is conserved:

$$H = \sum_i \dot{q}_i\frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot{q}_i} - L = \text{constant}$$

For $L = T - V$ with $T$ quadratic in velocities: $H = T + V = E$.

### Example 3: Central Force in Polar Coordinates

For central force $V(r)$, show angular momentum is conserved using Lagrangian.

**Solution:**

**Generalized coordinates:** $(r, \theta)$

**Kinetic energy:** $T = \frac{1}{2}m(\dot{r}^2 + r^2\dot{\theta}^2)$

**Lagrangian:**
$$L = \frac{1}{2}m(\dot{r}^2 + r^2\dot{\theta}^2) - V(r)$$

Note: $L$ doesn't depend on $\theta$ (only on $r$ and derivatives). So $\theta$ is **cyclic**.

**Conserved momentum:**
$$p_\theta = \frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot{\theta}} = mr^2\dot{\theta} = L_z$$

This is angular momentum! ✓

**Answer:** $L_z = mr^2\dot{\theta} = $ constant (angular momentum conserved)

## Constrained Systems

For constraints of form $f(q_1, ..., q_n) = 0$, use **Lagrange multipliers** $\lambda$:

**Modified Lagrangian:**
$$L' = L - \lambda f(q_1, ..., q_n)$$

Apply Euler-Lagrange to $L'$ along with constraint equation.

### Example 4: Atwood's Machine

Two masses $m_1$, $m_2$ connected by massless string over frictionless pulley. Find acceleration.

**Solution:**

**Generalized coordinates:** $x_1$ (position of $m_1$), $x_2$ (position of $m_2$)

**Constraint:** $x_1 + x_2 = \ell$ (constant string length), so $\dot{x}_1 = -\dot{x}_2$

**Alternative:** Use single coordinate $x = x_1$ (then $x_2 = \ell - x$).

**Lagrangian:**
$$L = \frac{1}{2}m_1\dot{x}^2 + \frac{1}{2}m_2\dot{x}^2 - m_1gx - m_2g(\ell - x)$$

$$= \frac{1}{2}(m_1 + m_2)\dot{x}^2 - (m_1 - m_2)gx - m_2g\ell$$

**Euler-Lagrange:**
$$\frac{\partial L}{\partial x} = -(m_1 - m_2)g$$

$$\frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot{x}} = (m_1 + m_2)\dot{x}$$

$$-(m_1 - m_2)g - (m_1 + m_2)\ddot{x} = 0$$

$$\ddot{x} = \frac{(m_2 - m_1)g}{m_1 + m_2}$$

**Answer:** $a = \frac{(m_2 - m_1)g}{m_1 + m_2}$ (standard Atwood result)

### Example 5: Bead on Rotating Wire

A bead of mass $m$ slides without friction on a wire rotating at constant $\omega$ about vertical axis. Wire makes angle $\alpha$ with vertical. Find equation of motion for bead position $r$ along wire.

**Solution:**

**Generalized coordinate:** $r$ (distance from rotation axis along wire)

In rotating frame with angular velocity $\omega$:

**Position in cylindrical coordinates:**
- Radial distance from axis: $\rho = r\sin\alpha$
- Height: $z = r\cos\alpha$
- Angular position: $\phi = \omega t$

**Velocity in lab frame:**
$$\dot{\mathbf{r}} = \dot{r}(\sin\alpha\hat{\boldsymbol{\rho}} + \cos\alpha\hat{\mathbf{z}}) + r\sin\alpha \cdot \omega\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}$$

$$v^2 = \dot{r}^2 + (r\omega\sin\alpha)^2$$

**Kinetic energy:**
$$T = \frac{1}{2}m(\dot{r}^2 + r^2\omega^2\sin^2\alpha)$$

**Potential energy:**
$$V = mgz = mgr\cos\alpha$$

**Lagrangian:**
$$L = \frac{1}{2}m(\dot{r}^2 + r^2\omega^2\sin^2\alpha) - mgr\cos\alpha$$

**Euler-Lagrange:**
$$\frac{\partial L}{\partial r} = mr\omega^2\sin^2\alpha - mg\cos\alpha$$

$$\frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot{r}} = m\dot{r}$$

$$mr\omega^2\sin^2\alpha - mg\cos\alpha - m\ddot{r} = 0$$

$$\ddot{r} = r\omega^2\sin^2\alpha - g\cos\alpha$$

**Answer:** $\ddot{r} = \omega^2\sin^2\alpha \cdot r - g\cos\alpha$
