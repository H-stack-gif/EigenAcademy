# Article 6.5: Scattering in Central Force Fields

When a particle approaches a central force center with non-zero impact parameter, it follows a hyperbolic trajectory—scattering rather than orbiting. Rutherford scattering, discovered through alpha particle bombardment of gold foil, revealed atomic nuclei and established quantum mechanics' need. The scattering angle depends on impact parameter, energy, and force law through a relationship solvable from orbital mechanics. Differential cross sections connect microscopic scattering to macroscopic experimental observables.

The effective potential framework elegantly analyzes scattering: particles with positive total energy approach from infinity, reach minimum distance determined by energy and angular momentum, then return to infinity at different angle. The scattering angle relates geometrically to the trajectory's angular deflection. For Coulomb/gravitational forces, Rutherford's formula $d\sigma/d\Omega \propto 1/\sin^4(\theta/2)$ gives exact cross section.

This article develops scattering theory from central force orbits: deriving scattering angle formulas, analyzing the effective potential for scattering, computing impact parameter relationships, and establishing Rutherford's cross section. The mathematics employs integral calculus, asymptotic analysis, and geometric arguments. Applications span atomic physics, astrophysics (gravitational slingshots), and particle physics.

## Scattering Geometry

**Setup:** Particle with energy $E > 0$ and impact parameter $b$ approaches force center.

**Impact parameter** $b$: perpendicular distance from force center to initial velocity line.

**Angular momentum:**
$$L = mv_{\infty}b$$

where $v_{\infty} = \sqrt{2E/m}$ is speed at infinity.

**Scattering angle** $\Theta$: angle between initial and final velocity directions.

$$\Theta = \pi - 2\theta_{\min}$$

where $\theta_{\min}$ is angle at closest approach $r_{\min}$.

### Example 1: Impact Parameter and Angular Momentum

A particle with mass $m = 1$ kg, speed $v_{\infty} = 1000$ m/s, and impact parameter $b = 2$ m approaches a force center. Find angular momentum.

**Solution:**

$$L = mv_{\infty}b = 1(1000)(2) = 2000 \text{ kg} \cdot \text {m}^2\text{/s}$$

**Answer:** $L = 2000$ kg} \cdot \text {m²/s

## Distance of Closest Approach

From energy conservation:
$$E = \frac{1}{2}m\dot{r}^2 + \frac{L^2}{2mr^2} + U(r)$$

At closest approach, $\dot{r} = 0$:
$$E = \frac{L^2}{2mr_{min}^2} + U(r_{min})$$

For repulsive Coulomb force ($U = k/r$ with $k > 0$):
$$E = \frac{L^2}{2mr_{min}^2} + \frac{k}{r_{min}}$$

Multiply by $2mr_{min}^2$:
$$2mEr_{min}^2 = L^2 + 2mkr_{min}$$

$$2mEr_{min}^2 - 2mkr_{min} - L^2 = 0$$

**Solution:**
$$r_{min} = \frac{2mk + \sqrt{4m^2k^2 + 8mEL^2}}{4mE}$$

$$= \frac{k + \sqrt{k^2 + 2EL^2/m}}{2E}$$

### Example 2: Closest Approach for Rutherford Scattering

Alpha particle ($m = 6.64 \times 10^{-27}$ kg, $E = 8 \times 10^{-13}$ J) with $b = 10^{-14}$ m scatters from gold nucleus ($Z = 79$). Find $r_{min}$.

**Solution:**

Easy formula:

$$r_{min} = \frac{k}{2E}(1 + \sec\alpha)$$

where $\alpha$ is related to scattering angle. For small angles (large $b$):
$$r_{min} \approx b$$

**Answer:** $r_{min} \approx 1.5 \times 10^{-14}$ m (few fm, nuclear scale)

## Scattering Angle Formula

The scattering angle is:
$$\Theta = \pi - 2\int_{r_{min}}^{\infty} \frac{L/r^2}{\sqrt{2m(E - U(r)) - L^2/r^2}}dr$$

For Coulomb/gravitational force ($U = \pm k/r$):

**Repulsive** ($U = +k/r$):
$$\cot\frac{\Theta}{2} = \frac{2EL}{mk} = \frac{2Eb}{k/v_{\infty}}$$

Or equivalently:
$$\tan\frac{\Theta}{2} = \frac{k}{2Eb}$$

**Impact parameter in terms of scattering angle:**
$$b = \frac{k}{2E}\cot\frac{\Theta}{2}$$

### Example 3: Scattering Angle Calculation

For Rutherford scattering with $k = 3.6 \times 10^{-26}$ J} \cdot \text {m, $E = 8 \times 10^{-13}$ J, and $b = 10^{-14}$ m, find scattering angle.

**Solution:**

$$\tan\frac{\Theta}{2} = \frac{k}{2Eb} = \frac{3.6 \times 10^{-26}}{2(8 \times 10^{-13})(10^{-14})} = \frac{3.6 \times 10^{-26}}{1.6 \times 10^{-26}} = 2.25$$

$$\frac{\Theta}{2} = \arctan(2.25) = 66.0°$$

$$\Theta = 132°$$

Large deflection angle!

**Answer:** $\Theta = 132°$ (backscattering)

## Rutherford Scattering Cross Section

**Differential cross section** $d\sigma/d\Omega$ relates incident flux to scattered flux into solid angle.

**Definition:** If incident flux is $\Phi_0$ (particles per unit area per time) and $dN$ particles scatter into solid angle $d\Omega$ per time:
$$dN = \Phi_0 \frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega}d\Omega$$

**Geometric relation:** Particles with impact parameters between $b$ and $b + db$ scatter into angles between $\Theta$ and $\Theta + d\Theta$:
$$2\pi b |db| = \frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega} \cdot 2\pi\sin\Theta |d\Theta|$$

$$\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega} = \frac{b}{\sin\Theta}\left|\frac{db}{d\Theta}\right|$$

**For Coulomb force:**
$$b = \frac{k}{2E}\cot\frac{\Theta}{2}$$

$$\frac{db}{d\Theta} = -\frac{k}{4E}\csc^2\frac{\Theta}{2}$$

**Rutherford formula:**
$$\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega} = \left(\frac{k}{4E}\right)^2\frac{1}{\sin^4(\Theta/2)}$$

### Example 4: Rutherford Cross Section

For alpha particles with $E = 5$ MeV scattering from gold ($Z = 79$), find differential cross section at $\Theta = 90°$.

**Solution:**

$$k = \frac{Z_1Z_2e^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0} = \frac{2(79)(1.44 \text{ MeV} \cdot \text {fm})}{1} = 227 \text{ MeV} \cdot \text {fm}$$

Energy: $E = 5$ MeV

$$\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega} = \left(\frac{227}{4 \cdot 5}\right)^2\frac{1}{\sin^4(45°)} = (11.35)^2 \cdot \frac{1}{(0.707)^4}$$

$$= 129 \cdot 4 = 516 \text{ fm}^2\text{/sr}$$

Converting: $1 \text{ fm}^2 = 10^{-30}$ m² $= 10$ mb (millibarns)

$$\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega} = 5160 \text{ mb/sr}$$

**Answer:** $5160$ mb/sr at 90° scattering

## Effective Potential Analysis of Scattering

For scattering ($E > 0$), the effective potential:
$$U_{eff}(r) = U(r) + \frac{L^2}{2mr^2}$$

**Repulsive force** ($U = +k/r$): $U_{eff}$ has no minimum, monotonically decreasing from $+\infty$ at $r = 0$.

Particle approaches from $r = \infty$, reaches $r_{min}$ where $E = U_{eff}(r_{min})$, then returns to infinity.

**Attractive force** ($U = -k/r$): $U_{eff}$ has minimum (circular orbit possible for $E < 0$).

For $E > 0$: particle approaches, may reach very small $r$ if $L$ small, then escapes.

### Example 5: Gravity Assist Trajectory

A spacecraft with $v_{\infty} = 10$ km/s and $b = 100,000$ km approaches Jupiter. Describe trajectory using effective potential.

**Solution:**

Jupiter: $GM_J = 1.27 \times 10^{17}$ m³/s²

Energy (in Jupiter frame):
$$E = \frac{1}{2}mv_{\infty}^2 = \frac{1}{2}m(10^4)^2 = 5 \times 10^7 m \text{ J/kg} > 0$$

Hyperbolic trajectory. The effective potential:
$$U_{eff}/m = -\frac{GM_J}{r} + \frac{L^2}{2m^2r^2}$$

With $L = mv_{\infty}b = m(10^4)(10^8) = 10^{12}m$ kg} \cdot \text {m²/s:

At large $r$: $U_{eff} \to 0$ from below.

Closest approach: solve $E = U_{eff}(r_{min})$

The spacecraft follows hyperbolic path, gains angular deflection, which in Sun's frame changes velocity direction and magnitude (gravity assist).

**Answer:** Hyperbolic flyby with deflection determined by $b$ and $v_{\infty}$
