# Article 4.3: Collisions

Collisions represent the quintessential application of momentum conservation. When two objects interact through large forces over short times, the impulses from these collision forces dominate external forces like gravity or friction. During the collision, momentum is conserved even if kinetic energy is not. The classification of collisions—elastic, inelastic, perfectly inelastic—depends on energy dissipation. Elastic collisions conserve both momentum and kinetic energy; inelastic collisions conserve momentum but dissipate some kinetic energy; perfectly inelastic collisions result in the objects sticking together.

The mathematical analysis of collisions employs vector momentum conservation combined with scalar energy considerations (for elastic collisions). In one dimension, this yields algebraic equations easily solved for final velocities. In two and three dimensions, momentum conservation gives vector equations, while elastic collision adds one scalar constraint. The center-of-mass frame often simplifies analysis: in this frame, momenta are equal and opposite before and after elastic collisions, with directions changing but magnitudes preserved.

This article develops collision analysis systematically, from one-dimensional cases through two-dimensional scattering, including the coefficient of restitution for partially elastic collisions. We emphasize both lab frame and CM frame perspectives, derive scattering angle relationships, and apply energy-momentum conservation to various collision types. The mathematics involves vector algebra, simultaneous equations, and coordinate transformations between reference frames.

## One-Dimensional Elastic Collisions

For two particles colliding elastically in one dimension:

**Conservation of momentum:**
$$m_1 v_{1i} + m_2 v_{2i} = m_1 v_{1f} + m_2 v_{2f}$$

**Conservation of kinetic energy:**
$$\frac{1}{2}m_1 v_{1i}^2 + \frac{1}{2}m_2 v_{2i}^2 = \frac{1}{2}m_1 v_{1f}^2 + \frac{1}{2}m_2 v_{2f}^2$$

**Solution:** These yield final velocities:
$$v_{1f} = \frac{(m_1 - m_2)v_{1i} + 2m_2 v_{2i}}{m_1 + m_2}$$

$$v_{2f} = \frac{(m_2 - m_1)v_{2i} + 2m_1 v_{1i}}{m_1 + m_2}$$

**Special cases:**
1. $m_1 = m_2$: particles exchange velocities ($v_{1f} = v_{2i}$, $v_{2f} = v_{1i}$)
2. $m_1 \gg m_2$, $v_{2i} = 0$: heavy object continues, light object bounces at $2v_{1i}$
3. $m_1 \ll m_2$, $v_{2i} = 0$: light object bounces back at $-v_{1i}$, heavy object barely moves

### Example 1: Equal Mass Elastic Collision

Two particles of equal mass $m$ undergo elastic collision. Particle 1 has $v_{1i} = 10$ m/s, particle 2 is at rest. Find final velocities.

**Solution:**

For $m_1 = m_2 = m$ and $v_{2i} = 0$:
$$v_{1f} = \frac{(m-m)(10) + 2m(0)}{2m} = 0$$

$$v_{2f} = \frac{(m-m)(0) + 2m(10)}{2m} = 10 \text{ m/s}$$

**Result:** Complete exchange of velocities.

**Verification:**
- Momentum: $m(10) + 0 = m(0) + m(10)$ ✓
- Energy: $\frac{1}{2}m(10)^2 = \frac{1}{2}m(10)^2$ ✓

**Answer:** $v_{1f} = 0$, $v_{2f} = 10$ m/s

## Inelastic Collisions

**Coefficient of restitution** $e$ measures "bounciness":
$$e = -\frac{v_{1f} - v_{2f}}{v_{1i} - v_{2i}} = \frac{\text{relative speed of separation}}{\text{relative speed of approach}}$$

**Classification:**
- $e = 1$: perfectly elastic
- $0 < e < 1$: partially inelastic
- $e = 0$: perfectly inelastic (objects stick)

For partially inelastic collision:
$$v_{1f} - v_{2f} = -e(v_{1i} - v_{2i})$$

Combined with momentum conservation, this determines final velocities.

### Example 2: Partially Inelastic Collision

A 2 kg ball moving at 5 m/s collides with a 3 kg ball at rest. The coefficient of restitution is $e = 0.6$. Find final velocities and energy loss.

**Solution:**

**Momentum:**
$$2(5) + 0 = 2v_{1f} + 3v_{2f}$$
$$10 = 2v_{1f} + 3v_{2f} \quad \text{...(1)}$$

**Restitution:**
$$v_{1f} - v_{2f} = -0.6(5 - 0) = -3 \quad \text{...(2)}$$

From (2): $v_{1f} = v_{2f} - 3$

Substitute into (1):
$$10 = 2(v_{2f} - 3) + 3v_{2f} = 5v_{2f} - 6$$
$$v_{2f} = 3.2 \text{ m/s}$$
$$v_{1f} = 3.2 - 3 = 0.2 \text{ m/s}$$

**Energy:**
$$K_i = \frac{1}{2}(2)(5)^2 = 25 \text{ J}$$
$$K_f = \frac{1}{2}(2)(0.2)^2 + \frac{1}{2}(3)(3.2)^2 = 0.04 + 15.36 = 15.4 \text{ J}$$
$$\Delta K = 25 - 15.4 = 9.6 \text{ J lost}$$

**Answer:** $v_{1f} = 0.2$ m/s, $v_{2f} = 3.2$ m/s, $9.6$ J dissipated

### Example 3: Perfectly Inelastic Collision

Two objects ($m_1 = 4$ kg at $8$ m/s, $m_2 = 6$ kg at rest) collide and stick. Find final velocity and fraction of energy lost.

**Solution:**

For perfectly inelastic ($e = 0$): $v_{1f} = v_{2f} = v_f$ (same final velocity)

Momentum:
$$m_1 v_{1i} = (m_1 + m_2)v_f$$
$$4(8) = 10v_f$$
$$v_f = 3.2 \text{ m/s}$$

**Energy:**
$$K_i = \frac{1}{2}(4)(8)^2 = 128 \text{ J}$$
$$K_f = \frac{1}{2}(10)(3.2)^2 = 51.2 \text{ J}$$

Fraction lost:
$$\frac{\Delta K}{K_i} = \frac{128 - 51.2}{128} = \frac{76.8}{128} = 0.6 = 60\%$$

**Answer:** $v_f = 3.2$ m/s, $60\%$ energy lost

## Two-Dimensional Collisions

In 2D, momentum conservation gives two component equations:
$$m_1 v_{1ix} + m_2 v_{2ix} = m_1 v_{1fx} + m_2 v_{2fx}$$
$$m_1 v_{1iy} + m_2 v_{2iy} = m_1 v_{1fy} + m_2 v_{2fy}$$

For elastic collisions, add energy conservation:
$$\frac{1}{2}m_1 v_{1i}^2 + \frac{1}{2}m_2 v_{2i}^2 = \frac{1}{2}m_1 v_{1f}^2 + \frac{1}{2}m_2 v_{2f}^2$$

This gives 3 equations for 4 unknowns (2 final velocity vectors). Need one more constraint, typically scattering angle or impact parameter.

### Example 4: 2D Elastic Collision

A 3 kg ball moving at $\mathbf{v}_1 = 5\hat{\mathbf{x}}$ m/s collides elastically with a 3 kg ball at rest. After collision, ball 1 moves at $30°$ above the $+x$ axis. Find final speeds and ball 2's direction.

**Solution:**

Equal masses, so speeds exchange in CM frame. In lab frame with $m_1 = m_2$:

**Conservation (x):**
$$3(5) + 0 = 3v_{1f}\cos(30°) + 3v_{2f}\cos\theta_2$$
$$15 = 3v_{1f}(0.866) + 3v_{2f}\cos\theta_2$$
$$5 = v_{1f}(0.866) + v_{2f}\cos\theta_2 \quad \text{...(1)}$$

**Conservation (y):**
$$0 = 3v_{1f}\sin(30°) + 3v_{2f}\sin\theta_2$$
$$0 = v_{1f}(0.5) + v_{2f}\sin\theta_2 \quad \text{...(2)}$$

**Energy (for equal mass elastic):**
$$5^2 = v_{1f}^2 + v_{2f}^2$$
$$25 = v_{1f}^2 + v_{2f}^2 \quad \text{...(3)}$$

From (2): $v_{2f}\sin\theta_2 = -0.5v_{1f}$

This means $\theta_2 < 0$ (below $x$-axis).

For equal mass elastic collision with one initially at rest, the particles emerge at $90°$ to each other.

So: $\theta_1 + |\theta_2| = 90°$
$$|\theta_2| = 90° - 30° = 60°$$

Thus $\theta_2 = -60°$ (below $x$-axis).

From (2): $v_{2f}\sin(-60°) = -0.5v_{1f}$
$$-v_{2f}(0.866) = -0.5v_{1f}$$
$$v_{2f} = \frac{0.5v_{1f}}{0.866} = 0.577v_{1f}$$

From (3):
$$25 = v_{1f}^2 + (0.577v_{1f})^2 = v_{1f}^2(1 + 0.333) = 1.333v_{1f}^2$$
$$v_{1f} = \sqrt{18.75} = 4.33 \text{ m/s}$$
$$v_{2f} = 0.577(4.33) = 2.5 \text{ m/s}$$

**Answer:** $v_{1f} = 4.33$ m/s at $30°$, $v_{2f} = 2.5$ m/s at $-60°$

## Center-of-Mass Frame Analysis

In CM frame, total momentum is zero. For two-particle elastic collision:

Before: $\mathbf{p}_1' = -\mathbf{p}_2'$ (equal and opposite)

After: $|\mathbf{p}_1'| = |\mathbf{p}_2'|$ (magnitudes preserved in elastic collision)

The collision simply rotates the momentum vectors by scattering angle $\theta_{\text{CM}}$.

**Relation to lab frame:** Use velocity transformations:
$$\mathbf{v}_i' = \mathbf{v}_i - \mathbf{v}_{\text{CM}}$$

### Example 5: CM Frame for Head-On Collision

Masses $m_1 = 2$ kg at $v_1 = 10$ m/s and $m_2 = 3$ kg at $v_2 = -5$ m/s collide elastically. Analyze in CM frame.

**Solution:**

CM velocity:
$$v_{\text{CM}} = \frac{2(10) + 3(-5)}{5} = \frac{20-15}{5} = 1 \text{ m/s}$$

**Velocities in CM frame:**
$$v_1' = 10 - 1 = 9 \text{ m/s}$$
$$v_2' = -5 - 1 = -6 \text{ m/s}$$

**Check:** $m_1v_1' + m_2v_2' = 2(9) + 3(-6) = 18 - 18 = 0$ ✓

In CM frame, elastic collision reverses velocities (head-on):
$$v_{1f}' = -9 \text{ m/s}$$
$$v_{2f}' = +6 \text{ m/s}$$

**Transform back to lab frame:**
$$v_{1f} = -9 + 1 = -8 \text{ m/s}$$
$$v_{2f} = 6 + 1 = 7 \text{ m/s}$$

**Verify momentum:** $2(-8) + 3(7) = -16 + 21 = 5$ kg} \cdot \text {m/s = initial ✓

**Answer:** In CM frame, velocities reverse; lab frame: $v_{1f} = -8$ m/s, $v_{2f} = 7$ m/s

For equal mass elastic collision (one at rest), final velocities are perpendicular ($90°$ apart).

**Problem 5: C**

In CM frame elastic collision, speeds remain constant but directions change (scattering).
