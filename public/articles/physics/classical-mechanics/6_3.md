# Article 6.3: Gravitational Potential and Field

The gravitational field $\mathbf{g}(\mathbf{r})$ and potential $\Phi(\mathbf{r})$ provide elegant formulations of gravity as a field theory. Rather than action-at-a-distance between masses, we view mass as creating a gravitational field permeating space, with other masses responding to the local field. The potential $\Phi$ satisfies Poisson's equation $\nabla^2\Phi = 4\pi G\rho$, connecting field to mass distribution. Gauss's law for gravity enables calculating fields from symmetry without integration.

For extended bodies—spheres, rods, disks—the gravitational potential requires integration over the mass distribution. Spherically symmetric bodies produce external fields identical to point masses (shell theorem), dramatically simplifying planetary and stellar dynamics. Escape velocity emerges from energy considerations: the minimum speed to reach infinite distance. Gravitational binding energy determines stellar structure stability and energy release in gravitational collapse.

This article develops gravitational potential theory systematically: deriving Gauss's law, proving the shell theorem, calculating potentials for extended bodies through integration, determining escape velocities, and analyzing gravitational binding energy. The mathematics employs vector calculus (divergence, Gauss's theorem), multiple integrals, and symmetry arguments. Applications span planetary physics, stellar structure, and orbital mechanics.

## Gravitational Field and Potential

**Gravitational field** at position $\mathbf{r}$ due to mass $M$ at origin:
$$\mathbf{g}(\mathbf{r}) = -\frac{GM}{r^2}\hat{\mathbf{r}}$$

Force on test mass $m$: $\mathbf{F} = m\mathbf{g}$

**Gravitational potential:**
$$\Phi(\mathbf{r}) = -\frac{GM}{r}$$

**Relation:** $\mathbf{g} = -\nabla\Phi$

**Proof:**
$$\nabla\Phi = \frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial r}\hat{\mathbf{r}} = \frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left(-\frac{GM}{r}\right)\hat{\mathbf{r}} = \frac{GM}{r^2}\hat{\mathbf{r}}$$

$$\mathbf{g} = -\nabla\Phi = -\frac{GM}{r^2}\hat{\mathbf{r}}$$ ✓

**Potential energy:** $U = m\Phi$

### Example 1: Gravitational Potential Energy

Calculate potential energy of Earth-Moon system. $M_E = 5.97 \times 10^{24}$ kg, $M_M = 7.35 \times 10^{22}$ kg, $r = 3.84 \times 10^8$ m.

**Solution:**

$$U = -\frac{GM_E M_M}{r} = -\frac{(6.67 \times 10^{-11})(5.97 \times 10^{24})(7.35 \times 10^{22})}{3.84 \times 10^8}$$

$$= -\frac{2.93 \times 10^{37}}{3.84 \times 10^8} = -7.63 \times 10^{28} \text{ J}$$

This is the energy that would be released if they fell together!

**Answer:** $U = -7.63 \times 10^{28}$ J

## Gauss's Law for Gravity

**Integral form:**
$$\oint_S \mathbf{g} \cdot d\mathbf{A} = -4\pi G M_{enc}$$

where $M_{enc}$ is mass enclosed by surface $S$.

**Differential form (Poisson's equation):**
$$\nabla \cdot \mathbf{g} = -4\pi G\rho$$

where $\rho$ is mass density.

Or in terms of potential:
$$\nabla^2\Phi = 4\pi G\rho$$

**Proof of integral form:** For point mass $M$ at origin, consider spherical surface radius $r$:

$$\oint_S \mathbf{g} \cdot d\mathbf{A} = \oint \left(-\frac{GM}{r^2}\hat{\mathbf{r}}\right) \cdot (dA \hat{\mathbf{r}})$$

$$= -\frac{GM}{r^2}\oint dA = -\frac{GM}{r^2}(4\pi r^2) = -4\pi GM$$ ✓

### Example 2: Field Inside Uniform Sphere

A uniform sphere of radius $R$, total mass $M$, density $\rho = 3M/(4\pi R^3)$. Find gravitational field at distance $r < R$ from center.

**Solution:**

By spherical symmetry, field is radial. Use Gauss's law on spherical surface radius $r$:

Mass enclosed:
$$M_{enc} = \rho \cdot \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3 = \frac{3M}{4\pi R^3} \cdot \frac{4\pi r^3}{3} = M\frac{r^3}{R^3}$$

Gauss's law:
$$g(4\pi r^2) = 4\pi GM_{enc} = 4\pi GM\frac{r^3}{R^3}$$

$$g(r) = \frac{GM r}{R^3}$$

**Field inside:** $\mathbf{g} = -\frac{GMr}{R^3}\hat{\mathbf{r}}$ (linear in $r$)

At surface ($r = R$): $g = GM/R^2$ ✓

**Answer:** $g(r) = GMr/R^3$ for $r < R$ (increases linearly)

## Shell Theorem

**Statement:** A uniform spherical shell of mass $M$ and radius $R$ produces:

1. **Outside** ($r > R$): field as if all mass at center: $\mathbf{g} = -GM/r^2 \hat{\mathbf{r}}$

2. **Inside** ($r < R$): zero field: $\mathbf{g} = \mathbf{0}$

**Proof (using Gauss's law):**

**Outside:** Gaussian surface $r > R$ encloses entire mass $M$:
$$g(4\pi r^2) = 4\pi GM \implies g = \frac{GM}{r^2}$$

**Inside:** Gaussian surface $r < R$ encloses zero mass:
$$g(4\pi r^2) = 0 \implies g = 0$$

**Consequence:** Solid uniform sphere produces same external field as point mass. Inside a spherical cavity, gravity is zero!

### Example 3: Tunnel Through Earth

Calculate gravitational field inside a tunnel through Earth's center. Assume uniform density $\rho$.

**Solution:**

At radius $r$ from center, only mass inside contributes (shell theorem):

$$M(r) = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3 \rho$$

$$g(r) = \frac{GM(r)}{r^2} = \frac{G \cdot \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3\rho}{r^2} = \frac{4\pi G\rho}{3}r$$

This gives **simple harmonic motion**! Equation:
$$m\ddot{r} = -mg(r) = -m\frac{4\pi G\rho}{3}r$$

$$\ddot{r} + \omega^2 r = 0$$

where $\omega^2 = 4\pi G\rho/3$.

For Earth: $\rho = 5520$ kg/m³

$$\omega = \sqrt{\frac{4\pi(6.67 \times 10^{-11})(5520)}{3}} = 1.24 \times 10^{-3} \text{ rad/s}$$

$$T = \frac{2\pi}{\omega} = 5070 \text{ s} = 84.5 \text{ min}$$

**Answer:** SHM with period 84.5 minutes (orbital period at surface!)

## Gravitational Potential of Extended Bodies

For continuous mass distribution $\rho(\mathbf{r}')$:
$$\Phi(\mathbf{r}) = -G\int \frac{\rho(\mathbf{r}')}{|\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}'|}d^3\mathbf{r}'$$

### Example 4: Potential on Axis of Uniform Rod

A uniform rod of length $L$, mass $M$, lies along $x$-axis from $0$ to $L$. Find potential at point $(x, 0, 0)$ with $x > L$.

**Solution:**

Linear mass density: $\lambda = M/L$

Element at position $x'$: $dm = \lambda dx'$

Distance to field point: $|x - x'|$

$$\Phi(x, 0, 0) = -G\int_0^L \frac{\lambda dx'}{x - x'} = -G\lambda\int_0^L \frac{dx'}{x - x'}$$

$$= -G\lambda[-\ln(x-x')]_0^L = G\lambda[\ln(x-L) - \ln(x)]$$

$$= G\lambda\ln\frac{x-L}{x} = \frac{GM}{L}\ln\left(1 - \frac{L}{x}\right)$$

For $x \gg L$: $\ln(1 - L/x) \approx -L/x$:
$$\Phi \approx -\frac{GM}{x}$$

(Recovers point mass result far away)

**Answer:** $\Phi = \frac{GM}{L}\ln(1 - L/x)$

## Escape Velocity

**Question:** What minimum speed needed to escape to infinity?

**Energy analysis:** At surface ($r = R$), speed $v_{esc}$:
$$E = \frac{1}{2}mv_{esc}^2 - \frac{GMm}{R}$$

At infinity: $E = 0$ (just barely escapes)

$$\frac{1}{2}mv_{esc}^2 = \frac{GMm}{R}$$

$$v_{esc} = \sqrt{\frac{2GM}{R}}$$

**For Earth:** $R = 6.37 \times 10^6$ m, $M = 5.97 \times 10^{24}$ kg
$$v_{esc} = \sqrt{\frac{2(6.67 \times 10^{-11})(5.97 \times 10^{24})}{6.37 \times 10^6}} = 1.12 \times 10^4 \text{ m/s} = 11.2 \text{ km/s}$$

**Relation to orbital speed:** $v_{esc} = \sqrt{2} \cdot v_{orbit}$

### Example 5: Black Hole Event Horizon

A black hole has escape velocity equal to speed of light $c$. Find the Schwarzschild radius $R_s$.

**Solution:**

$$c = \sqrt{\frac{2GM}{R_s}}$$

$$R_s = \frac{2GM}{c^2}$$

For solar mass black hole ($M = 2 \times 10^{30}$ kg):
$$R_s = \frac{2(6.67 \times 10^{-11})(2 \times 10^{30})}{(3 \times 10^8)^2} = \frac{2.67 \times 10^{20}}{9 \times 10^{16}} = 2.97 \times 10^3 \text{ m} = 3 \text{ km}$$

**Answer:** $R_s = 2GM/c^2 \approx 3$ km for solar mass
