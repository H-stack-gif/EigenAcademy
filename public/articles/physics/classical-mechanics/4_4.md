# Article 4.4: Angular Momentum

Angular momentum represents the rotational analog of linear momentum. Where linear momentum $\mathbf{p} = m\mathbf{v}$ measures translational motion, angular momentum $\mathbf{L} = \mathbf{r} \times \mathbf{p}$ measures rotational motion about a point. The cross product structure makes angular momentum a pseudovector pointing along the rotation axis (by right-hand rule). Torque $\boldsymbol{\tau} = \mathbf{r} \times \mathbf{F}$ serves as the rotational analog of force, with the equation $\boldsymbol{\tau} = d\mathbf{L}/dt$ paralleling Newton's second law.

The power of angular momentum lies in its conservation properties. For central forces—where force points toward a fixed center—torque vanishes and angular momentum is conserved. This immediately implies planar motion (motion in plane perpendicular to $\mathbf{L}$) and leads to Kepler's second law (equal areas in equal times). For systems of particles, total angular momentum obeys $d\mathbf{L}_{\text{total}}/dt = \boldsymbol{\tau}_{\text{ext}}$, with internal torques canceling by Newton's third law.

This article develops angular momentum from first principles, derives its equation of motion, explores angular momentum for particle systems, and establishes the connection to rotational dynamics. The mathematics involves vector cross products, differentiation of vector products, and the parallel axis theorem. Applications range from orbital motion to spinning ice skaters to planetary precession.

## Definition of Angular Momentum

For a particle at position $\mathbf{r}$ with momentum $\mathbf{p}$, **angular momentum** about the origin is:
$$\mathbf{L} = \mathbf{r} \times \mathbf{p} = \mathbf{r} \times (m\mathbf{v})$$

In Cartesian components:
$$\mathbf{L} = \begin{vmatrix} \hat{\mathbf{x}} & \hat{\mathbf{y}} & \hat{\mathbf{z}} \\ x & y & z \\ p_x & p_y & p_z \end{vmatrix}$$

$$= (yp_z - zp_y)\hat{\mathbf{x}} + (zp_x - xp_z)\hat{\mathbf{y}} + (xp_y - yp_x)\hat{\mathbf{z}}$$

**Magnitude:** $L = rp\sin\theta$ where $\theta$ is angle between $\mathbf{r}$ and $\mathbf{p}$.

**Direction:** Perpendicular to plane containing $\mathbf{r}$ and $\mathbf{p}$, given by right-hand rule.

**Units:** kg} \cdot \text {m²/s (or J} \cdot \text {s)

### Example 1: Angular Momentum of Particle in Circular Motion

A particle of mass $m = 2$ kg moves in a circle of radius $r = 3$ m with speed $v = 4$ m/s in the $xy$-plane. Find angular momentum about the center.

**Solution:**

For circular motion, $\mathbf{v} \perp \mathbf{r}$, so $\sin\theta = 1$:

$$L = mrv = 2(3)(4) = 24 \text{ kg} \cdot \text {m}^2\text{/s}$$

Direction: By right-hand rule, $\mathbf{L}$ points along $+z$ axis (for counterclockwise motion).

$$\mathbf{L} = 24\hat{\mathbf{z}} \text{ kg} \cdot \text {m}^2\text{/s}$$

**Alternative calculation:**
$$\mathbf{L} = \mathbf{r} \times m\mathbf{v}$$

At some instant, let $\mathbf{r} = 3\hat{\mathbf{x}}$ m and $\mathbf{v} = 4\hat{\mathbf{y}}$ m/s:
$$\mathbf{L} = (3\hat{\mathbf{x}}) \times 2(4\hat{\mathbf{y}}) = 24(\hat{\mathbf{x}} \times \hat{\mathbf{y}}) = 24\hat{\mathbf{z}}$$ ✓

**Answer:** $\mathbf{L} = 24\hat{\mathbf{z}}$ kg} \cdot \text {m²/s

## Torque and Angular Momentum Equation

**Torque** is defined as:
$$\boldsymbol{\tau} = \mathbf{r} \times \mathbf{F}$$

**Theorem:** Torque equals rate of change of angular momentum:
$$\boldsymbol{\tau} = \frac{d\mathbf{L}}{dt}$$

**Proof:** Differentiate $\mathbf{L} = \mathbf{r} \times \mathbf{p}$:
$$\frac{d\mathbf{L}}{dt} = \frac{d\mathbf{r}}{dt} \times \mathbf{p} + \mathbf{r} \times \frac{d\mathbf{p}}{dt}$$

$$= \mathbf{v} \times (m\mathbf{v}) + \mathbf{r} \times \mathbf{F}$$

The first term vanishes ($\mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{v} = \mathbf{0}$):
$$\frac{d\mathbf{L}}{dt} = \mathbf{r} \times \mathbf{F} = \boldsymbol{\tau}$$ ∎

This is the **rotational analog of Newton's second law**.

### Example 2: Torque on a Particle

A force $\mathbf{F} = 5\hat{\mathbf{x}} + 3\hat{\mathbf{y}}$ N acts on a particle at position $\mathbf{r} = 2\hat{\mathbf{x}} + 4\hat{\mathbf{y}}$ m. Find the torque about the origin.

**Solution:**
$$\boldsymbol{\tau} = \mathbf{r} \times \mathbf{F} = \begin{vmatrix} \hat{\mathbf{x}} & \hat{\mathbf{y}} & \hat{\mathbf{z}} \\ 2 & 4 & 0 \\ 5 & 3 & 0 \end{vmatrix}$$

$$= \hat{\mathbf{z}}[(2)(3) - (4)(5)] = \hat{\mathbf{z}}(6 - 20) = -14\hat{\mathbf{z}} \text{ N} \cdot \text {m}$$

The negative sign indicates clockwise torque (into page).

**Answer:** $\boldsymbol{\tau} = -14\hat{\mathbf{z}}$ N} \cdot \text {m

## Angular Momentum of Systems of Particles

For a system of $N$ particles:
$$\mathbf{L}_{\text{total}} = \sum_{i=1}^{N} \mathbf{L}_i = \sum_{i=1}^{N} \mathbf{r}_i \times \mathbf{p}_i$$

**Equation of motion:**
$$\frac{d\mathbf{L}_{\text{total}}}{dt} = \boldsymbol{\tau}_{\text{ext}}$$

where $\boldsymbol{\tau}_{\text{ext}} = \sum_i \mathbf{r}_i \times \mathbf{F}_i^{\text{ext}}$ is the total external torque.

**Proof:** Internal torques cancel by Newton's third law (similar to momentum proof).

**Decomposition:** Angular momentum can be split into orbital (CM motion) and spin (motion about CM):
$$\mathbf{L}_{\text{total}} = \mathbf{L}_{\text{orbital}} + \mathbf{L}_{\text{spin}}$$

where:
$$\mathbf{L}_{\text{orbital}} = \mathbf{r}_{\text{CM}} \times M\mathbf{v}_{\text{CM}}$$
$$\mathbf{L}_{\text{spin}} = \sum_i \mathbf{r}_i' \times m_i\mathbf{v}_i'$$

(with $\mathbf{r}_i'$ and $\mathbf{v}_i'$ relative to CM)

### Example 3: Angular Momentum of Two-Particle System

Two particles: $m_1 = 2$ kg at $\mathbf{r}_1 = 3\hat{\mathbf{x}}$ m with $\mathbf{v}_1 = 4\hat{\mathbf{y}}$ m/s; $m_2 = 3$ kg at $\mathbf{r}_2 = -2\hat{\mathbf{x}}$ m with $\mathbf{v}_2 = 2\hat{\mathbf{y}}$ m/s. Find total angular momentum.

**Solution:**

Individual angular momenta:
$$\mathbf{L}_1 = \mathbf{r}_1 \times m_1\mathbf{v}_1 = (3\hat{\mathbf{x}}) \times 2(4\hat{\mathbf{y}}) = 24\hat{\mathbf{z}} \text{ kg} \cdot \text {m}^2\text{/s}$$

$$\mathbf{L}_2 = \mathbf{r}_2 \times m_2\mathbf{v}_2 = (-2\hat{\mathbf{x}}) \times 3(2\hat{\mathbf{y}}) = -12\hat{\mathbf{z}} \text{ kg} \cdot \text {m}^2\text{/s}$$

Total:
$$\mathbf{L}_{\text{total}} = \mathbf{L}_1 + \mathbf{L}_2 = 24\hat{\mathbf{z}} - 12\hat{\mathbf{z}} = 12\hat{\mathbf{z}} \text{ kg} \cdot \text {m}^2\text{/s}$$

**Answer:** $\mathbf{L}_{\text{total}} = 12\hat{\mathbf{z}}$ kg} \cdot \text {m²/s

## Angular Momentum in Different Coordinate Systems

### Cartesian Coordinates
$$L_x = yp_z - zp_y = m(y\dot{z} - z\dot{y})$$
$$L_y = zp_x - xp_z = m(z\dot{x} - x\dot{z})$$
$$L_z = xp_y - yp_x = m(x\dot{y} - y\dot{x})$$

### Cylindrical Coordinates
For motion in a plane (2D polar):
$$L_z = mr^2\dot{\theta}$$

This is constant for central forces.

### Spherical Coordinates
Components become more complex, but for motion in the equatorial plane:
$$L = mr^2\dot{\phi}$$

### Example 4: Angular Momentum in Polar Coordinates

A particle of mass $m = 1$ kg moves with $r = 2t$ m and $\theta = 3t$ rad (where $t$ is time in seconds). Find $L_z$ at $t = 1$ s.

**Solution:**

At $t = 1$ s: $r = 2$ m, $\theta = 3$ rad

$$\dot{r} = 2 \text{ m/s}, \quad \dot{\theta} = 3 \text{ rad/s}$$

$$L_z = mr^2\dot{\theta} = 1(2)^2(3) = 12 \text{ kg} \cdot \text {m}^2\text{/s}$$

**Answer:** $L_z = 12$ kg} \cdot \text {m²/s

## Parallel Axis Theorem for Angular Momentum

Angular momentum about point $O'$ is related to angular momentum about origin $O$:
$$\mathbf{L}_{O'} = \mathbf{L}_O - \mathbf{R} \times \mathbf{P}$$

where $\mathbf{R}$ is position of $O'$ relative to $O$, and $\mathbf{P}$ is total momentum.

For angular momentum about CM:
$$\mathbf{L}_{\text{any point}} = \mathbf{L}_{\text{CM}} + \mathbf{r}_{\text{CM}} \times M\mathbf{v}_{\text{CM}}$$

### Example 5: Angular Momentum About Different Points

A 3 kg particle at $(2, 3, 0)$ m moves with velocity $(4, -2, 0)$ m/s. Find angular momentum about: (a) origin, (b) point $(1, 1, 0)$ m.

**Solution:**

**(a) About origin:**
$$\mathbf{L}_O = \mathbf{r} \times m\mathbf{v} = \begin{vmatrix} \hat{\mathbf{x}} & \hat{\mathbf{y}} & \hat{\mathbf{z}} \\ 2 & 3 & 0 \\ 12 & -6 & 0 \end{vmatrix}$$

$$= \hat{\mathbf{z}}[(2)(-6) - (3)(12)] = \hat{\mathbf{z}}(-12 - 36) = -48\hat{\mathbf{z}} \text{ kg} \cdot \text {m}^2\text{/s}$$

**(b) About $(1, 1, 0)$:**

Position relative to new point: $\mathbf{r}' = (2-1)\hat{\mathbf{x}} + (3-1)\hat{\mathbf{y}} = \hat{\mathbf{x}} + 2\hat{\mathbf{y}}$ m

$$\mathbf{L}' = \mathbf{r}' \times m\mathbf{v} = \begin{vmatrix} \hat{\mathbf{x}} & \hat{\mathbf{y}} & \hat{\mathbf{z}} \\ 1 & 2 & 0 \\ 12 & -6 & 0 \end{vmatrix}$$

$$= \hat{\mathbf{z}}[(1)(-6) - (2)(12)] = \hat{\mathbf{z}}(-6 - 24) = -30\hat{\mathbf{z}} \text{ kg} \cdot \text {m}^2\text{/s}$$

**Verification using parallel axis theorem:**
$$\mathbf{L}' = \mathbf{L}_O - \mathbf{R} \times \mathbf{P}$$

where $\mathbf{R} = \hat{\mathbf{x}} + \hat{\mathbf{y}}$ and $\mathbf{P} = 3(4\hat{\mathbf{x}} - 2\hat{\mathbf{y}}) = 12\hat{\mathbf{x}} - 6\hat{\mathbf{y}}$:

$$\mathbf{R} \times \mathbf{P} = \begin{vmatrix} \hat{\mathbf{x}} & \hat{\mathbf{y}} & \hat{\mathbf{z}} \\ 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 12 & -6 & 0 \end{vmatrix} = \hat{\mathbf{z}}(-6 - 12) = -18\hat{\mathbf{z}}$$

$$\mathbf{L}' = -48\hat{\mathbf{z}} - (-18\hat{\mathbf{z}}) = -30\hat{\mathbf{z}}$$ ✓

**Answer:** (a) $-48\hat{\mathbf{z}}$ kg} \cdot \text {m²/s, (b) $-30\hat{\mathbf{z}}$ kg} \cdot \text {m²/s
