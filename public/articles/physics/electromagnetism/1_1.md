# Maxwell's Equations — Topic 1.1

Short, friendly introduction to Maxwell's equations and why they're central to classical electrodynamics. We'll outline the four equations, give physical intuition for each, and show how they connect fields, charge, and current.

## Overview

- Gauss's law (electric)
- Gauss's law (magnetism)
- Faraday's law of induction
- Ampère's law (with Maxwell's correction)

## Key Ideas

Explain the integral and differential forms briefly, conservation of charge, and how Maxwell's correction makes the equations consistent with continuity.

## Practice Problems

1. Write down the four Maxwell equations in differential form and describe the physical meaning of each term.
2. Explain why there are no magnetic monopoles in Maxwell's equations and what this implies about divergence of B.
3. Given a time-varying electric field in a region with no charges or currents, which Maxwell equation predicts an accompanying magnetic field?

## Answer Key

1. Solutions: (a) ∇·E = ρ/ε0 (Gauss – electricity), (b) ∇·B = 0 (no magnetic monopoles), (c) ∇×E = -∂B/∂t (Faraday), (d) ∇×B = μ0J + μ0ε0∂E/∂t (Ampère–Maxwell). Short explanation for each.
2. Answer: ∇·B = 0 implies magnetic field lines are closed loops; if magnetic monopoles existed the divergence would be nonzero.
3. Answer: Faraday's law (∇×E = -∂B/∂t) and Ampère–Maxwell (∇×B = μ0ε0∂E/∂t) together predict time-varying fields.
