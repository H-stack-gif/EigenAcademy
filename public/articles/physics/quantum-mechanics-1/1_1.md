# Quantum Mechanics Topic 1.1: Hilbert Spaces, Operators, and Observables

Classical mechanics describes a particle by its position $x(t)$ and momentum $p(t)$—definite values evolving deterministically. Quantum mechanics abandons this certainty. A quantum particle exists in a **state** $|\psi\rangle$ that encodes not definite values but *probabilities* for all possible measurements. This state lives in an abstract mathematical structure called a **Hilbert space**—an infinite-dimensional vector space with an inner product that lets us compute probabilities and expectation values.

The radical shift from classical to quantum isn't just philosophical. It requires entirely new mathematics. Observables like position and momentum become **operators** acting on state vectors. The commutation relation $[\hat{x}, \hat{p}] = i\hbar$ encodes the uncertainty principle directly into the formalism. The wavefunction $\psi(x)$—that mysterious probability amplitude from introductory quantum—is simply the position representation of the abstract state vector: $\psi(x) = \langle x|\psi\rangle$.

This article develops the mathematical foundation of quantum mechanics from the ground up. We'll construct Hilbert spaces, define linear operators and their properties, introduce the canonical commutation relation, and connect abstract vectors to concrete wavefunctions via representations. You'll learn why observables must be Hermitian, how to switch between position and momentum representations using Fourier transforms, and why the mathematical structure forces probabilistic interpretation.

## Hilbert Spaces and State Vectors

A **Hilbert space** $\mathcal{H}$ is a complete inner product space. For quantum mechanics, it's an infinite-dimensional complex vector space with:

**Vector addition**: $|\psi\rangle + |\phi\rangle \in \mathcal{H}$

**Scalar multiplication**: $c|\psi\rangle \in \mathcal{H}$ for $c \in \mathbb{C}$

**Inner product**: $\langle\phi|\psi\rangle \in \mathbb{C}$ (sesquilinear, positive-definite)

**Completeness**: Cauchy sequences converge (technical condition for infinite dimensions)

### Example 1: Vector Space Axioms

Verify that quantum states satisfy linearity: if $|\psi_1\rangle$ and $|\psi_2\rangle$ are valid states, is $|\psi\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|\psi_1\rangle + |\psi_2\rangle)$ a valid state?

**Solution:**

Need to check normalization: $\langle\psi|\psi\rangle = 1$

$$\langle\psi|\psi\rangle = \frac{1}{2}\langle\psi_1 + \psi_2|\psi_1 + \psi_2\rangle$$

$$= \frac{1}{2}(\langle\psi_1|\psi_1\rangle + \langle\psi_1|\psi_2\rangle + \langle\psi_2|\psi_1\rangle + \langle\psi_2|\psi_2\rangle)$$

If $\langle\psi_1|\psi_1\rangle = \langle\psi_2|\psi_2\rangle = 1$ (normalized) and $\langle\psi_1|\psi_2\rangle = 0$ (orthogonal):

$$= \frac{1}{2}(1 + 0 + 0 + 1) = 1$$ ✓

**Answer:** Yes, if original states are orthonormal; generally need renormalization

### Example 2: Inner Product Properties

The inner product $\langle\phi|\psi\rangle$ is:
- Linear in second argument: $\langle\phi|c_1\psi_1 + c_2\psi_2\rangle = c_1\langle\phi|\psi_1\rangle + c_2\langle\phi|\psi_2\rangle$
- Antilinear in first argument: $\langle c_1\phi_1 + c_2\phi_2|\psi\rangle = c_1^*\langle\phi_1|\psi\rangle + c_2^*\langle\phi_2|\psi\rangle$

Verify: If $\langle\phi|\psi\rangle = 3 + 4i$, what is $\langle\psi|\phi\rangle$?

**Solution:**

Inner product property: $\langle\phi|\psi\rangle = \langle\psi|\phi\rangle^*$

$$\langle\psi|\phi\rangle = (3 + 4i)^* = 3 - 4i$$

**Answer:** $\langle\psi|\phi\rangle = 3 - 4i$ (complex conjugate)

### Example 3: Orthonormal Basis

A complete orthonormal set $\{|\phi_n\rangle\}$ satisfies:

**Orthonormality**: $\langle\phi_m|\phi_n\rangle = \delta_{mn}$

**Completeness**: $\sum_n |\phi_n\rangle\langle\phi_n| = \mathbb{1}$ (resolution of identity)

If $|\psi\rangle = \sum_n c_n|\phi_n\rangle$, find $c_n$.

**Solution:**

Take inner product with $\langle\phi_m|$:

$$\langle\phi_m|\psi\rangle = \sum_n c_n\langle\phi_m|\phi_n\rangle = \sum_n c_n\delta_{mn} = c_m$$

**Answer:** $c_n = \langle\phi_n|\psi\rangle$ (expansion coefficients from inner product)

## Position and Momentum Representations

Abstract state $|\psi\rangle$ can be represented in different bases:

**Position representation**: $\psi(x) = \langle x|\psi\rangle$ (wavefunction)

**Momentum representation**: $\tilde{\psi}(p) = \langle p|\psi\rangle$

These are related by **Fourier transform**.

### Example 4: Position Basis Completeness

The position basis satisfies:

$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} |x\rangle\langle x| dx = \mathbb{1}$$

Use this to expand arbitrary state: $|\psi\rangle = \int \psi(x)|x\rangle dx$

Verify by computing $\langle x'|\psi\rangle$.

**Solution:**

$$\langle x'|\psi\rangle = \langle x'|\int \psi(x)|x\rangle dx = \int \psi(x)\langle x'|x\rangle dx$$

Using $\langle x'|x\rangle = \delta(x' - x)$:

$$= \int \psi(x)\delta(x' - x) dx = \psi(x')$$ ✓

**Answer:** Recovers wavefunction $\psi(x')$ (consistency check)

### Example 5: Normalization in Position Representation

If $|\psi\rangle$ is normalized ($\langle\psi|\psi\rangle = 1$), what condition does $\psi(x)$ satisfy?

**Solution:**

$$\langle\psi|\psi\rangle = \int\int \langle\psi|x\rangle\langle x|x'\rangle\langle x'|\psi\rangle dx dx'$$

$$= \int\int \psi^*(x)\delta(x-x')\psi(x') dx dx'$$

$$= \int |\psi(x)|^2 dx = 1$$

**Answer:** $\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\psi(x)|^2 dx = 1$ (standard wavefunction normalization)

### Example 6: Fourier Transform Connection

The momentum and position representations are related:

$$\tilde{\psi}(p) = \langle p|\psi\rangle = \int \langle p|x\rangle\langle x|\psi\rangle dx = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\hbar}}\int e^{-ipx/\hbar}\psi(x) dx$$

This is the **Fourier transform**. If $\psi(x) = e^{-x^2/2a^2}$ (Gaussian), find $\tilde{\psi}(p)$.

**Solution:**

$$\tilde{\psi}(p) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\hbar}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-ipx/\hbar}e^{-x^2/2a^2} dx$$

Complete the square in exponent:

$$-\frac{x^2}{2a^2} - i\frac{px}{\hbar} = -\frac{1}{2a^2}\left(x + ia^2\frac{p}{\hbar}\right)^2 - \frac{a^2p^2}{2\hbar^2}$$

Gaussian integral: $\int e^{-x^2/2a^2} dx = \sqrt{2\pi}a$

$$\tilde{\psi}(p) = \frac{a}{\sqrt{\hbar}}e^{-a^2p^2/2\hbar^2}$$

**Answer:** Gaussian in position → Gaussian in momentum (with reciprocal width)

## Linear Operators

Physical observables are represented by **linear operators** $\hat{A}$ acting on states:

$$\hat{A}(c_1|\psi_1\rangle + c_2|\psi_2\rangle) = c_1\hat{A}|\psi_1\rangle + c_2\hat{A}|\psi_2\rangle$$

### Example 7: Position Operator

In position representation, the position operator acts as:

$$\hat{x}|\psi\rangle \to x\psi(x)$$

(multiplication by $x$)

If $\psi(x) = xe^{-x^2}$, what is $\hat{x}\psi(x)$?

**Solution:**

$$\hat{x}\psi(x) = x \cdot \psi(x) = x \cdot (xe^{-x^2}) = x^2e^{-x^2}$$

**Answer:** $\hat{x}\psi = x^2e^{-x^2}$ (position eigenvalue $x$ at each point)

### Example 8: Momentum Operator

In position representation, momentum operator is:

$$\hat{p} = -i\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial x}$$

(differential operator)

Compute $\hat{p}\psi$ for $\psi(x) = e^{ikx}$.

**Solution:**

$$\hat{p}\psi = -i\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial x}e^{ikx} = -i\hbar(ik)e^{ikx} = \hbar ke^{ikx}$$

**Answer:** $\hat{p}\psi = \hbar k\psi$ (momentum eigenstate with eigenvalue $\hbar k$)

### Example 9: Hamiltonian Operator

The Hamiltonian (total energy) is:

$$\hat{H} = \frac{\hat{p}^2}{2m} + V(\hat{x})$$

In position representation:

$$\hat{H}\psi = -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{\partial^2\psi}{\partial x^2} + V(x)\psi$$

For harmonic oscillator $V(x) = \frac{1}{2}m\omega^2x^2$, write $\hat{H}\psi$ explicitly.

**Solution:**

$$\hat{H}\psi = -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\psi'' + \frac{1}{2}m\omega^2x^2\psi$$

**Answer:** Time-independent Schrödinger operator for harmonic oscillator

## Hermitian Operators and Observables

**Hermitian (self-adjoint)** operators satisfy $\hat{A}^\dagger = \hat{A}$, where adjoint is defined:

$$\langle\phi|\hat{A}|\psi\rangle = \langle\hat{A}^\dagger\phi|\psi\rangle$$

**Physical requirement**: Observables must be Hermitian to ensure real eigenvalues.

### Example 10: Verifying Hermiticity

Show that position operator $\hat{x}$ is Hermitian.

**Solution:**

$$\langle\phi|\hat{x}|\psi\rangle = \int \phi^*(x) \cdot x\psi(x) dx$$

$$\langle\hat{x}\phi|\psi\rangle = \int (x\phi(x))^*\psi(x) dx = \int x^*\phi^*(x)\psi(x) dx$$

Since $x$ is real: $x^* = x$

$$= \int x\phi^*(x)\psi(x) dx = \langle\phi|\hat{x}|\psi\rangle$$ ✓

**Answer:** $\hat{x}$ is Hermitian (multiplication by real function)

### Example 11: Momentum Operator Hermiticity

Show $\hat{p} = -i\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial x}$ is Hermitian (with boundary conditions $\psi \to 0$ as $x \to \pm\infty$).

**Solution:**

$$\langle\phi|\hat{p}|\psi\rangle = \int \phi^*(x)\left(-i\hbar\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial x}\right) dx$$

Integration by parts:

$$= -i\hbar\left[\phi^*\psi\right]_{-\infty}^{\infty} + i\hbar\int \frac{\partial\phi^*}{\partial x}\psi dx$$

Boundary term vanishes (wavefunctions decay at infinity):

$$= i\hbar\int \psi\left(-i\hbar\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x}\right)^* dx = \langle\hat{p}\phi|\psi\rangle$$ ✓

**Answer:** $\hat{p}$ is Hermitian (derivative operator with proper boundary conditions)

## The Canonical Commutation Relation

The **commutator** of two operators:

$$[\hat{A}, \hat{B}] = \hat{A}\hat{B} - \hat{B}\hat{A}$$

**Fundamental relation**:

$$[\hat{x}, \hat{p}] = i\hbar$$

This encodes the uncertainty principle and distinguishes quantum from classical mechanics.

### Example 12: Computing the Canonical Commutator

Verify $[\hat{x}, \hat{p}]\psi = i\hbar\psi$ in position representation.

**Solution:**

$$[\hat{x}, \hat{p}]\psi = \hat{x}\hat{p}\psi - \hat{p}\hat{x}\psi$$

$$\hat{p}\psi = -i\hbar\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial x}$$

$$\hat{x}\hat{p}\psi = x\left(-i\hbar\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial x}\right) = -i\hbar x\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial x}$$

$$\hat{p}\hat{x}\psi = -i\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial x}(x\psi) = -i\hbar\left(\psi + x\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial x}\right)$$

$$[\hat{x}, \hat{p}]\psi = -i\hbar x\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial x} + i\hbar\psi + i\hbar x\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial x} = i\hbar\psi$$ ✓

**Answer:** Confirmed $[\hat{x}, \hat{p}] = i\hbar$ (order matters for operators!)

### Example 13: Commutator with Hamiltonian

For free particle $\hat{H} = \frac{\hat{p}^2}{2m}$, compute $[\hat{H}, \hat{p}]$.

**Solution:**

$$[\hat{H}, \hat{p}] = \left[\frac{\hat{p}^2}{2m}, \hat{p}\right] = \frac{1}{2m}[\hat{p}^2, \hat{p}]$$

$$[\hat{p}^2, \hat{p}] = \hat{p}\hat{p}\hat{p} - \hat{p}\hat{p}\hat{p} = 0$$

(momentum commutes with itself)

**Answer:** $[\hat{H}, \hat{p}] = 0$ (momentum is conserved for free particle)

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: C**
$\langle\psi|\psi\rangle$ is the norm squared of state vector
For normalized states: $\langle\psi|\psi\rangle = 1$
Ensures total probability = 1
Not energy (that's $\langle\psi|\hat{H}|\psi\rangle$)
Fundamental requirement: state must have unit norm

**Problem 2: B**
$\hat{p} = -i\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial x}$ in position basis
Differential operator (not multiplicative)
Note: sign convention ($-i$, not $+i$)
Gives $\hat{p}e^{ikx} = \hbar ke^{ikx}$ (plane wave eigenstate)
Fourier transform relates position/momentum

**Problem 3: B**
Observables must be Hermitian: $\hat{A}^\dagger = \hat{A}$
Hermitian operators have real eigenvalues (proven via $\langle\psi|\hat{A}|\psi\rangle = \langle\hat{A}\psi|\psi\rangle$)
Measurement outcomes are real numbers
Examples: energy, position, momentum all real
Complex eigenvalues would be unphysical

**Problem 4: D**
$[\hat{x}, \hat{p}] = i\hbar$ (fundamental quantum relation)
Distinguishes quantum from classical ($\hbar \to 0$ gives classical)
Encodes uncertainty principle
From $[\hat{x}, \hat{p}]\psi = i\hbar\psi$
Note: $i$ is crucial (pure imaginary)

**Problem 5: B**
Position representation: $\psi(x) = \langle x|\psi\rangle$
Projection onto position eigenstates
This is "the wavefunction" from intro QM
Generally complex-valued
$|\psi(x)|^2$ gives probability density
