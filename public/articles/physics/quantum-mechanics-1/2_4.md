# Quantum Mechanics Topic 2.4: Delta Function Potential and Tunneling

The **Dirac delta function potential** $V(x) = -\alpha\delta(x)$ seems like mathematical idealization—an infinitely deep, infinitesimally narrow well. Yet it captures essential physics: a point-like attractive interaction. Remarkably, no matter how weak the attraction ($\alpha > 0$), there's always exactly one bound state with energy $E = -\frac{m\alpha^2}{2\hbar^2}$. This illustrates a profound quantum fact: any attractive potential, however weak, supports at least one bound state. The wavefunction has a characteristic **cusp** at $x = 0$ where the derivative jumps discontinuously.

Tunneling—quantum particles penetrating classically forbidden regions—is perhaps quantum mechanics' most counterintuitive phenomenon. Place a particle with energy $E$ against a rectangular barrier of height $V_0 > E$. Classically, it bounces back with certainty. Quantum mechanically, the wavefunction penetrates exponentially into the barrier: $\psi \sim e^{-\kappa x}$ where $\kappa = \sqrt{2m(V_0 - E)}/\hbar$. If the barrier has finite width $a$, there's non-zero transmission probability $T \approx e^{-2\kappa a}$. This isn't sci-fi—it's measured in scanning tunneling microscopes and explains alpha decay.

This article explores point interactions and barrier penetration. We'll solve the delta function potential for bound and scattering states, derive the cusp condition connecting derivatives, compute tunneling coefficients through rectangular barriers, and explore applications from nuclear physics to modern electronics. You'll see that quantum mechanics allows the "impossible"—particles where they have no business being.

## Delta Function Potential: Bound State

**Potential**: $V(x) = -\alpha\delta(x)$ where $\alpha > 0$ (attractive)

**Time-independent Schrödinger equation**:

$$-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\psi'' - \alpha\delta(x)\psi = E\psi$$

For bound state: $E < 0$

### Example 1: Solution Away from Origin

For $x \neq 0$, solve equation.

**Solution:**

Where $\delta(x) = 0$ (i.e., $x \neq 0$):

$$-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\psi'' = E\psi$$

$$\psi'' = \kappa^2\psi$$ where $\kappa = \frac{\sqrt{-2mE}}{\hbar} > 0$

General solution: $\psi = Ae^{\kappa x} + Be^{-\kappa x}$

**Boundary conditions** (normalizability):
- For $x > 0$: $\psi \to 0$ as $x \to \infty$ requires $A = 0$, so $\psi(x) = Be^{-\kappa x}$
- For $x < 0$: $\psi \to 0$ as $x \to -\infty$ requires $B = 0$, so $\psi(x) = Ae^{\kappa x}$

**Answer:** Exponential decay on both sides (bound state localized)

### Example 2: Continuity at Origin

Wavefunction must be continuous at $x = 0$. What does this require?

**Solution:**

$$\lim_{x \to 0^-}\psi(x) = \lim_{x \to 0^+}\psi(x)$$

$$Ae^{0} = Be^{0} \Rightarrow A = B$$

Define $\psi_0 = \psi(0) = A = B$.

So:
$$\psi(x) = \psi_0 e^{-\kappa|x|}$$

**Answer:** Even function (symmetric about origin)

### Example 3: Cusp Condition

Integrate Schrödinger equation from $-\epsilon$ to $+\epsilon$ and take $\epsilon \to 0$.

**Solution:**

$$-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\int_{-\epsilon}^{\epsilon}\psi'' dx - \alpha\int_{-\epsilon}^{\epsilon}\delta(x)\psi dx = E\int_{-\epsilon}^{\epsilon}\psi dx$$

As $\epsilon \to 0$:

First term: $-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}[\psi'(\epsilon) - \psi'(-\epsilon)]$

Second term: $-\alpha\psi(0)$ (delta function picks out $x = 0$)

Third term: $\to 0$ (finite integrand over infinitesimal interval)

$$-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}[\psi'(0^+) - \psi'(0^-)] = \alpha\psi(0)$$

For $\psi(x) = \psi_0 e^{-\kappa|x|}$:

$$\psi'(0^+) = -\kappa\psi_0, \quad \psi'(0^-) = +\kappa\psi_0$$

$$-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}[-\kappa\psi_0 - \kappa\psi_0] = \alpha\psi_0$$

$$\frac{\hbar^2\kappa}{m}\psi_0 = \alpha\psi_0$$

**Answer:** Cusp condition $\kappa = \frac{m\alpha}{\hbar^2}$ (derivative discontinuity)

### Example 4: Energy of Bound State

From $\kappa = m\alpha/\hbar^2$ and $\kappa = \sqrt{-2mE}/\hbar$, find $E$.

**Solution:**

$$\frac{m\alpha}{\hbar^2} = \frac{\sqrt{-2mE}}{\hbar}$$

Square both sides:

$$\frac{m^2\alpha^2}{\hbar^4} = \frac{-2mE}{\hbar^2}$$

$$E = -\frac{m\alpha^2}{2\hbar^2}$$

**Key observation**: Energy depends only on $\alpha$ and $m$ (not on width—it's a point interaction!).

**Answer:** Unique bound state $E_0 = -\frac{m\alpha^2}{2\hbar^2}$ (always exists for any $\alpha > 0$)

### Example 5: Normalized Wavefunction

Normalize $\psi(x) = \psi_0 e^{-\kappa|x|}$.

**Solution:**

$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\psi|^2 dx = 1$$

$$|\psi_0|^2\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-2\kappa|x|} dx = 1$$

By symmetry:

$$|\psi_0|^2 \cdot 2\int_0^{\infty}e^{-2\kappa x} dx = 1$$

$$|\psi_0|^2 \cdot 2 \cdot \frac{1}{2\kappa} = 1$$

$$|\psi_0|^2 = \kappa = \frac{m\alpha}{\hbar^2}$$

**Answer:** $\psi(x) = \sqrt{\frac{m\alpha}{\hbar^2}}e^{-m\alpha|x|/\hbar^2}$

## Delta Function Scattering

For $E > 0$ (scattering states), particle incident from left with $k = \sqrt{2mE}/\hbar$.

### Example 6: Scattering State Ansatz

Set up scattering wavefunction.

**Solution:**

**Region $x < 0$**: Incident + reflected waves
$$\psi(x) = e^{ikx} + re^{-ikx}$$

**Region $x > 0$**: Transmitted wave
$$\psi(x) = te^{ikx}$$

where $r$ is reflection amplitude, $t$ is transmission amplitude.

**Answer:** Plane waves with reflection and transmission coefficients

### Example 7: Matching Conditions for Scattering

Apply continuity and cusp condition.

**Solution:**

**Continuity** at $x = 0$:
$$1 + r = t$$

**Cusp condition**: $\psi'(0^+) - \psi'(0^-) = -\frac{2m\alpha}{\hbar^2}\psi(0)$

$$ikt - ik(1 - r) = -\frac{2m\alpha}{\hbar^2}t$$

$$ik(t - 1 + r) = -\frac{2m\alpha}{\hbar^2}t$$

From first equation: $r = t - 1$, so $t - 1 + r = 2r$:

$$2ikr = -\frac{2m\alpha}{\hbar^2}t$$

$$r = -\frac{m\alpha}{\hbar^2ik}t$$

Substitute into $1 + r = t$:

$$1 - \frac{m\alpha}{\hbar^2ik}t = t$$

$$t = \frac{1}{1 + \frac{m\alpha}{\hbar^2ik}} = \frac{ik}{ik + m\alpha/\hbar^2}$$

**Answer:** Transmission $t = \frac{ik\hbar^2}{ik\hbar^2 + m\alpha}$

### Example 8: Transmission Probability

Compute $T = |t|^2$ (probability of transmission).

**Solution:**

$$t = \frac{ik\hbar^2}{ik\hbar^2 + m\alpha}$$

$$|t|^2 = \frac{k^2\hbar^4}{k^2\hbar^4 + (m\alpha)^2}$$

Using $k = \sqrt{2mE}/\hbar$:

$$T = \frac{1}{1 + \frac{m^2\alpha^2}{2mE\hbar^2}} = \frac{1}{1 + \frac{m\alpha^2}{2E\hbar^2}}$$

**Behavior**:
- Low energy ($E \to 0$): $T \to 0$ (mostly reflected)
- High energy ($E \gg m\alpha^2/\hbar^2$): $T \to 1$ (mostly transmitted)

**Answer:** $T = \frac{1}{1 + (m\alpha/(\hbar^2k))^2}$ (always some reflection unless $E \to \infty$)

## Rectangular Barrier Tunneling

**Potential**: 
$$V(x) = \begin{cases} V_0 & 0 < x < a \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$

For $E < V_0$, classically particle cannot penetrate.

### Example 9: Three-Region Setup

Write wavefunctions in each region.

**Solution:**

**Region I** ($x < 0$): $E$ relative to $V = 0$
$$\psi_I = e^{ikx} + re^{-ikx}$$ where $k = \sqrt{2mE}/\hbar$

**Region II** ($0 < x < a$): $E$ relative to $V_0$ (classically forbidden)
$$\psi_{II} = Ae^{\kappa x} + Be^{-\kappa x}$$ where $\kappa = \sqrt{2m(V_0 - E)}/\hbar$

**Region III** ($x > a$): 
$$\psi_{III} = te^{ikx}$$ (transmitted)

**Answer:** Exponential in barrier, plane waves outside

### Example 10: Tunneling Probability (WKB Approximation)

For thick barrier ($\kappa a \gg 1$), estimate transmission.

**Solution:**

Detailed matching gives complicated formula. For thick barrier:

$$T \approx e^{-2\kappa a}$$

where $\kappa = \frac{\sqrt{2m(V_0 - E)}}{\hbar}$

This is **Gamow factor** (exponential suppression).

**Key points**:
- Higher/wider barrier → smaller $T$ (exponentially suppressed)
- Closer energy to $V_0$ → larger $T$
- Never exactly zero! (always some tunneling)

**Answer:** $T \approx \exp\left[-\frac{2a}{\hbar}\sqrt{2m(V_0 - E)}\right]$ (WKB approximation)

### Example 11: Alpha Decay Application

Nucleus emits alpha particle (helium nucleus). Model as tunneling through Coulomb barrier.

**Solution:**

**Potential**: $V(r) = \frac{2Ze^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0 r}$ for $r > R$ (nuclear radius)

Alpha has energy $E$ (from nuclear binding).

Must tunnel through barrier from $r = R$ to classical turning point $r_0$ where $V(r_0) = E$.

Tunneling probability:

$$T \approx \exp\left[-\frac{2}{\hbar}\int_R^{r_0}\sqrt{2m(V(r) - E)} \, dr\right]$$

**Gamow theory** (1928): Exponential dependence of decay rate on $E$ explains huge variation in half-lives.

**Answer:** Tunneling explains why some nuclei live milliseconds, others billions of years

### Example 12: Scanning Tunneling Microscope (STM)

STM measures tunneling current between metallic tip and surface.

**Solution:**

Electrons at Fermi energy $E_F$ tunnel across gap of width $d$.

Barrier height $V_0 \approx$ work function $\sim 4$ eV.

Tunneling current: $I \propto T \approx e^{-2\kappa d}$

For typical values: $\kappa \sim 10$ nm$^{-1}$

Change in gap: $\Delta d = 0.1$ nm changes $I$ by factor $e^{2\kappa\Delta d} \approx e^2 = 7.4$

**Extreme sensitivity**: Atomic-scale resolution!

**Answer:** Exponential sensitivity allows atomic imaging

### Example 13: Resonant Tunneling

For double barrier (quantum well between barriers), transmission shows resonances.

**Solution:**

Transmission probability:

$$T(E) = \frac{T_1T_2}{(1 - R_1R_2)^2 + 4R_1R_2\sin^2(\phi)}$$

where $\phi$ depends on energy.

**Resonance**: When $\sin^2(\phi) = 0$ (constructive interference), $T \to 1$ (perfect transmission!) even though each barrier individually has low transmission.

**Quantum well states**: Discrete levels between barriers act as intermediate states.

**Application**: Resonant tunneling diodes (negative differential resistance).

**Answer:** Constructive interference gives sharp transmission peaks

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: C**
Always exactly one bound state for $V = -\alpha\delta(x)$
$E_0 = -\frac{m\alpha^2}{2\hbar^2}$ (no matter how small $\alpha$)
No matter how weak attraction, quantum binding occurs
Contrast with finite well (number depends on depth)
Point interaction → unique state

**Problem 2: B**
Derivative jumps at delta function
$\psi'(0^+) - \psi'(0^-) = -\frac{2m\alpha}{\hbar^2}\psi(0)$
Wavefunction itself continuous
Second derivative has delta function
Creates characteristic cusp shape

**Problem 3: B**
$T \approx e^{-2\kappa a}$ (Gamow factor)
Exponential in barrier width $a$
Exponential in $\sqrt{V_0 - E}$ (barrier height - energy)
Never exactly zero (quantum effect)
Thicker/higher barrier → much smaller $T$

**Problem 4: B**
WKB approximation valid for $\kappa a \gg 1$
Thick barrier (many decay lengths)
Thin barrier requires exact solution
Also requires $E < V_0$ (classically forbidden)
Gamow (1928): explained alpha decay

**Problem 5: B**
Current $I \propto e^{-2\kappa d}$ (exponential in gap $d$)
Angstrom change in $d$ → factor 10 in current
Maps surface at atomic scale
Not optical (electrons, not photons)
Quantum tunneling is key physics
