# Quantum Mechanics Topic 2.1: Infinite Square Well and Finite Square Well

A particle trapped in a box—what could be simpler? Classically, it bounces back and forth with any energy you choose. Quantum mechanically, everything changes. The **infinite square well** confines a particle between impenetrable walls at $x = 0$ and $x = L$. The wavefunction must vanish at the walls, forcing discrete energy levels: $E_n = \frac{n^2\pi^2\hbar^2}{2mL^2}$. The particle cannot have just any energy—quantization emerges naturally from boundary conditions, not imposed by hand.

This isn't mathematical artifice. The infinite well models electrons in metals, atoms in optical lattices, and quantum dots in semiconductors. The **finite square well** is even more realistic: walls have finite height $V_0$, allowing the wavefunction to penetrate classically forbidden regions. This **quantum tunneling** has no classical analog—the particle exists where it "shouldn't be," decaying exponentially into barriers. Transcendental equations determine energy levels; at least one bound state always exists, no matter how weak the potential.

This article solves both wells completely. We'll impose boundary conditions to quantize energy, compute wavefunctions and their node structure, explore the remarkable consequence of zero-point energy (the ground state has $E_1 > 0$, never zero), solve transcendental equations for the finite well, and understand tunneling as exponential tail penetration. You'll see how confinement itself—through boundary conditions on the wave equation—creates the discrete energy levels that define quantum systems.

## The Infinite Square Well

**Potential**:
$$V(x) = \begin{cases} 0 & 0 < x < L \\ \infty & x \leq 0 \text{ or } x \geq L \end{cases}$$

**Time-independent Schrödinger equation** inside well ($0 < x < L$):

$$-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{d^2\psi}{dx^2} = E\psi$$

Outside: $V = \infty$ forces $\psi = 0$ (particle cannot be where potential is infinite).

### Example 1: General Solution Inside Well

Solve $\psi'' = -k^2\psi$ where $k = \sqrt{2mE}/\hbar$.

**Solution:**

This is the harmonic oscillator equation in space (not time).

General solution: $\psi(x) = A\sin(kx) + B\cos(kx)$

Must apply boundary conditions next.

**Answer:** Superposition of sines and cosines (standing waves)

### Example 2: Applying Boundary Conditions

Boundary conditions: $\psi(0) = 0$ and $\psi(L) = 0$ (wavefunction continuous at walls).

**Solution:**

$\psi(0) = 0$:
$$A\sin(0) + B\cos(0) = 0 \Rightarrow B = 0$$

So $\psi(x) = A\sin(kx)$.

$\psi(L) = 0$:
$$A\sin(kL) = 0$$

Either $A = 0$ (trivial, no particle) or $\sin(kL) = 0$.

For non-trivial solution: $kL = n\pi$ where $n = 1, 2, 3, \ldots$ (positive integers)

**Answer:** Quantization condition $k_n = n\pi/L$

### Example 3: Energy Quantization

From $k_n = n\pi/L$ and $k = \sqrt{2mE}/\hbar$, find energy levels.

**Solution:**

$$\frac{\sqrt{2mE_n}}{\hbar} = \frac{n\pi}{L}$$

$$E_n = \frac{n^2\pi^2\hbar^2}{2mL^2} = \frac{n^2h^2}{8mL^2}$$ for $n = 1, 2, 3, \ldots$

**Key observations**:
- Discrete spectrum (not continuous)
- Quadratic in $n$: $E_2 = 4E_1$, $E_3 = 9E_1$, etc.
- Ground state $E_1 > 0$ (zero-point energy)

**Answer:** $E_n = \frac{n^2\pi^2\hbar^2}{2mL^2}$ (quantized energy levels)

### Example 4: Normalized Wavefunctions

Normalize $\psi_n(x) = A\sin(n\pi x/L)$.

**Solution:**

$$\int_0^L |\psi_n|^2 dx = 1$$

$$A^2\int_0^L \sin^2(n\pi x/L) dx = 1$$

Using $\int_0^L \sin^2(n\pi x/L) dx = L/2$:

$$A^2 \cdot \frac{L}{2} = 1 \Rightarrow A = \sqrt{\frac{2}{L}}$$

**Answer:** $\psi_n(x) = \sqrt{\frac{2}{L}}\sin\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right)$ (orthonormal eigenfunctions)

### Example 5: Zero-Point Energy

Why is $E_1 = \frac{\pi^2\hbar^2}{2mL^2} \neq 0$?

**Solution:**

**Classical**: Particle at rest has $E = 0$.

**Quantum**: If $E = 0$, then $k = 0$, so $\psi = A\sin(0) = 0$ everywhere.

But $\psi = 0$ means no particle! Contradiction.

**Uncertainty principle argument**: Confinement to length $L$ gives $\Delta x \sim L$.

Uncertainty: $\Delta p \geq \hbar/(2\Delta x) \sim \hbar/L$

Minimum kinetic energy: $E \sim \frac{(\Delta p)^2}{2m} \sim \frac{\hbar^2}{2mL^2}$ ✓

**Physical meaning**: Confinement requires minimum kinetic energy (cannot be at rest).

**Answer:** Quantum systems confined in space cannot have zero energy (zero-point energy)

### Example 6: Node Structure

How many nodes (zeros) does $\psi_n$ have?

**Solution:**

$$\psi_n(x) = \sqrt{\frac{2}{L}}\sin(n\pi x/L)$$

Zeros when $\sin(n\pi x/L) = 0$: $x = 0, L/n, 2L/n, \ldots, (n-1)L/n, L$

Interior nodes: $x = L/n, 2L/n, \ldots, (n-1)L/n$ (total: $n-1$ nodes)

**General rule**: $n$-th state has $n-1$ interior nodes.

Higher energy → more nodes → more oscillation → higher curvature → higher kinetic energy ✓

**Answer:** $\psi_n$ has $n-1$ interior nodes (ground state has zero nodes)

## The Finite Square Well

**Potential**:
$$V(x) = \begin{cases} -V_0 & |x| < a \\ 0 & |x| > a \end{cases}$$

(Attractive well of depth $V_0$ and width $2a$)

For **bound states** ($-V_0 < E < 0$), particle is localized near well.

### Example 7: Setting Up Regions

Divide space into three regions. What are the Schrödinger equations?

**Solution:**

**Region I** ($x < -a$): $V = 0$
$$-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\psi'' = E\psi \Rightarrow \psi'' = \kappa^2\psi$$ where $\kappa = \sqrt{-2mE}/\hbar > 0$

**Region II** ($|x| < a$): $V = -V_0$
$$-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\psi'' + (-V_0)\psi = E\psi \Rightarrow \psi'' = -k^2\psi$$ where $k = \sqrt{2m(E + V_0)}/\hbar$

**Region III** ($x > a$): Same as Region I

**Answer:** Exponential decay outside ($\psi \sim e^{-\kappa|x|}$), oscillatory inside ($\psi \sim \sin, \cos$)

### Example 8: Even Solutions

For even parity $\psi(-x) = \psi(x)$, find matching condition.

**Solution:**

**Region II**: $\psi(x) = A\cos(kx)$ (even)

**Region III**: $\psi(x) = Ce^{-\kappa x}$ (decays as $x \to \infty$)

**Matching at $x = a$**: Continuity of $\psi$ and $\psi'$:

$$A\cos(ka) = Ce^{-\kappa a}$$
$$-Ak\sin(ka) = -C\kappa e^{-\kappa a}$$

Divide equations:
$$k\tan(ka) = \kappa$$

Using $\kappa = \sqrt{-2mE}/\hbar$ and $k = \sqrt{2m(E+V_0)}/\hbar$:

Transcendental equation: $k\tan(ka) = \sqrt{(k_0^2 - k^2)}$ where $k_0 = \sqrt{2mV_0}/\hbar$

**Answer:** Must solve graphically or numerically (no closed form)

### Example 9: Odd Solutions

For odd parity $\psi(-x) = -\psi(x)$, derive matching condition.

**Solution:**

**Region II**: $\psi(x) = B\sin(kx)$ (odd)

**Region III**: $\psi(x) = Ce^{-\kappa x}$

**Matching at $x = a$**:

$$B\sin(ka) = Ce^{-\kappa a}$$
$$Bk\cos(ka) = -C\kappa e^{-\kappa a}$$

Divide:
$$k\cot(ka) = -\kappa$$

Or: $k\cot(ka) = -\sqrt{k_0^2 - k^2}$

**Answer:** Different transcendental equation than even case

### Example 10: At Least One Bound State

Prove finite well always has at least one bound state, no matter how weak $V_0$.

**Solution:**

For even states: $k\tan(ka) = \kappa$

As $k \to 0$ (very weak binding, $E \to -V_0$):
- LHS: $k\tan(ka) \to ka$ (small angle)
- RHS: $\kappa \to k_0 = \sqrt{2mV_0}/\hbar$

So need $ka \approx k_0$ at small $k$.

For any $V_0 > 0$, can always find solution near $k \to 0$.

**Physical reasoning**: Even infinitesimally weak attractive potential has at least one bound state (particle "sticks" slightly).

**Answer:** At least one bound state guaranteed (even for shallow wells)

### Example 11: Tunneling into Barrier

Why does wavefunction penetrate into classically forbidden region ($|x| > a$)?

**Solution:**

In Region III: $\psi \sim e^{-\kappa x}$ where $\kappa = \sqrt{2m|E|}/\hbar$

Classically: $E < V = 0$ in this region → particle cannot be here (negative kinetic energy).

Quantum: Wavefunction doesn't vanish discontinuously at boundary.

$|\psi|^2 \neq 0$ for $x > a$ means **non-zero probability** of finding particle in classically forbidden region.

**Tunneling depth**: Typical penetration $\delta \sim 1/\kappa \sim \hbar/\sqrt{2m|E|}$

**Answer:** Quantum tunneling—exponential tail into forbidden region

### Example 12: Number of Bound States

For given $V_0$ and $a$, how many bound states exist?

**Solution:**

Define dimensionless parameter: $z_0 = k_0 a = a\sqrt{2mV_0}/\hbar$

Graphical solution: Plot $z\tan z$ vs. $z$ and $\sqrt{z_0^2 - z^2}$ (circle).

Number of even states = number of intersections in $[0, \pi/2, \pi, 3\pi/2, \ldots]$

Estimate: $n_{\text{bound}} \approx \frac{z_0}{\pi/2} = \frac{2a\sqrt{2mV_0}}{\pi\hbar}$

Deeper or wider well → more bound states.

**Answer:** Increases with well depth $V_0$ and width $a$

### Example 13: Comparison to Infinite Well

What happens to finite well as $V_0 \to \infty$?

**Solution:**

As $V_0 \to \infty$:
- $\kappa \to \infty$ (exponential decay steeper)
- Wavefunction vanishes rapidly outside well
- Boundary conditions approach $\psi(\pm a) = 0$ (hard walls)

Energy levels: $E + V_0 \to \frac{n^2\pi^2\hbar^2}{2m(2a)^2}$ (infinite well of width $2a$)

**Answer:** Finite well → infinite well limit as $V_0 \to \infty$

A) Is exactly zero
B) Decays exponentially

A) $n$
B) $n-1$

D) $2n$

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: B**
$E_n = \frac{n^2\pi^2\hbar^2}{2mL^2}$ (quadratic in $n$)
Not linear: energy spacing increases with $n$
$E_2 = 4E_1$, $E_3 = 9E_1$, $E_4 = 16E_1$
From $k_n = n\pi/L$ and $E = \hbar^2k^2/2m$
Higher levels more widely spaced

**Problem 2: B**
Ground state $E_1 = \frac{\pi^2\hbar^2}{2mL^2} > 0$ (never zero)
Uncertainty principle: confinement requires kinetic energy
Cannot simultaneously have $\Delta x$ finite and $p = 0$
Fundamental quantum effect (no classical analog)
Prevents collapse of atoms

**Problem 3: B**
Bound states: negative energy relative to $V = 0$ outside
But higher than bottom of well: $E > -V_0$
Range: $-V_0 < E < 0$
$E \geq 0$ are scattering states (not bound)
Discrete spectrum for bound states

**Problem 4: B**
$\psi(x) \sim e^{-\kappa x}$ for $x > a$ where $\kappa = \sqrt{2m|E|}/\hbar$
Exponential decay (not zero!)
Tunneling: quantum particle penetrates barrier
Decay length $\sim 1/\kappa$ (penetration depth)
Probability $|\psi|^2$ decreases but non-zero

**Problem 5: B**
$n$-th state has $n-1$ interior nodes
Ground state ($n=1$): zero nodes
First excited ($n=2$): one node at $x = L/2$
Higher $n$ → more nodes → more oscillation
Node theorem: general result for 1D bound states
