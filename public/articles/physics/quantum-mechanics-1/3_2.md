# Quantum Mechanics Topic 3.2: Orbital Angular Momentum

Angular momentum in classical mechanics is $\mathbf{L} = \mathbf{r} \times \mathbf{p}$—a vector perpendicular to the plane of rotation with magnitude $L = rp\sin\theta$. Conservation of angular momentum governs planetary orbits, spinning tops, and rotating galaxies. In quantum mechanics, orbital angular momentum $\hat{\mathbf{L}} = \mathbf{r} \times \hat{\mathbf{p}}$ becomes an operator with profoundly non-classical properties. You cannot simultaneously measure all three components $L_x, L_y, L_z$—they don't commute. The magnitude is quantized as $L = \hbar\sqrt{l(l+1)}$ (not $\hbar l$!), and you can know at most one component, conventionally $L_z = \hbar m$.

The mathematics reveals deep structure. The components satisfy the **angular momentum algebra** $[\hat{L}_i, \hat{L}_j] = i\hbar\epsilon_{ijk}\hat{L}_k$—the Lie algebra of the rotation group SO(3). This commutation relation determines everything: the spectrum of eigenvalues, the ladder operators $\hat{L}_\pm$ that raise and lower $m$, and the impossibility of simultaneously measuring different components. The **spherical harmonics** $Y_l^m(\theta,\phi)$ emerge as the unique solutions—simultaneous eigenfunctions of $\hat{L}^2$ and $\hat{L}_z$.

This article develops orbital angular momentum rigorously. We'll derive commutators from the canonical relation $[\hat{x}_i, \hat{p}_j] = i\hbar\delta_{ij}$, construct ladder operators that change $m$ while preserving $l$, prove eigenvalues must satisfy $l = 0, 1, 2, \ldots$ and $m = -l, \ldots, +l$, understand space quantization (angular momentum precesses around the $z$-axis), and connect to spherical harmonics. You'll see that rotational symmetry—encoded in commutators—determines the quantum structure completely.

## Angular Momentum Operator

**Definition** in position representation:

$$\hat{\mathbf{L}} = \mathbf{r} \times \hat{\mathbf{p}} = -i\hbar(\mathbf{r} \times \nabla)$$

Components:
$$\hat{L}_x = \hat{y}\hat{p}_z - \hat{z}\hat{p}_y = -i\hbar\left(y\frac{\partial}{\partial z} - z\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\right)$$

$$\hat{L}_y = \hat{z}\hat{p}_x - \hat{x}\hat{p}_z = -i\hbar\left(z\frac{\partial}{\partial x} - x\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\right)$$

$$\hat{L}_z = \hat{x}\hat{p}_y - \hat{y}\hat{p}_x = -i\hbar\left(x\frac{\partial}{\partial y} - y\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\right)$$

### Example 1: Computing a Commutator

Compute $[\hat{L}_x, \hat{L}_y]$.

**Solution:**

$$\hat{L}_x = \hat{y}\hat{p}_z - \hat{z}\hat{p}_y, \quad \hat{L}_y = \hat{z}\hat{p}_x - \hat{x}\hat{p}_z$$

$$[\hat{L}_x, \hat{L}_y] = [\hat{y}\hat{p}_z - \hat{z}\hat{p}_y, \hat{z}\hat{p}_x - \hat{x}\hat{p}_z]$$

Expand (16 terms, most vanish):

$$= [\hat{y}\hat{p}_z, \hat{z}\hat{p}_x] + [\hat{z}\hat{p}_y, \hat{x}\hat{p}_z]$$

Using $[\hat{y}\hat{p}_z, \hat{z}\hat{p}_x] = \hat{y}[\hat{p}_z, \hat{z}]\hat{p}_x = \hat{y}(-i\hbar)\hat{p}_x = -i\hbar\hat{y}\hat{p}_x$

And $[\hat{z}\hat{p}_y, \hat{x}\hat{p}_z] = \hat{x}[\hat{z}, \hat{p}_z]\hat{p}_y = \hat{x}(i\hbar)\hat{p}_y = i\hbar\hat{x}\hat{p}_y$

$$[\hat{L}_x, \hat{L}_y] = i\hbar(\hat{x}\hat{p}_y - \hat{y}\hat{p}_x) = i\hbar\hat{L}_z$$

**Answer:** $[\hat{L}_x, \hat{L}_y] = i\hbar\hat{L}_z$ (fundamental commutator)

### Example 2: General Commutation Relations

The pattern from Example 1 generalizes to all components.

**Solution:**

$$[\hat{L}_x, \hat{L}_y] = i\hbar\hat{L}_z$$
$$[\hat{L}_y, \hat{L}_z] = i\hbar\hat{L}_x$$
$$[\hat{L}_z, \hat{L}_x] = i\hbar\hat{L}_y$$

Compact notation using Levi-Civita symbol:

$$[\hat{L}_i, \hat{L}_j] = i\hbar\epsilon_{ijk}\hat{L}_k$$

where $\epsilon_{ijk} = +1$ for cyclic permutations, $-1$ for anticyclic, $0$ if indices repeat.

**Physical meaning**: Cannot simultaneously measure all components (uncertainty).

**Answer:** Angular momentum algebra (Lie algebra of SO(3) rotation group)

### Example 3: Total Angular Momentum Squared

Define $\hat{L}^2 = \hat{L}_x^2 + \hat{L}_y^2 + \hat{L}_z^2$.

Show $[\hat{L}^2, \hat{L}_z] = 0$.

**Solution:**

$$[\hat{L}^2, \hat{L}_z] = [\hat{L}_x^2 + \hat{L}_y^2 + \hat{L}_z^2, \hat{L}_z]$$

$$= [\hat{L}_x^2, \hat{L}_z] + [\hat{L}_y^2, \hat{L}_z] + 0$$

Using $[\hat{A}^2, \hat{B}] = \hat{A}[\hat{A}, \hat{B}] + [\hat{A}, \hat{B}]\hat{A}$:

$$[\hat{L}_x^2, \hat{L}_z] = \hat{L}_x[\hat{L}_x, \hat{L}_z] + [\hat{L}_x, \hat{L}_z]\hat{L}_x$$

From $[\hat{L}_x, \hat{L}_z] = -i\hbar\hat{L}_y$:

$$= \hat{L}_x(-i\hbar\hat{L}_y) + (-i\hbar\hat{L}_y)\hat{L}_x = -i\hbar(\hat{L}_x\hat{L}_y + \hat{L}_y\hat{L}_x)$$

Similarly, $[\hat{L}_y^2, \hat{L}_z] = i\hbar(\hat{L}_x\hat{L}_y + \hat{L}_y\hat{L}_x)$

Sum: $[\hat{L}^2, \hat{L}_z] = 0$ ✓

**Answer:** $\hat{L}^2$ commutes with all $\hat{L}_i$ (can measure magnitude and one component simultaneously)

## Eigenvalues and Eigenstates

Since $[\hat{L}^2, \hat{L}_z] = 0$, they have simultaneous eigenstates $|l, m\rangle$:

$$\hat{L}^2|l, m\rangle = \hbar^2\lambda|l, m\rangle$$
$$\hat{L}_z|l, m\rangle = \hbar m|l, m\rangle$$

Goal: Find allowed values of $\lambda$ and $m$.

### Example 4: Ladder Operators

Define raising and lowering operators:

$$\hat{L}_+ = \hat{L}_x + i\hat{L}_y$$
$$\hat{L}_- = \hat{L}_x - i\hat{L}_y$$

Compute $[\hat{L}_z, \hat{L}_+]$.

**Solution:**

$$[\hat{L}_z, \hat{L}_+] = [\hat{L}_z, \hat{L}_x] + i[\hat{L}_z, \hat{L}_y]$$

$$= i\hbar\hat{L}_y + i(-i\hbar\hat{L}_x) = i\hbar\hat{L}_y + \hbar\hat{L}_x$$

$$= \hbar(\hat{L}_x + i\hat{L}_y) = \hbar\hat{L}_+$$

Similarly: $[\hat{L}_z, \hat{L}_-] = -\hbar\hat{L}_-$

**Answer:** $[\hat{L}_z, \hat{L}_\pm] = \pm\hbar\hat{L}_\pm$ (ladder commutators)

### Example 5: Ladder Operations

Show $\hat{L}_+|l, m\rangle \propto |l, m+1\rangle$.

**Solution:**

$$\hat{L}_z(\hat{L}_+|l, m\rangle) = (\hat{L}_+\hat{L}_z + [\hat{L}_z, \hat{L}_+])|l, m\rangle$$

$$= (\hat{L}_+\hat{L}_z + \hbar\hat{L}_+)|l, m\rangle$$

$$= \hat{L}_+(\hbar m)|l, m\rangle + \hbar\hat{L}_+|l, m\rangle$$

$$= \hbar(m+1)\hat{L}_+|l, m\rangle$$

So $\hat{L}_+|l, m\rangle$ is eigenstate of $\hat{L}_z$ with eigenvalue $\hbar(m+1)$.

Must be proportional to $|l, m+1\rangle$!

**Answer:** $\hat{L}_+$ raises $m$ by 1, $\hat{L}_-$ lowers by 1 (while preserving $l$)

### Example 6: Maximum and Minimum $m$

There must be maximum $m_{\max}$ and minimum $m_{\min}$. Why?

**Solution:**

For any $m$: $\langle l, m|\hat{L}_x^2 + \hat{L}_y^2|l, m\rangle \geq 0$

$$\langle\hat{L}^2\rangle - \langle\hat{L}_z^2\rangle \geq 0$$

$$\hbar^2\lambda - \hbar^2 m^2 \geq 0$$

$$m^2 \leq \lambda$$

So $m$ is bounded: $|m| \leq \sqrt{\lambda}$

There exist $m_{\max}$ satisfying $\hat{L}_+|l, m_{\max}\rangle = 0$ (can't go higher).

Similarly $m_{\min}$ with $\hat{L}_-|l, m_{\min}\rangle = 0$.

**Answer:** $m$ bounded above and below (finite range)

### Example 7: Determining Eigenvalues

From $\hat{L}_+|l, m_{\max}\rangle = 0$, find relation between $\lambda$ and $m_{\max}$.

**Solution:**

$$\hat{L}_+\hat{L}_- = (\hat{L}_x + i\hat{L}_y)(\hat{L}_x - i\hat{L}_y) = \hat{L}_x^2 + \hat{L}_y^2 + i[\hat{L}_x, \hat{L}_y]$$

$$= \hat{L}^2 - \hat{L}_z^2 - \hbar\hat{L}_z$$

At top of ladder ($m = m_{\max}$):

$$\hat{L}_-\hat{L}_+|l, m_{\max}\rangle = 0$$

But also:

$$\hat{L}_-\hat{L}_+ = \hat{L}^2 - \hat{L}_z^2 + \hbar\hat{L}_z$$

$$(\hbar^2\lambda - \hbar^2 m_{\max}^2 + \hbar^2 m_{\max})|l, m_{\max}\rangle = 0$$

$$\lambda = m_{\max}(m_{\max} - 1)$$

Similarly at bottom: $\lambda = m_{\min}(m_{\min} + 1)$

**Answer:** $\lambda = m_{\max}(m_{\max} - 1) = m_{\min}(m_{\min} + 1)$

### Example 8: Quantization Condition

From Example 7, show $m_{\max} - m_{\min}$ must be integer.

**Solution:**

$$m_{\max}(m_{\max} - 1) = m_{\min}(m_{\min} + 1)$$

Rearrange:

$$m_{\max}^2 - m_{\max} = m_{\min}^2 + m_{\min}$$

$$(m_{\max} - m_{\min})(m_{\max} + m_{\min}) = m_{\max} + m_{\min}$$

If $m_{\max} + m_{\min} \neq 0$: $m_{\max} - m_{\min} = 1$ (impossible, since ladder steps by 1)

So $m_{\max} + m_{\min} = 0 \Rightarrow m_{\min} = -m_{\max}$

Ladder from $m_{\min}$ to $m_{\max}$ in integer steps:

$$m_{\max} - m_{\min} = 2m_{\max} = \text{integer}$$

Define $l = m_{\max}$, so $l$ is integer or half-integer.

**For orbital angular momentum**: $l$ must be integer (from $\psi$ single-valued).

**Answer:** $l = 0, 1, 2, 3, \ldots$ and $m = -l, -l+1, \ldots, l-1, l$

### Example 9: Eigenvalue Formula

Using $\lambda = l(l+1)$, write eigenvalue equations.

**Solution:**

$$\hat{L}^2|l, m\rangle = \hbar^2 l(l+1)|l, m\rangle$$

$$\hat{L}_z|l, m\rangle = \hbar m|l, m\rangle$$

**Magnitude**: $L = \hbar\sqrt{l(l+1)}$ (not $\hbar l$!)

**$z$-component**: $L_z = \hbar m$

**Key observation**: $L_z < L$ always (can never align fully with $z$-axis).

**Answer:** Eigenvalues $\hbar^2 l(l+1)$ and $\hbar m$ with $l = 0, 1, 2, \ldots$, $m = -l, \ldots, +l$

## Space Quantization

### Example 10: Angular Momentum Vector Precession

If $|\hat{L}| = \hbar\sqrt{l(l+1)}$ but $L_z = \hbar m < \hbar\sqrt{l(l+1)}$, where is $\mathbf{L}$?

**Solution:**

$L_x$ and $L_y$ undefined (don't commute with $L_z$).

But: $\langle L_x\rangle = \langle L_y\rangle = 0$ (by symmetry in eigenstate of $L_z$)

And: $\langle L_x^2 + L_y^2\rangle = \langle L^2 - L_z^2\rangle = \hbar^2[l(l+1) - m^2] > 0$ for $m < l$

**Interpretation**: $\mathbf{L}$ precesses around $z$-axis with fixed $L_z$ and $|\mathbf{L}|$.

Cone of uncertainty: $L_x, L_y$ fluctuate such that $L_x^2 + L_y^2 = \hbar^2[l(l+1) - m^2]$ on average.

**Answer:** Space quantization—angular momentum vector precesses around measurement axis

### Example 11: Example with $l = 1$

For $l = 1$ ("p-orbital"), find possible $L_z$ values and angle with $z$-axis.

**Solution:**

$m = -1, 0, +1$, so $L_z = -\hbar, 0, +\hbar$

Magnitude: $L = \hbar\sqrt{1(1+1)} = \hbar\sqrt{2}$

Angle: $\cos\theta = \frac{L_z}{L} = \frac{\hbar m}{\hbar\sqrt{2}} = \frac{m}{\sqrt{2}}$

For $m = +1$: $\cos\theta = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \Rightarrow \theta = 45°$

For $m = 0$: $\cos\theta = 0 \Rightarrow \theta = 90°$

For $m = -1$: $\cos\theta = -\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \Rightarrow \theta = 135°$

**Answer:** Discrete angles (cannot point exactly along $z$!)

## Connection to Spherical Harmonics

### Example 12: Spherical Harmonics as Eigenfunctions

In position representation, $|l, m\rangle \leftrightarrow Y_l^m(\theta,\phi)$.

Verify $\hat{L}_z Y_1^0 = 0$.

**Solution:**

In spherical coordinates:

$$\hat{L}_z = -i\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi}$$

$$Y_1^0 = \sqrt{\frac{3}{4\pi}}\cos\theta$$ (independent of $\phi$)

$$\hat{L}_z Y_1^0 = -i\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi}\left(\sqrt{\frac{3}{4\pi}}\cos\theta\right) = 0$$ ✓

This confirms $m = 0$ (no $z$-component).

**Answer:** $Y_l^m$ are position-space eigenfunctions of $\hat{L}^2$ and $\hat{L}_z$

### Example 13: Ladder Operators on Spherical Harmonics

The ladder operators act:

$$\hat{L}_\pm Y_l^m = \hbar\sqrt{l(l+1) - m(m\pm 1)} Y_l^{m\pm 1}$$

Verify $\hat{L}_+ Y_1^0 \propto Y_1^1$.

**Solution:**

$$\hat{L}_+ Y_1^0 = \hbar\sqrt{1(2) - 0(1)} Y_1^1 = \hbar\sqrt{2} Y_1^1$$

Explicitly in spherical coordinates (tedious but doable):

$$\hat{L}_+ = \hbar e^{i\phi}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta} + i\cot\theta\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi}\right)$$

Acting on $Y_1^0 \propto \cos\theta$ gives $Y_1^1 \propto \sin\theta e^{i\phi}$ ✓

**Answer:** Ladder operators connect different $m$ states at same $l$

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: B**
$[\hat{L}_i, \hat{L}_j] = i\hbar\epsilon_{ijk}\hat{L}_k$ (angular momentum algebra)
Specifically: $[\hat{L}_x, \hat{L}_y] = i\hbar\hat{L}_z$
Components don't commute → can't measure all simultaneously
Fundamental to quantum angular momentum
Lie algebra of SO(3) rotation group

**Problem 2: D**
$\hat{L}^2|l,m\rangle = \hbar^2 l(l+1)|l,m\rangle$
Magnitude: $L = \hbar\sqrt{l(l+1)}$ (not $\hbar l$!)
Quantum correction: $\sqrt{l(l+1)} > l$ always
Example: $l=1$ gives $L = \hbar\sqrt{2}$, not $\hbar$
Maximum $L_z = \hbar l < L$ (can't align fully)

**Problem 3: C**
Orbital: $l = 0, 1, 2, 3, \ldots$ (integer)
From single-valuedness of wavefunction
Half-integer possible for intrinsic spin (not orbital)
Spectroscopic notation: $s, p, d, f, \ldots$ for $l = 0, 1, 2, 3, \ldots$
Orbital always integer

**Problem 4: D**
$m = -l, \ldots, +l$ (total $2l+1$ values)
For $l=2$: $m = -2, -1, 0, +1, +2$ (5 values)
$(2 \times 2 + 1 = 5)$ ✓
These are $d$-orbitals in chemistry
All have same energy (degeneracy)

**Problem 5: C**
$L_z$ definite, $L_x$ and $L_y$ uncertain
$\mathbf{L}$ vector precesses on cone around $z$-axis
Cone angle: $\cos\theta = m/\sqrt{l(l+1)}$
Cannot point exactly along any axis
Stern-Gerlach: discrete deflection angles
