# Quantum Mechanics Topic 3.4: Radial Equation and Effective Potential

The radial equation $-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}u'' + V_{\text{eff}}(r)u = Eu$ looks deceptively like a one-dimensional Schrödinger equation. But the **effective potential** $V_{\text{eff}}(r) = V(r) + \frac{\hbar^2 l(l+1)}{2mr^2}$ contains crucial physics. The second term—the **centrifugal barrier**—is purely quantum mechanical, arising from angular momentum. It diverges as $r \to 0$, creating an effective repulsion that prevents the particle from reaching the origin for $l > 0$. This explains why $p, d, f$ orbitals vanish at the nucleus while $s$-orbitals ($l=0$) penetrate.

The effective potential framework unifies bound states and scattering. For bound states, $E < V_{\text{eff}}(\infty)$ and the particle is confined between classical turning points. The **radial quantum number** $n_r = n - l - 1$ counts nodes in the radial wavefunction. For scattering, $E > V_{\text{eff}}(\infty)$ and asymptotic phase shifts encode all collision information. The centrifugal barrier dominates at small $r$, suppressing low-energy scattering for high $l$—only $s$-wave ($l=0$) scattering survives as $E \to 0$.

This article explores the radial equation deeply. We'll analyze the effective potential for different $l$ values, understand boundary conditions at origin and infinity, determine classical turning points and forbidden regions, count radial nodes and understand the node theorem, examine penetration versus exclusion from the nucleus, and connect to scattering phase shifts. You'll see how a single barrier term encodes rotational dynamics in radial motion.

## The Effective Potential

**Radial equation** (with $u = rR$):

$$-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{d^2u}{dr^2} + V_{\text{eff}}(r)u = Eu$$

where 

$$V_{\text{eff}}(r) = V(r) + \frac{\hbar^2 l(l+1)}{2mr^2}$$

### Example 1: Classical Analogy

The centrifugal term has classical origin. What is it?

**Solution:**

**Classical mechanics**: Particle with angular momentum $L$ in central potential.

Effective 1D radial problem with:

$$V_{\text{eff}}^{\text{classical}}(r) = V(r) + \frac{L^2}{2mr^2}$$

Second term is centrifugal potential energy.

**Quantum**: Replace $L^2 \to \hbar^2 l(l+1)$ (angular momentum eigenvalue):

$$V_{\text{eff}}^{\text{quantum}}(r) = V(r) + \frac{\hbar^2 l(l+1)}{2mr^2}$$

**Answer:** Quantum version of classical centrifugal potential

### Example 2: Behavior for Small $r$

Near origin, how does $V_{\text{eff}}$ behave for different $l$?

**Solution:**

As $r \to 0$:

$$V_{\text{eff}}(r) \approx V(r) + \frac{\hbar^2 l(l+1)}{2mr^2}$$

**$l = 0$**: $V_{\text{eff}} \approx V(r)$ (no barrier)

**$l > 0$**: $V_{\text{eff}} \to +\infty$ as $r \to 0$ (infinite repulsive barrier)

**Wavefunction behavior**:
- $l = 0$: $u \sim r$ near origin → $R = u/r$ finite at $r=0$
- $l > 0$: $u \sim r^{l+1}$ near origin → $R \sim r^l \to 0$ as $r \to 0$

**Answer:** Centrifugal barrier diverges for $l > 0$, excluding wavefunction from origin

### Example 3: Hydrogen Effective Potential

For hydrogen $V(r) = -ke^2/r$, plot $V_{\text{eff}}(r)$ for different $l$.

**Solution:**

$$V_{\text{eff}}(r) = -\frac{ke^2}{r} + \frac{\hbar^2 l(l+1)}{2mr^2}$$

**$l = 0$**: Pure Coulomb attraction $-ke^2/r$ (monotonic)

**$l > 0$**: Competition between attraction ($-1/r$) and repulsion ($+1/r^2$)

**Minimum** at $r_{\min}$: $\frac{dV_{\text{eff}}}{dr} = 0$

$$\frac{ke^2}{r^2} - \frac{\hbar^2 l(l+1)}{mr^3} = 0 \Rightarrow r_{\min} = \frac{\hbar^2 l(l+1)}{mke^2}$$

For $l = 1$: $r_{\min} = 2a_0$

**Shape**: Barrier with minimum, then rises to $0$ as $r \to \infty$.

**Answer:** Potential well with centrifugal barrier; deeper and narrower for higher $l$

## Boundary Conditions and Asymptotic Behavior

### Example 4: Boundary Condition at Origin

What requires $u(0) = 0$?

**Solution:**

Need $R(r)$ finite everywhere (normalizable).

Since $R = u/r$:

$$R(0) = \lim_{r \to 0}\frac{u(r)}{r}$$

For this to be finite: $u(r) \to 0$ as $r \to 0$.

More specifically: $u(r) \sim r^{l+1}$ near $r = 0$ (from solving ODE).

**Answer:** $u(0) = 0$ ensures $R(r)$ remains finite at origin

### Example 5: Asymptotic Form for Bound States

For $E < 0$ (bound state), what is $u(r)$ for large $r$?

**Solution:**

For $r \to \infty$, $V_{\text{eff}} \to 0$:

$$u'' \approx -\frac{2mE}{\hbar^2}u = \kappa^2 u$$

where $\kappa = \sqrt{-2mE}/\hbar > 0$ (since $E < 0$).

Solution: $u = Ae^{\kappa r} + Be^{-\kappa r}$

Normalizability requires $A = 0$:

$$u(r) \sim e^{-\kappa r} \quad (r \to \infty)$$

Decay length: $\delta = 1/\kappa = \hbar/\sqrt{-2mE}$

**Answer:** Exponential decay at rate determined by binding energy

### Example 6: Classical Turning Points

For given energy $E$ and $l$, where are turning points?

**Solution:**

**Classical turning point**: Where $E = V_{\text{eff}}(r)$

$$E = V(r) + \frac{\hbar^2 l(l+1)}{2mr^2}$$

**Classically allowed**: $E > V_{\text{eff}}(r)$ (kinetic energy positive)

**Classically forbidden**: $E < V_{\text{eff}}(r)$ (would need negative kinetic energy)

For hydrogen with $E < 0$:
- Inner turning point $r_{\min}$ where $V_{\text{eff}}(r_{\min}) = E$
- No outer turning point (bound state)

**Quantum**: Wavefunction tunnels into forbidden region.

**Answer:** Boundaries of classically allowed region; wavefunction penetrates beyond

## Radial Nodes and the Node Theorem

### Example 7: Radial Quantum Number

Define $n_r = n - l - 1$ (number of radial nodes).

**Solution:**

For hydrogen-like atoms:

Principal $n = 1, 2, 3, \ldots$

Orbital $l = 0, 1, \ldots, n-1$

**Radial nodes**: Zeros of $R_{nl}(r)$ (excluding origin and infinity)

$$n_r = n - l - 1$$

**Examples**:
- $1s$ ($n=1, l=0$): $n_r = 0$ (no nodes)
- $2s$ ($n=2, l=0$): $n_r = 1$ (one node)
- $2p$ ($n=2, l=1$): $n_r = 0$ (no nodes)
- $3d$ ($n=3, l=2$): $n_r = 0$ (no nodes)
- $4f$ ($n=4, l=3$): $n_r = 0$ (no nodes)

**Answer:** $n_r$ counts radial nodes; increases with principal $n$ for fixed $l$

### Example 8: Node Theorem

General principle: Higher energy → more nodes.

**Solution:**

For fixed $l$, energy increases with $n$:

$$E_1 < E_2 < E_3 < \cdots$$

Number of nodes also increases:

$$n_r = 0, 1, 2, \ldots$$

**Physical reasoning**: More nodes → more oscillation → higher curvature → higher kinetic energy.

**General theorem**: For 1D-like bound state problems, eigenstate $n$ has $n-1$ nodes.

For radial: State with $n_r$ nodes is $(n_r + 1)$-th state for that $l$.

**Answer:** Node count increases monotonically with energy (fundamental theorem)

### Example 9: Penetration to Nucleus

For equal energy, do $s$ or $p$ electrons get closer to nucleus on average?

**Solution:**

**$s$-states** ($l=0$): No centrifugal barrier, $R(0) \neq 0$

Penetrate to nucleus.

**$p$-states** ($l=1$): Centrifugal barrier, $R(0) = 0$

Excluded from nucleus.

For same $n$:
- $ns$ electron: $\langle 1/r \rangle_{ns}$ larger (spends more time near nucleus)
- $np$ electron: $\langle 1/r \rangle_{np}$ smaller

**Applications**:
- **Hyperfine structure**: $s$-electrons interact with nuclear magnetic moment
- **Isotope shifts**: $s$-electron wavefunction overlaps nuclear charge
- **Shielding**: $s$-electrons penetrate inner shells more effectively

**Answer:** $s$-electrons penetrate nucleus; higher $l$ excluded by centrifugal barrier

## Connection to Scattering

### Example 10: Scattering States

For $E > 0$, particle not bound. What is asymptotic form?

**Solution:**

For $r \to \infty$ with $V_{\text{eff}} \to 0$:

$$u'' = -k^2u$$ where $k = \sqrt{2mE}/\hbar$

General solution: $u \sim A\sin(kr + \delta_l)$ (oscillatory)

**Phase shift** $\delta_l$ encodes scattering information.

For $r \to 0$: $u \sim r^{l+1}$ (same as bound states)

**Answer:** Oscillatory at infinity with phase shift $\delta_l$ (scattering observable)

### Example 11: Low-Energy Scattering

As $E \to 0$, which partial wave dominates?

**Solution:**

Centrifugal barrier height: $\frac{\hbar^2 l(l+1)}{2mr^2}$

For small $E$, particle cannot penetrate high $l$ barriers.

**$l = 0$ (s-wave)**: No barrier → scattering possible even at $E \to 0$

**$l > 0$**: Barrier suppresses scattering at low energy.

Scattering amplitude: $f \approx f_0$ (only $s$-wave contributes)

**Applications**: Cold atoms, neutron scattering, Feshbach resonances.

**Answer:** $s$-wave ($l=0$) dominates at low energy; higher $l$ suppressed by barrier

### Example 12: Levinson's Theorem

Relation between phase shifts and bound states.

**Solution:**

**Theorem**: For potential $V(r) \to 0$ faster than $1/r$:

$$\delta_l(k=0) = n_l\pi$$

where $n_l$ is number of bound states with angular momentum $l$.

**Physical meaning**: Phase shift at zero energy counts bound states.

**Example**: Hydrogen $s$-wave ($l=0$) has infinite bound states, but Coulomb $1/r$ violates theorem condition.

**Answer:** Phase shift at threshold related to number of bound states (topological)

### Example 13: Effective Range Expansion

For low-energy $s$-wave scattering:

$$k\cot\delta_0 = -\frac{1}{a_s} + \frac{1}{2}r_e k^2 + O(k^4)$$

where $a_s$ is **scattering length**, $r_e$ is **effective range**.

**Solution:**

**Scattering length**: Characterizes low-energy scattering strength.
- $a_s > 0$: Repulsive
- $a_s < 0$: Attractive

**Effective range**: Next correction, sets energy scale.

For $ka_s \ll 1$: Scattering cross section

$$\sigma = 4\pi a_s^2$$

Independent of energy (s-wave unitarity limit)!

**Answer:** Two parameters ($a_s, r_e$) characterize low-energy scattering

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: C**
$V_{\text{centrifugal}} = \frac{\hbar^2 l(l+1)}{2mr^2}$ (always $\geq 0$)
Zero only for $l = 0$ (s-wave)
Repulsive (positive) for $l > 0$
Diverges at origin for any $l > 0$
Prevents particle from reaching nucleus

**Problem 2: D**
$4s$: $n=4, l=0$
$n_r = n - l - 1 = 4 - 0 - 1 = 3$ radial nodes
More nodes than $4p$ (2), $4d$ (1), $4f$ (0)
Node count increases with $n$ for fixed $l$

**Problem 3: A**
$1s$: $l=0$ (no centrifugal barrier)
$R(0)$ finite, nonzero
Hyperfine structure (nuclear spin coupling)
Higher $l$ orbitals vanish at origin
Only $s$-orbitals penetrate

**Problem 4: A**
$s$-wave ($l=0$) has no barrier
Higher $l$ suppressed by centrifugal barrier
Cold atom physics: s-wave scattering
Cross section $\sigma = 4\pi a_s^2$ (unitarity limit)
Feshbach resonances tune $a_s$

**Problem 5: C**
Classical turning point: $E = V_{\text{eff}}(r)$
Includes both $V(r)$ and centrifugal term
Boundary of classically allowed region
Kinetic energy zero at turning point
Quantum wavefunction penetrates beyond
