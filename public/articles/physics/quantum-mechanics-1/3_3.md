# Quantum Mechanics Topic 3.3: Hydrogen Atom

The hydrogen atom—one proton, one electron, bound by Coulomb attraction—is the cornerstone of atomic physics. It's the simplest atom, yet solving its quantum mechanics exactly reveals the entire architecture of the periodic table. The **Coulomb potential** $V(r) = -\frac{e^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0 r}$ is central, enabling separation in spherical coordinates. The radial equation, though more complex than previous examples, yields exact solutions in terms of **associated Laguerre polynomials**. The result: energy levels $E_n = -\frac{13.6 \text{ eV}}{n^2}$, reproducing Bohr's semi-classical formula but now derived from first principles.

The quantum numbers tell the complete story. The **principal quantum number** $n = 1, 2, 3, \ldots$ determines energy. The **orbital quantum number** $l = 0, 1, \ldots, n-1$ determines angular momentum magnitude. The **magnetic quantum number** $m = -l, \ldots, +l$ determines angular momentum projection. Each state $|n, l, m\rangle$ has wavefunction $\psi_{nlm}(r,\theta,\phi) = R_{nl}(r)Y_l^m(\theta,\phi)$. Remarkably, energy depends only on $n$—not on $l$ or $m$. This **accidental degeneracy** (beyond the expected $(2l+1)$-fold from rotational symmetry) is special to Coulomb potential.

This article solves the hydrogen atom completely. We'll set up the radial equation with Coulomb potential, introduce the Bohr radius $a_0 = 0.529$ Å as the natural length scale, solve for energy eigenvalues and wavefunctions, explore the quantum numbers and degeneracies, examine key states like the ground state $1s$ and excited $2s, 2p$ orbitals, understand radial probability distributions, and connect to atomic spectroscopy. You'll see why quantum mechanics explains chemistry.

## The Coulomb Potential and Radial Equation

**Potential energy**: $V(r) = -\frac{e^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0 r} = -\frac{ke^2}{r}$ where $k = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}$

**Radial equation** (from separation of variables):

$$-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{d^2u}{dr^2} + \left[-\frac{ke^2}{r} + \frac{\hbar^2 l(l+1)}{2mr^2}\right]u = Eu$$

where $u(r) = rR(r)$ and boundary conditions $u(0) = u(\infty) = 0$.

### Example 1: Bohr Radius

Define natural length scale $a_0$ by balancing kinetic and potential energy scales.

**Solution:**

Kinetic energy scale: $\frac{\hbar^2}{ma_0^2}$

Potential energy scale: $\frac{ke^2}{a_0}$

Balance:

$$\frac{\hbar^2}{ma_0^2} \sim \frac{ke^2}{a_0}$$

$$a_0 = \frac{\hbar^2}{mke^2} = \frac{4\pi\epsilon_0\hbar^2}{me^2}$$

Numerically: $a_0 = 0.529$ Å (Bohr radius)

**Physical meaning**: Typical size of hydrogen atom ground state.

**Answer:** $a_0 = \frac{4\pi\epsilon_0\hbar^2}{me^2} = 0.529$ Å (fundamental atomic length)

### Example 2: Rydberg Energy

Define natural energy scale.

**Solution:**

From Bohr radius:

$$E_0 = \frac{ke^2}{a_0} = \frac{(ke^2)^2 m}{\hbar^2} = \frac{me^4}{2(4\pi\epsilon_0)^2\hbar^2}$$

This is **Rydberg energy** (or twice it, depending on convention):

$$E_R = \frac{me^4}{2(4\pi\epsilon_0)^2\hbar^2} = 13.6 \text{ eV}$$

**Physical meaning**: Ionization energy of hydrogen ground state.

**Answer:** $E_R = 13.6$ eV (natural energy scale for atomic physics)

### Example 3: Dimensionless Radial Equation

Introduce dimensionless $\rho = r/a_0$ and $\epsilon = E/E_R$.

**Solution:**

$$\frac{d^2u}{d\rho^2} = \left[\frac{l(l+1)}{\rho^2} - \frac{2}{\rho} + \epsilon\right]u$$

For bound states: $E < 0$, so $\epsilon < 0$.

**Asymptotic behavior**:
- $\rho \to 0$: $u \sim \rho^{l+1}$ (regularity)
- $\rho \to \infty$: $u \sim e^{-\kappa\rho}$ where $\kappa = \sqrt{-\epsilon}$

**Answer:** Dimensionless form ready for series solution

## Energy Levels and Quantum Numbers

### Example 4: Energy Quantization

Series solution (via Frobenius method, tedious) gives quantization condition:

$$\kappa = \frac{1}{n}$$ where $n = l + 1, l + 2, l + 3, \ldots$

Find energy $E_n$.

**Solution:**

From $\kappa = \sqrt{-\epsilon} = \sqrt{-E/E_R}$:

$$\sqrt{-E/E_R} = \frac{1}{n}$$

$$-\frac{E}{E_R} = \frac{1}{n^2}$$

$$E_n = -\frac{E_R}{n^2} = -\frac{13.6 \text{ eV}}{n^2}$$

**Key feature**: Energy depends only on $n$, not on $l$ or $m$ (accidental degeneracy).

**Answer:** $E_n = -\frac{13.6 \text{ eV}}{n^2}$ for $n = 1, 2, 3, \ldots$

### Example 5: Quantum Number Ranges

For given $n$, what values can $l$ and $m$ take?

**Solution:**

From quantization: $n = l + 1$ at minimum, so $l \leq n - 1$.

**Principal**: $n = 1, 2, 3, 4, \ldots$

**Orbital**: $l = 0, 1, 2, \ldots, n-1$ (for each $n$)

**Magnetic**: $m = -l, -l+1, \ldots, l-1, l$ (for each $l$)

**Degeneracy for energy $E_n$**:

$$g_n = \sum_{l=0}^{n-1}(2l+1) = n^2$$

(Sum first $n$ odd numbers = $n^2$)

**Answer:** $l = 0, \ldots, n-1$; $m = -l, \ldots, +l$; degeneracy = $n^2$

### Example 6: Spectroscopic Notation

States labeled by $nlm$ or more commonly just $nl$.

**Solution:**

**$n$**: principal quantum number (energy level)

**$l$**: designated by letter:
- $l = 0$: $s$ (sharp)
- $l = 1$: $p$ (principal)
- $l = 2$: $d$ (diffuse)
- $l = 3$: $f$ (fundamental)
- $l = 4, 5, 6, \ldots$: $g, h, i, \ldots$ (alphabetical)

**Examples**:
- Ground state: $n=1, l=0$ → $1s$
- First excited: $n=2, l=0$ → $2s$; $n=2, l=1$ → $2p$
- Second excited: $3s, 3p, 3d$

**Answer:** Standard notation $nl$ (e.g., $1s, 2p, 3d$)

## Wavefunctions and Radial Probability

### Example 7: Ground State Wavefunction

For $n=1, l=0$ (1s state), find $\psi_{100}$.

**Solution:**

Radial function: $R_{10}(r) = 2\left(\frac{1}{a_0}\right)^{3/2}e^{-r/a_0}$

Angular: $Y_0^0 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi}}$

$$\psi_{100}(r,\theta,\phi) = R_{10}(r)Y_0^0 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi a_0^3}}e^{-r/a_0}$$

**Probability density**: $|\psi_{100}|^2 = \frac{1}{\pi a_0^3}e^{-2r/a_0}$

Maximum at $r = 0$ (nucleus)!

**Answer:** Spherically symmetric Gaussian-like decay with scale $a_0$

### Example 8: Radial Probability Distribution

Probability of finding electron between $r$ and $r + dr$ is:

$$P(r)dr = |R_{nl}(r)|^2 r^2 dr$$

For ground state, find most probable radius.

**Solution:**

$$P(r) = r^2|R_{10}|^2 = r^2 \cdot 4\left(\frac{1}{a_0}\right)^3 e^{-2r/a_0} = \frac{4r^2}{a_0^3}e^{-2r/a_0}$$

Maximize: $\frac{dP}{dr} = 0$

$$\frac{d}{dr}\left(r^2 e^{-2r/a_0}\right) = 2r e^{-2r/a_0} - r^2 \cdot \frac{2}{a_0}e^{-2r/a_0} = 0$$

$$2r - \frac{2r^2}{a_0} = 0 \Rightarrow r = a_0$$

**Answer:** Most probable radius is exactly $a_0$ (Bohr radius!)

### Example 9: Excited State: $2s$

For $n=2, l=0$, radial function has one node.

**Solution:**

$$R_{20}(r) = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}\left(\frac{1}{a_0}\right)^{3/2}\left(2 - \frac{r}{a_0}\right)e^{-r/2a_0}$$

**Node** where $R_{20} = 0$:

$$2 - \frac{r}{a_0} = 0 \Rightarrow r = 2a_0$$

**Number of nodes**: $n - l - 1 = 2 - 0 - 1 = 1$ ✓

**Answer:** One radial node at $r = 2a_0$

### Example 10: Excited State: $2p$

For $n=2, l=1$, no radial nodes but angular structure.

**Solution:**

$$R_{21}(r) = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{6}}\left(\frac{1}{a_0}\right)^{3/2}\frac{r}{a_0}e^{-r/2a_0}$$

**Radial nodes**: $n - l - 1 = 2 - 1 - 1 = 0$ ✓ (no nodes)

**Angular**: $Y_1^m$ ($m = -1, 0, +1$) give $p_x, p_y, p_z$ orbitals (oriented lobes).

**Penetration**: $R_{21}(0) = 0$ (vanishes at origin for $l > 0$).

**Answer:** No radial nodes; angular nodes from $Y_1^m$ (two lobes)

## Properties and Physical Insights

### Example 11: Size Scaling with $n$

Average radius $\langle r \rangle$ scales with $n$.

**Solution:**

Detailed calculation (integration with Laguerre polynomials) gives:

$$\langle r \rangle_n = \frac{a_0}{2}[3n^2 - l(l+1)]$$

For large $n$ with $l \ll n$:

$$\langle r \rangle_n \approx \frac{3a_0n^2}{2} \propto n^2$$

**Physical meaning**: Higher energy levels are larger (less tightly bound).

**Answer:** Atom size grows as $n^2$ (Bohr's result)

### Example 12: Accidental Degeneracy

Why does $E_n$ not depend on $l$ (unlike 3D harmonic oscillator)?

**Solution:**

**Special to Coulomb $1/r$ potential** (and harmonic oscillator $r^2$).

Related to **hidden symmetry**: Runge-Lenz vector conserved in Kepler problem.

Group theory: SO(4) symmetry (not just SO(3) rotation).

**Breaking degeneracy**: Relativistic corrections (fine structure) split $E_{nl}$.

**Answer:** Accidental degeneracy from special Coulomb symmetry; rare in other potentials

### Example 13: Ionization Energy

Energy required to remove electron from ground state.

**Solution:**

Ground state: $E_1 = -13.6$ eV (bound)

Ionized: $E = 0$ (free electron at rest)

Ionization energy:

$$I = E_{\text{final}} - E_{\text{initial}} = 0 - (-13.6 \text{ eV}) = 13.6 \text{ eV}$$

This is the **Rydberg constant** times $hc$ (in spectroscopy).

**Applications**: Photoelectric effect, ionization detectors, plasma physics.

**Answer:** $I = 13.6$ eV (binding energy of hydrogen ground state)

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: B**
$E_n = -\frac{13.6 \text{ eV}}{n^2}$ for $n = 1, 2, 3, \ldots$
Reproduces Bohr formula (now from Schrödinger)
Energy scales as $1/n^2$ (not $1/n$)
Ground state most negative (most bound)
Ionization at $E = 0$

**Problem 2: C**
Degeneracy = $n^2$ (for principal number $n$)
$n=3$: $3^2 = 9$
States: $3s$ (1), $3p$ (3), $3d$ (5) = $1+3+5 = 9$ ✓
Accidental degeneracy (special to Coulomb)
Fine structure breaks some degeneracy

**Problem 3: B**
$a_0 = 0.529$ Å $= 0.0529$ nm
Angstrom ($10^{-10}$ m) natural atomic unit
Sets scale of all atoms
Inversely proportional to electron mass
Positronium: $a_0 \to 2a_0$ (reduced mass)

**Problem 4: C**
Radial probability $P(r) = r^2|R|^2$ maximized at $r = a_0$
Not at origin! (factor $r^2$ from volume)
Bohr's classical orbit radius
Average $\langle r \rangle = \frac{3a_0}{2}$ (slightly larger)
Probability density max at origin, but volume vanishes

**Problem 5: A**
$3d$: $n=3, l=2$
Radial nodes: $n - l - 1 = 3 - 2 - 1 = 0$
No radial nodes (but angular nodes from $Y_2^m$)
$3p$: 1 radial node
$3s$: 2 radial nodes
