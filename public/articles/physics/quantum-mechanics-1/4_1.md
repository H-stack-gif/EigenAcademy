# Quantum Mechanics Topic 4.1: Spin-1/2 and Pauli Matrices

Orbital angular momentum $\mathbf{L} = \mathbf{r} \times \mathbf{p}$ arises from spatial motion—electrons orbiting nuclei, planets circling stars. But the **Stern-Gerlach experiment** (1922) revealed something astonishing: silver atoms passing through an inhomogeneous magnetic field split into two beams, not a continuous distribution. The atoms possess intrinsic angular momentum unrelated to their motion through space. This **spin** has no classical analog—it's purely quantum mechanical, an internal degree of freedom like charge or mass.

For electrons, protons, and neutrons, spin takes the simplest non-trivial form: **spin-1/2**. The particle exists in a two-dimensional Hilbert space spanned by $|+\rangle$ (spin-up) and $|-\rangle$ (spin-down). Measuring spin along any axis yields exactly two outcomes: $+\hbar/2$ or $-\hbar/2$. The **Pauli matrices** $\sigma_x, \sigma_y, \sigma_z$ represent spin operators, satisfying the angular momentum algebra $[\sigma_i, \sigma_j] = 2i\epsilon_{ijk}\sigma_k$ but compressed into $2\times 2$ matrices. The **Bloch sphere** visualizes pure states as points on a unit sphere—a geometric representation of quantum superposition.

This article develops spin-1/2 from the ground up. We'll construct the two-dimensional spin space, derive eigenvalues and eigenvectors of Pauli matrices, explore spinor notation and the Bloch sphere representation, compute spin precession in magnetic fields, understand rotation operators for spin, and see why $2\pi$ rotation gives $-1$ (spinor property). You'll discover that spin-1/2 is the fundamental building block of quantum information—qubits are spin-1/2 particles.

## Spin-1/2 Hilbert Space

**Fundamental postulate**: Electron has intrinsic angular momentum **spin** $\mathbf{S}$ satisfying angular momentum algebra but with eigenvalues limited to $s = 1/2$.

**Hilbert space**: Two-dimensional, spanned by basis $\{|+\rangle, |-\rangle\}$ (eigenstates of $\hat{S}_z$).

### Example 1: Eigenvalues of Spin-1/2

From general angular momentum: $\hat{S}^2|s,m\rangle = \hbar^2 s(s+1)|s,m\rangle$ with $m = -s, \ldots, +s$.

For $s = 1/2$: $m = -1/2, +1/2$.

**Solution:**

$$\hat{S}^2|\pm\rangle = \hbar^2 \cdot \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}+1\right)|\pm\rangle = \frac{3}{4}\hbar^2|\pm\rangle$$

$$\hat{S}_z|\pm\rangle = \pm\frac{\hbar}{2}|\pm\rangle$$

**Magnitude**: $|\mathbf{S}| = \hbar\sqrt{s(s+1)} = \hbar\sqrt{\frac{3}{4}} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\hbar$

**$z$-component**: $S_z = \pm\frac{\hbar}{2}$

**Key**: Only **two** possible measurement outcomes for any spin component!

**Answer:** $\hat{S}^2$ eigenvalue $\frac{3\hbar^2}{4}$; $\hat{S}_z$ eigenvalues $\pm\frac{\hbar}{2}$

### Example 2: Spinor Notation

General spin state as two-component column vector.

**Solution:**

$$|\chi\rangle = \alpha|+\rangle + \beta|-\rangle$$

**Matrix representation** with basis:

$$|+\rangle = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}, \quad |-\rangle = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}$$

General spinor:

$$|\chi\rangle = \begin{pmatrix} \alpha \\ \beta \end{pmatrix}$$

where $|\alpha|^2 + |\beta|^2 = 1$ (normalization).

**Probability**: Measure $S_z$:
- $P(+\hbar/2) = |\alpha|^2$
- $P(-\hbar/2) = |\beta|^2$

**Answer:** Two-component spinor $\chi = \begin{pmatrix} \alpha \\ \beta \end{pmatrix}$ (complex 2D vector)

### Example 3: Pauli Matrices

Spin operators in units of $\hbar/2$: $\hat{S}_i = \frac{\hbar}{2}\sigma_i$

**Solution:**

**Pauli matrices**:

$$\sigma_x = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \quad \sigma_y = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -i \\ i & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \quad \sigma_z = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$$

**Properties**:
- Hermitian: $\sigma_i^\dagger = \sigma_i$
- Traceless: $\text{Tr}(\sigma_i) = 0$
- Eigenvalues: $\pm 1$ (all three)
- Determinant: $\det(\sigma_i) = -1$

**Identity**: $\sigma_0 = \mathbb{1} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$

**Answer:** Three Pauli matrices plus identity span all $2\times 2$ Hermitian matrices

## Pauli Matrix Algebra

### Example 4: Commutation Relations

Compute $[\sigma_x, \sigma_y]$.

**Solution:**

$$[\sigma_x, \sigma_y] = \sigma_x\sigma_y - \sigma_y\sigma_x$$

$$\sigma_x\sigma_y = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 0 & -i \\ i & 0 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} i & 0 \\ 0 & -i \end{pmatrix} = i\sigma_z$$

$$\sigma_y\sigma_x = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -i \\ i & 0 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -i & 0 \\ 0 & i \end{pmatrix} = -i\sigma_z$$

$$[\sigma_x, \sigma_y] = i\sigma_z - (-i\sigma_z) = 2i\sigma_z$$

**General**: $[\sigma_i, \sigma_j] = 2i\epsilon_{ijk}\sigma_k$

For spin operators: $[\hat{S}_i, \hat{S}_j] = i\hbar\epsilon_{ijk}\hat{S}_k$ ✓

**Answer:** $[\sigma_x, \sigma_y] = 2i\sigma_z$ (angular momentum algebra)

### Example 5: Anticommutation Relations

Compute $\{\sigma_x, \sigma_y\} = \sigma_x\sigma_y + \sigma_y\sigma_x$.

**Solution:**

From Example 4:

$$\sigma_x\sigma_y = i\sigma_z, \quad \sigma_y\sigma_x = -i\sigma_z$$

$$\{\sigma_x, \sigma_y\} = i\sigma_z + (-i\sigma_z) = 0$$

**General**: $\{\sigma_i, \sigma_j\} = 2\delta_{ij}\mathbb{1}$

For $i = j$: $\sigma_i^2 = \mathbb{1}$ (squares to identity!)

**Answer:** $\{\sigma_i, \sigma_j\} = 2\delta_{ij}$ (Clifford algebra)

### Example 6: Eigenvectors of $\sigma_x$

Find eigenstates of spin in $x$-direction.

**Solution:**

Eigenvalue equation: $\sigma_x|x\pm\rangle = \pm|x\pm\rangle$

$$\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} a \\ b \end{pmatrix} = \pm\begin{pmatrix} a \\ b \end{pmatrix}$$

For $+1$: $b = a$, so $|x+\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}$

For $-1$: $b = -a$, so $|x-\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix}$

**In $z$-basis**:

$$|x+\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|+\rangle + |-\rangle)$$
$$|x-\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|+\rangle - |-\rangle)$$

**Answer:** Equal superposition of up and down (with relative phase)

### Example 7: Measurement in Different Bases

Particle in state $|+\rangle$ (spin-up along $z$). Measure $S_x$. What are probabilities?

**Solution:**

Express $|+\rangle$ in $x$-basis:

$$|+\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}|x+\rangle + \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}|x-\rangle$$

(Check: $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} + \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}$ ✓)

**Probabilities**:
- $P(S_x = +\hbar/2) = |1/\sqrt{2}|^2 = 1/2$
- $P(S_x = -\hbar/2) = |1/\sqrt{2}|^2 = 1/2$

**Physical meaning**: Perpendicular measurements completely uncertain (50-50).

**Answer:** Equal probability $1/2$ for both outcomes

## Bloch Sphere Representation

### Example 8: Bloch Sphere Parametrization

Any pure state can be written:

$$|\psi\rangle = \cos(\theta/2)|+\rangle + e^{i\phi}\sin(\theta/2)|-\rangle$$

where $0 \leq \theta \leq \pi$, $0 \leq \phi < 2\pi$.

**Solution:**

This parametrizes unit sphere in 3D!

**Coordinates**: $(\theta, \phi)$ are spherical angles.

**North pole** $\theta = 0$: $|\psi\rangle = |+\rangle$

**South pole** $\theta = \pi$: $|\psi\rangle = e^{i\phi}|-\rangle \sim |-\rangle$ (global phase)

**Equator** $\theta = \pi/2$: Superpositions like $|x\pm\rangle, |y\pm\rangle$

**Expectation value**:

$$\langle\mathbf{S}\rangle = \frac{\hbar}{2}\hat{\mathbf{n}}$$

where $\hat{\mathbf{n}} = (\sin\theta\cos\phi, \sin\theta\sin\phi, \cos\theta)$ points to position on sphere.

**Answer:** Bloch sphere maps pure states to points on unit sphere

### Example 9: Measuring Spin Along Arbitrary Direction

For direction $\hat{\mathbf{n}}$, spin operator is:

$$\hat{S}_{\mathbf{n}} = \mathbf{S} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{n}} = \frac{\hbar}{2}(\boldsymbol{\sigma} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{n}})$$

Find eigenvalues.

**Solution:**

$$\boldsymbol{\sigma} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{n}} = n_x\sigma_x + n_y\sigma_y + n_z\sigma_z$$

For unit vector: $(\boldsymbol{\sigma} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{n}})^2 = \mathbb{1}$ (using Pauli algebra)

Eigenvalues of $\boldsymbol{\sigma} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{n}}$: $\pm 1$

Eigenvalues of $\hat{S}_{\mathbf{n}}$: $\pm\hbar/2$

**Universal**: Measuring spin along **any** direction gives only two outcomes $\pm\hbar/2$!

**Answer:** All spin measurements are binary (fundamental to spin-1/2)

## Spin Dynamics

### Example 10: Spin Precession in Magnetic Field

Hamiltonian: $\hat{H} = -\boldsymbol{\mu} \cdot \mathbf{B} = -\gamma \mathbf{S} \cdot \mathbf{B}$ where $\gamma$ is gyromagnetic ratio.

For $\mathbf{B} = B_0\hat{\mathbf{z}}$:

$$\hat{H} = -\gamma B_0 \hat{S}_z = -\frac{\gamma B_0\hbar}{2}\sigma_z$$

Find time evolution.

**Solution:**

Heisenberg equation: $\frac{d\hat{S}_i}{dt} = \frac{i}{\hbar}[\hat{H}, \hat{S}_i]$

$$\frac{d\hat{S}_x}{dt} = \frac{i}{\hbar}\left[-\gamma B_0 \hat{S}_z, \hat{S}_x\right] = \frac{i}{\hbar}(-\gamma B_0)(i\hbar\hat{S}_y) = \gamma B_0 \hat{S}_y$$

Similarly: $\frac{d\hat{S}_y}{dt} = -\gamma B_0 \hat{S}_x$, $\frac{d\hat{S}_z}{dt} = 0$

**Solution**: $\mathbf{S}$ precesses around $\mathbf{B}$ with **Larmor frequency** $\omega_L = \gamma B_0$!

**Answer:** Spin precesses around magnetic field (classical gyroscope analog)

### Example 11: Rotation Operators

Rotation by angle $\alpha$ around axis $\hat{\mathbf{n}}$:

$$\hat{R}_{\mathbf{n}}(\alpha) = e^{-i\alpha(\hat{\mathbf{n}} \cdot \boldsymbol{\sigma})/2}$$

**Solution:**

For rotation around $z$-axis:

$$\hat{R}_z(\alpha) = e^{-i\alpha\sigma_z/2} = \begin{pmatrix} e^{-i\alpha/2} & 0 \\ 0 & e^{+i\alpha/2} \end{pmatrix}$$

**$2\pi$ rotation**:

$$\hat{R}_z(2\pi) = \begin{pmatrix} e^{-i\pi} & 0 \\ 0 & e^{+i\pi} \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix} = -\mathbb{1}$$

**Spinor property**: $2\pi$ rotation gives $-1$, not $+1$!

Need $4\pi$ rotation to return to original state.

**Answer:** Spinors are double-valued under rotations (topological property)

### Example 12: Magnetic Resonance

Apply oscillating transverse field $B_1\cos(\omega t)$ perpendicular to static $B_0$.

**Solution:**

**Resonance condition**: $\omega = \omega_L = \gamma B_0$

When frequency matches Larmor precession, spin can flip from $|+\rangle$ to $|-\rangle$.

**Rabi oscillations**: Probability oscillates:

$$P_{+\to-}(t) = \sin^2\left(\frac{\omega_R t}{2}\right)$$

where $\omega_R = \gamma B_1$ is Rabi frequency.

**Applications**: NMR, ESR, MRI, quantum computing (qubit rotations).

**Answer:** Resonant driving flips spin (basis of magnetic resonance)

### Example 13: Stern-Gerlach Experiment

Silver atoms with spin-1/2 pass through inhomogeneous field $\frac{\partial B_z}{\partial z}$.

**Solution:**

Force on magnetic moment:

$$F_z = \mu_z \frac{\partial B_z}{\partial z} = \gamma S_z \frac{\partial B_z}{\partial z}$$

Since $S_z = \pm\hbar/2$:

$$F_z = \pm\frac{\gamma\hbar}{2}\frac{\partial B_z}{\partial z}$$

Beam splits into **two** components (not continuous smear).

**Discovery**: First direct evidence for space quantization (1922).

**Answer:** Discrete deflections prove quantization of angular momentum

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: B**
All three Pauli matrices have eigenvalues $\pm 1$
Spin operators $\hat{S}_i = \frac{\hbar}{2}\sigma_i$ have eigenvalues $\pm\hbar/2$
Hermitian, traceless $2\times 2$ matrices
$\det(\sigma_i) = -1$ (product of eigenvalues)
Binary measurement outcomes

**Problem 2: C**
$\{\sigma_i, \sigma_j\} = 2\delta_{ij}\mathbb{1}$
For $i = j$: $\sigma_i^2 = \mathbb{1}$
So $\{\sigma_x, \sigma_x\} = 2\sigma_x^2 = 2\mathbb{1}$
All Pauli matrices square to identity
Clifford algebra property

**Problem 3: C**
$|+\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|x+\rangle + |x-\rangle)$
Equal superposition in $x$-basis
$P(+\hbar/2) = P(-\hbar/2) = 1/2$
Perpendicular components maximally uncertain
Complementarity principle

**Problem 4: B**
Rotation operator: $\hat{R}_z(2\pi) = e^{-i\pi\sigma_z} = -\mathbb{1}$
Spinors are double-valued (minus sign)
Need $4\pi$ rotation to return to original
Topological property (not measurable directly)
Distinguishes spinors from vectors

**Problem 5: B**
$\omega_L = \gamma B_0$ (Larmor frequency)
Linear in magnetic field strength
$\gamma = gq/(2m)$ (gyromagnetic ratio)
For electron: $\omega_L \approx 2.8$ MHz/Gauss
NMR/ESR spectroscopy foundation
