# Quantum Mechanics Topic 3.5: 3D Harmonic Oscillator

The three-dimensional isotropic harmonic oscillator—potential $V(\mathbf{r}) = \frac{1}{2}m\omega^2 r^2$—is the 3D analog of the 1D oscillator that underlies so much of quantum mechanics. Unlike the hydrogen atom, the 3D oscillator separates in **both** Cartesian and spherical coordinates, giving two complementary descriptions of the same physics. This dual separability leads to **accidental degeneracy**: energy levels have degeneracy greater than the $(2l+1)$-fold expected from rotational symmetry. States with the same $n = 2n_r + l$ but different $(n_r, l)$ have identical energy.

The mathematics reveals beautiful structure. In Cartesian coordinates, the 3D oscillator factors into three independent 1D oscillators: $E = \hbar\omega(n_x + n_y + n_z + \frac{3}{2})$. The degeneracy $g_n = \frac{(n+1)(n+2)}{2}$ counts ways to partition $n$ into three non-negative integers. In spherical coordinates, separation gives energy $E = \hbar\omega(2n_r + l + \frac{3}{2})$ where the radial quantum number $n_r = 0, 1, 2, \ldots$ and orbital $l = 0, 1, 2, \ldots$ satisfy $n = 2n_r + l$. The zero-point energy $\frac{3}{2}\hbar\omega$ is three times the 1D value—one quantum per dimension.

This article explores the 3D oscillator completely. We'll solve in Cartesian coordinates to find energy levels and degeneracies, separate in spherical coordinates to obtain radial wavefunctions, understand the relationship between quantum numbers in the two descriptions, count states systematically to verify degeneracy formulas, and explore applications to nuclear shell model and quantum dots. You'll see how one potential admits multiple exact solutions—rare treasure in quantum mechanics.

## Cartesian Separation

**Potential**: $V(x, y, z) = \frac{1}{2}m\omega^2(x^2 + y^2 + z^2) = \frac{1}{2}m\omega^2 r^2$

**Schrödinger equation**:

$$-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\nabla^2\psi + \frac{1}{2}m\omega^2(x^2 + y^2 + z^2)\psi = E\psi$$

### Example 1: Separating in Cartesian Coordinates

Try $\psi(x,y,z) = X(x)Y(y)Z(z)$.

**Solution:**

Substitute and divide by $XYZ$:

$$-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\left(\frac{X''}{X} + \frac{Y''}{Y} + \frac{Z''}{Z}\right) + \frac{1}{2}m\omega^2(x^2 + y^2 + z^2) = E$$

Rearrange:

$$\left[-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{X''}{X} + \frac{1}{2}m\omega^2 x^2\right] + \left[-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{Y''}{Y} + \frac{1}{2}m\omega^2 y^2\right] + \left[-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{Z''}{Z} + \frac{1}{2}m\omega^2 z^2\right] = E$$

Each bracket depends on only one variable → each equals constant:

$$E_x + E_y + E_z = E$$

Three independent 1D harmonic oscillators!

**Answer:** Separable into three 1D oscillators with $E = E_x + E_y + E_z$

### Example 2: Energy Levels in Cartesian Basis

From 1D oscillator: $E_{n_i} = \hbar\omega(n_i + \frac{1}{2})$ for each direction.

**Solution:**

$$E_{n_x, n_y, n_z} = \hbar\omega\left(n_x + \frac{1}{2}\right) + \hbar\omega\left(n_y + \frac{1}{2}\right) + \hbar\omega\left(n_z + \frac{1}{2}\right)$$

$$= \hbar\omega\left(n_x + n_y + n_z + \frac{3}{2}\right)$$

Define total excitation: $n = n_x + n_y + n_z$ where $n_x, n_y, n_z = 0, 1, 2, \ldots$

$$E_n = \hbar\omega\left(n + \frac{3}{2}\right)$$ for $n = 0, 1, 2, 3, \ldots$

**Zero-point energy**: $E_0 = \frac{3}{2}\hbar\omega$ (three dimensions!)

**Answer:** $E_n = \hbar\omega(n + \frac{3}{2})$ with $n = n_x + n_y + n_z$

### Example 3: Degeneracy in Cartesian Basis

For given $n$, how many ways to partition into $(n_x, n_y, n_z)$?

**Solution:**

Need to count non-negative integer solutions to:

$$n_x + n_y + n_z = n$$

**Combinatorics**: "Stars and bars" method:

$$g_n = \binom{n + 2}{2} = \frac{(n+1)(n+2)}{2}$$

**Examples**:
- $n = 0$: $(0,0,0)$ → $g_0 = 1$ ✓
- $n = 1$: $(1,0,0), (0,1,0), (0,0,1)$ → $g_1 = 3$ ✓
- $n = 2$: $(2,0,0), (0,2,0), (0,0,2), (1,1,0), (1,0,1), (0,1,1)$ → $g_2 = 6$ ✓

**Answer:** Degeneracy $g_n = \frac{(n+1)(n+2)}{2}$ (grows quadratically)

## Spherical Separation

In spherical coordinates, the isotropic oscillator also separates.

### Example 4: Radial Equation for Oscillator

With $V(r) = \frac{1}{2}m\omega^2 r^2$, write radial equation.

**Solution:**

From general form with $u = rR$:

$$-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}u'' + \left[\frac{1}{2}m\omega^2 r^2 + \frac{\hbar^2 l(l+1)}{2mr^2}\right]u = Eu$$

**Asymptotic behavior**:
- $r \to 0$: $u \sim r^{l+1}$ (centrifugal)
- $r \to \infty$: $u \sim e^{-\alpha r^2}$ where $\alpha = \frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}$ (Gaussian envelope)

**Ansatz**: $u(r) = r^{l+1}e^{-\alpha r^2}F(r)$ where $F$ is polynomial.

**Answer:** Radial equation with oscillator potential plus centrifugal barrier

### Example 5: Energy Quantization in Spherical Basis

Series solution gives condition (detailed derivation omitted):

**Solution:**

Energy levels:

$$E_{n_r, l} = \hbar\omega(2n_r + l + \frac{3}{2})$$

where $n_r = 0, 1, 2, 3, \ldots$ (radial quantum number) and $l = 0, 1, 2, 3, \ldots$

**Relation to Cartesian $n$**:

$$n = 2n_r + l$$

For given $n$:
- $l$ must have same parity as $n$ (both even or both odd)
- $l = n, n-2, n-4, \ldots, 1$ or $0$

**Answer:** $E_{n_r,l} = \hbar\omega(2n_r + l + \frac{3}{2})$ with $n = 2n_r + l$

### Example 6: Ground State

For $n = 0$, what are $(n_r, l, m)$?

**Solution:**

$$0 = 2n_r + l$$

Only solution: $n_r = 0, l = 0$ (and $m = 0$)

$$E_0 = \hbar\omega\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)$$

**Wavefunction**: $\psi_{000} \propto e^{-m\omega r^2/2\hbar}$ (spherically symmetric Gaussian)

**Degeneracy**: $g_0 = 1$ ✓ (matches Cartesian)

**Answer:** Unique ground state $(n_r, l, m) = (0, 0, 0)$ with $E_0 = \frac{3}{2}\hbar\omega$

### Example 7: First Excited Level

For $n = 1$, enumerate states.

**Solution:**

$$1 = 2n_r + l$$

**Spherical**: $l$ must be odd.
- $n_r = 0, l = 1$: gives $m = -1, 0, +1$ (3 states)

**Cartesian**: $(n_x, n_y, n_z)$ with sum $= 1$:
- $(1, 0, 0), (0, 1, 0), (0, 0, 1)$ (3 states) ✓

Both give $g_1 = 3$.

**Wavefunctions**: Spherical $p$-orbitals $\leftrightarrow$ Cartesian excited oscillators.

**Answer:** $n=1$ has $(2l+1) = 3$ states from $l=1$ (same in both bases)

### Example 8: Second Excited Level and Accidental Degeneracy

For $n = 2$, show degeneracy exceeds $(2l+1)$.

**Solution:**

$$2 = 2n_r + l$$

**Spherical**: $l$ must be even.
- $n_r = 0, l = 2$: gives $m = -2, -1, 0, +1, +2$ (5 states)
- $n_r = 1, l = 0$: gives $m = 0$ (1 state)

Total: $5 + 1 = 6$ states

**Cartesian**: $g_2 = \frac{3 \cdot 4}{2} = 6$ ✓

**Accidental degeneracy**: Different $(n_r, l)$ pairs give same energy!

Not just rotational $(2l+1)$ degeneracy.

**Answer:** $g_2 = 6$ from two $(n_r, l)$ values (accidental degeneracy)

## Correspondence Between Bases

### Example 9: General Degeneracy Formula

Verify Cartesian and spherical counting agree.

**Solution:**

**Cartesian**: $g_n = \frac{(n+1)(n+2)}{2}$

**Spherical**: Sum over allowed $l$ values.

For even $n$: $l = 0, 2, 4, \ldots, n$ (each with $n_r = (n-l)/2$)

$$g_n = \sum_{l \text{ even}}^{n}(2l+1) = 1 + 5 + 9 + \cdots$$

For odd $n$: $l = 1, 3, 5, \ldots, n$

$$g_n = \sum_{l \text{ odd}}^{n}(2l+1) = 3 + 7 + 11 + \cdots$$

Summing arithmetic series:

Both give $g_n = \frac{(n+1)(n+2)}{2}$ ✓

**Answer:** Cartesian and spherical degeneracies match (consistency check)

### Example 10: Parity

States have definite parity $(-1)^n$.

**Solution:**

**Cartesian**: $\psi \propto x^{n_x}y^{n_y}z^{n_z} \times \text{Gaussian}$

Under inversion $(x,y,z) \to (-x,-y,-z)$:

$$\psi \to (-1)^{n_x + n_y + n_z}\psi = (-1)^n\psi$$

**Spherical**: $\psi \propto r^l Y_l^m \times \text{Gaussian}$

Parity of $Y_l^m$ is $(-1)^l$, and $r^l$ has parity $(-1)^l$:

$$\text{Parity} = (-1)^l = (-1)^{n - 2n_r} = (-1)^n$$

(since $2n_r$ is even)

**Answer:** All states with same $n$ have parity $(-1)^n$ (good quantum number)

## Applications

### Example 11: Nuclear Shell Model

3D oscillator approximates nuclear potential.

**Solution:**

Nucleons in nucleus experience approximately harmonic potential from mean field.

**Magic numbers** (closed shells): $2, 8, 20, 40, 70, 112, \ldots$

Cumulative degeneracies:

$$N_n = \sum_{k=0}^{n}g_k = \frac{(n+1)(n+2)(n+3)}{6}$$

Including spin (2-fold): double all numbers.

**Example**: $n=0$ shell holds 2 nucleons, $n \leq 1$ holds $2(1+3) = 8$, etc.

Explains stability of certain nuclei.

**Answer:** Oscillator shells organize nuclear structure (simplified model)

### Example 12: Quantum Dots

Electrons in parabolic confining potential.

**Solution:**

2D semiconductor quantum dot: $V(x,y) = \frac{1}{2}m^*\omega^2(x^2 + y^2)$

Energy levels: $E_n = \hbar\omega(n + 1)$ where $n = n_x + n_y$

Degeneracy: $g_n = n + 1$ (2D version)

**Filling shell structure**: Explains conductance quantization.

**Fock-Darwin states**: Include magnetic field (modified oscillator).

**Answer:** Oscillator model describes artificial atoms (quantum dots)

### Example 13: Comparison to Hydrogen

Why do oscillator and hydrogen have different degeneracies?

**Solution:**

**Hydrogen**: $g_n = n^2$ (accidental from SO(4) symmetry)

Energy depends only on $n$, not on $l$.

**Oscillator**: $g_n = \frac{(n+1)(n+2)}{2}$ (accidental from SU(3) symmetry)

Energy depends on $n = 2n_r + l$, not separately.

**Both**: Accidental degeneracies from hidden symmetries (beyond SO(3) rotation).

**Hydrogen**: Kepler problem → Runge-Lenz vector → SO(4)

**Oscillator**: $n = 2n_r + l$ constraint → SU(3)

**Answer:** Different symmetries give different degeneracy patterns

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: C**
$E_0 = \hbar\omega(0 + \frac{3}{2}) = \frac{3}{2}\hbar\omega$
Sum of three 1D zero-point energies
One quantum per dimension
Minimum energy from uncertainty principle
Cannot be zero (confinement)

**Problem 2: C**
$g_3 = \frac{(3+1)(3+2)}{2} = \frac{4 \cdot 5}{2} = 10$
Cartesian: $(3,0,0)$ permutations + $(2,1,0)$ permutations + $(1,1,1)$
Spherical: $l=3$ (7 states) + $l=1$ (3 states) = 10 ✓
Quadratic growth in degeneracy

**Problem 3: C**
$n = 2n_r + l$ with $n=2$
Same parity as $n$ (both even)
$l = 2$ (with $n_r=0$) or $l=0$ (with $n_r=1$)
Not $l=1$ (would give odd $n$)
Two $(n_r, l)$ combinations

**Problem 4: A**
$E = \hbar\omega(n_x + n_y + n_z + \frac{3}{2})$
Linear in sum of quantum numbers
Not quadratic (unlike free particle)
Separable Cartesian problem
Defines total excitation $n$

**Problem 5: C**
Degeneracy $> (2l+1)$ from rotation alone
Different $(n_r, l)$ give same $n = 2n_r + l$
Hidden symmetry (SU(3) for oscillator)
Compare hydrogen: SO(4) symmetry
Rare in quantum systems
