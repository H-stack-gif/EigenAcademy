# Quantum Mechanics Topic 4.2: Addition of Angular Momentum and Clebsch-Gordan

A hydrogen atom has orbital angular momentum $\mathbf{L}$ from the electron's motion and spin angular momentum $\mathbf{S}$ from its intrinsic spin. What is the total angular momentum $\mathbf{J} = \mathbf{L} + \mathbf{S}$? Classical vector addition seems straightforward, but quantum mechanics imposes strict rules. The **triangle inequality** $|j_1 - j_2| \leq j \leq j_1 + j_2$ limits which total angular momenta can exist. Two spin-1/2 particles ($j_1 = j_2 = 1/2$) combine to give either $j = 1$ (triplet) or $j = 0$ (singlet), not a continuum.

The mathematical framework transforms between two bases. The **uncoupled basis** $|j_1, m_1\rangle|j_2, m_2\rangle$ treats subsystems independently. The **coupled basis** $|j, m\rangle$ describes the combined system. **Clebsch-Gordan coefficients** $\langle j_1, j_2; m_1, m_2|j, m\rangle$ perform this change of basis, encoding how individual angular momenta combine. These coefficients determine selection rules for atomic transitions, multiplet structure in spectroscopy, and coupling schemes in nuclear physics.

This article develops angular momentum addition systematically. We'll derive the triangle inequality from commutators, construct coupled states from uncoupled ones, compute Clebsch-Gordan coefficients for simple cases, understand singlet and triplet states of two spin-1/2 particles, apply selection rules to spectroscopic transitions, and introduce the Wigner-Eckart theorem that factorizes matrix elements. You'll see that adding angular momenta is highly constrained—quantum addition follows geometric rules.

## Total Angular Momentum

**Definition**: For two angular momenta $\hat{\mathbf{J}}_1$ and $\hat{\mathbf{J}}_2$:

$$\hat{\mathbf{J}} = \hat{\mathbf{J}}_1 + \hat{\mathbf{J}}_2$$

Components add: $\hat{J}_z = \hat{J}_{1z} + \hat{J}_{2z}$

### Example 1: Commutation Relations

Show $[\hat{J}^2, \hat{J}_{1z}] \neq 0$ but $[\hat{J}^2, \hat{J}_z] = 0$.

**Solution:**

$$\hat{J}^2 = (\hat{\mathbf{J}}_1 + \hat{\mathbf{J}}_2)^2 = \hat{J}_1^2 + \hat{J}_2^2 + 2\hat{\mathbf{J}}_1 \cdot \hat{\mathbf{J}}_2$$

$$[\hat{J}^2, \hat{J}_{1z}] = [2\hat{\mathbf{J}}_1 \cdot \hat{\mathbf{J}}_2, \hat{J}_{1z}]$$

Since $\hat{\mathbf{J}}_1 \cdot \hat{\mathbf{J}}_2 = \hat{J}_{1x}\hat{J}_{2x} + \hat{J}_{1y}\hat{J}_{2y} + \hat{J}_{1z}\hat{J}_{2z}$:

$$[\hat{J}_{1x}\hat{J}_{2x}, \hat{J}_{1z}] = \hat{J}_{2x}[\hat{J}_{1x}, \hat{J}_{1z}] = i\hbar\hat{J}_{2x}\hat{J}_{1y} \neq 0$$

So $[\hat{J}^2, \hat{J}_{1z}] \neq 0$ (not compatible!)

But:
$$[\hat{J}^2, \hat{J}_z] = [\hat{J}^2, \hat{J}_{1z} + \hat{J}_{2z}] = 0$$

(Total $\hat{J}^2$ and $\hat{J}_z$ are compatible)

**Answer:** Cannot simultaneously diagonalize $\hat{J}^2$ and $\hat{J}_{1z}$ (must choose basis)

### Example 2: Two Bases

**Uncoupled basis**: $|j_1, m_1\rangle|j_2, m_2\rangle$ (or $|j_1, j_2; m_1, m_2\rangle$)

Eigenstates of: $\hat{J}_1^2, \hat{J}_{1z}, \hat{J}_2^2, \hat{J}_{2z}$

**Coupled basis**: $|j, m\rangle$ (or $|j_1, j_2; j, m\rangle$)

Eigenstates of: $\hat{J}_1^2, \hat{J}_2^2, \hat{J}^2, \hat{J}_z$

**Solution:**

Both are complete orthonormal bases for same Hilbert space.

**Dimension**: $(2j_1 + 1)(2j_2 + 1)$ states in each basis.

**Change of basis**: Linear transformation (unitary).

**Answer:** Two equally valid descriptions of composite system

### Example 3: Triangle Inequality

For given $j_1, j_2$, allowed values of $j$ satisfy:

$$|j_1 - j_2| \leq j \leq j_1 + j_2$$

and $j$ differs from $j_1 + j_2$ by integers.

**Solution:**

Maximum $m = m_1 + m_2$ occurs when both maximal:

$$m_{\max} = j_1 + j_2$$

This corresponds to $j_{\max} = j_1 + j_2$.

Ladder operators lower $j$ by integer steps:

$$j = j_1 + j_2, \, j_1 + j_2 - 1, \, j_1 + j_2 - 2, \, \ldots$$

Minimum occurs at $j_{\min} = |j_1 - j_2|$ (dimension constraint).

**Example**: $j_1 = 1, j_2 = 1/2$ gives $j = 3/2, 1/2$.

**Answer:** Quantum "vector addition" obeys geometric triangle rule

## Two Spin-1/2 Particles

### Example 4: Uncoupled States

Two electrons with spins $\mathbf{S}_1$ and $\mathbf{S}_2$ (both spin-1/2).

**Solution:**

**Uncoupled basis**: Four states

$$|++\rangle, \quad |+-\rangle, \quad |-+\rangle, \quad |--\rangle$$

(First symbol: particle 1, second: particle 2)

Each eigenstate of $\hat{S}_{1z}$ and $\hat{S}_{2z}$ with eigenvalues $\pm\hbar/2$.

**Total $S_z$**: $m\hbar$ where $m = m_1 + m_2$:
- $|++\rangle$: $m = 1$
- $|+-\rangle, |-+\rangle$: $m = 0$
- $|--\rangle$: $m = -1$

**Answer:** Four uncoupled basis states from two spin-1/2 particles

### Example 5: Coupled States (Triplet)

For $s = 1$ (triplet), construct $|s=1, m\rangle$ states.

**Solution:**

**$m = 1$**: Only $|++\rangle$ has $m_1 + m_2 = 1$:

$$|1, 1\rangle = |++\rangle$$

**$m = 0$**: Symmetric combination of $|+-\rangle$ and $|-+\rangle$:

$$|1, 0\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|+-\rangle + |-+\rangle)$$

**$m = -1$**: Only $|--\rangle$ has $m_1 + m_2 = -1$:

$$|1, -1\rangle = |--\rangle$$

**Symmetry**: All triplet states symmetric under particle exchange.

**Answer:** Triplet (symmetric) with three states ($2s+1 = 3$)

### Example 6: Coupled State (Singlet)

For $s = 0$ (singlet), construct $|0, 0\rangle$.

**Solution:**

Must have $m = 0$ and be orthogonal to $|1, 0\rangle$.

**Antisymmetric combination**:

$$|0, 0\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|+-\rangle - |-+\rangle)$$

**Verification**: 

$$\langle 1, 0|0, 0\rangle = \frac{1}{2}[(+)(-) + (-)( +)] = 0$$ ✓

**Total spin**: $\hat{S}^2|0,0\rangle = 0$ (no angular momentum!)

**Symmetry**: Antisymmetric under exchange.

**Answer:** Singlet (antisymmetric) with one state

### Example 7: Dimension Check

Verify uncoupled and coupled bases have same dimension.

**Solution:**

**Uncoupled**: $(2s_1 + 1)(2s_2 + 1) = (2)(2) = 4$ states

**Coupled**: 

For $j_1 = 1/2, j_2 = 1/2$: $j = 0, 1$

Triplet ($j=1$): 3 states
Singlet ($j=0$): 1 state

Total: $3 + 1 = 4$ ✓

**General formula**:

$$\sum_{j=|j_1-j_2|}^{j_1+j_2}(2j+1) = (2j_1+1)(2j_2+1)$$

**Answer:** Both bases span same 4D space (completeness)

## Clebsch-Gordan Coefficients

**Definition**: Expansion coefficients between bases:

$$|j_1, j_2; m_1, m_2\rangle = \sum_{j,m}\langle j_1, j_2; j, m|j_1, j_2; m_1, m_2\rangle|j_1, j_2; j, m\rangle$$

or inversely:

$$|j_1, j_2; j, m\rangle = \sum_{m_1, m_2}\langle j_1, j_2; m_1, m_2|j_1, j_2; j, m\rangle|j_1, j_2; m_1, m_2\rangle$$

### Example 8: Selection Rules for CG Coefficients

When are Clebsch-Gordan coefficients non-zero?

**Solution:**

$$\langle j_1, j_2; m_1, m_2|j, m\rangle \neq 0$$ only if:

1. **$m$ conservation**: $m = m_1 + m_2$
2. **Triangle inequality**: $|j_1 - j_2| \leq j \leq j_1 + j_2$
3. **Parity**: $j + j_1 + j_2 = \text{integer}$ (automatically satisfied for orbital AM)

Otherwise coefficient is zero.

**Answer:** Strict selection rules from angular momentum conservation

### Example 9: Example CG Coefficient

For $j_1 = 1, j_2 = 1/2, j = 3/2, m = 1/2$, find $\langle 1, 1/2; m_1, m_2|3/2, 1/2\rangle$.

**Solution:**

Need $m_1 + m_2 = 1/2$:

Options: $(m_1, m_2) = (1, -1/2)$ or $(0, +1/2)$

From tables or calculation:

$$|3/2, 1/2\rangle = \sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}|1, -1/2\rangle + \sqrt{\frac{1}{3}}|0, 1/2\rangle$$

So:
- $\langle 1, 1/2; 1, -1/2|3/2, 1/2\rangle = \sqrt{2/3}$
- $\langle 1, 1/2; 0, 1/2|3/2, 1/2\rangle = \sqrt{1/3}$

**Answer:** Two non-zero coefficients from two possible $(m_1, m_2)$ pairs

### Example 10: Orbital + Spin (Hydrogen Fine Structure)

For $l = 1$ (p-orbital) and $s = 1/2$, what are total $j$ values?

**Solution:**

Triangle inequality: $|1 - 1/2| \leq j \leq 1 + 1/2$

$$j = 1/2 \text{ or } 3/2$$

**States**:
- $j = 3/2$: 4 states ($m_j = -3/2, -1/2, 1/2, 3/2$)
- $j = 1/2$: 2 states ($m_j = -1/2, 1/2$)

Total: $4 + 2 = 6 = (2l+1)(2s+1) = 3 \times 2$ ✓

**Spectroscopic notation**: $^2P_{3/2}$ and $^2P_{1/2}$ (fine structure doublet)

**Answer:** $p$-electron splits into $j = 1/2$ and $j = 3/2$ levels

## Selection Rules and Transitions

### Example 11: Electric Dipole Selection Rules

Electric dipole operator $\mathbf{r}$ carries angular momentum $l = 1$.

**Solution:**

Transition amplitude: $\langle n', l', j'|\mathbf{r}|n, l, j\rangle$

Adding photon AM ($l_{\gamma} = 1$) to initial $j$:

$$|j - 1| \leq j' \leq j + 1$$

$$\Delta j = 0, \pm 1$$ (but $j = 0 \not\to j' = 0$)

Also: $\Delta l = \pm 1$ (parity flip)

**Example**: $2P_{3/2} \to 1S_{1/2}$ allowed ($\Delta j = -1$, $\Delta l = -1$)

**Answer:** Selection rules from angular momentum conservation

### Example 12: Forbidden Transitions

Why is $1S_{1/2} \to 2S_{1/2}$ forbidden for electric dipole?

**Solution:**

Initial: $l = 0, j = 1/2$

Final: $l' = 0, j' = 1/2$

**Electric dipole**: Requires $\Delta l = \pm 1$ (parity change)

But $\Delta l = 0$ here! Forbidden.

**Transition possible via**:
- Two-photon processes
- Magnetic dipole ($\Delta l = 0$, weaker)
- Collisional excitation

**Answer:** Selection rules forbid certain transitions (metastable states)

### Example 13: Wigner-Eckart Theorem

Matrix elements of vector operator $\hat{V}_q$ ($q = -1, 0, +1$ components):

$$\langle j', m'|\hat{V}_q|j, m\rangle = \langle j, 1; m, q|j', m'\rangle \frac{\langle j'||\hat{V}||j\rangle}{\sqrt{2j'+1}}$$

**Solution:**

**Factorization**:
- CG coefficient: Angular dependence
- Reduced matrix element $\langle j'||\hat{V}||j\rangle$: Radial/internal dynamics

**Power**: All $m, m', q$ dependence isolated in CG coefficient!

Measure one transition → predict all others in multiplet.

**Answer:** Separates geometry (CG) from dynamics (reduced element)

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: C**
$j_1 = j_2 = 1/2$ gives $|1/2 - 1/2| \leq j \leq 1/2 + 1/2$
So $0 \leq j \leq 1$: $j = 0, 1$
Singlet ($s=0$, 1 state) + Triplet ($s=1$, 3 states)
Total 4 states from 2×2 uncoupled
Antisymmetric (singlet) vs symmetric (triplet)

**Problem 2: A**
$|2 - 1| \leq j \leq 2 + 1$ gives $1 \leq j \leq 3$
Integer steps: $j = 1, 2, 3$
Dimensions: $3 + 5 + 7 = 15 = 5 \times 3$ ✓
Not $j=0$ (outside range)
Not $j=4$ (exceeds maximum)

**Problem 3: B**
$|0,0\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|+-\rangle - |-+\rangle)$
Antisymmetric under particle exchange
Total spin zero (no angular momentum)
Pauli exclusion: fermions favor antisymmetric
Basis for entanglement (EPR pairs)

**Problem 4: C**
Two necessary conditions:
1. $m = m_1 + m_2$ ($z$-component conservation)
2. Triangle inequality for $j$
Both must be satisfied
Otherwise CG coefficient vanishes
Reduces table size significantly

**Problem 5: C**
$\Delta j = 0, \pm 1$ from photon angular momentum
Exception: $j = 0 \not\to j' = 0$ forbidden
Also need $\Delta l = \pm 1$ (parity)
Combined with $\Delta m = 0, \pm 1$ (polarization)
Higher multipoles (quadrupole, etc.) have different rules
