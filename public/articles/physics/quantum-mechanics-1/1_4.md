# Quantum Mechanics Topic 1.4: Dirac Notation and Matrix Mechanics

Schrödinger's wave mechanics uses differential equations and wavefunctions $\psi(x)$. Heisenberg's matrix mechanics uses matrices and commutators. Born and Jordan proved these are equivalent—just different representations of the same underlying structure. **Dirac notation** unifies both approaches in an elegant, coordinate-free formalism. The ket $|\psi\rangle$ is an abstract state vector living in Hilbert space. The bra $\langle\phi|$ is its dual. Together they form the inner product $\langle\phi|\psi\rangle$, and the outer product $|\psi\rangle\langle\phi|$ builds operators.

This abstract formalism isn't mathematical pedantry—it's incredibly powerful. Dirac notation makes calculations cleaner, reveals structure hidden in differential equations, and generalizes effortlessly to spin and multiple particles. The **completeness relation** $\sum_n|\psi_n\rangle\langle\psi_n| = \mathbb{1}$ lets us insert identity operators anywhere, switching representations at will. The **spectral decomposition** $\hat{A} = \sum_n a_n|\psi_n\rangle\langle\psi_n|$ expresses any operator in terms of its eigenstates. The **trace** $\text{Tr}(\hat{A}) = \sum_n\langle\psi_n|\hat{A}|\psi_n\rangle$ gives basis-independent quantities.

This article develops Dirac notation systematically. We'll master bra-ket manipulations, use completeness to derive resolutions of identity, construct operators from outer products, perform basis transformations via unitary matrices, and compute matrix elements in different representations. You'll see that wave mechanics and matrix mechanics are just different coordinate systems on the same abstract Hilbert space.

## Bra-Ket Notation Basics

**Ket**: $|\psi\rangle$ (column vector, state)

**Bra**: $\langle\phi|$ (row vector, dual state)

**Inner product**: $\langle\phi|\psi\rangle$ (complex number)

**Outer product**: $|\psi\rangle\langle\phi|$ (operator)

### Example 1: Inner Product Properties

If $|\psi\rangle = 3|0\rangle + 4i|1\rangle$ and $|\phi\rangle = 2|0\rangle - i|1\rangle$ (orthonormal basis $\{|0\rangle, |1\rangle\}$), compute $\langle\phi|\psi\rangle$.

**Solution:**

$$\langle\phi| = 2^*\langle 0| + (-i)^*\langle 1| = 2\langle 0| + i\langle 1|$$

$$\langle\phi|\psi\rangle = (2\langle 0| + i\langle 1|)(3|0\rangle + 4i|1\rangle)$$

Using orthonormality $\langle 0|0\rangle = \langle 1|1\rangle = 1$, $\langle 0|1\rangle = 0$:

$$= 2 \cdot 3 + i \cdot 4i = 6 - 4 = 2$$

**Answer:** $\langle\phi|\psi\rangle = 2$

### Example 2: Normalization Check

For $|\psi\rangle = a|0\rangle + b|1\rangle$, find condition for normalization.

**Solution:**

$$\langle\psi|\psi\rangle = (a^*\langle 0| + b^*\langle 1|)(a|0\rangle + b|1\rangle)$$

$$= |a|^2\langle 0|0\rangle + a^*b\langle 0|1\rangle + b^*a\langle 1|0\rangle + |b|^2\langle 1|1\rangle$$

$$= |a|^2 + |b|^2$$

For normalization: $\langle\psi|\psi\rangle = 1$

**Answer:** $|a|^2 + |b|^2 = 1$ (probability conservation)

### Example 3: Outer Product as Operator

The outer product $|0\rangle\langle 1|$ acts on states. Compute $(|0\rangle\langle 1|)|1\rangle$.

**Solution:**

$$|0\rangle\langle 1|1\rangle = |0\rangle \cdot (\langle 1|1\rangle) = |0\rangle \cdot 1 = |0\rangle$$

**Interpretation**: Projects $|1\rangle$ onto direction $|0\rangle$.

Also compute: $(|0\rangle\langle 1|)|0\rangle = |0\rangle\langle 1|0\rangle = |0\rangle \cdot 0 = 0$

**Answer:** Outer products are projection/transition operators

## Completeness and Resolution of Identity

**Completeness relation** for orthonormal basis $\{|\psi_n\rangle\}$:

$$\sum_n |\psi_n\rangle\langle\psi_n| = \mathbb{1}$$

This is the **resolution of identity**—incredibly useful for changing bases and inserting identities.

### Example 4: Expanding in Complete Basis

Express $|\psi\rangle$ in basis $\{|n\rangle\}$ using completeness.

**Solution:**

$$|\psi\rangle = \mathbb{1}|\psi\rangle = \left(\sum_n|n\rangle\langle n|\right)|\psi\rangle = \sum_n |n\rangle\langle n|\psi\rangle$$

$$= \sum_n c_n|n\rangle$$ where $c_n = \langle n|\psi\rangle$

**Answer:** Completeness gives expansion coefficients automatically

### Example 5: Position Representation from Completeness

Starting with abstract $|\psi\rangle$, derive wavefunction $\psi(x)$.

**Solution:**

$$|\psi\rangle = \int|x\rangle\langle x|\psi\rangle dx$$

Project onto $\langle x'|$:

$$\langle x'|\psi\rangle = \int\langle x'|x\rangle\langle x|\psi\rangle dx = \int\delta(x' - x)\langle x|\psi\rangle dx$$

$$= \langle x'|\psi\rangle \equiv \psi(x')$$

**Answer:** Wavefunction emerges from projection onto position basis

### Example 6: Changing Representations

Express momentum wavefunction $\tilde{\psi}(p)$ in terms of position wavefunction $\psi(x)$.

**Solution:**

$$\tilde{\psi}(p) = \langle p|\psi\rangle = \langle p|\left(\int|x\rangle\langle x|dx\right)|\psi\rangle$$

$$= \int\langle p|x\rangle\langle x|\psi\rangle dx = \int\langle p|x\rangle\psi(x) dx$$

From $\langle p|x\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\hbar}}e^{-ipx/\hbar}$:

$$\tilde{\psi}(p) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\hbar}}\int e^{-ipx/\hbar}\psi(x) dx$$

**Answer:** Fourier transform via completeness relation

## Matrix Representation of Operators

In discrete basis $\{|n\rangle\}$, operator $\hat{A}$ becomes matrix:

$$A_{mn} = \langle m|\hat{A}|n\rangle$$ (matrix element)

### Example 7: Computing Matrix Elements

For harmonic oscillator with $\hat{a}_\pm|n\rangle = \sqrt{n+1}|n+1\rangle$ (raising) or $\sqrt{n}|n-1\rangle$ (lowering):

Find matrix elements of $\hat{a}_+$.

**Solution:**

$$(a_+)_{mn} = \langle m|\hat{a}_+|n\rangle = \langle m|\sqrt{n+1}|n+1\rangle$$

$$= \sqrt{n+1}\langle m|n+1\rangle = \sqrt{n+1}\delta_{m,n+1}$$

Non-zero only when $m = n + 1$:

$$\hat{a}_+ = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & \cdots \\ \sqrt{1} & 0 & 0 & 0 & \cdots \\ 0 & \sqrt{2} & 0 & 0 & \cdots \\ 0 & 0 & \sqrt{3} & 0 & \cdots \\ \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \ddots \end{pmatrix}$$

**Answer:** Upper diagonal matrix (raises $n \to n+1$)

### Example 8: Position Operator Matrix

For harmonic oscillator, $\hat{x} = \sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}}(\hat{a}_+ + \hat{a}_-)$.

Find matrix elements $x_{mn}$.

**Solution:**

$$x_{mn} = \sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}}\langle m|\hat{a}_+ + \hat{a}_-|n\rangle$$

$$= \sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}}\left[\sqrt{n+1}\delta_{m,n+1} + \sqrt{n}\delta_{m,n-1}\right]$$

Non-zero only on adjacent diagonals (tri-diagonal matrix).

**Answer:** Connects neighboring energy levels only

### Example 9: Spectral Decomposition

Any Hermitian operator can be written:

$$\hat{A} = \sum_n a_n|\psi_n\rangle\langle\psi_n|$$

where $\hat{A}|\psi_n\rangle = a_n|\psi_n\rangle$ (eigenstates/eigenvalues).

Verify this gives correct action on eigenstates.

**Solution:**

$$\hat{A}|\psi_m\rangle = \sum_n a_n|\psi_n\rangle\langle\psi_n|\psi_m\rangle = \sum_n a_n|\psi_n\rangle\delta_{nm}$$

$$= a_m|\psi_m\rangle$$ ✓

**Answer:** Spectral decomposition reproduces eigenvalue equation

## Projection Operators

**Projection** onto eigenstate $|n\rangle$:

$$\hat{P}_n = |n\rangle\langle n|$$

Properties: $\hat{P}_n^2 = \hat{P}_n$ (idempotent), $\hat{P}_n^\dagger = \hat{P}_n$ (Hermitian)

### Example 10: Measurement as Projection

Measuring observable $\hat{A}$ and obtaining $a_n$ projects state:

$$|\psi\rangle \to \frac{\hat{P}_n|\psi\rangle}{\sqrt{\langle\psi|\hat{P}_n|\psi\rangle}} = \frac{|n\rangle\langle n|\psi\rangle}{|\langle n|\psi\rangle|}$$

Simplifies to: $|\psi\rangle \to |n\rangle$

Verify probability: $P(a_n) = \langle\psi|\hat{P}_n|\psi\rangle$.

**Solution:**

$$P(a_n) = \langle\psi|n\rangle\langle n|\psi\rangle = |\langle n|\psi\rangle|^2$$ ✓

(Born rule)

**Answer:** Projection operators implement measurement postulate

### Example 11: Completeness from Projections

Show $\sum_n\hat{P}_n = \mathbb{1}$ (sum of projections is identity).

**Solution:**

$$\sum_n\hat{P}_n = \sum_n|n\rangle\langle n| = \mathbb{1}$$

This is completeness relation!

**Physical meaning**: Sum over all possible measurement outcomes gives unity.

**Answer:** Probabilities sum to 1: $\sum_n P(a_n) = \sum_n|\langle n|\psi\rangle|^2 = \langle\psi|\psi\rangle = 1$

## Basis Transformations

Change of basis: $|n\rangle \to |n'\rangle$ via unitary matrix $U_{n'n} = \langle n'|n\rangle$

**Operator transformation**: $\hat{A}' = \hat{U}\hat{A}\hat{U}^\dagger$

**State transformation**: $|\psi'\rangle = \hat{U}|\psi\rangle$

### Example 12: Rotation in Spin Space

For spin-1/2, basis $\{|z+\rangle, |z-\rangle\}$ (eigenstates of $\hat{S}_z$).

Rotate to $\{|x+\rangle, |x-\rangle\}$ (eigenstates of $\hat{S}_x$).

Transformation: $|x\pm\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|z+\rangle \pm |z-\rangle)$

Write unitary matrix $\hat{U}$.

**Solution:**

$$\hat{U} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$$

Verify unitary: $\hat{U}^\dagger\hat{U} = \frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & -1 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & -1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ ✓

**Answer:** Hadamard-like transformation between spin bases

### Example 13: Trace Invariance

Show $\text{Tr}(\hat{A})$ is basis-independent.

**Solution:**

In basis $\{|n\rangle\}$: $\text{Tr}(\hat{A}) = \sum_n\langle n|\hat{A}|n\rangle$

In new basis $\{|n'\rangle\}$:

$$\text{Tr}(\hat{A}) = \sum_{n'}\langle n'|\hat{A}|n'\rangle = \sum_{n'}\langle n'|\left(\sum_n|n\rangle\langle n|\right)\hat{A}\left(\sum_m|m\rangle\langle m|\right)|n'\rangle$$

$$= \sum_{n',n,m}\langle n'|n\rangle\langle n|\hat{A}|m\rangle\langle m|n'\rangle$$

$$= \sum_{n,m}\langle n|\hat{A}|m\rangle\left(\sum_{n'}\langle m|n'\rangle\langle n'|n\rangle\right)$$

$$= \sum_{n,m}\langle n|\hat{A}|m\rangle\delta_{mn} = \sum_n\langle n|\hat{A}|n\rangle$$ ✓

**Answer:** Trace invariant under unitary transformations (physical observable)

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: D**
$|\psi\rangle\langle\phi|$ is operator (maps kets to kets)
Acts as: $(|\psi\rangle\langle\phi|)|χ\rangle = |\psi\rangle(\langle\phi|χ\rangle)$
Inner product $\langle\phi|χ\rangle$ is number
Result is $|\psi\rangle$ times constant
Used to build operators from states

**Problem 2: B**
Completeness: basis is complete (spans space)
Any $|\psi\rangle = \sum_n c_n|n\rangle$ (can expand)
Resolution of identity
Insert $\mathbb{1} = \sum|n\rangle\langle n|$ anywhere
Not about orthogonality (that's $\langle m|n\rangle = \delta_{mn}$)

**Problem 3: C**
Spectral decomposition uses operator's own eigenstates
$\hat{A}|n\rangle = a_n|n\rangle$
Diagonal in its eigenbasis
Any Hermitian operator has this form
Generalizes eigenvalue decomposition from linear algebra

**Problem 4: B**
Idempotent: $\hat{P}_n^2 = \hat{P}_n$
$(|n\rangle\langle n|)(|n\rangle\langle n|) = |n\rangle\langle n|n\rangle\langle n| = |n\rangle\langle n|$
Projecting twice = projecting once
Also Hermitian: $\hat{P}_n^\dagger = \hat{P}_n$
Eigenvalues 0 or 1

**Problem 5: C**
$\text{Tr}(\hat{A}) = \sum_n\langle n|\hat{A}|n\rangle$ independent of basis choice
Invariant under unitary transformations
Physical observable (e.g., $\text{Tr}(\hat{\rho}) = 1$ for density matrix)
Can be complex (not necessarily real unless $\hat{A}$ Hermitian)
Cyclic: $\text{Tr}(\hat{A}\hat{B}) = \text{Tr}(\hat{B}\hat{A})$
