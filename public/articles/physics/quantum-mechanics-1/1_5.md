# Quantum Mechanics Topic 1.5: Heisenberg Picture and Equations of Motion

In Schrödinger's picture, states evolve while operators stay fixed: $|\psi(t)\rangle$ rotates through Hilbert space, but $\hat{A}$ remains constant. This mirrors everyday intuition—a particle moves through space while measurement devices stand still. Yet there's an equivalent formulation where **operators evolve** and **states stay fixed**. This is the **Heisenberg picture**, discovered by Heisenberg before Schrödinger's wave mechanics. It connects quantum mechanics directly to classical Hamiltonian mechanics via the correspondence $\frac{df}{dt} = \{f, H\}_{\text{Poisson}} \leftrightarrow \frac{d\hat{A}}{dt} = \frac{i}{\hbar}[\hat{H}, \hat{A}]$.

The pictures are completely equivalent—they're just different perspectives, like describing a rotating platform from a rotating or stationary reference frame. Physical predictions (expectation values) match: $\langle\psi(t)|\hat{A}|\psi(t)\rangle_S = \langle\psi(0)|\hat{A}(t)|\psi(0)\rangle_H$. But the Heisenberg picture offers advantages. It's manifestly covariant (no preferred time). Operators obey classical-like equations of motion. Constants of motion emerge naturally: $[\hat{H}, \hat{A}] = 0 \Rightarrow \frac{d\hat{A}}{dt} = 0$. And it provides the foundation for quantum field theory, where creation/annihilation operators must evolve.

This article develops the Heisenberg picture systematically. We'll transform between pictures via unitary operators, derive the Heisenberg equation of motion, identify conserved quantities from commutators, solve for harmonic oscillator operators as functions of time, and prove the equivalence to Schrödinger's picture. You'll see that Heisenberg's operator dynamics is the quantum analog of Hamilton's equations.

## The Two Pictures

**Schrödinger picture** (subscript $S$):
- States evolve: $|\psi_S(t)\rangle = e^{-i\hat{H}t/\hbar}|\psi_S(0)\rangle$
- Operators constant: $\hat{A}_S$

**Heisenberg picture** (subscript $H$):
- States fixed: $|\psi_H\rangle = |\psi_S(0)\rangle$
- Operators evolve: $\hat{A}_H(t) = e^{i\hat{H}t/\hbar}\hat{A}_S e^{-i\hat{H}t/\hbar}$

### Example 1: Equivalence of Expectation Values

Verify $\langle\psi_S(t)|\hat{A}_S|\psi_S(t)\rangle = \langle\psi_H|\hat{A}_H(t)|\psi_H\rangle$.

**Solution:**

**Schrödinger**:
$$\langle\psi_S(t)|\hat{A}_S|\psi_S(t)\rangle = \langle\psi_S(0)|e^{i\hat{H}t/\hbar}\hat{A}_S e^{-i\hat{H}t/\hbar}|\psi_S(0)\rangle$$

**Heisenberg**:
$$\langle\psi_H|\hat{A}_H(t)|\psi_H\rangle = \langle\psi_S(0)|e^{i\hat{H}t/\hbar}\hat{A}_S e^{-i\hat{H}t/\hbar}|\psi_S(0)\rangle$$

These are identical! ✓

**Answer:** Same physical predictions in both pictures

### Example 2: Transformation Between Pictures

Given $\hat{A}_S$, find $\hat{A}_H(t)$ explicitly.

**Solution:**

$$\hat{A}_H(t) = \hat{U}^\dagger(t)\hat{A}_S\hat{U}(t)$$ where $\hat{U}(t) = e^{-i\hat{H}t/\hbar}$

Since $\hat{U}$ is unitary: $\hat{U}^\dagger = e^{i\hat{H}t/\hbar}$

$$\hat{A}_H(t) = e^{i\hat{H}t/\hbar}\hat{A}_S e^{-i\hat{H}t/\hbar}$$

**Answer:** Unitary transformation by time-evolution operator

### Example 3: Initial Conditions Match

At $t = 0$, what is relation between $\hat{A}_S$ and $\hat{A}_H(0)$?

**Solution:**

$$\hat{A}_H(0) = e^{i\hat{H} \cdot 0/\hbar}\hat{A}_S e^{-i\hat{H} \cdot 0/\hbar} = \mathbb{1} \cdot \hat{A}_S \cdot \mathbb{1} = \hat{A}_S$$

**Answer:** Operators agree at $t = 0$ (pictures coincide initially)

## Heisenberg Equation of Motion

Take time derivative of $\hat{A}_H(t) = e^{i\hat{H}t/\hbar}\hat{A}_S e^{-i\hat{H}t/\hbar}$:

$$\frac{d\hat{A}_H}{dt} = \frac{i}{\hbar}[\hat{H}, \hat{A}_H] + \left(\frac{\partial\hat{A}}{\partial t}\right)_H$$

(Last term if $\hat{A}$ has explicit time dependence)

For time-independent $\hat{A}$:

$$\frac{d\hat{A}_H}{dt} = \frac{i}{\hbar}[\hat{H}, \hat{A}_H]$$

### Example 4: Deriving Heisenberg Equation

Prove $\frac{d\hat{A}_H}{dt} = \frac{i}{\hbar}[\hat{H}, \hat{A}_H]$ for time-independent $\hat{A}_S$.

**Solution:**

$$\frac{d\hat{A}_H}{dt} = \frac{d}{dt}\left(e^{i\hat{H}t/\hbar}\hat{A}_S e^{-i\hat{H}t/\hbar}\right)$$

Product rule:

$$= \frac{i\hat{H}}{\hbar}e^{i\hat{H}t/\hbar}\hat{A}_S e^{-i\hat{H}t/\hbar} + e^{i\hat{H}t/\hbar}\hat{A}_S\left(-\frac{i\hat{H}}{\hbar}\right)e^{-i\hat{H}t/\hbar}$$

$$= \frac{i}{\hbar}\hat{H}\hat{A}_H - \frac{i}{\hbar}\hat{A}_H\hat{H} = \frac{i}{\hbar}[\hat{H}, \hat{A}_H]$$ ✓

**Answer:** Commutator with Hamiltonian generates time evolution

### Example 5: Position Operator Evolution

For free particle $\hat{H} = \frac{\hat{p}^2}{2m}$, find $\frac{d\hat{x}_H}{dt}$.

**Solution:**

$$\frac{d\hat{x}_H}{dt} = \frac{i}{\hbar}\left[\frac{\hat{p}_H^2}{2m}, \hat{x}_H\right]$$

Using $[\hat{x}, \hat{p}^2] = -2i\hbar\hat{p}$ (computed via $[\hat{x}, \hat{p}] = i\hbar$):

$$\frac{d\hat{x}_H}{dt} = \frac{i}{\hbar}\left[\hat{H}, \hat{x}_H\right] = \frac{i}{\hbar} \cdot (-2i\hbar) \cdot \frac{\hat{p}_H}{2m} = \frac{\hat{p}_H}{m}$$ ✓

**Answer:** $\frac{d\hat{x}}{dt} = \frac{\hat{p}}{m}$ (quantum analog of $\dot{x} = p/m$)

### Example 6: Momentum Evolution

For potential $V(\hat{x})$, find $\frac{d\hat{p}_H}{dt}$.

**Solution:**

$$\hat{H} = \frac{\hat{p}^2}{2m} + V(\hat{x})$$

$$\frac{d\hat{p}_H}{dt} = \frac{i}{\hbar}[\hat{H}, \hat{p}_H]$$

Kinetic term: $[\hat{p}^2, \hat{p}] = 0$ (momentum commutes with itself)

Potential term: $[V(\hat{x}), \hat{p}] = i\hbar\frac{\partial V}{\partial x}$ (generalized commutator)

$$\frac{d\hat{p}_H}{dt} = \frac{i}{\hbar} \cdot i\hbar\frac{\partial V}{\partial \hat{x}} = -\frac{\partial V}{\partial \hat{x}}$$

**Answer:** Force law $\frac{d\hat{p}}{dt} = -\nabla V$ (quantum Newton's second law)

## Conservation Laws

**Theorem**: If $[\hat{H}, \hat{A}] = 0$, then $\hat{A}$ is a **constant of motion**.

$$\frac{d\hat{A}_H}{dt} = \frac{i}{\hbar}[\hat{H}, \hat{A}_H] = 0$$

### Example 7: Energy Conservation

Show energy is always conserved.

**Solution:**

$$\frac{d\hat{H}_H}{dt} = \frac{i}{\hbar}[\hat{H}, \hat{H}] = 0$$

(Any operator commutes with itself)

**Answer:** Energy conserved in all closed systems

### Example 8: Free Particle Momentum

For free particle ($V = 0$), show momentum is conserved.

**Solution:**

$$\hat{H} = \frac{\hat{p}^2}{2m}$$

$$[\hat{H}, \hat{p}] = \left[\frac{\hat{p}^2}{2m}, \hat{p}\right] = 0$$

(Momentum commutes with any function of itself)

$$\frac{d\hat{p}_H}{dt} = 0$$ ✓

**Answer:** Translation symmetry → momentum conservation (Noether)

### Example 9: Harmonic Oscillator

For $\hat{H} = \hbar\omega(\hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a} + \frac{1}{2})$, show number operator $\hat{N} = \hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a}$ is conserved.

**Solution:**

$$[\hat{H}, \hat{N}] = \hbar\omega[\hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a}, \hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a}] = 0$$

$$\frac{d\hat{N}_H}{dt} = 0$$

**Physical meaning**: Particle doesn't change energy levels without perturbation.

**Answer:** Number of quanta conserved for isolated oscillator

## Correspondence to Classical Mechanics

Classical Hamiltonian mechanics: $\frac{df}{dt} = \{f, H\}_{\text{Poisson}}$

Quantum mechanics: $\frac{d\hat{A}}{dt} = \frac{i}{\hbar}[\hat{H}, \hat{A}]$

**Correspondence principle**: $[\hat{A}, \hat{B}] \to i\hbar\{A, B\}_{\text{Poisson}}$ as $\hbar \to 0$

### Example 10: Poisson Bracket of Position and Momentum

Classical Poisson bracket: $\{x, p\} = \frac{\partial x}{\partial x}\frac{\partial p}{\partial p} - \frac{\partial x}{\partial p}\frac{\partial p}{\partial x} = 1$

Quantum commutator: $[\hat{x}, \hat{p}] = i\hbar$

Verify correspondence.

**Solution:**

$$[\hat{x}, \hat{p}] = i\hbar \Rightarrow \frac{1}{i\hbar}[\hat{x}, \hat{p}] = 1 = \{x, p\}$$ ✓

**Answer:** Commutator/$i\hbar$ → Poisson bracket in classical limit

### Example 11: Hamilton's Equations

Classical: $\dot{x} = \frac{\partial H}{\partial p}$, $\dot{p} = -\frac{\partial H}{\partial x}$

Quantum (from Examples 5-6): $\frac{d\hat{x}}{dt} = \frac{\hat{p}}{m}$, $\frac{d\hat{p}}{dt} = -\frac{\partial V}{\partial \hat{x}}$

These match for $H = \frac{p^2}{2m} + V(x)$!

**Answer:** Heisenberg equations are quantum Hamilton equations

## Harmonic Oscillator in Heisenberg Picture

### Example 12: Ladder Operator Evolution

For $\hat{H} = \hbar\omega\hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a} + \frac{\hbar\omega}{2}$, find $\frac{d\hat{a}_H}{dt}$.

**Solution:**

$$\frac{d\hat{a}_H}{dt} = \frac{i}{\hbar}[\hat{H}, \hat{a}_H] = \frac{i}{\hbar}\hbar\omega[\hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a}, \hat{a}]$$

Using $[\hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a}, \hat{a}] = -\hat{a}$ (from commutation relations):

$$= i\omega(-\hat{a}_H) = -i\omega\hat{a}_H$$

Differential equation: $\frac{d\hat{a}_H}{dt} = -i\omega\hat{a}_H$

**Solution**: $\hat{a}_H(t) = \hat{a}_H(0)e^{-i\omega t} = \hat{a}e^{-i\omega t}$

**Answer:** Ladder operators oscillate with frequency $\omega$

### Example 13: Position and Momentum in Time

For oscillator, $\hat{x} = \sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}}(\hat{a} + \hat{a}^\dagger)$, $\hat{p} = i\sqrt{\frac{m\hbar\omega}{2}}(\hat{a}^\dagger - \hat{a})$.

Find $\hat{x}_H(t)$ and $\hat{p}_H(t)$.

**Solution:**

From Example 12: $\hat{a}_H(t) = \hat{a}e^{-i\omega t}$, $\hat{a}^\dagger_H(t) = \hat{a}^\dagger e^{i\omega t}$

$$\hat{x}_H(t) = \sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}}(\hat{a}e^{-i\omega t} + \hat{a}^\dagger e^{i\omega t})$$

$$= \sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}}\left[(\hat{a} + \hat{a}^\dagger)\cos\omega t + i(\hat{a}^\dagger - \hat{a})\sin\omega t\right]$$

$$= \hat{x}\cos\omega t + \frac{\hat{p}}{m\omega}\sin\omega t$$

Similarly:
$$\hat{p}_H(t) = \hat{p}\cos\omega t - m\omega\hat{x}\sin\omega t$$

**Answer:** Operators oscillate like classical $x(t) = x_0\cos\omega t + \frac{p_0}{m\omega}\sin\omega t$

## Practice Problems

**Problem 1:** In Heisenberg picture:

A) States evolve, operators fixed
B) Operators evolve, states fixed
C) Both evolve
D) Neither evolves
E) Only energy evolves

**Problem 2:** Heisenberg equation of motion is:

A) $i\hbar\frac{\partial|\psi\rangle}{\partial t} = \hat{H}|\psi\rangle$
B) $\frac{d\hat{A}}{dt} = [\hat{H}, \hat{A}]$
C) $\frac{d\hat{A}}{dt} = \frac{i}{\hbar}[\hat{H}, \hat{A}]$
D) $\frac{d\hat{A}}{dt} = 0$
E) $\hat{H}\hat{A} = \hat{A}\hat{H}$

**Problem 3:** If $[\hat{H}, \hat{A}] = 0$, then:

A) $\hat{A}$ varies rapidly
B) $\hat{A}$ is conserved
C) $\hat{H} = 0$
D) Energy not conserved
E) System is unstable

**Problem 4:** The correspondence to classical mechanics is:

A) $[\hat{A}, \hat{B}] \to \{A, B\}$
B) $[\hat{A}, \hat{B}] \to i\hbar\{A, B\}$
C) $\frac{1}{i\hbar}[\hat{A}, \hat{B}] \to \{A, B\}$
D) Both B and C
E) No correspondence exists

**Problem 5:** Schrödinger and Heisenberg pictures give:

A) Different physics
B) Same expectation values
C) Different energies
D) Incompatible results
E) Different probabilities

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: B**
Heisenberg picture: operators time-dependent
$\hat{A}_H(t) = e^{i\hat{H}t/\hbar}\hat{A}_S e^{-i\hat{H}t/\hbar}$
States fixed: $|\psi_H\rangle = |\psi_S(0)\rangle$
Active transformation (operators rotate)
Schrödinger: passive (states rotate)

**Problem 2: C**
$\frac{d\hat{A}_H}{dt} = \frac{i}{\hbar}[\hat{H}, \hat{A}_H]$ (Heisenberg equation)
Quantum analog of $\dot{f} = \{f, H\}$ (Hamilton)
Note: $\frac{i}{\hbar}$ factor (not just commutator)
If $\hat{A}$ explicit time dependence, add $\frac{\partial\hat{A}}{\partial t}$
Generates operator dynamics

**Problem 3: B**
$[\hat{H}, \hat{A}] = 0 \Rightarrow \frac{d\hat{A}}{dt} = 0$
Conserved quantity (constant of motion)
Examples: energy itself, free particle momentum
Symmetry → conservation (Noether)
Can measure simultaneously with energy

**Problem 4: D**
Both correct:
$[\hat{A}, \hat{B}] = i\hbar\{A, B\} + O(\hbar^2)$
Or: $\frac{1}{i\hbar}[\hat{A}, \hat{B}] \to \{A, B\}$ as $\hbar \to 0$
Correspondence principle (Dirac)
Example: $[\hat{x}, \hat{p}] = i\hbar$, $\{x, p\} = 1$

**Problem 5: B**
Expectation values identical:
$\langle\psi_S(t)|\hat{A}_S|\psi_S(t)\rangle = \langle\psi_H|\hat{A}_H(t)|\psi_H\rangle$
Pictures are equivalent (unitary transformation)
Same physics, different mathematical convenience
Heisenberg: closer to classical
Schrödinger: easier for most problems
