# Quantum Mechanics Topic 2.3: Free Particle and Wave Packets

A truly free particle—no forces, no potential—seems trivial. Classically, it moves with constant velocity forever. Quantum mechanically, the free particle reveals the deepest mystery of quantum mechanics: **wave-particle duality**. The energy eigenstates are **plane waves** $\psi_k(x) = Ae^{ikx}$ extending infinitely in space with definite momentum $p = \hbar k$ but completely undefined position. To localize a particle, you must superpose many plane waves, creating a **wave packet**—a traveling lump of probability that spreads inexorably over time.

This spreading isn't measurement error or environmental decoherence. It's fundamental. A Gaussian wave packet of width $a$ spreads as $\Delta x(t) = a\sqrt{1 + (\hbar t/ma^2)^2}$, approaching $\Delta x \propto t$ for large $t$. The narrower the initial packet (better position localization), the faster it spreads (more momentum components). The group velocity $v_g = \hbar k_0/m$ matches the classical particle velocity, while the phase velocity $v_p = \hbar k/2m$ has no physical meaning.

This article explores free particle quantum mechanics completely. We'll solve for plane wave eigenstates with continuous spectrum, construct Gaussian wave packets via Fourier transforms, derive the time evolution and spreading formula, distinguish group from phase velocity, and understand the quadratic dispersion relation $\omega = \hbar k^2/2m$ that causes spreading. You'll see that quantum localization is inherently unstable—wavepackets inevitably disperse.

## The Free Particle: Plane Wave Solutions

**Potential**: $V(x) = 0$ everywhere

**Time-independent Schrödinger equation**:

$$-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{d^2\psi}{dx^2} = E\psi$$

### Example 1: Solving for Energy Eigenstates

Find solutions to free particle equation.

**Solution:**

$$\psi'' = -k^2\psi$$ where $k = \frac{\sqrt{2mE}}{\hbar}$

General solution: $\psi_k(x) = Ae^{ikx} + Be^{-ikx}$

Or equivalently: $\psi_k(x) = C\sin(kx) + D\cos(kx)$

**Key difference from bound states**: No boundary conditions! $k$ can be any real number.

For traveling wave to the right: $\psi_k(x) = Ae^{ikx}$

**Answer:** Plane waves $e^{ikx}$ with continuous $k$ (not discrete)

### Example 2: Energy-Momentum Relation

Express energy in terms of momentum $p = \hbar k$.

**Solution:**

From $k = \sqrt{2mE}/\hbar$:

$$k^2 = \frac{2mE}{\hbar^2} \Rightarrow E = \frac{\hbar^2k^2}{2m}$$

Using $p = \hbar k$:

$$E = \frac{p^2}{2m}$$

This is classical kinetic energy!

**Answer:** $E = \frac{p^2}{2m} = \frac{\hbar^2k^2}{2m}$ (continuous spectrum $E \geq 0$)

### Example 3: Normalization Issue

Try to normalize plane wave: $\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|Ae^{ikx}|^2 dx = 1$.

**Solution:**

$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|A|^2 dx = |A|^2 \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} dx = \infty$$

Cannot normalize in usual sense!

**Resolution**: Use **Dirac delta normalization**:

$$\langle k'|k\rangle = \delta(k' - k)$$

In position space:

$$\psi_k(x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{ikx}$$ (normalization factor)

$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\psi_{k'}^*(x)\psi_k(x) dx = \delta(k' - k)$$ ✓

**Answer:** Plane waves not normalizable; use delta normalization for continuous spectrum

### Example 4: Probability Current

For plane wave $\psi = Ae^{ikx}$, compute probability current $j$.

**Solution:**

$$j = \frac{\hbar}{m}\text{Im}(\psi^*\psi')$$

$$\psi^* = A^*e^{-ikx}, \quad \psi' = ikAe^{ikx}$$

$$\psi^*\psi' = A^*A \cdot ik = ik|A|^2$$

$$j = \frac{\hbar}{m}\text{Im}(ik|A|^2) = \frac{\hbar k}{m}|A|^2 = \frac{p}{m}|\psi|^2$$

Velocity: $v = j/\rho = p/m$ (classical velocity!)

**Answer:** Constant probability flow with velocity $v = \hbar k/m$

## Wave Packets: Gaussian Superposition

To localize particle, superpose plane waves:

$$\psi(x, t) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\phi(k)e^{i(kx - \omega(k)t)} dk$$

where $\omega(k) = \hbar k^2/2m$ (dispersion relation).

### Example 5: Gaussian Momentum Distribution

At $t = 0$, take $\phi(k) = \left(\frac{2a^2}{\pi}\right)^{1/4}e^{-a^2(k - k_0)^2}$.

Find $\psi(x, 0)$.

**Solution:**

$$\psi(x, 0) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\frac{2a^2}{\pi}\right)^{1/4}e^{-a^2(k-k_0)^2}e^{ikx} dk$$

Complete the square in exponent:

$$-a^2(k - k_0)^2 + ikx = -a^2\left[k - \left(k_0 + \frac{ix}{2a^2}\right)\right]^2 + ik_0x - \frac{x^2}{4a^2}$$

Gaussian integral: $\int e^{-(k - k_c)^2a^2} dk = \frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{a}$

$$\psi(x, 0) = \left(\frac{1}{2\pi a^2}\right)^{1/4}e^{ik_0x}e^{-x^2/4a^2}$$

**Answer:** Gaussian in position space with width $\Delta x_0 = \sqrt{2}a$, centered at origin, oscillating with wave number $k_0$

### Example 6: Initial Uncertainties

For Gaussian packet in Example 5, find $\Delta x(0)$ and $\Delta p(0)$.

**Solution:**

Position distribution: $|\psi(x, 0)|^2 \propto e^{-x^2/2a^2}$

Width: $\Delta x(0) = a$

Momentum distribution: $|\phi(k)|^2 \propto e^{-2a^2(k - k_0)^2}$

Width: $\Delta k = \frac{1}{2a}$, so $\Delta p(0) = \hbar\Delta k = \frac{\hbar}{2a}$

Product:
$$\Delta x(0) \cdot \Delta p(0) = a \cdot \frac{\hbar}{2a} = \frac{\hbar}{2}$$ ✓

**Answer:** Minimum uncertainty packet (Gaussian saturates Heisenberg relation)

## Time Evolution and Spreading

### Example 7: Dispersion Relation

For free particle, $\omega = E/\hbar = \frac{\hbar k^2}{2m}$ (quadratic in $k$).

Why does this cause spreading?

**Solution:**

Different $k$ components travel at different speeds!

**Phase velocity**: $v_p = \omega/k = \frac{\hbar k}{2m}$ (depends on $k$)

**Group velocity**: $v_g = \frac{d\omega}{dk} = \frac{\hbar k}{m}$ (constant for fixed $k_0$)

Quadratic $\omega(k)$ means $v_p \neq v_g$ → components separate → packet spreads.

**Answer:** Dispersion (frequency-dependent phase velocity) causes spreading

### Example 8: Gaussian Packet Time Evolution

For Gaussian at $t = 0$ with width $a$, find $\Delta x(t)$.

**Solution:**

(Full derivation involves Fourier transforms and Gaussian integrals—sketch only)

Time evolution:
$$\psi(x, t) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int\phi(k)e^{i(kx - \hbar k^2t/2m)} dk$$

After completing integrals (messy but doable):

$$|\psi(x, t)|^2 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\Delta x(t)^2}}e^{-x^2/2\Delta x(t)^2}$$

where

$$\Delta x(t) = a\sqrt{1 + \left(\frac{\hbar t}{ma^2}\right)^2}$$

**Answer:** Width increases with time (spreading is inevitable)

### Example 9: Spreading Timescale

For electron with initial width $a = 1$ nm, how long to double in width?

**Solution:**

$$\Delta x(t) = a\sqrt{1 + \left(\frac{\hbar t}{ma^2}\right)^2} = 2a$$

$$\sqrt{1 + \left(\frac{\hbar t}{ma^2}\right)^2} = 2$$

$$\left(\frac{\hbar t}{ma^2}\right)^2 = 3$$

$$t = \frac{\sqrt{3}ma^2}{\hbar}$$

For electron: $m = 9.1 \times 10^{-31}$ kg, $a = 10^{-9}$ m, $\hbar = 1.05 \times 10^{-34}$ J·s

$$t \approx \frac{1.73 \times 9.1 \times 10^{-31} \times 10^{-18}}{1.05 \times 10^{-34}} \approx 1.5 \times 10^{-14} \text{ s} = 15 \text{ fs}$$

**Answer:** Femtosecond timescale (extremely fast spreading for nanoscale packets)

### Example 10: Asymptotic Spreading

For $t \gg ma^2/\hbar$, what is $\Delta x(t)$?

**Solution:**

$$\Delta x(t) = a\sqrt{1 + \left(\frac{\hbar t}{ma^2}\right)^2} \approx a \cdot \frac{\hbar t}{ma^2} = \frac{\hbar t}{ma}$$

Linear growth: $\Delta x \propto t$

Rate: $\frac{d(\Delta x)}{dt} = \frac{\hbar}{ma} = \frac{\Delta p}{m}$

This is uncertainty in velocity times time!

**Answer:** Eventually spreads linearly $\Delta x \approx \frac{\hbar t}{ma} = (\Delta p/m) \cdot t$

## Group and Phase Velocities

### Example 11: Group Velocity Derivation

For wave packet centered at $k_0$, show center moves at $v_g = d\omega/dk|_{k_0}$.

**Solution:**

Wave packet peak satisfies **stationary phase** condition:

$$\frac{d}{dk}[kx - \omega(k)t]|_{k_0} = 0$$

$$x - \frac{d\omega}{dk}\bigg|_{k_0} t = 0$$

$$x = \left(\frac{d\omega}{dk}\bigg|_{k_0}\right)t$$

Peak position moves with velocity:

$$v_g = \frac{x}{t} = \frac{d\omega}{dk}\bigg|_{k_0} = \frac{\hbar k_0}{m}$$

This matches classical particle velocity $v = p/m = \hbar k_0/m$ ✓

**Answer:** Wave packet envelope moves at group velocity (classical velocity)

### Example 12: Phase Velocity Has No Physical Meaning

For free particle, $v_p = \omega/k = \hbar k/2m = v_g/2$.

Why doesn't this contradict group velocity?

**Solution:**

**Phase velocity**: speed of constant-phase surfaces (e.g., wave crests)

Individual crests move at $v_p = \hbar k/2m$.

**Group velocity**: speed of envelope (where particle is likely)

Envelope moves at $v_g = \hbar k/m = 2v_p$.

**Observable**: Particle probability $|\psi|^2$ moves at $v_g$, not $v_p$.

Phase of individual plane wave components isn't directly observable.

**Answer:** Only group velocity is physically meaningful (packet envelope speed)

### Example 13: Non-Dispersive vs. Dispersive

Compare free particle ($\omega = \hbar k^2/2m$) to photon ($\omega = ck$).

**Solution:**

**Photon**: $\omega = ck$ (linear)

$$v_g = \frac{d\omega}{dk} = c, \quad v_p = \frac{\omega}{k} = c$$

$v_g = v_p$ → no dispersion → wave packet doesn't spread!

**Free particle**: $\omega = \hbar k^2/2m$ (quadratic)

$$v_g = \frac{\hbar k}{m}, \quad v_p = \frac{\hbar k}{2m}$$

$v_g \neq v_p$ → dispersion → wave packet spreads.

**Answer:** Linear dispersion (photons) → no spreading; quadratic (matter) → spreading

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: B**
$E = \frac{\hbar^2k^2}{2m}$ with any $k \in \mathbb{R}$ (continuous)
No boundary conditions → no quantization
All $E \geq 0$ allowed
Contrast with bound states (discrete)
Delta-function normalization needed

**Problem 2: B**
Cannot normalize: $\int|e^{ikx}|^2 dx = \infty$
Use Dirac delta: $\langle k'|k\rangle = \delta(k' - k)$
Continuous spectrum requirement
Plane waves extend infinitely
Position completely undefined ($\Delta x = \infty$)

**Problem 3: C**
$\omega = \hbar k^2/2m$ (quadratic, not linear)
Different $k$ travel at different speeds
Phase velocity $v_p = \omega/k$ depends on $k$
Fundamental quantum effect (not measurement)
Linear dispersion (photons) doesn't spread

**Problem 4: B**
$v_g = \frac{d\omega}{dk}$ (derivative at center frequency)
Speed of envelope (wave packet center)
For free particle: $v_g = \hbar k/m = p/m$ (classical)
Phase velocity $v_p = \omega/k$ is different
Only $v_g$ is physically observable

**Problem 5: A**
For $t \gg ma^2/\hbar$: $\Delta x(t) \approx \frac{\hbar t}{ma}$
Linear growth (not quadratic or exponential)
Rate = uncertainty in velocity
Eventually all packets spread indefinitely
Narrower initial packet → faster spreading
