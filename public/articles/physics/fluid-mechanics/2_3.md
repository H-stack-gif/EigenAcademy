# Article 2.3: Conservation of Mass: Continuity Equation

Conservation of mass is the most fundamental constraint in fluid mechanics: matter cannot be created or destroyed. For a fluid, this principle translates into the **continuity equation**, which relates the rate of density change to the divergence of velocity. The continuity equation takes different forms depending on whether we adopt an integral (control volume) or differential (infinitesimal) perspective, but both express the same physical law. The **Reynolds Transport Theorem** provides the mathematical bridge, converting between Lagrangian (following fluid) and Eulerian (fixed volume) descriptions.

For **incompressible flow**—arguably the most important special case—density remains constant following fluid particles, and the continuity equation simplifies dramatically to $\nabla \cdot \mathbf{u} = 0$. This **solenoidal condition** means velocity is divergence-free: as much fluid enters a region as leaves. Most liquid flows and low-speed gas flows satisfy this condition. For 2D incompressible flow, a powerful tool emerges: the **stream function** $\psi$, which automatically satisfies continuity and provides an elegant representation where streamlines are simply curves of constant $\psi$.

This article derives the continuity equation from first principles using the Reynolds Transport Theorem, explores the integral and differential forms and their relationship, treats the incompressible limit in detail, introduces the stream function for 2D flows and its properties, connects stream function to vorticity, demonstrates applications in various geometries, and works through extensive examples involving mass balance, coordinate transformations, and stream function calculations. The mathematics involves heavy vector calculus (divergence theorem), partial differential equations, and integration techniques.

## Reynolds Transport Theorem (RTT)

Consider extensive property $\mathcal{B}$ (mass, momentum, energy) with intensive property $\phi = d\mathcal{B}/dm$ (property per unit mass).

For material volume $V(t)$ moving with fluid:

$$\boxed{\frac{d}{dt}\int_{V(t)} \rho \phi \, dV = \int_{V(t)} \rho\frac{D\phi}{Dt} \, dV}$$

This says: rate of change of total property = sum of rate of change of property for each particle.

**Alternative form** (control volume):

$$\boxed{\frac{d}{dt}\int_{V(t)} \rho \phi \, dV = \int_{V(t)} \frac{\partial(\rho\phi)}{\partial t} \, dV + \int_{S(t)} \rho \phi (\mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{n}) \, dA}$$

**Terms**:
- First: rate of change within volume
- Second: net flux across surface

**Physical interpretation**: Change in $\mathcal{B}$ = accumulation + net outflow

## Integral Form of Mass Conservation

Set $\phi = 1$ (mass per unit mass) in RTT:

$$\frac{d}{dt}\int_{V(t)} \rho \, dV = \int_{V(t)} \rho\frac{D \cdot 1}{Dt} \, dV = 0$$

Since $D(1)/Dt = 0$, total mass in material volume is constant.

**Control volume form**:

$$\boxed{\frac{d}{dt}\int_{V} \rho \, dV = -\int_{S} \rho \mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{n} \, dA}$$

**Physical meaning**: Rate of mass increase in volume = negative of net mass flux out.

**Steady flow** ($\frac{\partial \rho}{\partial t} = 0$):
$$\int_S \rho \mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{n} \, dA = 0$$

Mass flow rate in = mass flow rate out.

## Differential Form: Continuity Equation

Apply divergence theorem to integral form with fixed control volume:

$$\int_V \frac{\partial \rho}{\partial t} \, dV = -\int_S \rho \mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{n} \, dA = -\int_V \nabla \cdot (\rho \mathbf{u}) \, dV$$

Since volume arbitrary:

$$\boxed{\frac{\partial \rho}{\partial t} + \nabla \cdot (\rho \mathbf{u}) = 0}$$

This is the **continuity equation** in conservative form.

**Alternative forms**:

Expand divergence:
$$\frac{\partial \rho}{\partial t} + \mathbf{u} \cdot \nabla\rho + \rho\nabla \cdot \mathbf{u} = 0$$

Using material derivative:
$$\boxed{\frac{D\rho}{Dt} + \rho\nabla \cdot \mathbf{u} = 0}$$

**Physical interpretation**: $\nabla \cdot \mathbf{u}$ is volumetric expansion rate; if $\nabla \cdot \mathbf{u} > 0$, density must decrease to conserve mass.

## Incompressible Flow

**Definition**: Density constant following fluid particles:

$$\frac{D\rho}{Dt} = 0$$

From continuity equation:

$$\boxed{\nabla \cdot \mathbf{u} = 0}$$

This is the **incompressibility condition** (solenoidal velocity field).

**Important**: "Incompressible" means constant density for each fluid particle, NOT that density is uniform everywhere. Stratified flows (e.g., ocean) can have varying $\rho(\mathbf{r})$ but still be incompressible if $D\rho/Dt = 0$.

**When valid?**:
- Liquids (nearly always): bulk modulus very large
- Gases at low Mach number: Ma $< 0.3$ typically
- Boussinesq approximation: small density variations, $\rho = \rho_0(1 - \beta\Delta T)$

**Advantage**: Continuity becomes simpler, and density decouples from momentum equation.

## Stream Function (2D Incompressible)

For 2D incompressible flow ($u$, $v$ functions of $x$, $y$ only, $w = 0$):

$$\frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial v}{\partial y} = 0$$

Define **stream function** $\psi(x, y)$ such that:

$$\boxed{u = \frac{\partial \psi}{\partial y}, \quad v = -\frac{\partial \psi}{\partial x}}$$

**Verification**: Automatically satisfies continuity:

$$\nabla \cdot \mathbf{u} = \frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial v}{\partial y} = \frac{\partial^2 \psi}{\partial x\partial y} - \frac{\partial^2 \psi}{\partial y\partial x} = 0$$ ✓

**Properties**:

1. **Streamlines**: Curves of constant $\psi$

   Along streamline: $d\psi = \frac{\partial \psi}{\partial x}dx + \frac{\partial \psi}{\partial y}dy = -v \, dx + u \, dy = 0$

   This is equivalent to $dy/dx = v/u$ (streamline equation) ✓

2. **Volume flux**: Between two streamlines $\psi_1$ and $\psi_2$, volume flux per unit depth = $\psi_2 - \psi_1$

3. **Vorticity relation**: In 2D,
   $$\omega_z = \frac{\partial v}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial u}{\partial y} = -\frac{\partial^2 \psi}{\partial x^2} - \frac{\partial^2 \psi}{\partial y^2}$$

   $$\boxed{\omega_z = -\nabla^2\psi}$$

**Cylindrical coordinates**:
$$u_r = \frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial \psi}{\partial \theta}, \quad u_\theta = -\frac{\partial \psi}{\partial r}$$

## Continuity in Different Coordinates

**Cartesian**:
$$\frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial v}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial w}{\partial z} = 0$$

**Cylindrical** $(r, \theta, z)$:
$$\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial (ru_r)}{\partial r} + \frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial u_\theta}{\partial \theta} + \frac{\partial u_z}{\partial z} = 0$$

**Spherical** $(r, \theta, \phi)$:
$$\frac{1}{r^2}\frac{\partial (r^2 u_r)}{\partial r} + \frac{1}{r\sin\theta}\frac{\partial (u_\theta\sin\theta)}{\partial \theta} + \frac{1}{r\sin\theta}\frac{\partial u_\phi}{\partial \phi} = 0$$

## Example 1: Mass Flow Rate in Pipe

Circular pipe radius $R$ with velocity profile $u(r) = U_0(1 - r^2/R^2)$ (parabolic). Find mass flow rate.

**Solution:**

$$\dot{m} = \int_S \rho u \, dA = \int_0^R \rho U_0\left(1 - \frac{r^2}{R^2}\right) 2\pi r \, dr$$

$$= 2\pi\rho U_0 \int_0^R \left(r - \frac{r^3}{R^2}\right) dr$$

$$= 2\pi\rho U_0 \left[\frac{r^2}{2} - \frac{r^4}{4R^2}\right]_0^R$$

$$= 2\pi\rho U_0 \left(\frac{R^2}{2} - \frac{R^2}{4}\right) = 2\pi\rho U_0 \cdot \frac{R^2}{4} = \frac{\pi R^2 \rho U_0}{2}$$

**Compare to uniform flow**: $\dot{m}_{uniform} = \rho U_{avg} \pi R^2$

Here: $U_{avg} = U_0/2$, so $\dot{m} = \frac{\pi R^2 \rho U_0}{2}$ ✓

**Answer:** $\dot{m} = \frac{\pi R^2 \rho U_0}{2}$

## Example 2: Continuity in 2D

Given $u = x^2 - y^2$, find $v$ such that continuity is satisfied, with $v = 0$ at $y = 0$.

**Solution:**

Continuity: $\frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial v}{\partial y} = 0$

$$\frac{\partial u}{\partial x} = 2x$$

$$\frac{\partial v}{\partial y} = -2x$$

Integrate:
$$v = -2xy + f(x)$$

Boundary condition $v(x, 0) = 0$:
$$0 = 0 + f(x) \implies f(x) = 0$$

$$v = -2xy$$

**Verification**: $\frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial v}{\partial y} = 2x - 2x = 0$ ✓

**Answer:** $v = -2xy$

---

## Example 3: Streamfunction for Uniform Flow

For $\mathbf{u} = (U, 0, 0)$, find stream function.

**Solution:**

$$u = \frac{\partial \psi}{\partial y} = U$$

Integrate:
$$\psi = Uy + f(x)$$

$$v = -\frac{\partial \psi}{\partial x} = -f'(x) = 0$$

$$f(x) = C$$

Choose $C = 0$:
$$\boxed{\psi = Uy}$$

**Streamlines**: $\psi = \text{const}$ → $y = \text{const}$ (horizontal lines) ✓

**Answer:** $\psi = Uy$

---

## Example 4: Source Flow

In polar coordinates, $u_r = \frac{Q}{2\pi r}$, $u_\theta = 0$ represents flow from point source.

Find stream function.

**Solution:**

$$u_r = \frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial \psi}{\partial \theta} = \frac{Q}{2\pi r}$$

$$\frac{\partial \psi}{\partial \theta} = \frac{Q}{2\pi}$$

Integrate:
$$\psi = \frac{Q\theta}{2\pi} + f(r)$$

$$u_\theta = -\frac{\partial \psi}{\partial r} = -f'(r) = 0$$

$$f(r) = C$$

Choose $C = 0$:
$$\boxed{\psi = \frac{Q\theta}{2\pi}}$$

**Streamlines**: $\theta = \text{const}$ (radial lines) ✓

**Answer:** $\psi = \frac{Q\theta}{2\pi}$

---

## Example 5: Volume Flux Between Streamlines

For $\psi = Uy$, calculate volume flux per unit depth between $y = 0$ and $y = h$.

**Solution:**

$$Q = \psi(h) - \psi(0) = Uh - 0 = Uh$$

**Verification by integration**:
$$Q = \int_0^h u \, dy = \int_0^h U \, dy = Uh$$ ✓

**Answer:** $Q = Uh$ per unit depth

---

## Example 6: Corner Flow Stream Function

For $\mathbf{u} = (kx, -ky, 0)$, find stream function.

**Solution:**

$$u = \frac{\partial \psi}{\partial y} = kx$$

Integrate:
$$\psi = kxy + f(x)$$

$$v = -\frac{\partial \psi}{\partial x} = -ky - f'(x) = -ky$$

$$f'(x) = 0 \implies f = C$$

Choose $C = 0$:
$$\boxed{\psi = kxy}$$

**Streamlines**: $xy = \text{const}$ (rectangular hyperbolae)

**Answer:** $\psi = kxy$

## Example 7: Compressible Flow

Gas flows through expanding duct with $u = U_0 x/L$, $v = 0$, $w = 0$.

If $\rho = \rho_0 L/x$, verify continuity.

**Solution:**

Continuity: $\frac{\partial \rho}{\partial t} + \nabla \cdot (\rho \mathbf{u}) = 0$

$$\frac{\partial \rho}{\partial t} = 0$$ (steady)

$$\nabla \cdot (\rho \mathbf{u}) = \frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left(\frac{\rho_0 L}{x} \cdot \frac{U_0 x}{L}\right) = \frac{\partial}{\partial x}(\rho_0 U_0)$$

$$= 0$$ ✓

**Physical interpretation**: Density decreases inversely with $x$ to compensate velocity increase.

**Answer:** Continuity satisfied

---

## Example 8: Axisymmetric Stagnation Flow

In cylindrical coordinates: $u_r = -\alpha r$, $u_z = 2\alpha z$, $u_\theta = 0$.

Verify continuity.

**Solution:**

$$\nabla \cdot \mathbf{u} = \frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial (ru_r)}{\partial r} + \frac{\partial u_z}{\partial z}$$

$$= \frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}(r \cdot (-\alpha r)) + \frac{\partial}{\partial z}(2\alpha z)$$

$$= \frac{1}{r}(-2\alpha r) + 2\alpha = -2\alpha + 2\alpha = 0$$ ✓

**Answer:** Continuity satisfied

---

## Example 9: Vorticity from Stream Function

For $\psi = xy^2$, calculate vorticity.

**Solution:**

$$u = \frac{\partial \psi}{\partial y} = 2xy$$

$$v = -\frac{\partial \psi}{\partial x} = -y^2$$

$$\omega_z = \frac{\partial v}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial u}{\partial y} = 0 - 2x = -2x$$

**Alternative**: $\omega_z = -\nabla^2\psi$

$$\nabla^2\psi = \frac{\partial^2\psi}{\partial x^2} + \frac{\partial^2\psi}{\partial y^2} = 0 + 2x = 2x$$

$$\omega_z = -2x$$ ✓

**Answer:** $\omega_z = -2x$

## Example 10: Junction Flow

Two pipes merge: Pipe 1 ($D_1 = 5$ cm, $U_1 = 2$ m/s), Pipe 2 ($D_2 = 3$ cm, $U_2 = 3$ m/s) → Pipe 3 ($D_3 = 6$ cm).

Find $U_3$ (same fluid density).

**Solution:**

Mass balance:
$$\rho A_1 U_1 + \rho A_2 U_2 = \rho A_3 U_3$$

$$A_1 U_1 + A_2 U_2 = A_3 U_3$$

$$\frac{\pi D_1^2}{4}U_1 + \frac{\pi D_2^2}{4}U_2 = \frac{\pi D_3^2}{4}U_3$$

$$D_1^2 U_1 + D_2^2 U_2 = D_3^2 U_3$$

$$(0.05)^2(2) + (0.03)^2(3) = (0.06)^2 U_3$$

$$0.0050 + 0.0027 = 0.0036 U_3$$

$$U_3 = \frac{0.0077}{0.0036} = 2.14 \text{ m/s}$$

**Answer:** $U_3 = 2.14$ m/s

---

## Example 11: Continuity in Spherical Coordinates

Radial flow from point source: $u_r = Q/(4\pi r^2)$, $u_\theta = u_\phi = 0$.

Verify continuity.

**Solution:**

$$\nabla \cdot \mathbf{u} = \frac{1}{r^2}\frac{\partial (r^2 u_r)}{\partial r}$$

$$= \frac{1}{r^2}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left(r^2 \cdot \frac{Q}{4\pi r^2}\right) = \frac{1}{r^2}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left(\frac{Q}{4\pi}\right) = 0$$ ✓

**Answer:** Continuity satisfied

---

## Example 12: Stream Function Superposition

Given $\psi_1 = Uy$ (uniform flow) and $\psi_2 = Q\theta/(2\pi)$ (source).

Find velocity for $\psi = \psi_1 + \psi_2$.

**Solution:**

In Cartesian with $\theta = \arctan(y/x)$:

$$u = \frac{\partial \psi}{\partial y} = U + \frac{Q}{2\pi}\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\arctan(y/x)$$

$$= U + \frac{Q}{2\pi} \cdot \frac{1}{1+(y/x)^2} \cdot \frac{1}{x} = U + \frac{Qx}{2\pi(x^2 + y^2)}$$

$$v = -\frac{\partial \psi}{\partial x} = 0 - \frac{Q}{2\pi}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\arctan(y/x)$$

$$= -\frac{Q}{2\pi} \cdot \frac{1}{1+(y/x)^2} \cdot \frac{-y}{x^2} = \frac{Qy}{2\pi(x^2 + y^2)}$$

**Physical interpretation**: Uniform flow plus source creates flow past a half-body (Rankine half-body).

**Answer:** $u = U + \frac{Qx}{2\pi(x^2+y^2)}$, $v = \frac{Qy}{2\pi(x^2+y^2)}$
