# Article 4.5: Lubrication Theory

When a thin fluid film separates two solid surfaces in relative motion, viscous stresses within the film generate **hydrodynamic pressure** capable of supporting substantial loads without solid-solid contact. This phenomenon—**hydrodynamic lubrication**—operates in journal bearings, thrust bearings, slider bearings, and biological joints. The key is geometry: converging gaps squeeze fluid, building pressure; diverging gaps expand fluid, dropping pressure. For thin films where $h \ll L$ (film thickness much smaller than bearing length), systematic asymptotic analysis reduces Navier-Stokes to the **Reynolds lubrication equation**: $\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left(h^3\frac{\partial p}{\partial x}\right) + \frac{\partial}{\partial z}\left(h^3\frac{\partial p}{\partial z}\right) = 6\mu U\frac{\partial h}{\partial x}$ for steady 2D, capturing how film geometry $h(x,z)$ and surface velocity $U$ generate pressure distribution $p(x,z)$. **Squeeze films**—time-dependent compression—add term $12\mu\frac{\partial h}{\partial t}$ to the right side.

**Slider bearings** (inclined surfaces) exemplify hydrodynamic action: converging gap generates positive pressure, diverging gap drops pressure, net upward force supports load. **Load capacity** $W = \int\int (p - p_a)dA$ peaks at optimal inclination (typically $h_{exit}/h_{inlet} \approx 0.5$ for maximum $W$). **Coefficient of friction** $\mu_f = F/W$ in lubricated bearing drops dramatically below dry friction: $\mu_f \sim 10^{-3}$ vs. $\mu_{dry} \sim 0.1-1$ (factor of 100-1000 reduction!). **Journal bearings** (shaft rotating in cylindrical sleeve with small clearance) develop eccentric pressure distribution supporting radial load. The **Sommerfeld number** $S = \frac{\mu N}{P}(\frac{R}{c})^2$ (where $N$ = rotation rate, $P$ = load per projected area, $c$ = radial clearance) governs performance: large $S$ → thick film, small friction; small $S$ → thin film, risk of contact.

This article derives Reynolds lubrication equation via **asymptotic reduction** of Navier-Stokes for thin films ($h/L \ll 1$), analyzes slider bearing with linear taper including pressure distribution $p(x)$ and integrated load capacity, treats squeeze film dynamics with time-dependent $h(t)$ and damping, discusses journal bearing geometry and Sommerfeld analysis, introduces cavitation (negative pressure → liquid vaporizes/gas dissolves out) and Reynolds boundary condition ($p = p_{cav}$ where film ruptures), covers tribological applications (minimizing friction/wear via fluid films), and works through extensive examples involving Reynolds equation solutions, load calculations, friction coefficients, optimal geometries, squeeze film forces/times, journal bearing design parameters, and practical lubrication system analysis. The mathematics involves **elliptic PDEs (Reynolds equation), integration for forces/moments, perturbation methods (small clearance parameter $c/R$), and asymptotic analysis**.

## Thin Film Assumption

**Aspect ratio**: $\epsilon = h/L \ll 1$ where $h$ = typical film thickness, $L$ = bearing length.

**Scaling** (order of magnitude):
- Streamwise: $u \sim U$, $\frac{\partial}{\partial x} \sim 1/L$
- Normal: $v \sim ?$, $\frac{\partial}{\partial y} \sim 1/h$

Continuity: $\frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial v}{\partial y} \sim \frac{U}{L} + \frac{v}{h} = 0$

$$v \sim U\frac{h}{L} = U\epsilon \ll U$$

**Momentum** ($x$-component):
$$u\frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + v\frac{\partial u}{\partial y} \sim -\frac{1}{\rho}\frac{\partial p}{\partial x} + \nu\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial y^2}$$

Orders: $\frac{U^2}{L}$, $U\epsilon\frac{U}{h} = \frac{U^2\epsilon}{h}$, $\frac{\Delta p}{\rho L}$, $\nu\frac{U}{h^2}$

**Dominant balance**: Pressure gradient balances viscous diffusion.

$$\frac{\Delta p}{L} \sim \frac{\mu U}{h^2}$$

**Momentum** ($y$-component):
$$\frac{\partial p}{\partial y} \sim \rho v\frac{\partial v}{\partial y} \sim \rho\frac{(U\epsilon)^2}{h} \sim \frac{\Delta p\epsilon^2}{L}$$

**Negligible** → $\frac{\partial p}{\partial y} \approx 0$ → **pressure constant across film**.

## Reynolds Lubrication Equation Derivation

**Simplified momentum** ($x$-direction):

$$\frac{\partial p}{\partial x} = \mu\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial y^2}$$

Integrate twice with BCs ($u = 0$ at $y = 0$, $u = U$ at $y = h$):

$$u = \frac{1}{2\mu}\frac{\partial p}{\partial x}y(y - h) + U\frac{y}{h}$$

**Flow rate per unit width**:
$$q = \int_0^h u \, dy = -\frac{h^3}{12\mu}\frac{\partial p}{\partial x} + \frac{Uh}{2}$$

Couette term (shear) + Poiseuille term (pressure).

**Continuity** (incompressible, steady):

$$\frac{\partial q}{\partial x} = 0$$

$$\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left(-\frac{h^3}{12\mu}\frac{\partial p}{\partial x} + \frac{Uh}{2}\right) = 0$$

**Reynolds equation** (1D):

$$\boxed{\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left(h^3\frac{\partial p}{\partial x}\right) = 6\mu U\frac{\partial h}{\partial x}}$$

**2D version** (including $z$-direction):

$$\boxed{\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left(h^3\frac{\partial p}{\partial x}\right) + \frac{\partial}{\partial z}\left(h^3\frac{\partial p}{\partial z}\right) = 6\mu U\frac{\partial h}{\partial x}}$$

**Squeeze film** (time-dependent):

$$\boxed{\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left(h^3\frac{\partial p}{\partial x}\right) = 12\mu\frac{\partial h}{\partial t}}$$

(no surface motion, only compression/expansion)

## Slider Bearing (Inclined Plane)

**Geometry**: $h(x) = h_1 + (h_2 - h_1)\frac{x}{L}$ (linear taper)

Let $h_1$ = inlet thickness, $h_2$ = exit thickness.

**Reynolds equation**:
$$\frac{d}{dx}\left(h^3\frac{dp}{dx}\right) = 6\mu U\frac{dh}{dx}$$

$$\frac{dh}{dx} = \frac{h_2 - h_1}{L}$$

**Boundary conditions**: $p(0) = p(L) = p_a$ (atmospheric at ends)

**Solution**: Integrate once:
$$h^3\frac{dp}{dx} = 6\mu U(h - h_1) + C_1$$

At $x = 0$: $h = h_1$, $\frac{dp}{dx}$ finite → $C_1 = 0$

$$\frac{dp}{dx} = \frac{6\mu U(h - h_1)}{h^3}$$

Integrate (messy algebra):

$$p(x) = p_a + 6\mu UL\frac{h - h_1}{h_2 - h_1}\cdot\frac{1}{h_1 h_2}\left[\frac{h_2 + h_1}{2h} - \frac{1}{h_1}\ln\frac{h}{h_1}\right]$$

**Load capacity** (per unit width):

$$W = \int_0^L (p - p_a)dx$$

After integration:

$$\boxed{W = \frac{6\mu U L^2}{(h_2 - h_1)^2}\ln\frac{h_1}{h_2}\left(1 - \frac{2(h_2 - h_1)}{h_1 + h_2}\cdot\frac{1}{\ln(h_1/h_2)}\right)}$$

**Optimal taper**: $h_2/h_1 \approx 0.5$ maximizes $W$.

## Journal Bearing

**Geometry**: Shaft radius $R$, bearing radius $R + c$ where $c \ll R$ (clearance).

Shaft center displaced by eccentricity $e < c$.

**Film thickness**: $h(\theta) = c(1 + \epsilon\cos\theta)$ where $\epsilon = e/c$ (eccentricity ratio).

**Sommerfeld number**:

$$\boxed{S = \frac{\mu N}{P}\left(\frac{R}{c}\right)^2}$$

where $N$ = rotation rate (rev/s), $P$ = load per projected area.

**Large $S$**: Thick film, low friction, $\epsilon$ small
**Small $S$**: Thin film, high friction, risk of contact ($\epsilon \to 1$)

**Pressure distribution**: Eccentric → pressure builds on converging side, supports load.

**Friction coefficient**:

$$\mu_f \sim \frac{c}{R}\frac{1}{S} \sim 10^{-3}$$

(extremely low compared to dry friction!)

## Squeeze Film

**Setup**: Two parallel plates, film thickness $h(t)$ decreasing.

**Reynolds**:
$$\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left(h^3\frac{\partial p}{\partial x}\right) = 12\mu\frac{\partial h}{\partial t}$$

For circular plates (radius $R$), axisymmetric:

$$\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left(rh^3\frac{\partial p}{\partial r}\right) = 12\mu\frac{dh}{dt}$$

**Boundary**: $p(R) = p_a$

**Solution**: $p(r) = p_a + \frac{3\mu}{2h^3}\frac{dh}{dt}(R^2 - r^2)$

**Load capacity**:

$$W = \int_0^R (p - p_a)\cdot 2\pi r \, dr = \frac{3\pi\mu R^4}{2h^3}\left(-\frac{dh}{dt}\right)$$

**Force resists compression**: $F = -W$ (squeeze film force opposes approach).

**Application**: Damping in squeeze film dampers (turbomachinery).

## Cavitation

When pressure drops to **vapor pressure** $p_v$, liquid **cavitates** (vaporizes/gas evolves).

**Reynolds boundary condition**: Where $p = p_v$, film ruptures → cavitated region.

In cavitated zone: $p = p_v$ (constant), no pressure gradient, flow rate from mass conservation.

**Swift-Stieber condition**: Pressure and gradient vanish smoothly at rupture: $p = p_v$, $\frac{dp}{dx} = 0$.

**Practical**: Cavitation limits load capacity, can cause erosion/noise.

## Example 1: Slider Bearing Pressure Maximum

Linear taper slider: $h_1 = 0.002$ m, $h_2 = 0.001$ m, $L = 0.1$ m.

Where does maximum pressure occur?

**Solution:**

Pressure rises from inlet, peaks somewhere mid-bearing, drops to exit.

From theory: Maximum near $x/L \approx 0.6$ for $h_2/h_1 = 0.5$.

**Numerical integration** required for exact location.

**Answer:** Maximum near $x/L \approx 0.6$

## Example 2: Load Capacity Calculation

Slider bearing: $L = 0.1$ m, width $W = 0.05$ m, $U = 10$ m/s, oil $\mu = 0.05$ Pa·s.

$h_1 = 100$ μm, $h_2 = 50$ μm.

Find total load.

**Solution:**

Use formula (per unit width):

$$W' = \frac{6\mu UL^2}{(h_2 - h_1)^2}\ln\frac{h_1}{h_2} \cdot (\text{correction factor})$$

**Approximate** (neglecting log correction for estimate):

$$W' \sim \frac{6\mu UL^2}{h_1 h_2} \sim \frac{6(0.05)(10)(0.01)}{(10^{-4})(5 \times 10^{-5})}$$

$$= \frac{0.03}{5 \times 10^{-9}} = 6 \times 10^6 \text{ N/m}$$

Total: $W_{total} = W' \cdot W = (6 \times 10^6)(0.05) = 3 \times 10^5$ N = 300 kN

**Huge load from thin film**!

**Answer:** $W \sim 300$ kN

## Example 3: Sommerfeld Number

Journal bearing: $R = 0.05$ m, $c = 0.0001$ m, $N = 30$ rev/s, $P = 10^6$ Pa, oil $\mu = 0.02$ Pa·s.

Find $S$.

**Solution:**

$$S = \frac{\mu N}{P}\left(\frac{R}{c}\right)^2 = \frac{(0.02)(30)}{10^6}\left(\frac{0.05}{0.0001}\right)^2$$

$$= \frac{0.6}{10^6}(500)^2 = \frac{0.6 \cdot 2.5 \times 10^5}{10^6} = 0.15$$

**Moderate $S$** → moderate film thickness, acceptable operation.

**Answer:** $S = 0.15$

---

## Example 4: Friction Coefficient Estimate

For Example 3, estimate $\mu_f$.

**Solution:**

$$\mu_f \sim \frac{c}{R}\frac{1}{S} = \frac{0.0001}{0.05}\frac{1}{0.15}$$

$$= 0.002 \cdot 6.67 = 0.0133$$

$$\approx 0.013$$

**Very low** compared to dry friction ($\mu_{dry} \sim 0.1-0.5$).

**Answer:** $\mu_f \approx 0.013$

## Example 5: Squeeze Film Force

Circular plates $R = 0.05$ m, $h = 0.001$ m, approach velocity $\frac{dh}{dt} = -0.01$ m/s, oil $\mu = 0.1$ Pa·s.

Find resistive force.

**Solution:**

$$W = \frac{3\pi\mu R^4}{2h^3}\left(-\frac{dh}{dt}\right)$$

$$= \frac{3\pi(0.1)(0.05)^4}{2(0.001)^3}(0.01)$$

$$= \frac{3\pi(0.1)(6.25 \times 10^{-7})}{2 \times 10^{-9}}(0.01)$$

$$= \frac{3\pi \cdot 6.25 \times 10^{-8}}{2 \times 10^{-9}}(0.01)$$

$$= \frac{5.89 \times 10^{-7}}{2 \times 10^{-9}}(0.01) = 2945 \text{ N} \approx 3 \text{ kN}$$

**Strong damping force**!

**Answer:** $F = 3$ kN

## Example 6: Optimal Slider Taper

Show that $h_2/h_1 \approx 0.5$ maximizes load (qualitatively).

**Solution:**

**Too flat** ($h_2/h_1 \to 1$): Little convergence → small pressure → low load.

**Too steep** ($h_2/h_1 \to 0$): Exit very thin → cavitation, large pressure gradients but small volume → medium load.

**Optimal**: Balance between pressure magnitude and pressurized volume.

**Theory/numerics**: Maximum at $h_2/h_1 \approx 0.5$.

**Answer:** Optimal taper ratio ≈ 0.5

## Example 7: Couette vs. Poiseuille Flow Rates

In lubrication film, compare shear-driven vs. pressure-driven contributions.

**Solution:**

$$q = -\frac{h^3}{12\mu}\frac{dp}{dx} + \frac{Uh}{2}$$

**Couette** (shear): $q_C = \frac{Uh}{2}$

**Poiseuille** (pressure): $q_P = -\frac{h^3}{12\mu}\frac{dp}{dx}$

**Typical lubrication**: $|q_P| \sim |q_C|$ (both important).

**Depends on geometry**: Steep taper → large $\frac{dp}{dx}$ → $q_P$ dominates locally.

**Answer:** Both contributions comparable

## Example 8: Cavitation Pressure

Oil vapor pressure $p_v = 5$ kPa (abs). Atmospheric $p_a = 101$ kPa.

If film pressure drops to what gauge pressure does cavitation start?

**Solution:**

Cavitation at $p = p_v = 5$ kPa (abs).

Gauge: $p_{gauge} = p_{abs} - p_a = 5 - 101 = -96$ kPa

**Large suction** (negative gauge pressure).

**Answer:** $p_{gauge} = -96$ kPa

## Example 9: Journal Bearing Power Loss

Bearing: torque $T = 10$ N·m, $N = 20$ rev/s.

Find power dissipated.

**Solution:**

$$\dot{W} = T\Omega = T(2\pi N) = (10)(2\pi \cdot 20) = (10)(125.7) = 1257 \text{ W} \approx 1.3 \text{ kW}$$

**Dissipated as heat** in oil film → need cooling!

**Answer:** $\dot{W} = 1.3$ kW

Journal bearing must keep $\epsilon < 0.8$ (avoid contact).

If $c = 0.0002$ m, maximum eccentricity?

**Solution:**

$$\epsilon = \frac{e}{c} < 0.8$$

$$e < 0.8c = 0.8(0.0002) = 0.00016 \text{ m} = 0.16 \text{ mm}$$

**Answer:** $e_{max} = 0.16$ mm

## Example 11: Reynolds Equation Nondimensionalization

Nondimensionalize slider bearing Reynolds equation.

**Solution:**

Define: $\bar{x} = x/L$, $\bar{h} = h/h_1$, $\bar{p} = (p - p_a)h_1^2/(\mu UL)$

Reynolds: $\frac{d}{dx}\left(h^3\frac{dp}{dx}\right) = 6\mu U\frac{dh}{dx}$

becomes:

$$\frac{d}{d\bar{x}}\left(\bar{h}^3\frac{d\bar{p}}{d\bar{x}}\right) = 6\frac{d\bar{h}}{d\bar{x}}$$

**Dimensionless** → solution depends only on $h_2/h_1$ (geometry ratio).

**Answer:** Nondimensional form removes parameters

## Example 12: Squeeze Film Damping Time

Plates $R = 0.03$ m, initial $h_0 = 0.001$ m, load $W = 1000$ N, $\mu = 0.05$ Pa·s.

Estimate time to reach $h = 0.0005$ m.

**Solution:**

From squeeze film:
$$W = \frac{3\pi\mu R^4}{2h^3}\left(-\frac{dh}{dt}\right)$$

$$\frac{dh}{dt} = -\frac{2Wh^3}{3\pi\mu R^4}$$

Separate/integrate:

$$\int_{h_0}^{h_f} \frac{dh}{h^3} = -\frac{2W}{3\pi\mu R^4}\int_0^t dt$$

$$\left[-\frac{1}{2h^2}\right]_{h_0}^{h_f} = -\frac{2Wt}{3\pi\mu R^4}$$

$$\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{h_f^2} - \frac{1}{h_0^2}\right) = \frac{2Wt}{3\pi\mu R^4}$$

$$t = \frac{3\pi\mu R^4}{4W}\left(\frac{1}{h_f^2} - \frac{1}{h_0^2}\right)$$

$$= \frac{3\pi(0.05)(0.03)^4}{4(1000)}\left(\frac{1}{(5 \times 10^{-4})^2} - \frac{1}{(10^{-3})^2}\right)$$

$$= \frac{3\pi(0.05)(8.1 \times 10^{-7})}{4000}\left(4 \times 10^6 - 10^6\right)$$

$$= \frac{1.27 \times 10^{-7}}{4000}(3 \times 10^6) = 9.5 \times 10^{-2} \text{ s} = 95 \text{ ms}$$

**Answer:** $t \approx 95$ ms

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: B** - Reynolds equation derived via asymptotic analysis assuming thin film: $\epsilon = h/L \ll 1$ allows neglecting certain terms in N-S, leading to simplified lubrication equation.

**Problem 2: B** - Optimal slider bearing taper for maximum load capacity: $h_2/h_1 \approx 0.5$ balances pressure magnitude with pressurized volume.

**Problem 3: E** - Sommerfeld number: $S = \frac{\mu N}{P}(R/c)^2$ is proportional to viscosity $\mu$ and rotation rate $N$, inversely to load/pressure $P$.

**Problem 4: C** - Squeeze film: resists normal approach of surfaces (plates compressing film) via viscous pressure generation, provides damping.

**Problem 5: C** - Cavitation: when local pressure drops to vapor pressure $p_v$, liquid vaporizes or dissolved gas comes out of solution, film ruptures.
