# Article 6.1: Thermodynamics of Compressible Flow

High-speed flows where density varies significantly with pressure demand thermodynamic coupling between mechanical and thermal phenomena—compressibility becomes paramount. Unlike incompressible flow where $\rho = \text{const}$, **compressible flow** requires tracking density $\rho(P,T)$ via **equation of state** $P = \rho RT$ (ideal gas), linking pressure, density, and temperature. The **speed of sound** $c = \sqrt{\gamma RT}$—information propagation speed through fluid—sets fundamental timescale; fluid velocity $U$ compared to $c$ defines **Mach number** $\text{Ma} = U/c$, the single most important parameter in gas dynamics. **Subsonic** ($\text{Ma} < 1$), **transonic** ($\text{Ma} \approx 1$), **supersonic** ($\text{Ma} > 1$), and **hypersonic** ($\text{Ma} > 5$) regimes exhibit qualitatively different physics: disturbances propagate upstream in subsonic flow but cannot in supersonic; shock waves (discontinuous compressions) appear only supersonically; aerodynamic heating becomes critical at hypersonic speeds.

**Isentropic relations** $P/\rho^\gamma = \text{const}$ for reversible adiabatic processes link thermodynamic states without entropy generation. **Stagnation properties** $T_0$, $P_0$ (values if flow brought to rest isentropically) provide convenient reference states: $T_0 = T(1 + \frac{\gamma-1}{2}\text{Ma}^2)$, $P_0 = P(1 + \frac{\gamma-1}{2}\text{Ma}^2)^{\gamma/(\gamma-1)}$—critical for nozzle analysis and Bernoulli-like relations in compressible flow. The **specific heat ratio** $\gamma = c_p/c_v$ (typically 1.4 for air) governs compressibility effects; monatomic gases ($\gamma = 5/3$) differ from diatomic ($\gamma = 7/5$). **Enthalpy** $h = c_pT$ (energy including pressure work) proves more useful than internal energy $e = c_vT$ for open systems; total enthalpy $h_0 = h + \frac{1}{2}u^2$ conserved in adiabatic flow.

This article develops ideal gas equation of state and thermodynamic properties ($e$, $h$, $c_p$, $c_v$, $\gamma$), derives **speed of sound** from isentropic bulk modulus $c = \sqrt{(\partial P/\partial\rho)_s}$, introduces **Mach number** and flow regime classification, develops **isentropic relations** for pressure, temperature, and density ratios, derives **stagnation property formulas** with detailed thermodynamic arguments, discusses entropy and second law constraints, treats calorically perfect gas assumptions, and works through extensive examples involving Mach number calculations, stagnation properties, isentropic state changes, sound speed variations, and compressibility parameter estimation.

## Ideal Gas Equation of State

**Perfect gas law**:
$$\boxed{P = \rho RT}$$

where $R = R_u/M$ (specific gas constant), $R_u = 8314$ J/(kmol·K) (universal), $M$ = molecular weight.

**For air**: $R = 287$ J/(kg·K), $M = 29$ kg/kmol

**Links** three state variables: specify two, get third.

## Example 1: Density from Ideal Gas Law

Air at $P = 101.3$ kPa, $T = 300$ K.

Find density.

**Solution:**

$$\rho = \frac{P}{RT} = \frac{101,300}{(287)(300)} = \frac{101,300}{86,100} = 1.176 \text{ kg/m}^3$$

**Close to standard** $\rho_0 = 1.225$ kg/m³ at sea level.

**Answer:** $\rho = 1.18$ kg/m³

---

## Specific Heats and Internal Energy

**Internal energy** (translational + rotational + vibrational):
$$e = c_v T$$

**Enthalpy** (includes flow work):
$$h = e + \frac{P}{\rho} = e + RT = c_p T$$

**Specific heats**:
- Constant volume: $c_v = \left(\frac{\partial e}{\partial T}\right)_v$
- Constant pressure: $c_p = \left(\frac{\partial h}{\partial T}\right)_P$

**Relation**: $c_p - c_v = R$ (Mayer's relation)

**Specific heat ratio**:
$$\boxed{\gamma = \frac{c_p}{c_v}}$$

**Typical values**:
- Air (diatomic): $\gamma = 1.4$, $c_p = 1005$ J/(kg·K), $c_v = 718$ J/(kg·K)
- Monatomic (He, Ar): $\gamma = 5/3 = 1.667$
- Triatomic (CO₂): $\gamma \approx 1.3$

## Speed of Sound Derivation

**Physical**: Speed at which small pressure disturbances propagate.

**Thermodynamic definition**:
$$c = \sqrt{\left(\frac{\partial P}{\partial \rho}\right)_s}$$

(isentropic derivative—disturbances too fast for heat transfer)

For ideal gas with $P/\rho^\gamma = \text{const}$:

$$P = C\rho^\gamma$$

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial \rho} = C\gamma\rho^{\gamma-1} = \gamma\frac{P}{\rho}$$

Using $P = \rho RT$:

$$c^2 = \gamma\frac{P}{\rho} = \gamma RT$$

$$\boxed{c = \sqrt{\gamma RT}}$$

**Temperature dependence**: $c \propto \sqrt{T}$

## Example 2: Speed of Sound Calculation

Air at $T = 288$ K (15°C).

Find speed of sound.

**Solution:**

$$c = \sqrt{\gamma RT} = \sqrt{(1.4)(287)(288)}$$

$$= \sqrt{115,660.8} = 340.1 \text{ m/s}$$

**Standard sea level**: $c_0 = 340$ m/s (close match!)

**At altitude** ($T$ lower): sound speed decreases.

**Answer:** $c = 340$ m/s

---

## Mach Number

**Definition**:
$$\boxed{\text{Ma} = \frac{U}{c}}$$

**Flow regimes**:
- **Subsonic**: $\text{Ma} < 1$ (disturbances propagate upstream)
- **Transonic**: $0.8 < \text{Ma} < 1.2$ (mixed subsonic/supersonic)
- **Supersonic**: $1 < \text{Ma} < 5$ (shock waves form)
- **Hypersonic**: $\text{Ma} > 5$ (severe heating, real gas effects)

**Critical**: Determines whether flow can "communicate" upstream.

**Compressibility parameter**: $\frac{\Delta\rho}{\rho} \sim \text{Ma}^2$ (for small Ma)

## Example 3: Mach Number for Aircraft

Aircraft flying at $U = 250$ m/s at altitude where $T = 250$ K.

Find Mach number.

**Solution:**

$$c = \sqrt{(1.4)(287)(250)} = \sqrt{100,450} = 317 \text{ m/s}$$

$$\text{Ma} = \frac{250}{317} = 0.789$$

**High subsonic** (approaching transonic buffet).

**Answer:** $\text{Ma} = 0.79$

---

## Isentropic Relations

**Entropy**: $s = c_p\ln T - R\ln P + \text{const}$

**Isentropic** ($ds = 0$): Reversible adiabatic

From $ds = 0$:

$$c_p\frac{dT}{T} = R\frac{dP}{P}$$

$$\frac{dT}{T} = \frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}\frac{dP}{P}$$

Integrate:

$$\boxed{\frac{T_2}{T_1} = \left(\frac{P_2}{P_1}\right)^{(\gamma-1)/\gamma}}$$

Also:

$$\boxed{\frac{P}{\rho^\gamma} = \text{const}}$$

$$\boxed{\frac{T_2}{T_1} = \left(\frac{\rho_2}{\rho_1}\right)^{\gamma-1}}$$

**Poisson's relations**—fundamental for compressible flow analysis.

## Example 4: Isentropic Compression

Gas compressed isentropically from $P_1 = 100$ kPa, $T_1 = 300$ K to $P_2 = 500$ kPa.

Find $T_2$ (air, $\gamma = 1.4$).

**Solution:**

$$T_2 = T_1\left(\frac{P_2}{P_1}\right)^{(\gamma-1)/\gamma} = 300\left(\frac{500}{100}\right)^{0.4/1.4}$$

$$= 300(5)^{2/7} = 300(5)^{0.2857} = 300(1.584) = 475.2 \text{ K}$$

**Significant heating** from compression work.

**Answer:** $T_2 = 475$ K

---

## Stagnation Properties

**Stagnation state**: Where flow brought to rest ($u = 0$) **isentropically**.

**Energy equation** (adiabatic):
$$h + \frac{u^2}{2} = h_0 = \text{const}$$

$$c_pT + \frac{u^2}{2} = c_pT_0$$

$$\boxed{T_0 = T + \frac{u^2}{2c_p} = T\left(1 + \frac{\gamma-1}{2}\frac{u^2}{\gamma RT}\right) = T\left(1 + \frac{\gamma-1}{2}\text{Ma}^2\right)}$$

**Stagnation pressure** (from isentropic relation):

$$\boxed{P_0 = P\left(1 + \frac{\gamma-1}{2}\text{Ma}^2\right)^{\gamma/(\gamma-1)}}$$

**Stagnation density**:

$$\boxed{\rho_0 = \rho\left(1 + \frac{\gamma-1}{2}\text{Ma}^2\right)^{1/(\gamma-1)}}$$

**Total quantities** remain constant in adiabatic flow; very useful!

## Example 5: Stagnation Temperature

Supersonic flow: $U = 600$ m/s, $T = 250$ K (air).

Find $T_0$.

**Solution:**

$$c = \sqrt{(1.4)(287)(250)} = 317 \text{ m/s}$$

$$\text{Ma} = \frac{600}{317} = 1.89$$

$$T_0 = T\left(1 + \frac{0.4}{2}(1.89)^2\right) = 250(1 + 0.2 \times 3.576)$$

$$= 250(1 + 0.715) = 250(1.715) = 428.75 \text{ K}$$

**Significant kinetic heating** at supersonic speeds!

**Answer:** $T_0 = 429$ K

---

## Compressibility Effects

**Dynamic pressure**: $q = \frac{1}{2}\rho u^2$

**Compressible correction**:

$$\frac{P_0 - P}{q} = \left(1 + \frac{\gamma-1}{2}\text{Ma}^2\right)^{\gamma/(\gamma-1)} - 1 \approx 1 + \frac{\text{Ma}^2}{4} + \ldots$$

**For Ma < 0.3**: Incompressible approximation valid (density change $< 5$%).

**For Ma > 0.3**: Must account for compressibility.

## Example 6: Stagnation Pressure Ratio

Flow at $\text{Ma} = 0.5$.

Find $P_0/P$.

**Solution:**

$$\frac{P_0}{P} = \left(1 + 0.2(0.5)^2\right)^{3.5} = (1 + 0.05)^{3.5}$$

$$= (1.05)^{3.5} = 1.190$$

**19% increase** from dynamic pressure.

**Compare incompressible**: $P_0/P \approx 1 + \frac{1}{2}\gamma\text{Ma}^2 = 1 + 0.175 = 1.175$

**Good agreement** at moderate Ma.

**Answer:** $P_0/P = 1.19$

---

## Entropy and Second Law

**Entropy change**:

$$s_2 - s_1 = c_p\ln\frac{T_2}{T_1} - R\ln\frac{P_2}{P_1}$$

**Second law**: $\Delta s \geq 0$ (equality for reversible)

**Isentropic**: Ideal limit (no losses)

**Real flows**: Shocks, friction, heat transfer increase entropy.

## Example 7: Temperature from Mach Number

At $\text{Ma} = 2$, $T_0 = 500$ K.

Find static temperature $T$.

**Solution:**

$$T = \frac{T_0}{1 + \frac{\gamma-1}{2}\text{Ma}^2} = \frac{500}{1 + 0.2(4)} = \frac{500}{1.8} = 277.8 \text{ K}$$

**Lower static T** due to expansion (KE increase).

**Answer:** $T = 278$ K

---

## Calorically Perfect Gas

**Assumption**: $c_p$, $c_v$ constant (independent of $T$).

**Valid**: Moderate temperatures ($T < 1000$ K for air).

**High $T$**: Vibrational modes activate → $\gamma$ decreases.

**Very high $T$** ($> 2000$ K): Dissociation, ionization → "real gas" effects.

## Sound Speed Variation

$$c = \sqrt{\gamma RT} \propto \sqrt{T}$$

**Altitude**: $T$ drops → $c$ drops.

**Standard atmosphere**:
- Sea level: $c = 340$ m/s
- 11 km (tropopause): $c = 295$ m/s

## Example 8: Mach Number vs. Velocity

At what velocity is $\text{Ma} = 1$ at $T = 200$ K vs. $T = 300$ K?

**Solution:**

**At $T = 200$ K**:
$$c = \sqrt{(1.4)(287)(200)} = \sqrt{80,360} = 283.5 \text{ m/s}$$

**At $T = 300$ K**:
$$c = \sqrt{(1.4)(287)(300)} = \sqrt{120,540} = 347.2 \text{ m/s}$$

**22% difference** in sonic velocity!

**Answer:** 284 m/s vs. 347 m/s

---

## Stagnation Enthalpy

**Total enthalpy**:
$$h_0 = h + \frac{u^2}{2} = c_pT_0$$

**Conserved** in adiabatic flow (with or without losses).

**Energy equation**: $h_{01} = h_{02}$ (no heat transfer)

**Useful**: Even through shocks, $h_0$ constant (though $P_0$ drops due to entropy rise).

## Example 9: Incompressible Limit

Show that for $\text{Ma} \to 0$, compressible Bernoulli → incompressible Bernoulli.

**Solution:**

$$P_0 = P\left(1 + \frac{\gamma-1}{2}\text{Ma}^2\right)^{\gamma/(\gamma-1)}$$

**Expand** for small Ma:

$$\ln(P_0/P) = \frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}\ln\left(1 + \frac{\gamma-1}{2}\text{Ma}^2\right)$$

$$\approx \frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1} \cdot \frac{\gamma-1}{2}\text{Ma}^2 = \frac{\gamma}{2}\text{Ma}^2$$

$$P_0 \approx P\left(1 + \frac{\gamma}{2}\text{Ma}^2\right) = P + \frac{\gamma}{2}P\text{Ma}^2$$

$$= P + \frac{\gamma}{2}\rho RT \cdot \frac{u^2}{\gamma RT} = P + \frac{1}{2}\rho u^2$$

$$\boxed{P_0 = P + \frac{1}{2}\rho u^2}$$

**Incompressible Bernoulli** recovered!

**Answer:** Limit gives $P_0 = P + \frac{1}{2}\rho u^2$ ✓

---

## Example 10: Critical Mach Number

For $\gamma = 1.4$, at what Ma is density change 5%?

**Solution:**

$$\frac{\rho_0}{\rho} = \left(1 + 0.2\text{Ma}^2\right)^{1/0.4} = \left(1 + 0.2\text{Ma}^2\right)^{2.5}$$

Set $\rho_0/\rho = 1.05$:

$$1.05 = \left(1 + 0.2\text{Ma}^2\right)^{2.5}$$

$$(1.05)^{1/2.5} = 1 + 0.2\text{Ma}^2$$

$$1.0196 = 1 + 0.2\text{Ma}^2$$

$$\text{Ma}^2 = \frac{0.0196}{0.2} = 0.098$$

$$\text{Ma} = 0.313$$

**Below Ma ≈ 0.3**: Incompressible approximation acceptable.

**Answer:** $\text{Ma} \approx 0.31$

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: C** - Speed of sound: $c = \sqrt{\gamma RT}$ depends only on **temperature** (for given gas with fixed $\gamma$ and $R$). Pressure and density cancel via ideal gas law.

**Problem 2: B** - Ma = 0.85 is **high subsonic** approaching **transonic** regime (typically 0.8 < Ma < 1.2), where mixed flow and shock formation begin.

**Problem 3: B** - Stagnation temperature: $T_0 = T(1 + \frac{\gamma-1}{2}\text{Ma}^2)$ **increases with Ma** (kinetic energy converted to thermal energy in stagnation process).

**Problem 4: B** - Isentropic relation: $P/\rho^\gamma = \text{const}$ for reversible adiabatic process (entropy constant), fundamental to compressible flow analysis.

**Problem 5: C** - Air (diatomic): $\gamma = c_p/c_v = 1.4$ at moderate temperatures. Monatomic gases have $\gamma = 5/3$, polyatomic lower (~1.3).
