# Article 4.2: Laminar Boundary Layers: Prandtl's Theory

At high Reynolds numbers, the full Navier-Stokes equations simplify dramatically near solid surfaces where viscous effects concentrate in a thin **boundary layer**. This revolutionary concept, introduced by **Ludwig Prandtl in 1904**, recognized that for $\text{Re} \gg 1$, the flow naturally divides into two regions: a thin viscous layer adhering to the surface where velocity transitions from zero (no-slip) to the freestream value, and an outer inviscid region where potential flow theory applies. The **boundary layer thickness** $\delta$ scales as $\delta/L \sim \text{Re}^{-1/2}$, becoming vanishingly thin at high Reynolds numbers yet containing all viscous effects. Within this layer, convective and viscous terms balance; outside, viscosity is negligible. This asymptotic structure—**matched asymptotic expansions** connecting inner boundary layer and outer potential flow—provides the mathematical framework for solving high-Re flows analytically.

The **Prandtl boundary layer equations**, derived via systematic order-of-magnitude analysis exploiting $\delta \ll L$, reduce the full Navier-Stokes to a parabolic system amenable to marching solutions. Key simplifications: transverse velocity $v \ll u$, pressure constant across layer (set by outer inviscid flow via Bernoulli), and streamwise diffusion negligible compared to transverse. The **Blasius solution** for flow past a flat plate—obtained through ingenious similarity transformation collapsing $(x,y)$ dependence into single variable $\eta = y\sqrt{U/(\nu x)}$—yields the paradigmatic solution: $\delta = 5.0\sqrt{\nu x/U}$, skin friction $C_f = 0.664/\sqrt{\text{Re}_x}$. The **von Kármán momentum integral equation** provides an alternative approximate method, integrating across the layer to relate wall shear stress to momentum thickness evolution—invaluable when exact solutions fail. **Falkner-Skan solutions** extend Blasius to wedge flows with pressure gradients, revealing how favorable gradients thin the layer while adverse gradients thicken it toward separation.

This article derives Prandtl's boundary layer equations via **heavy asymptotic scaling analysis**, introduces displacement thickness $\delta^* = \int_0^\infty(1 - u/U)dy$ (effective blockage) and momentum thickness $\theta = \int_0^\infty(u/U)(1-u/U)dy$ (momentum deficit), develops the Blasius similarity solution in detail including the **nonlinear ODE** $f''' + \frac{1}{2}ff'' = 0$, explores Falkner-Skan solutions for wedge flows with pressure gradients governed by $f''' + ff'' + \beta(1-f'^2) = 0$, derives the von Kármán momentum integral equation $\frac{d\theta}{dx} + \frac{\theta}{U}\frac{dU}{dx}(H+2) = \frac{\tau_w}{\rho U^2}$, discusses matched asymptotic expansions connecting inner viscous and outer inviscid regions, treats boundary layer growth and skin friction calculations, and works through extensive examples involving similarity transformations, **numerical solutions via shooting methods**, integral methods, and displacement effects.

## Boundary Layer Concept and Scaling

For flow past flat plate at high $\text{Re} = UL/\nu \gg 1$:

**Key observation**: Viscous effects confined to thin layer $\delta \ll L$ near surface.

**Scaling arguments** (order of magnitude):

Streamwise: $u \sim U$, $\frac{\partial}{\partial x} \sim \frac{1}{L}$

Normal: $v \sim ?$, $\frac{\partial}{\partial y} \sim \frac{1}{\delta}$

From continuity: $\frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial v}{\partial y} = 0$

$$\frac{U}{L} \sim \frac{v}{\delta} \implies v \sim U\frac{\delta}{L} \ll U$$

Momentum equation terms:

Convection: $u\frac{\partial u}{\partial x} \sim \frac{U^2}{L}$

Transverse convection: $v\frac{\partial u}{\partial y} \sim U\frac{\delta}{L} \cdot \frac{U}{\delta} = \frac{U^2}{L}$ (same order!)

Viscous: $\nu\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial y^2} \sim \nu\frac{U}{\delta^2}$

**Balance** (convection ~ viscous):

$$\frac{U^2}{L} \sim \nu\frac{U}{\delta^2}$$

$$\boxed{\delta \sim \sqrt{\frac{\nu L}{U}} = \sqrt{\frac{L}{\text{Re}}}}$$

$$\frac{\delta}{L} \sim \text{Re}^{-1/2}$$

For $\text{Re} = 10^6$: $\delta/L \sim 10^{-3}$ (very thin!)

## Prandtl's Boundary Layer Equations

**Continuity**:
$$\boxed{\frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial v}{\partial y} = 0}$$

**Momentum** (x-component):
$$\boxed{u\frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + v\frac{\partial u}{\partial y} = U\frac{dU}{dx} + \nu\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial y^2}}$$

where $U(x) = U_e(x)$ is edge velocity from outer inviscid flow.

**Key simplifications** from full N-S:
1. $\frac{\partial P}{\partial y} = 0$ → pressure set by outer flow: $P(x) = P_e(x)$
2. From Bernoulli (outer): $U\frac{dU}{dx} = -\frac{1}{\rho}\frac{dP}{dx}$
3. $\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x^2}$ dropped (small by factor $(\delta/L)^2$)
4. **Parabolic** in $x$: can march from leading edge downstream

**Boundary conditions**:
- Wall: $u(x,0) = v(x,0) = 0$ (no-slip)
- Edge: $u(x,\infty) = U_e(x)$ (match outer flow)

## Displacement and Momentum Thickness

**Displacement thickness** $\delta^*$: effective outward shift of streamlines due to velocity deficit.

$$\boxed{\delta^* = \int_0^\infty \left(1 - \frac{u}{U}\right)dy}$$

**Physical meaning**: Distance by which outer flow is "displaced" outward to account for slower moving fluid in BL.

**Momentum thickness** $\theta$: momentum deficit in boundary layer.

$$\boxed{\theta = \int_0^\infty \frac{u}{U}\left(1 - \frac{u}{U}\right)dy}$$

**Physical meaning**: Distance carrying momentum equivalent to deficit.

**Shape factor**: $H = \delta^*/\theta$ (characterizes profile shape)

- Laminar Blasius: $H = 2.59$
- Turbulent: $H \approx 1.3-1.4$
- Near separation: $H \to \infty$ (large displacement, little momentum)

## Blasius Solution (Flat Plate, Zero Pressure Gradient)

For flat plate: $U_e = U$ (constant), $\frac{dU}{dx} = 0$

**Boundary layer equations**:
$$u\frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + v\frac{\partial u}{\partial y} = \nu\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial y^2}$$

**Similarity transformation**: Look for solution depending on single variable.

Define **similarity variable**:
$$\eta = y\sqrt{\frac{U}{\nu x}}$$

Define **stream function**: $\psi(x,y) = \sqrt{\nu U x} f(\eta)$

Then:
$$u = \frac{\partial\psi}{\partial y} = U f'(\eta)$$

$$v = -\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial x} = \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{\nu U}{x}}[\eta f'(\eta) - f(\eta)]$$

Substitute into momentum equation → **Blasius equation**:

$$\boxed{f''' + \frac{1}{2}ff'' = 0}$$

**Boundary conditions**:
- $f(0) = 0$ (no normal flow at wall)
- $f'(0) = 0$ (no-slip)
- $f'(\infty) = 1$ (match freestream)

**Solution**: Requires numerical integration (**shooting method**).

**Key result**: $f''(0) = 0.33206$ (universal constant!)

**Velocity profile**: $\frac{u}{U} = f'(\eta)$

**Boundary layer thickness** (where $u = 0.99U$):

At $\eta_{0.99} = 5.0$:
$$\boxed{\delta = 5.0\sqrt{\frac{\nu x}{U}} = \frac{5.0x}{\sqrt{\text{Re}_x}}}$$

**Displacement thickness**:
$$\delta^* = \sqrt{\frac{\nu x}{U}}\int_0^\infty (1 - f')d\eta = 1.721\sqrt{\frac{\nu x}{U}}$$

**Momentum thickness**:
$$\theta = \sqrt{\frac{\nu x}{U}}\int_0^\infty f'(1-f')d\eta = 0.664\sqrt{\frac{\nu x}{U}}$$

**Shape factor**: $H = 1.721/0.664 = 2.59$

**Skin friction coefficient**:
$$\tau_w = \mu\frac{\partial u}{\partial y}\bigg|_{y=0} = \mu U\sqrt{\frac{U}{\nu x}}f''(0) = 0.332\mu U\sqrt{\frac{U}{\nu x}}$$

$$C_f = \frac{\tau_w}{\frac{1}{2}\rho U^2} = \frac{0.664}{\sqrt{\text{Re}_x}}$$

$$\boxed{C_f = \frac{0.664}{\sqrt{\text{Re}_x}}}$$

## Falkner-Skan Solutions (Wedge Flows)

For **power-law outer velocity**: $U_e(x) = U_0 x^m$

**Pressure gradient**: $\frac{dU_e}{dx} = mU_0 x^{m-1}$

**Wedge parameter**: $\beta = \frac{2m}{m+1}$ (relates to wedge half-angle $\beta\pi/2$)

**Falkner-Skan equation**:
$$\boxed{f''' + ff'' + \beta(1 - f'^2) = 0}$$

**Special cases**:
- $\beta = 0$ ($m = 0$): Blasius (flat plate)
- $\beta = 1$ ($m = 1$): Hiemenz (stagnation point flow, $U \propto x$)
- $\beta > 0$: Favorable pressure gradient (accelerating flow) → thinner BL
- $\beta < 0$: Adverse pressure gradient (decelerating flow) → thicker BL

**Separation criterion**: For $\beta < \beta_{sep} \approx -0.0904$, no solution exists → boundary layer separates.

## Von Kármán Momentum Integral Equation

**Derivation**: Integrate BL momentum equation across layer.

Start with:
$$u\frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + v\frac{\partial u}{\partial y} = U\frac{dU}{dx} + \nu\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial y^2}$$

Integrate $\int_0^\infty (\cdot)dy$, use continuity, manipulate:

$$\boxed{\frac{d\theta}{dx} + \frac{\theta}{U}\frac{dU}{dx}(H + 2) = \frac{\tau_w}{\rho U^2}}$$

**Alternative form**:
$$\frac{d\theta}{dx} + (2 + H)\frac{\theta}{U}\frac{dU}{dx} = \frac{C_f}{2}$$

**Application**: Assume velocity profile shape (e.g., polynomial), calculate $\theta$ and $H$, relate $\tau_w$ to $\theta$, solve ODE for $\theta(x)$ or $\delta(x)$.

## Matched Asymptotic Expansions

**Inner problem** (boundary layer): $\bar{y} = y/\delta$ fixed as $\text{Re} \to \infty$

**Outer problem** (potential flow): $y/L$ fixed as $\text{Re} \to \infty$

**Matching principle**: Inner limit of outer solution = outer limit of inner solution

**Physical**: Outer flow sees wall displaced outward by $\delta^*(x)$ → effective body shape modified.

**Iterative solution**: Solve outer potential flow → get $U_e(x)$ → solve BL → get $\delta^*(x)$ → modify body → repeat.

## Example 1: Blasius at Specific Location

Flat plate, $U = 20$ m/s, $x = 0.5$ m (air $\nu = 1.5 \times 10^{-5}$ m²/s).

Find $\delta$, $\delta^*$, $\theta$, $C_f$.

**Solution:**

$$\text{Re}_x = \frac{Ux}{\nu} = \frac{(20)(0.5)}{1.5 \times 10^{-5}} = 6.67 \times 10^5$$

$$\delta = \frac{5.0x}{\sqrt{\text{Re}_x}} = \frac{5.0(0.5)}{\sqrt{6.67 \times 10^5}} = \frac{2.5}{816.5} = 0.00306 \text{ m} = 3.1 \text{ mm}$$

$$\delta^* = 1.721\sqrt{\frac{\nu x}{U}} = 1.721\sqrt{\frac{(1.5 \times 10^{-5})(0.5)}{20}} = 1.721\sqrt{3.75 \times 10^{-7}} = 1.05 \text{ mm}$$

$$\theta = 0.664\sqrt{\frac{\nu x}{U}} = 0.664(6.12 \times 10^{-4}) = 0.406 \text{ mm}$$

$$C_f = \frac{0.664}{\sqrt{6.67 \times 10^5}} = 8.13 \times 10^{-4}$$

$$H = \frac{\delta^*}{\theta} = \frac{1.05}{0.406} = 2.59$$ ✓

**Answer:** $\delta = 3.1$ mm, $\delta^* = 1.05$ mm, $\theta = 0.41$ mm, $C_f = 8.1 \times 10^{-4}$

## Example 2: Boundary Layer Growth

Compare $\delta$ at $x = 0.1$ m and $x = 1$ m for $U = 10$ m/s.

**Solution:**

$$\delta \propto \sqrt{x}$$

$$\frac{\delta_2}{\delta_1} = \sqrt{\frac{x_2}{x_1}} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{0.1}} = \sqrt{10} = 3.16$$

**Boundary layer thickens as $\sqrt{x}$** (diffusive spreading).

$$\delta_1 = 5.0\sqrt{\frac{(1.5 \times 10^{-5})(0.1)}{10}} = 6.1 \text{ mm}$$

$$\delta_2 = 3.16(6.1) = 19.3 \text{ mm}$$

**Answer:** $\delta$ grows by factor 3.16

## Example 3: Total Drag on Flat Plate

Plate length $L = 2$ m, width $W = 1$ m, $U = 30$ m/s (air), both sides.

**Solution:**

Drag per unit width (one side):
$$D' = \int_0^L \tau_w \, dx = \int_0^L C_f \cdot \frac{1}{2}\rho U^2 \, dx$$

$$= \int_0^L \frac{0.664}{\sqrt{U/(\ x)}} \cdot \frac{1}{2}\rho U^2 \, dx$$

$$= 0.664\sqrt{\frac{\nu}{U}} \cdot \frac{1}{2}\rho U^2 \int_0^L \frac{dx}{\sqrt{x}}$$

$$= 0.664\sqrt{\nu U} \cdot \frac{1}{2}\rho U^2 \cdot 2\sqrt{L}$$

$$= 0.664\rho U^2\sqrt{\nu UL}$$

$$= 0.664(1.2)(900)\sqrt{(1.5 \times 10^{-5})(30)(2)}$$

$$= 716.4\sqrt{9 \times 10^{-4}} = 716.4(0.03) = 21.5 \text{ N/m}$$

Total (both sides):
$$D = 2D'W = 2(21.5)(1) = 43 \text{ N}$$

**Answer:** $D = 43$ N

## Example 4: Similarity Variable Calculation

At $x = 0.2$ m, $y = 2$ mm, $U = 15$ m/s, find $\eta$ and $u/U$.

**Solution:**

$$\eta = y\sqrt{\frac{U}{\nu x}} = (0.002)\sqrt{\frac{15}{(1.5 \times 10^{-5})(0.2)}}$$

$$= 0.002\sqrt{\frac{15}{3 \times 10^{-6}}} = 0.002\sqrt{5 \times 10^6} = 0.002(2236) = 4.47$$

From Blasius table/numerical solution: $f'(4.47) \approx 0.95$

$$\frac{u}{U} = 0.95$$

**Near edge of boundary layer** ($\eta = 5$ gives $u/U = 0.99$).

**Answer:** $\eta = 4.47$, $u/U = 0.95$

## Example 5: Blasius ODE Numerical Shooting

Describe shooting method for $f''' + \frac{1}{2}ff'' = 0$.

**Solution:**

**Shooting method**:
1. Convert to first-order system: Let $f_1 = f$, $f_2 = f'$, $f_3 = f''$
   - $\frac{df_1}{d\eta} = f_2$
   - $\frac{df_2}{d\eta} = f_3$
   - $\frac{df_3}{d\eta} = -\frac{1}{2}f_1 f_3$

2. **ICs at $\eta = 0$**: $f_1(0) = 0$, $f_2(0) = 0$, $f_3(0) = s$ (guess)

3. **Integrate** to large $\eta$ (say $\eta = 10$)

4. **Check** if $f_2(\infty) \to 1$

5. **Adjust $s$** via bisection/Newton-Raphson

6. **Converge** to $s = f''(0) = 0.33206$

**Answer:** Shooting method converges to $f''(0) = 0.332$

## Example 6: Falkner-Skan Stagnation Flow

For $\beta = 1$ (Hiemenz, $U_e = Cx$), find $f''(0)$.

**Solution:**

$$f''' + ff'' + (1 - f'^2) = 0$$

**Numerical solution**: $f''(0) = 1.23259$

**Higher than Blasius** (0.332) → **higher wall shear** due to favorable pressure gradient.

**Velocity profile**: Fuller than Blasius.

**BL thickness**: $\delta \sim \sqrt{\nu/C}$ (independent of $x$! - strong favorable gradient prevents growth)

**Answer:** $f''(0) = 1.233$ (Hiemenz)

## Example 7: Von Kármán Integral Method (Flat Plate)

Use profile $u/U = 2(\frac{y}{\delta}) - (\frac{y}{\delta})^2$ for $y < \delta$.

**Solution:**

Calculate:
$$\theta = \int_0^\delta \frac{u}{U}\left(1 - \frac{u}{U}\right)dy$$

Let $\xi = y/\delta$:

$$\theta = \delta\int_0^1 (2\xi - \xi^2)(1 - 2\xi + \xi^2)d\xi = \delta\int_0^1 (2\xi - 4\xi^2 + 2\xi^3)d\xi$$

$$= \delta\left[\xi^2 - \frac{4\xi^3}{3} + \frac{\xi^4}{2}\right]_0^1 = \delta\left(1 - \frac{4}{3} + \frac{1}{2}\right) = \frac{2\delta}{15}$$

Wall shear:
$$\tau_w = \mu\frac{\partial u}{\partial y}\bigg|_{y=0} = \mu U\frac{2}{\delta}$$

$$\frac{\tau_w}{\rho U^2} = \frac{2\nu}{U\delta}$$

Von Kármán (flat plate, $\frac{dU}{dx} = 0$):
$$\frac{d\theta}{dx} = \frac{\tau_w}{\rho U^2}$$

$$\frac{d}{dx}\left(\frac{2\delta}{15}\right) = \frac{2\nu}{U\delta}$$

$$\delta d\delta = \frac{15\nu dx}{U}$$

Integrate from $x = 0$:
$$\frac{\delta^2}{2} = \frac{15\nu x}{U}$$

$$\delta = \sqrt{\frac{30\nu x}{U}} = 5.48\sqrt{\frac{\nu x}{U}}$$

**Compare Blasius**: $\delta = 5.0\sqrt{\nu x/U}$

**Error**: 9.6% (quite good for simple polynomial!)

**Answer:** $\delta = 5.48\sqrt{\nu x/U}$ (approximate)

## Example 9: Matched Asymptotics Displacement Effect

For $\delta^* = 2$ mm at $x = 1$ m, how does outer flow respond?

**Solution:**

**Outer potential flow** sees effective body thickened by $\delta^*$.

**For flat plate**: Outer flow sees source distribution of strength $\frac{d\delta^*}{dx}$ per unit length.

**Pressure coefficient** modified: $C_p = -2\frac{u}{U}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}$ includes displacement effect.

**Iterative matching**: Outer $U_e(x)$ → BL solution → $\delta^*(x)$ → modified outer → repeat until consistent.

**Answer:** Outer flow sees body displaced by $\delta^* = 2$ mm

## Example 10: Adverse Gradient Effect

Compare BL at $x$ for $\beta = 0$ (Blasius) vs. $\beta = -0.05$ (adverse).

**Solution:**

**Blasius** ($\beta = 0$): $f''(0) = 0.332$

**Adverse** ($\beta = -0.05$): $f''(0) \approx 0.22$ (numerical)

**Wall shear reduced** by ~34%!

**BL thickens** (adverse gradient decelerates near-wall fluid).

**Closer to separation** ($\beta_{sep} = -0.0904$).

**Answer:** Adverse gradient reduces wall shear, thickens BL

## Example 11: Transition Reynolds Number

Typical transition $\text{Re}_x \approx 5 \times 10^5$ for flat plate.

For $U = 50$ m/s (air), at what $x$ does transition occur?

**Solution:**

$$\text{Re}_x = \frac{Ux}{\nu} = 5 \times 10^5$$

$$x_{trans} = \frac{5 \times 10^5 \cdot \nu}{U} = \frac{(5 \times 10^5)(1.5 \times 10^{-5})}{50} = \frac{7.5}{50} = 0.15 \text{ m}$$

**Blasius valid** for $x < 15$ cm.

**Beyond**: Turbulent BL (different scaling, higher skin friction).

**Answer:** Transition at $x = 15$ cm

## Example 12: Displacement Thickness Physical Meaning

Explain why $\delta^*$ matters for external aerodynamics.

**Solution:**

**Displacement thickness** $\delta^*$: effective blockage of flow.

**For wing**: $\delta^*$ adds to airfoil thickness → modified pressure distribution → affects lift/drag.

**For wind tunnel**: Walls with BL → effective test section smaller by $\delta^*$ → blockage corrections needed.

**For subsonic aircraft**: $\delta^*$ growth along fuselage → effective body shape changes → pressure drag component.

**Answer:** $\delta^*$ represents effective flow blockage, modifies aerodynamic characteristics
