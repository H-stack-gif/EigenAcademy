# Article 6.5: Quasi-One-Dimensional Flow with Friction and Heat Transfer

Real duct flows experience **friction** (wall shear) and **heat transfer**, violating isentropic assumptions. **Fanno flow** (adiabatic with friction) and **Rayleigh flow** (frictionless with heat addition) represent limiting cases, each driving flow toward Ma = 1 but via different mechanisms. **Fanno line** on h-s diagram shows possible states for given mass flux and area with friction; entropy **must increase** (second law), restricting to portion where $s$ increases with length. **Rayleigh line** (T-s or h-s diagram) shows states for given mass flux with heat addition; **subsonic** flow with heating accelerates toward Ma = 1, **supersonic** flow decelerates toward Ma = 1—both approach **thermal choking**. Friction always increases entropy regardless of subsonic/supersonic; heat addition can increase or decrease entropy depending on temperature change direction.

**Fanno flow analysis**: Momentum equation $dP + \rho u \, du + \tau_w \frac{P_{wet}}{A}dx = 0$ with wall shear $\tau_w = \frac{1}{2}\rho u^2 f$ (friction factor $f$) yields differential equations relating Ma change to friction. Maximum duct length $L_{max}$ exists for given initial conditions before choking; operating point must have $L < L_{max}$. **Rayleigh flow**: Energy equation $dh + u \, du = \delta q$ with heat transfer modifies temperature, density via coupling. **Thermal choking**: Heat addition sufficient to reach Ma = 1 limits further addition (cannot increase mass flow). Combined Fanno-Rayleigh effects appear in combustors (friction + heat release) and exhaust systems. T-s diagrams elegantly visualize processes: Fanno curves rightward (entropy increase), Rayleigh can curve left or right depending on heating/cooling.

This article derives **Fanno flow equations** including friction term integration, develops Fanno line on h-s diagram with choking limit, treats maximum duct length calculations $L^*/D = f(\text{Ma})$, derives **Rayleigh flow equations** with heat addition term, develops Rayleigh line on T-s diagram, discusses thermal choking and stagnation property changes, treats combined effects in combustors, analyzes shock appearance on T-s diagram, and works through extensive examples involving friction-limited duct length, heat addition effects on Ma, choking calculations, property changes, and T-s diagram interpretation.

## Fanno Flow: Governing Equations

**Adiabatic** ($\delta q = 0$), **friction** ($\tau_w \neq 0$), **constant area**.

**Energy**: $h_0 = \text{const}$ (adiabatic)

**Momentum** (with friction):
$$dP + \rho u \, du + \frac{4\tau_w}{D}dx = 0$$

**Friction**: $\tau_w = \frac{1}{2}f\rho u^2$ (Darcy friction factor)

**Result**: Flow tends toward Ma = 1.
- **Subsonic**: Accelerates (Ma increases)
- **Supersonic**: Decelerates (Ma decreases)

## Example 1: Fanno Flow Direction

$\text{Ma} = 0.5$ entering duct with friction.

Does Ma increase or decrease?

**Solution:**

**Subsonic** → friction accelerates flow.

**Intuition**: Friction drops pressure, energy conserved → velocity increases.

**Ma increases** toward 1.

**Answer:** Ma increases

---

## Maximum Duct Length

**Choking**: Duct reaches Ma = 1 at exit.

**Maximum length** $L_{max}$ from initial Ma to Ma = 1:

$$\boxed{\frac{fL_{max}}{D} = \frac{1-\text{Ma}^2}{\gamma\text{Ma}^2} + \frac{\gamma+1}{2\gamma}\ln\left[\frac{(\gamma+1)\text{Ma}^2}{2(1+\frac{\gamma-1}{2}\text{Ma}^2)}\right]}$$

**Tabulated** vs. Ma for convenience.

**Physical**: Cannot exceed $L_{max}$ without reducing $\dot{m}$ or changing inlet conditions.

## Example 2: Maximum Length Calculation

$\text{Ma}_1 = 0.5$, $D = 0.1$ m, $f = 0.02$ (air).

Find $L_{max}$.

**Solution:**

From tables: $\frac{fL_{max}}{D}\bigg|_{\text{Ma}=0.5} \approx 1.069$

$$L_{max} = \frac{1.069 \times D}{f} = \frac{1.069 \times 0.1}{0.02} = 5.345 \text{ m}$$

**Duct longer than 5.35 m** → flow chokes (back pressure cannot be met).

**Answer:** $L_{max} = 5.3$ m

---

## Fanno Line (h-s Diagram)

**Plot**: Enthalpy $h$ vs. entropy $s$ for constant $\dot{m}/A$.

**Upper branch**: Subsonic (Ma < 1)
**Lower branch**: Supersonic (Ma > 1)
**Peak**: Ma = 1 (maximum entropy)

**Friction** always moves rightward (entropy increases).

**Subsonic**: Moves down-right (h decreases, s increases)
**Supersonic**: Moves up-right (h increases, s increases)

**Both converge** to Ma = 1.

## Example 3: Entropy Increase in Fanno Flow

$\text{Ma}_1 = 0.3$, $\text{Ma}_2 = 0.6$ due to friction (air).

Find $\Delta s/R$.

**Solution:**

**From tables**:
- At Ma = 0.3: $s/R - s^*/R = -1.945$
- At Ma = 0.6: $s/R - s^*/R = -0.363$

$$\Delta s/R = -0.363 - (-1.945) = 1.582$$

**Positive** entropy change ✓ (second law).

**Answer:** $\Delta s/R = 1.58$

---

## Rayleigh Flow: Heat Addition

**Frictionless** ($f = 0$), **heat transfer** ($\delta q \neq 0$), **constant area**.

**Energy**: $dh + u \, du = \delta q$

**Result**:
- **Subsonic + heating**: Ma increases (accelerates)
- **Supersonic + heating**: Ma decreases (decelerates)
- **Both** → Ma = 1 with sufficient heat

**Cooling**: Opposite effects.

## Example 4: Heat Addition Effect

Subsonic flow $\text{Ma} = 0.4$ with heat addition.

What happens to Ma?

**Solution:**

**Subsonic + heat**: **Ma increases** (flow accelerates).

**Mechanism**: Heat raises $h$ → must increase $u$ (energy) while maintaining $\dot{m}$.

**Toward** Ma = 1 (thermal choking possible).

**Answer:** Ma increases

---

## Thermal Choking

**Heat addition** sufficient to reach Ma = 1 at exit.

**Cannot add more heat** without changing inlet or reducing $\dot{m}$.

**Stagnation pressure** $P_0$ **decreases** with heat addition (even though frictionless!) due to Ma approach to 1.

## Rayleigh Line (T-s Diagram)

**Plot**: Temperature $T$ vs. entropy $s$ for constant $\dot{m}/A$.

**Upper branch**: Subsonic
**Lower branch**: Supersonic  
**Peak** ($\frac{dT}{ds} = 0$): Ma = 1

**Heat addition**:
- **Subsonic**: Rightward (T increases, s increases)
- **Supersonic (Ma close to 1)**: Rightward (T increases, s increases initially)
- **Supersonic (high Ma)**: Can move left initially then right

## Example 5: Maximum Temperature Location

On Rayleigh line, where is $T_{max}$?

**Solution:**

**At Ma = 1** (sonic point).

**Physical**: Compromise between static heating and kinetic energy extraction.

**Answer:** Ma = 1 (sonic)

---

## Combined Fanno-Rayleigh (Combustor)

**Real combustor**: **Both friction and heat**.

**Resultant curve**: Between pure Fanno and pure Rayleigh.

**Typical**: Start subsonic, add heat + friction → Ma increases, may choke.

**Design**: Balance geometry, fuel addition rate, pressure drop.

## Example 6: Stagnation Pressure Change

Rayleigh flow: $\text{Ma}_1 = 0.5$ heats to $\text{Ma}_2 = 0.8$.

Does $P_0$ increase or decrease?

**Solution:**

**Rayleigh**: Even though frictionless, **$P_0$ decreases** (entropy increases from heat at finite Ma).

**From tables**:
- Ma = 0.5: $P_0/P_0^* \approx 1.114$
- Ma = 0.8: $P_0/P_0^* \approx 1.020$

$$P_{02}/P_{01} = 1.020/1.114 = 0.916$$

**8.4% loss** in stagnation pressure!

**Answer:** Decreases ($P_{02}/P_{01} = 0.92$)

---

## Shock on T-s Diagram

**Normal shock**: Vertical line (constant $T_0$) moving rightward (entropy increases).

**Distinguishes** shock from continuous processes.

**Fanno/Rayleigh**: Curves
**Shock**: Straight vertical

## Example 7: Heating Supersonic Flow

$\text{Ma}_1 = 2.0$, heat addition until Ma = 1.5.

Find $T_2/T_1$ (air).

**Solution:**

**From Rayleigh tables**:
- Ma = 2.0: $T/T^* \approx 0.793$
- Ma = 1.5: $T/T^* \approx 0.908$

$$T_2/T_1 = 0.908/0.793 = 1.145$$

**14.5% temperature increase** from heat + deceleration effects.

**Answer:** $T_2/T_1 = 1.15$

---

## Entropy Generation Comparison

**Fanno** (friction): $\Delta s > 0$ always (subsonic and supersonic).

**Rayleigh** (heat):
- **Heating** (subsonic/supersonic near 1): $\Delta s > 0$
- **Cooling**: $\Delta s < 0$ (possible!)

**Shock**: $\Delta s > 0$ (always).

## Example 8: Cooling Supersonic Rayleigh Flow

$\text{Ma}_1 = 3$, cool to $\text{Ma}_2 = 4$.

Does entropy increase or decrease?

**Solution:**

**Rayleigh cooling** at high Ma can **decrease entropy** (moving leftward on T-s, away from peak).

**From tables** (roughly):
- Ma = 3: $s/R - s^*/R = -0.185$
- Ma = 4: $s/R - s^*/R = -0.305$

$$\Delta s/R = -0.305 - (-0.185) = -0.12 < 0$$

**Entropy decreases** ✓ (allowed with cooling).

**Answer:** Decreases (cooling extracts entropy)

---

## Fanno vs. Rayleigh: Key Differences

**Fanno**:
- Adiabatic ($h_0 = \text{const}$)
- Friction → entropy increases
- $P_0$ decreases

**Rayleigh**:
- Frictionless (no wall shear)
- Heat transfer → $h_0$ changes
- $P_0$ still decreases (unless cooling from high Ma)

## Example 9: Mass Flow Limit

Duct: $A = 0.01$ m², $P_0 = 500$ kPa, $T_0 = 600$ K.

Maximum $\dot{m}$ (air)?

**Solution:**

**Choking**: Ma = 1 at exit.

$$\dot{m}_{max} = \frac{AP_0}{\sqrt{T_0}}\sqrt{\frac{\gamma}{R}}\left(\frac{2}{\gamma+1}\right)^{(\gamma+1)/2(\gamma-1)}$$

$$= \frac{(0.01)(500,000)}{\sqrt{600}} \times 0.0404 = \frac{5000}{24.49} \times 0.0404$$

$$= 204.2 \times 0.0404 = 8.25 \text{ kg/s}$$

**Answer:** $\dot{m}_{max} = 8.3$ kg/s

---

## Example 10: T-s Diagram Process Identification

Process: $T$ increases, $s$ increases, $T_0$ constant.

Fanno, Rayleigh, or shock?

**Solution:**

**$T_0$ constant** → **not Rayleigh** (heat changes $T_0$).

**$T$ increases, $s$ increases** → **supersonic Fanno** (friction decelerates supersonic → $T$ rises, $s$ rises).

**Not shock** (shock is discontinuous, also $T$ jumps but represented differently).

**Answer:** Supersonic Fanno flow
