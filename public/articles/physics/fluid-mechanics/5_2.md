# Article 5.2: Reynolds Decomposition and Averaged Equations

Turbulent flows exhibit chaotic, unpredictable fluctuations superimposed on mean motion—a random field defying deterministic Navier-Stokes solution. The **Reynolds decomposition** $u_i = \overline{u_i} + u_i'$ separates mean $\overline{u_i}$ from fluctuation $u_i'$, enabling statistical description. **Time averaging** $\overline{u_i} = \lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\int_0^T u_i dt$ for statistically stationary turbulence (or **ensemble averaging** over realizations) yields mean equations. Substituting decomposition into Navier-Stokes and averaging produces **Reynolds-Averaged Navier-Stokes (RANS)** equations, identical to laminar form except for additional **Reynolds stress tensor** $\tau_{ij}^R = -\rho\overline{u_i'u_j'}$ representing turbulent momentum flux. These correlations $\overline{u_i'u_j'}$ arise from nonlinear convective term: $\overline{u_i\frac{\partial u_j}{\partial x_k}}$ generates products of fluctuations after averaging.

The **closure problem**—more unknowns (6 independent Reynolds stresses) than equations (3 momentum)—is THE fundamental challenge of turbulence. Unlike molecular viscosity (fluid property), **turbulent stresses** depend on flow history, geometry, and mean gradients. **Reynolds stress production** $\mathcal{P}_{ij} = -\overline{u_i'u_k'}\frac{\partial \overline{u_j}}{\partial x_k} - \overline{u_j'u_k'}\frac{\partial \overline{u_i}}{\partial x_k}$ transfers energy from mean to turbulence when positive. **Dissipation** $\epsilon_{ij} = 2\nu\overline{s_{ij}'s_{ij}'}$ (strain rate fluctuations) converts turbulent kinetic energy to heat at small scales. The **turbulent kinetic energy budget** $\frac{D k}{Dt} = \mathcal{P} - \epsilon + T + D$ (production, dissipation, transport, diffusion) governs energy pathways. Understanding Reynolds stresses physically—normal stresses $\overline{u'^2}, \overline{v'^2}, \overline{w'^2}$ (velocity variances) and shear stress $\overline{u'v'}$ (momentum flux)—is crucial for turbulence modeling and physical insight.

This article derives Reynolds decomposition properties (linearity, averaging twice), develops RANS equations from Navier-Stokes with detailed averaging of nonlinear term, introduces **Reynolds stress tensor** symmetry and physical interpretation, analyzes the closure problem and its fundamental nature, develops turbulent kinetic energy equation $\frac{Dk}{Dt} = \mathcal{P} - \epsilon + \ldots$, discusses statistical moments and correlation functions, treats anisotropy of Reynolds stresses, introduces ergodic hypothesis (time average = ensemble average), and works through extensive examples involving Reynolds stress calculations, TKE budgets, mean flow calculations, anisotropy quantification, and production/dissipation balances.

## Reynolds Decomposition

**Velocity decomposition**:
$$\boxed{u_i(\mathbf{x}, t) = \overline{u_i}(\mathbf{x}) + u_i'(\mathbf{x}, t)}$$

Mean: $\overline{u_i}$, Fluctuation: $u_i'$

**Time averaging** (statistically stationary):
$$\overline{u_i} = \lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\int_0^T u_i(\mathbf{x},t) \, dt$$

**Properties**:
1. Linearity: $\overline{a u + b v} = a\overline{u} + b\overline{v}$
2. Average of mean: $\overline{\overline{u}} = \overline{u}$
3. **Average of fluctuation: $\overline{u'} = 0$** (by definition)
4. Average of product: $\overline{uv} = \overline{u}\,\overline{v} + \overline{u'v'}$

**Other quantities**:
- Pressure: $P = \overline{P} + P'$
- Velocity products: $u_i u_j = \overline{u_i u_j} + u_i'u_j + u_i u_j' + u_i'u_j'$

After averaging: $\overline{u_i u_j} = \overline{u_i}\,\overline{u_j} + \overline{u_i'u_j'}$

## Example 1: Fluctuation Average

Given $u(t) = 10 + 2\sin(t)$, find $\overline{u}$ and $u'$.

**Solution:**

$$\overline{u} = \lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\int_0^T [10 + 2\sin(t)]dt$$

$$= 10 + 2\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\left[-\cos(t)\right]_0^T = 10 + 0 = 10$$

$$u' = u - \overline{u} = 2\sin(t)$$

**Check**: $\overline{u'} = \overline{2\sin(t)} = 0$ ✓

**Answer:** $\overline{u} = 10$, $u' = 2\sin(t)$

## RANS Derivation

**Navier-Stokes** (incompressible):
$$\frac{\partial u_i}{\partial t} + u_j\frac{\partial u_i}{\partial x_j} = -\frac{1}{\rho}\frac{\partial P}{\partial x_i} + \nu\frac{\partial^2 u_i}{\partial x_j^2}$$

**Substitute** $u_i = \overline{u_i} + u_i'$:

$$\frac{\partial(\overline{u_i} + u_i')}{\partial t} + (\overline{u_j} + u_j')\frac{\partial(\overline{u_i} + u_i')}{\partial x_j} = -\frac{1}{\rho}\frac{\partial(\overline{P} + P')}{\partial x_i} + \nu\frac{\partial^2(\overline{u_i} + u_i')}{\partial x_j^2}$$

**Time average** entire equation (use properties):

**Continuity averaged**: $\frac{\partial \overline{u_i}}{\partial x_i} = 0$

**Momentum averaged** (after lengthy algebra):

$$\boxed{\rho\left(\frac{\partial \overline{u_i}}{\partial t} + \overline{u_j}\frac{\partial \overline{u_i}}{\partial x_j}\right) = -\frac{\partial \overline{P}}{\partial x_i} + \mu\frac{\partial^2\overline{u_i}}{\partial x_j^2} - \frac{\partial}{\partial x_j}(\rho\overline{u_i'u_j'})}$$

**Key term**: $-\frac{\partial}{\partial x_j}(\rho\overline{u_i'u_j'})$ from averaging $\overline{u_j'\frac{\partial u_i'}{\partial x_j}}$

## Reynolds Stress Tensor

Define:
$$\boxed{\tau_{ij}^R = -\rho\overline{u_i'u_j'}}$$

**Symmetric**: $\tau_{ij}^R = \tau_{ji}^R$ (6 independent components in 3D)

**Components**:
- **Normal stresses**: $\tau_{11}^R = -\rho\overline{u'^2}$, $\tau_{22}^R = -\rho\overline{v'^2}$, $\tau_{33}^R = -\rho\overline{w'^2}$
- **Shear stresses**: $\tau_{12}^R = -\rho\overline{u'v'}$, etc.

**Physical meaning**: Turbulent momentum flux

$\overline{u'v'} > 0$: High-speed fluid ($u' > 0$) moving upward ($v' > 0$) transports $x$-momentum upward.

## Example 2: Reynolds Stress Calculation

Measurements give $\overline{u'^2} = 4$ m²/s², $\overline{v'^2} = 2$ m²/s², $\overline{u'v'} = -1.5$ m²/s².

Find $\tau_{11}^R$, $\tau_{22}^R$, $\tau_{12}^R$ (water, $\rho = 1000$ kg/m³).

**Solution:**

$$\tau_{11}^R = -\rho\overline{u'^2} = -(1000)(4) = -4000 \text{ Pa}$$

$$\tau_{22}^R = -\rho\overline{v'^2} = -(1000)(2) = -2000 \text{ Pa}$$

$$\tau_{12}^R = -\rho\overline{u'v'} = -(1000)(-1.5) = 1500 \text{ Pa}$$

**Shear stress positive** (momentum flux opposing gradient).

**Answer:** $\tau_{11}^R = -4$ kPa, $\tau_{22}^R = -2$ kPa, $\tau_{12}^R = 1.5$ kPa

## Closure Problem

**Count unknowns** (3D RANS):
- Mean velocities: $\overline{u}, \overline{v}, \overline{w}$ (3)
- Mean pressure: $\overline{P}$ (1)
- Reynolds stresses: $\overline{u'^2}, \overline{v'^2}, \overline{w'^2}, \overline{u'v'}, \overline{u'w'}, \overline{v'w'}$ (6)
- **Total: 10 unknowns**

**Count equations**:
- Continuity: 1
- Momentum: 3
- **Total: 4 equations**

**Underdetermined!** $10 > 4$ → Need **closure models** for $\tau_{ij}^R$.

**Fundamental challenge**: No universal closure (turbulence is chaotic, history-dependent).

## Example 3: Simple Channel Flow Closure

2D channel flow: $\overline{u}(y)$ only, $\overline{v} = 0$.

How many unknowns and equations?

**Solution:**

**Unknowns**: $\overline{u}(y)$, $\overline{P}(x,y)$, $\overline{u'v'}(y)$ → 3

**Equations**:
- Continuity: $\frac{\partial \overline{u}}{\partial x} = 0$ (satisfied)
- $x$-momentum: $0 = -\frac{\partial \overline{P}}{\partial x} + \mu\frac{d^2\overline{u}}{dy^2} + \frac{d}{dy}(\rho\overline{u'v'})$
- $y$-momentum: $0 = -\frac{\partial \overline{P}}{\partial y}$

2 equations, but $\overline{u'v'}$ unknown → **need closure model**.

**Answer:** 3 unknowns, 2 equations → closure required

## Turbulent Kinetic Energy

**TKE**: $k = \frac{1}{2}\overline{u_i'u_i'} = \frac{1}{2}(\overline{u'^2} + \overline{v'^2} + \overline{w'^2})$

**Derive TKE equation**: Multiply fluctuating N-S by $u_i'$, average, sum indices.

**Result**:
$$\boxed{\frac{Dk}{Dt} = \mathcal{P} - \epsilon + T + D}$$

where:
- **Production**: $\mathcal{P} = -\overline{u_i'u_j'}\frac{\partial \overline{u_i}}{\partial x_j}$
- **Dissipation**: $\epsilon = \nu\overline{\frac{\partial u_i'}{\partial x_j}\frac{\partial u_i'}{\partial x_j}}$
- **Transport**: $T = -\frac{\partial}{\partial x_j}\left(\overline{u_j'\left(\frac{1}{2}u_i'u_i' + \frac{P'}{\rho}\right)}\right)$
- **Diffusion**: $D = \nu\frac{\partial^2 k}{\partial x_j^2}$

**Energy cascade**: Production extracts from mean → TKE → cascade to small scales → dissipation.

## Example 4: TKE Production

Shear flow $\overline{u} = 10y$ (units m/s, m), $\overline{u'v'} = -2$ m²/s².

Find production rate $\mathcal{P}$.

**Solution:**

$$\frac{\partial \overline{u}}{\partial y} = 10 \text{ s}^{-1}$$

$$\mathcal{P} = -\overline{u'v'}\frac{\partial \overline{u}}{\partial y} = -(-2)(10) = 20 \text{ m}^2\text{/s}^3$$

**Positive production** → TKE increases (energy extracted from mean shear).

**Answer:** $\mathcal{P} = 20$ m²/s³

## Anisotropy

**Isotropic turbulence**: $\overline{u'^2} = \overline{v'^2} = \overline{w'^2}$, all shear stresses zero.

**Anisotropic**: Reynolds stresses directionally dependent.

**Anisotropy tensor**:
$$b_{ij} = \frac{\overline{u_i'u_j'}}{2k} - \frac{1}{3}\delta_{ij}$$

Measures deviation from isotropy. Traceless: $b_{ii} = 0$.

**Near walls**: Highly anisotropic ($\overline{v'^2} \ll \overline{u'^2}$ due to wall blocking).

## Example 5: Isotropy Check

Turbulence with $\overline{u'^2} = 6$, $\overline{v'^2} = 6$, $\overline{w'^2} = 6$ m²/s², all shear stresses zero.

Is it isotropic?

**Solution:**

**Equal normal stresses**: $\overline{u'^2} = \overline{v'^2} = \overline{w'^2}$ ✓

**Zero shear stresses**: $\overline{u'v'} = \overline{u'w'} = \overline{v'w'} = 0$ ✓

**Isotropic turbulence**.

$$k = \frac{1}{2}(6 + 6 + 6) = 9 \text{ m}^2\text{/s}^2$$

**Answer:** Yes, isotropic; $k = 9$ m²/s²

## Statistical Moments

**First moment**: Mean $\overline{u}$

**Second moment**: Variance $\overline{u'^2}$, Covariance $\overline{u'v'}$

**Higher moments**: Skewness $\overline{u'^3}$, Flatness $\overline{u'^4}$

**Correlation coefficient**:
$$R_{uv} = \frac{\overline{u'v'}}{\sqrt{\overline{u'^2}\overline{v'^2}}}$$

$-1 \leq R_{uv} \leq 1$

## Example 6: Correlation Coefficient

$\overline{u'^2} = 4$, $\overline{v'^2} = 9$, $\overline{u'v'} = -3$ m²/s².

Find $R_{uv}$.

**Solution:**

$$R_{uv} = \frac{-3}{\sqrt{(4)(9)}} = \frac{-3}{\sqrt{36}} = \frac{-3}{6} = -0.5$$

**Moderate negative correlation** (high $u'$ tends to pair with low $v'$).

**Answer:** $R_{uv} = -0.5$

## Ergodic Hypothesis

**Statistically stationary turbulence**: Time average = Ensemble average.

$$\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\int_0^T u(t)dt = \lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^N u_n$$

**Allows**: Use time series from single realization instead of many experiments.

**Assumes**: System ergodic (visits all states given enough time).

## Turbulent Diffusion

**Enhanced mixing**: Turbulent fluctuations transport scalars (heat, mass, momentum) much faster than molecular diffusion.

**Gradient transport hypothesis**: $\overline{u_i'c'} = -D_t\frac{\partial \overline{c}}{\partial x_i}$

where $D_t$ = turbulent diffusivity ($\gg D_{molecular}$).

## Example 7: Turbulent vs. Molecular Diffusivity

Turbulent diffusivity $D_t = 0.01$ m²/s, molecular $D = 10^{-5}$ m²/s.

Factor increase?

**Solution:**

$$\frac{D_t}{D} = \frac{0.01}{10^{-5}} = 1000$$

**Turbulence enhances mixing by factor 1000!**

**Answer:** 1000× enhancement

## Mean Flow Calculation from RANS

**Example**: Channel flow, steady, fully developed.

RANS $x$-momentum:
$$0 = -\frac{d\overline{P}}{dx} + \mu\frac{d^2\overline{u}}{dy^2} + \rho\frac{d}{dy}(\overline{u'v'})$$

**Integrate**:
$$\mu\frac{d\overline{u}}{dy} - \rho\overline{u'v'} = \left(-\frac{d\overline{P}}{dx}\right)y + C$$

At centerline: $\frac{d\overline{u}}{dy} = 0$, $\overline{u'v'} = 0$ → $C = 0$

**Total shear stress**:
$$\tau_{total} = \mu\frac{d\overline{u}}{dy} - \rho\overline{u'v'}$$

**Linear profile** of total stress!

## Example 8: Total Stress at Wall

Channel half-height $h = 0.05$ m, pressure gradient $-\frac{dP}{dx} = 100$ Pa/m.

Find wall shear stress.

**Solution:**

At wall ($y = h$): $\overline{u'v'} = 0$ (no turbulent fluctuation)

$$\tau_w = \left(-\frac{dP}{dx}\right)h = (100)(0.05) = 5 \text{ Pa}$$

**Answer:** $\tau_w = 5$ Pa

## Reynolds Stress Transport

**Full Reynolds stress transport equations** (for $\overline{u_i'u_j'}$):

$$\frac{D\overline{u_i'u_j'}}{Dt} = P_{ij} + \Pi_{ij} - \epsilon_{ij} + D_{ij} + T_{ij}$$

Production, Pressure-strain, Dissipation, Diffusion, Transport.

**More complex** than TKE equation. **Reynolds Stress Models (RSM)** solve these directly (expensive).

## Example 9: Reynolds Stress Production Term

For $\tau_{12}^R = -\rho\overline{u'v'}$:

$$P_{12} = -\overline{u'u'}\frac{\partial \overline{v}}{\partial x} - \overline{u'v'}\left(\frac{\partial \overline{u}}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial \overline{v}}{\partial x}\right) - \overline{v'v'}\frac{\partial \overline{u}}{\partial y}$$

**Complex coupling** between all components.

## Dissipation Rate

**Dissipation tensor**: $\epsilon_{ij} = 2\nu\overline{s_{ij}'s_{ij}'}$

**Scalar dissipation**: $\epsilon = \frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{ii} = \nu\overline{\frac{\partial u_i'}{\partial x_j}\frac{\partial u_i'}{\partial x_j}}$

**Units**: [m²/s³] (energy per unit mass per time)

**Dissipation rate** sets smallest scales of turbulence (Kolmogorov scales, Article 5.4).

## Example 10: Dissipation Estimate

Turbulence with $k = 5$ m²/s², dissipation $\epsilon = 0.1$ m²/s³.

Timescale $\tau = k/\epsilon$?

**Solution:**

$$\tau = \frac{k}{\epsilon} = \frac{5}{0.1} = 50 \text{ s}$$

**Turbulence decay timescale** (how long for TKE to dissipate absent production).

**Answer:** $\tau = 50$ s

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: C** - By definition of Reynolds decomposition, fluctuation has **zero mean**: $\overline{u'} = \overline{u - \overline{u}} = \overline{u} - \overline{u} = 0$.

**Problem 2: C** - Reynolds stress: $[\tau_{ij}^R] = [\rho][\overline{u'v'}] = \frac{kg}{m^3} \cdot \frac{m^2}{s^2} = \frac{kg}{m \cdot s^2} = Pa$ (pressure/stress units).

**Problem 3: E** - Closure problem: RANS introduces 6 Reynolds stresses but only 4 equations → **underdetermined** + turbulence's chaotic nature prevents universal closure.

**Problem 4: C** - Turbulent kinetic energy per unit mass: $k = \frac{1}{2}\overline{u_i'u_i'} = \frac{1}{2}(\overline{u'^2} + \overline{v'^2} + \overline{w'^2})$ (sum of variance components).

**Problem 5: B** - Production $\mathcal{P} = -\overline{u_i'u_j'}\frac{\partial \overline{u_i}}{\partial x_j} > 0$ → Reynolds stress works against mean shear → **energy extracted from mean flow to turbulence**.
