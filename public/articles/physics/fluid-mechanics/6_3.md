# Article 6.3: Normal and Oblique Shock Waves

Supersonic flow encountering obstacles or adverse pressure gradients cannot adjust smoothly—information cannot propagate upstream. Instead, **shock waves** form: nearly discontinuous compressions (thickness ~$10^{-5}$ m, few molecular mean free paths) where flow properties jump abruptly. **Normal shocks** (perpendicular to flow) decelerate supersonic to subsonic, increase pressure/temperature/density drastically, and **generate entropy** (irreversible). **Rankine-Hugoniot jump conditions**—conservation of mass, momentum, and energy across infinitesimal control volume—provide algebraic relations: $\rho_1u_1 = \rho_2u_2$, $P_1 + \rho_1u_1^2 = P_2 + \rho_2u_2^2$, $h_1 + u_1^2/2 = h_2 + u_2^2/2$. Crucially, **second law** $s_2 > s_1$ demands $\text{Ma}_1 > 1$ upstream and $\text{Ma}_2 < 1$ downstream—shocks convert supersonic to subsonic. **Pressure ratio** $P_2/P_1 = 1 + \frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}(\text{Ma}_1^2 - 1)$ shows strong compression for high Ma (Ma = 3 gives 10× pressure jump!).

**Oblique shocks** appear when supersonic flow deflects around corners or wedges. Flow tangent to shock inclined at angle $\beta$ to freestream; decomposing velocity into normal ($u_n$) and tangential ($u_t$) components reveals that **tangential unchanged** across shock (no forces parallel to shock) while **normal component obeys normal shock relations**. The **shock angle-deflection relation** $\tan\theta = 2\cot\beta\frac{\text{Ma}_1^2\sin^2\beta - 1}{\text{Ma}_1^2(\gamma + \cos 2\beta) + 2}$ relates deflection angle $\theta$ to shock angle $\beta$ and upstream Ma—admits **two solutions** (weak and strong shocks) for given $\theta < \theta_{max}$. When $\theta > \theta_{max}(\text{Ma}_1)$, oblique shock **detaches**, forming curved bow shock ahead of body. **Stagnation pressure loss** $P_{02}/P_{01} < 1$ quantifies irreversibility; stronger shocks incur greater losses, motivating weak-shock design in supersonic inlets.

This article derives Rankine-Hugoniot conditions from control volume analysis, solves for normal shock property ratios (pressure, density, temperature, Mach number) in terms of upstream $\text{Ma}_1$, analyzes entropy generation and second law implications, extends to oblique shocks via velocity decomposition, derives **shock angle-deflection relation** and maximum deflection angle, discusses weak vs. strong shock solutions, treats shock detachment on blunt bodies, analyzes stagnation pressure loss, and works through extensive examples involving shock calculations, Mach number jumps, oblique shock angles, detachment prediction, and inlet performance analysis.

## Normal Shock: Conservation Laws

**Control volume** across shock (stationary shock frame):

**Mass**:
$$\boxed{\rho_1 u_1 = \rho_2 u_2}$$

**Momentum** ($x$-direction):
$$\boxed{P_1 + \rho_1 u_1^2 = P_2 + \rho_2 u_2^2}$$

**Energy** (adiabatic, no work):
$$\boxed{h_1 + \frac{u_1^2}{2} = h_2 + \frac{u_2^2}{2}}$$

**Plus**: Ideal gas $P = \rho RT$, $h = c_pT$

**Five equations**, five unknowns ($\rho_2, u_2, P_2, T_2$ plus shock speed if needed) given upstream state.

## Pressure Ratio Across Normal Shock

From momentum:
$$P_2 - P_1 = \rho_1 u_1^2 - \rho_2 u_2^2 = \rho_1 u_1^2\left(1 - \frac{\rho_1}{\rho_2}\right)$$

From energy and ideal gas (lengthy algebra):

$$\boxed{\frac{P_2}{P_1} = 1 + \frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}(\text{Ma}_1^2 - 1)}$$

**For air** ($\gamma = 1.4$):
$$\frac{P_2}{P_1} = 1 + \frac{2.8}{2.4}(\text{Ma}_1^2 - 1) = 1 + 1.167(\text{Ma}_1^2 - 1)$$

## Example 1: Normal Shock Pressure Jump

$\text{Ma}_1 = 2.0$.

Find $P_2/P_1$.

**Solution:**

$$\frac{P_2}{P_1} = 1 + 1.167(4 - 1) = 1 + 1.167(3) = 1 + 3.5 = 4.5$$

**4.5× pressure increase!**

**Severe compression** even at moderate supersonic speeds.

**Answer:** $P_2/P_1 = 4.5$

## Density and Temperature Ratios

**Density ratio**:
$$\boxed{\frac{\rho_2}{\rho_1} = \frac{u_1}{u_2} = \frac{(\gamma+1)\text{Ma}_1^2}{(\gamma-1)\text{Ma}_1^2 + 2}}$$

**Maximum density ratio** (as $\text{Ma}_1 \to \infty$):
$$\frac{\rho_2}{\rho_1}\bigg|_{max} = \frac{\gamma+1}{\gamma-1} = 6 \text{ (for air)}$$

**Temperature ratio** (from $P = \rho RT$):
$$\boxed{\frac{T_2}{T_1} = \frac{P_2}{P_1}\frac{\rho_1}{\rho_2}}$$

## Example 2: Normal Shock Density Ratio

$\text{Ma}_1 = 3.0$ (air).

Find $\rho_2/\rho_1$.

**Solution:**

$$\frac{\rho_2}{\rho_1} = \frac{2.4(9)}{0.4(9) + 2} = \frac{21.6}{3.6 + 2} = \frac{21.6}{5.6} = 3.857$$

**Nearly 4× density increase.**

**Approaching maximum** (6 for air).

**Answer:** $\rho_2/\rho_1 = 3.86$

---

## Mach Number Across Shock

**Downstream Mach number**:

$$\boxed{\text{Ma}_2^2 = \frac{(\gamma-1)\text{Ma}_1^2 + 2}{2\gamma\text{Ma}_1^2 - (\gamma-1)}}$$

**Always**: $\text{Ma}_2 < 1$ (subsonic downstream) if $\text{Ma}_1 > 1$ (supersonic upstream).

**Stronger shock** (higher $\text{Ma}_1$) → lower $\text{Ma}_2$.

## Example 3: Downstream Mach Number

$\text{Ma}_1 = 2.5$.

Find $\text{Ma}_2$ (air).

**Solution:**

$$\text{Ma}_2^2 = \frac{0.4(6.25) + 2}{2(1.4)(6.25) - 0.4} = \frac{2.5 + 2}{17.5 - 0.4} = \frac{4.5}{17.1} = 0.263$$

$$\text{Ma}_2 = 0.513$$

**Subsonic downstream** as required by second law.

**Answer:** $\text{Ma}_2 = 0.51$

---

## Entropy Increase

**Second law**: $\Delta s = s_2 - s_1 > 0$ (irreversible).

$$\Delta s = c_p\ln\frac{T_2}{T_1} - R\ln\frac{P_2}{P_1}$$

**Entropy increase** quantifies irreversibility.

**Weak shocks** ($\text{Ma}_1$ slightly > 1): Small $\Delta s$
**Strong shocks** ($\text{Ma}_1 \gg 1$): Large $\Delta s$

## Stagnation Pressure Loss

**Key**: $T_{02} = T_{01}$ (energy conserved) but $P_{02} < P_{01}$ (entropy increases).

$$\boxed{\frac{P_{02}}{P_{01}} = \frac{P_{02}}{P_2}\frac{P_2}{P_1}\frac{P_1}{P_{01}}}$$

Using isentropic relations and shock relations:

$$\frac{P_{02}}{P_{01}} = \left[\frac{(\gamma+1)\text{Ma}_1^2}{(\gamma-1)\text{Ma}_1^2 + 2}\right]^{\gamma/(\gamma-1)}\left[\frac{1 + \frac{\gamma-1}{2}\text{Ma}_1^2}{\frac{\gamma+1}{2}\text{Ma}_1^2}\right]^{1/(\gamma-1)}$$

**Measure of loss**: Lower $P_{02}/P_{01}$ means more irreversibility.

## Example 4: Stagnation Pressure Ratio

$\text{Ma}_1 = 2.0$.

Find $P_{02}/P_{01}$ (air).

**Solution:**

Use tables or formula:

$$\frac{P_{02}}{P_{01}} \approx 0.7209$$

**28% loss** in stagnation pressure!

**Significant inefficiency** from shock.

**Answer:** $P_{02}/P_{01} = 0.72$

## Oblique Shock Geometry

**Shock angle** $\beta$: Angle between shock and upstream flow.

**Deflection angle** $\theta$: Flow turning angle.

**Velocity components**:
- Normal: $u_{1n} = u_1\sin\beta$
- Tangential: $u_{1t} = u_1\cos\beta$

**Key**: **Tangential component unchanged** across shock (no forces parallel).

$$u_{2t} = u_{1t}$$

**Normal component** obeys normal shock with $\text{Ma}_{1n} = \text{Ma}_1\sin\beta$.

## Example 5: Oblique Shock Component

$\text{Ma}_1 = 3$, $\beta = 40°$.

Find normal and tangential Mach numbers.

**Solution:**

$$\text{Ma}_{1n} = \text{Ma}_1\sin\beta = 3\sin(40°) = 3(0.643) = 1.929$$

$$\text{Ma}_{1t} = \text{Ma}_1\cos\beta = 3\cos(40°) = 3(0.766) = 2.298$$

**Normal component** $> 1$ → shock forms.

**Tangential conserved**: $\text{Ma}_{2t} = \text{Ma}_{2n}\tan\theta$ (geometric relation).

**Answer:** $\text{Ma}_{1n} = 1.93$, $\text{Ma}_{1t} = 2.30$

---

## Shock Angle-Deflection Relation

**Theta-beta-Mach relation** (from geometric and conservation constraints):

$$\boxed{\tan\theta = 2\cot\beta\frac{\text{Ma}_1^2\sin^2\beta - 1}{\text{Ma}_1^2(\gamma + \cos 2\beta) + 2}}$$

**Given**: $\text{Ma}_1$ and $\theta$ → solve for $\beta$ (nonlinear, iterate).

**Two solutions**: **Weak shock** (smaller $\beta$, higher $\text{Ma}_2$) and **strong shock** (larger $\beta$, lower $\text{Ma}_2$).

**Nature prefers weak** (unless forced otherwise by downstream pressure).

## Example 6: Oblique Shock Angle

Wedge deflection $\theta = 15°$, $\text{Ma}_1 = 2.5$.

Find weak shock angle $\beta$ (air).

**Solution:**

**Iterate** or use oblique shock charts:

From tables: $\beta_{weak} \approx 41.4°$

**Weak shock** preferred (unless downstream forces strong).

**Check**: $\text{Ma}_{1n} = 2.5\sin(41.4°) = 2.5(0.661) = 1.653$ > 1 ✓

**Answer:** $\beta \approx 41°$

---

## Maximum Deflection Angle

For given $\text{Ma}_1$, **maximum** $\theta$ exists.

**Beyond** $\theta_{max}$: **Shock detaches** (curved bow shock forms upstream).

**Detached shock**: Part of shock curved, subsonic region behind, complex flow.

**For air**:
- $\text{Ma} = 2$: $\theta_{max} \approx 23°$
- $\text{Ma} = 3$: $\theta_{max} \approx 34°$
- $\text{Ma} \to \infty$: $\theta_{max} \to 45.6°$

## Example 7: Shock Detachment

Cone half-angle $\theta = 30°$, $\text{Ma}_1 = 2.5$.

Does shock attach?

**Solution:**

For $\text{Ma} = 2.5$: $\theta_{max} \approx 29°$ (from charts).

Since $30° > 29°$: **Shock detaches!**

**Bow shock** forms ahead of cone.

**Drag increases** (detached shock stronger than attached).

**Answer:** Detached (θ > θ_max)

---

## Weak vs. Strong Shock

**Weak shock**:
- Smaller $\beta$
- Higher downstream Ma
- Lower entropy increase
- **Preferred** (lower drag)

**Strong shock**:
- Larger $\beta$
- Lower downstream Ma (closer to normal shock)
- Higher entropy increase
- Occurs if downstream pressure forces it

## Example 8: Pressure Ratio Oblique Shock

$\text{Ma}_1 = 3$, $\beta = 35°$.

Find $P_2/P_1$.

**Solution:**

$$\text{Ma}_{1n} = 3\sin(35°) = 3(0.574) = 1.722$$

**Use normal shock relation** with $\text{Ma}_{1n}$:

$$\frac{P_2}{P_1} = 1 + 1.167(1.722^2 - 1) = 1 + 1.167(2.965 - 1)$$

$$= 1 + 1.167(1.965) = 1 + 2.293 = 3.293$$

**3.3× pressure jump** (weaker than normal at Ma = 3 which gives 10×).

**Answer:** $P_2/P_1 = 3.29$

**Graphical representation**: $P_2/P_1$ vs. $\theta$ for given $\text{Ma}_1$.

**Shows**: Weak/strong branches, maximum deflection.

**Useful**: Designing inlets, analyzing interactions.

## Example 9: Normal vs. Oblique Shock Loss

$\text{Ma}_1 = 2$: Compare $P_{02}/P_{01}$ for normal shock vs. weak oblique at $\theta = 10°$.

**Solution:**

**Normal**: $P_{02}/P_{01} = 0.721$ (from Example 4)

**Oblique** ($\theta = 10°$, $\beta \approx 39.3°$):
$\text{Ma}_{1n} = 2\sin(39.3°) = 1.267$

Weaker $\text{Ma}_{1n}$ → less loss: $P_{02}/P_{01} \approx 0.987$

**Oblique much better!** (1.3% loss vs. 28%)

**Answer:** Oblique: 0.987; Normal: 0.721

---

## Example 10: Supersonic Inlet Design

Inlet must slow $\text{Ma} = 2.5$ to subsonic with minimum loss.

Compare: (a) Single normal shock, (b) Oblique shock + normal.

**Solution:**

**(a) Normal at Ma = 2.5**: $P_{02}/P_{01} \approx 0.499$ (50% loss!)

**(b) Oblique** ($\theta = 15°$) to Ma ≈ 2.0, then normal:
- After oblique: $P_{02}/P_{01} \approx 0.96$
- After normal at Ma = 2.0: $P_{02}/P_{01} = 0.96 \times 0.721 = 0.692$

**(b) much better**: 69% vs. 50% recovery.

**Multiple weak shocks** even better (3-4 ramps).

**Answer:** Multi-shock: 69% vs. single: 50%

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: B** - Normal shock: $\text{Ma}_1 > 1$ upstream → $\text{Ma}_2 < 1$ downstream **always** (second law requirement, subsonic after compression).

**Problem 2: C** - Shock is **irreversible** → entropy **increases** ($\Delta s > 0$), quantifies losses, stronger shocks = more entropy generation.

**Problem 3: C** - Oblique shock: **no forces parallel** to shock → tangential velocity component **unchanged** ($u_{2t} = u_{1t}$), only normal component jumps.

**Problem 4: B** - Maximum deflection $\theta_{max}$ depends on $\text{Ma}_1$: ~23° for Ma=2, ~46° as Ma→∞ (for air); beyond $\theta_{max}$ shock detaches.

**Problem 5: C** - Energy equation: $h_0 = h + u^2/2$ conserved across shock → **$T_{02} = T_{01}$** (constant), but $P_{02} < P_{01}$ due to entropy increase.
