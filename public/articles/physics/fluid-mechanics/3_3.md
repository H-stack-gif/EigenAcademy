# Article 3.3: Complex Potential and Conformal Mapping

For 2D potential flow, the velocity potential $\phi$ and stream function $\psi$ satisfy Laplace's equation and are related by the **Cauchy-Riemann equations**—precisely the conditions for a function to be analytic (holomorphic) in complex analysis! This profound connection allows us to harness the full power of complex variable theory: the **complex potential** $w(z) = \phi + i\psi$ where $z = x + iy$ is an analytic function, and its derivative $dw/dz = u - iv$ gives the velocity. Elementary complex potentials correspond to basic flows—uniform flow, source, vortex, doublet—and combining them through addition or composition generates complex flow patterns. **Conformal mapping** transforms one flow domain into another while preserving angles, allowing solutions for complicated geometries (airfoils, corners, obstacles) by mapping from simple domains (circles, half-planes).

The **Joukowski transformation** $\zeta = z + a^2/z$ maps circles in the $z$-plane to airfoil-like shapes in the $\zeta$-plane. A circle passing through $z = \pm a$ becomes a flat plate; a circle offset from the origin becomes a cambered airfoil. Combined with circulation around the circle, this generates lift—the **Kutta-Joukowski theorem** $L' = \rho U\Gamma$ quantifies lift per unit span in terms of circulation $\Gamma$ and freestream velocity $U$. The **Kutta condition** (flow leaves trailing edge smoothly, requiring specific $\Gamma$ to eliminate stagnation point there) determines circulation uniquely for a given angle of attack. These classical results form the foundation of thin airfoil theory and explain how wings generate lift.

This article introduces complex potentials and their properties, derives the Cauchy-Riemann conditions and their connection to potential flow, presents elementary complex potentials in closed form, demonstrates superposition and composition, introduces conformal mapping and its angle-preserving property, develops the Joukowski transformation and airfoil generation, derives the Kutta-Joukowski theorem for lift, discusses the Blasius theorem for forces, explores circle theorems and image systems, and works through extensive examples involving complex velocity calculations, streamline plotting, mapping transformations, force calculations, and airfoil flow. The mathematics involves heavy complex analysis (analytic functions, Cauchy-Riemann, residue theorem, Laurent series, branch cuts, conformal maps).

## Complex Potential Definition

For 2D flow with potential $\phi(x,y)$ and stream function $\psi(x,y)$, define:

$$\boxed{w(z) = \phi(x,y) + i\psi(x,y)}$$

where $z = x + iy$ is the complex coordinate.

**Example**: Uniform flow $U$ in $x$-direction:
$$\phi = Ux, \quad \psi = Uy$$
$$w(z) = Ux + iUy = U(x + iy) = Uz$$

## Cauchy-Riemann Equations

For $w(z)$ to be analytic (differentiable in complex sense), must satisfy:

$$\boxed{\frac{\partial \phi}{\partial x} = \frac{\partial \psi}{\partial y}, \quad \frac{\partial \phi}{\partial y} = -\frac{\partial \psi}{\partial x}}$$

**But these are exactly the conditions for potential flow!**

From $\mathbf{u} = \nabla\phi$:
$$u = \frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x}, \quad v = \frac{\partial\phi}{\partial y}$$

From $\psi$:
$$u = \frac{\partial\psi}{\partial y}, \quad v = -\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial x}$$

Combining:
$$\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x} = \frac{\partial\psi}{\partial y}, \quad \frac{\partial\phi}{\partial y} = -\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial x}$$

**Conclusion**: Potential flow $\iff$ $w(z)$ analytic!

## Complex Velocity

Define **complex velocity**:

$$\boxed{\frac{dw}{dz} = u - iv}$$

**Derivation**:
$$\frac{dw}{dz} = \frac{\partial w}{\partial x} = \frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x} + i\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial x} = u + i(-v) = u - iv$$

**Physical interpretation**: 
- Real part: $x$-component of velocity
- Imaginary part: negative $y$-component
- Magnitude: $|dw/dz| = \sqrt{u^2 + v^2}$ = speed
- Argument: $\arg(dw/dz) = -\arctan(v/u)$ (negative of flow angle)

## Elementary Complex Potentials

**Uniform Flow**:
$$w = Uz$$
$$\frac{dw}{dz} = U$$

**Source** (strength $m$):
$$w = \frac{m}{2\pi}\ln z$$
$$\frac{dw}{dz} = \frac{m}{2\pi z}$$

**Vortex** (circulation $\Gamma$):
$$w = -i\frac{\Gamma}{2\pi}\ln z$$
$$\frac{dw}{dz} = -i\frac{\Gamma}{2\pi z}$$

**Doublet** (strength $\mu$, pointing in $-x$ direction):
$$w = -\frac{\mu}{2\pi z}$$
$$\frac{dw}{dz} = \frac{\mu}{2\pi z^2}$$

**Note**: $\ln z$ is multi-valued (branch cut needed), but velocity $dw/dz = 1/z$ is single-valued.

## Flow Past Cylinder with Circulation

**Combine**: Uniform flow + Doublet + Vortex

$$w = Uz + \frac{\mu}{2\pi z} - i\frac{\Gamma}{2\pi}\ln z$$

Choose $\mu = 2\pi Ua^2$ (cylinder radius $a$):

$$\boxed{w = U\left(z + \frac{a^2}{z}\right) - i\frac{\Gamma}{2\pi}\ln z}$$

**Velocity**:
$$\frac{dw}{dz} = U\left(1 - \frac{a^2}{z^2}\right) - i\frac{\Gamma}{2\pi z}$$

At $z = a$: Velocity tangent to circle (satisfies boundary condition).

**Lift per unit span** (Kutta-Joukowski):

$$\boxed{L' = \rho U\Gamma}$$

## Conformal Mapping

**Definition**: Transformation $\zeta = f(z)$ is **conformal** at point $z_0$ if $f'(z_0) \neq 0$.

**Property**: Preserves angles (magnitude and sense).

**Implication**: If $w(z)$ is a solution in $z$-plane, then $w(f^{-1}(\zeta))$ is solution in $\zeta$-plane.

**Application**: Solve simple geometry (circle) in $z$-plane, map to complex geometry (airfoil) in $\zeta$-plane.

## Joukowski Transformation

$$\boxed{\zeta = z + \frac{a^2}{z}}$$

**Properties**:
- Maps circle $|z| = a$ to line segment $[-2a, 2a]$ (flat plate)
- Maps circle $|z| = R > a$ to ellipse
- Maps circle through $z = \pm a$ to flat plate at angle
- Maps offset circle to cambered airfoil

**Critical points**: $z = \pm a$ where $\frac{d\zeta}{dz} = 1 - \frac{a^2}{z^2} = 0$

These map to trailing edge (sharp edge required for Kutta condition).

## Kutta Condition

For flow past airfoil, circulation $\Gamma$ is determined by requiring flow to leave **trailing edge smoothly** (no infinite velocity).

At trailing edge, velocity must be finite → stagnation point moves to trailing edge.

This **uniquely determines** $\Gamma$ for given angle of attack $\alpha$.

**Result**: 
$$\Gamma = 4\pi Ua\sin\alpha$$ (thin airfoil, angle $\alpha$)

$$L' = \rho U\Gamma = 4\pi\rho U^2 a\sin\alpha$$

## Blasius Theorem

**Force** on body:

$$F_x - iF_y = \frac{i\rho}{2}\oint_C \left(\frac{dw}{dz}\right)^2 dz$$

where $C$ is contour around body.

**By residue theorem**: 
$$F_x - iF_y = -i\pi\rho \times (\text{residue at infinity})$$

For cylinder with circulation:

Residue of $(dw/dz)^2$ at $z = 0$:

$$\left(\frac{dw}{dz}\right)^2 \approx \left(-i\frac{\Gamma}{2\pi z}\right)^2 \times (\text{terms}) = \cdots$$

After calculation:
$$F_x = 0, \quad F_y = \rho U\Gamma$$

**Kutta-Joukowski theorem** derived!

## Example 1: Complex Velocity for Source

For $w = \frac{m}{2\pi}\ln z$, find velocity at $z = 1 + i$.

**Solution:**

$$\frac{dw}{dz} = \frac{m}{2\pi z}$$

At $z = 1 + i$:

$$\frac{dw}{dz} = \frac{m}{2\pi(1 + i)} = \frac{m}{2\pi} \cdot \frac{1-i}{(1+i)(1-i)} = \frac{m}{2\pi} \cdot \frac{1-i}{2} = \frac{m(1-i)}{4\pi}$$

$$u - iv = \frac{m(1-i)}{4\pi}$$

$$u = \frac{m}{4\pi}, \quad v = \frac{m}{4\pi}$$

**Speed**: $|\mathbf{u}| = \sqrt{u^2 + v^2} = \frac{m}{4\pi}\sqrt{2} = \frac{m\sqrt{2}}{4\pi}$

**Answer:** $u = \frac{m}{4\pi}$, $v = \frac{m}{4\pi}$

## Example 2: Streamlines for $w = z^2$

Find streamlines for $w = z^2 = (x+iy)^2$.

**Solution:**

$$w = x^2 - y^2 + 2ixy$$

$$\phi = x^2 - y^2, \quad \psi = 2xy$$

Streamlines: $\psi = C$

$$2xy = C \implies y = \frac{C}{2x}$$ (rectangular hyperbolae)

**Physical interpretation**: Flow in $90°$ corner.

**Answer:** Streamlines are hyperbolae $xy = \text{const}$

---

## Example 3: Joukowski Map of Circle

Map circle $|z| = 2$ (with $a = 1$) using Joukowski $\zeta = z + 1/z$.

**Solution:**

Parametrize circle: $z = 2e^{i\theta}$

$$\zeta = 2e^{i\theta} + \frac{1}{2}e^{-i\theta}$$

$$= 2(\cos\theta + i\sin\theta) + \frac{1}{2}(\cos\theta - i\sin\theta)$$

$$= \frac{5}{2}\cos\theta + i\frac{3}{2}\sin\theta$$

**Ellipse** with semi-axes $a_{\zeta} = 5/2$, $b_{\zeta} = 3/2$.

**Answer:** Maps to ellipse $\frac{4\zeta_x^2}{25} + \frac{4\zeta_y^2}{9} = 1$

## Example 4: Velocity on Cylinder with Circulation

For $w = U(z + a^2/z) - i\frac{\Gamma}{2\pi}\ln z$, find velocity at $\theta = 90°$ on surface $r = a$.

**Solution:**

$$\frac{dw}{dz} = U\left(1 - \frac{a^2}{z^2}\right) - i\frac{\Gamma}{2\pi z}$$

At $z = ae^{i\pi/2} = ia$:

$$\frac{dw}{dz} = U\left(1 - \frac{a^2}{(ia)^2}\right) - i\frac{\Gamma}{2\pi(ia)}$$

$$= U(1 - \frac{a^2}{-a^2}) - i\frac{\Gamma}{2\pi ia}$$

$$= U(1 + 1) + \frac{\Gamma}{2\pi a} = 2U + \frac{\Gamma}{2\pi a}$$

In polar: $u - iv = (u_r - iu_\theta)e^{i\theta}$

At $\theta = 90°$: $e^{i\pi/2} = i$

$$u_\theta = 2U + \frac{\Gamma}{2\pi a}$$

**Answer:** $u_\theta = 2U + \frac{\Gamma}{2\pi a}$

---

## Example 5: Stagnation Points on Cylinder

For cylinder with circulation, find stagnation point locations.

**Solution:**

$$\frac{dw}{dz} = U\left(1 - \frac{a^2}{z^2}\right) - i\frac{\Gamma}{2\pi z} = 0$$

On surface $z = ae^{i\theta}$:

$$U\left(1 - e^{-2i\theta}\right) - i\frac{\Gamma}{2\pi a}e^{-i\theta} = 0$$

Complex equation; separate real/imaginary. For small $\Gamma$:

Stagnation moves from $\theta = 0, \pi$ to:

$$\sin\theta_s = -\frac{\Gamma}{4\pi Ua}$$

For $|\Gamma| < 4\pi Ua$: two stagnation points on cylinder.

For $|\Gamma| = 4\pi Ua$: stagnation at top ($\theta = \pi/2$).

For $|\Gamma| > 4\pi Ua$: stagnation off cylinder (in flow).

**Answer:** $\sin\theta_s = -\frac{\Gamma}{4\pi Ua}$

## Example 6: Lift on Cylinder

Cylinder radius $a = 0.5$ m, $U = 10$ m/s, $\Gamma = 20$ m²/s (air $\rho = 1.2$ kg/m³).

Find lift per unit span.

**Solution:**

$$L' = \rho U\Gamma = (1.2)(10)(20) = 240 \text{ N/m}$$

**Drag**: $D' = 0$ (d'Alembert's paradox—inviscid flow has no drag!)

**Answer:** $L' = 240$ N/m

## Example 7: Source-Vortex Combination

Combine source $m$ and vortex $\Gamma$ at origin: $w = \frac{m}{2\pi}\ln z - i\frac{\Gamma}{2\pi}\ln z$.

Simplify and find streamlines.

**Solution:**

$$w = \frac{m - i\Gamma}{2\pi}\ln z$$

$$= \frac{m - i\Gamma}{2\pi}(\ln r + i\theta)$$

$$\phi = \frac{m\ln r + \Gamma\theta}{2\pi}$$

$$\psi = \frac{-\Gamma\ln r + m\theta}{2\pi}$$

Streamlines: $\psi = C$

$$-\Gamma\ln r + m\theta = C'$$

$$r = e^{(m\theta - C')/\Gamma}$$

**Logarithmic spirals**!

**Answer:** Streamlines are logarithmic spirals

## Example 8: Complex Potential for Corner

Flow in $60°$ corner: $w = Az^{3}$ (where $A$ is constant).

Find opening angle.

**Solution:**

$$w = A(re^{i\theta})^{3} = Ar^{3}e^{3i\theta}$$

$$\psi = Ar^{3}\sin(3\theta)$$

Boundaries: $\psi = 0$

$$\sin(3\theta) = 0 \implies 3\theta = 0, \pi, 2\pi, \ldots$$

$$\theta = 0, \frac{\pi}{3}, \frac{2\pi}{3}, \pi, \ldots$$

**Corner angle**: $\Delta\theta = \pi/3 = 60°$ ✓

**General**: $w = Az^{n}$ gives corner angle $\pi/n$.

**Answer:** $60°$ corner confirmed

## Example 9: Schwarz-Christoffel (Concept)

Map upper half-plane to polygon (e.g., channel, rectangle).

**Formula** (simplified):

$$\frac{d\zeta}{dz} = A\prod_{k=1}^n (z - z_k)^{\alpha_k/\pi - 1}$$

where $z_k$ are points on real axis mapping to vertices, $\alpha_k$ are exterior angles.

**Application**: Design ducts, channels, airfoils with sharp edges.

**Answer:** Powerful method for polygon flows

## Example 10: Circle Theorem

Uniform flow past circular cylinder at off-center position:

Use **circle theorem** (image system):

For flow in presence of cylinder, add image singularity at $z_i = a^2/\bar{z}_s$ (inverse point) plus another at center.

**Complex but elegant**: Preserves $u_r = 0$ on $|z| = a$.

**Answer:** Systematic method for cylinder near walls/other bodies

## Example 11: Flat Plate Lift

Joukowski airfoil degenerates to flat plate at angle $\alpha$:

$$L' = 2\pi\rho U^2 c\sin(2\alpha)$$ (where $c$ is chord)

For small $\alpha$: $L' \approx 4\pi\rho U^2 c\alpha$

**Lift coefficient**:

$$C_L = \frac{L'}{\frac{1}{2}\rho U^2 c} = 2\pi\sin(2\alpha) \approx 4\pi\alpha$$

Compare to experiment: **overestimates** (inviscid theory).

Real airfoil: $C_L \approx 2\pi\alpha$ per radian.

**Answer:** Thin airfoil theory: $C_L \approx 2\pi\alpha$

---

## Example 12: Branch Cut for Vortex

For $w = -i\frac{\Gamma}{2\pi}\ln z$, $\ln z$ is multi-valued.

**Branch cut**: Choose cut along negative real axis.

$$\ln z = \ln r + i\theta, \quad -\pi < \theta \leq \pi$$

Crossing branch cut: $\theta$ jumps by $2\pi$, $\psi$ jumps by $\Gamma$.

**Physical**: Circulation around vortex measured as discontinuity in $\psi$.

**Answer:** Branch cut encodes circulation

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: B** - Complex potential $w(z)$ must be analytic, requiring Cauchy-Riemann equations $\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x} = \frac{\partial\psi}{\partial y}$, $\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial y} = -\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial x}$.

**Problem 2: B** - Complex velocity is $\frac{dw}{dz} = u - iv$ (note minus sign on $v$).

**Problem 3: B** - Joukowski $\zeta = z + a^2/z$ maps circles to airfoils, ellipses, or flat plates depending on circle position.

**Problem 4: C** - Kutta-Joukowski: $L' = \rho U\Gamma$ (lift per span = density × velocity × circulation).

**Problem 5: C** - D'Alembert's paradox: inviscid flow predicts zero drag (resolved by viscosity/boundary layers in reality).
