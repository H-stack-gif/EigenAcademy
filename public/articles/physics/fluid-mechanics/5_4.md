# Article 5.4: Energy Cascade and Kolmogorov Theory

Turbulent energy flows through scale space in a **Richardson cascade**: large eddies ($\sim L$, integral scale) extract energy from mean flow, break into progressively smaller eddies transferring energy without dissipation (inertial range), until reaching viscosity-dominated small scales where dissipation converts kinetic energy to heat. **Kolmogorov's 1941 theory**, one of physics' most elegant dimensional arguments, posits universal behavior at small scales: given only dissipation rate $\epsilon$ and viscosity $\nu$, dimensional analysis yields **Kolmogorov scales** — length $\eta = (\nu^3/\epsilon)^{1/4}$, velocity $u_\eta = (\nu\epsilon)^{1/4}$, time $\tau_\eta = (\nu/\epsilon)^{1/2}$ — marking transition to viscous dissipation. In the **inertial range** ($\eta \ll r \ll L$), eddies are statistically isotropic, independent of large-scale forcing and small-scale viscosity, characterized solely by cascade rate $\epsilon$. The **energy spectrum** $E(k)$ (energy per wavenumber) follows **Kolmogorov's $-5/3$ law**: $E(k) = C\epsilon^{2/3}k^{-5/3}$ where $k = 2\pi/\lambda$ is wavenumber—a power law observed across atmospheric flows, oceanic turbulence, and laboratory experiments.

**Spectral analysis** via Fourier transforms decomposes turbulent velocity field $u_i(x)$ into modes $\hat{u}_i(k)$, with energy density $E(k) = \frac{1}{2}\int |\hat{u}(k)|^2 d\Omega_k$ integrated over wavenumber shells. Total TKE: $k = \int_0^\infty E(k)dk$. Dissipation spectrum peaks at $k\eta \sim 1$ (Kolmogorov scale). The **Taylor microscale** $\lambda = \sqrt{10\nu k/\epsilon}$ characterizes intermediate scales between integral $L$ and Kolmogorov $\eta$, related to Reynolds number: $\text{Re}_\lambda = \sqrt{k}\lambda/\nu$. **Intermittency**—deviations from Kolmogorov's ideal isotropy—manifests in higher-order statistics (flatness, skewness), particularly strong at small scales where intense dissipation structures (sheets, filaments) concentrate.

This article develops Richardson cascade concept qualitatively, derives **Kolmogorov scales** via dimensional analysis, proves **$-5/3$ law** from inertial range arguments, introduces Fourier transforms and energy spectra $E(k)$, defines Taylor microscale $\lambda$ and its relation to $\epsilon$ and $k$, discusses integral scale $L \sim k^{3/2}/\epsilon$ and Reynolds number $\text{Re}_L = UL/\nu$, treats correlation functions $R_{ij}(r) = \overline{u_i'(x)u_j'(x+r)}$ and their relation to spectra, analyzes isotropy assumption and its validity range, mentions intermittency and higher-order corrections, and works through extensive examples involving scale calculations, spectral integration, dissipation estimates, Reynolds number ranges, correlation length determination, and scale separation verification.

## Richardson Energy Cascade

**Conceptual picture**:

1. **Large eddies** ($\sim L$): Extract energy from mean flow via production $\mathcal{P}$
2. **Inertial transfer**: Vortex stretching breaks large eddies → smaller eddies
3. **Cascade continues**: Energy flows to progressively smaller scales
4. **Dissipation** ($\sim \eta$): Viscosity converts kinetic energy → heat

**Key insight**: Energy input rate (large scales) = Energy cascade rate = Dissipation rate (small scales)

$$\boxed{\mathcal{P} \sim \epsilon}$$

**Self-similar** process in inertial range (scale-invariant statistics).

## Kolmogorov Scales Derivation

**Assumptions**:
1. At small scales, flow statistics depend only on $\epsilon$ and $\nu$
2. Universal behavior (independent of large-scale geometry)

**Dimensional analysis**:

**Length scale**: $[\eta] = L$

Construct from $[\epsilon] = L^2T^{-3}$ and $[\nu] = L^2T^{-1}$:

$$\eta \sim \nu^a \epsilon^b$$

$$L = (L^2T^{-1})^a (L^2T^{-3})^b = L^{2a+2b}T^{-a-3b}$$

**Match dimensions**:
- Length: $1 = 2a + 2b$
- Time: $0 = -a - 3b$

Solve: $a = 3/4$, $b = -1/4$

$$\boxed{\eta = \left(\frac{\nu^3}{\epsilon}\right)^{1/4}}$$

**Velocity scale**:

$$\boxed{u_\eta = (\nu\epsilon)^{1/4}}$$

**Time scale**:

$$\boxed{\tau_\eta = \left(\frac{\nu}{\epsilon}\right)^{1/2}}$$

**Reynolds number** at Kolmogorov scale:

$$\text{Re}_\eta = \frac{u_\eta\eta}{\nu} = 1$$

(By construction!)

## Example 1: Kolmogorov Scale Calculation

Turbulence with $\epsilon = 0.1$ m²/s³, $\nu = 10^{-6}$ m²/s (water).

Find $\eta$, $u_\eta$, $\tau_\eta$.

**Solution:**

$$\eta = \left(\frac{(10^{-6})^3}{0.1}\right)^{1/4} = \left(\frac{10^{-18}}{0.1}\right)^{1/4} = (10^{-17})^{1/4} = 10^{-4.25} \text{ m}$$

$$= 5.62 \times 10^{-5} \text{ m} = 56 \text{ μm}$$

$$u_\eta = [(10^{-6})(0.1)]^{1/4} = (10^{-7})^{1/4} = 0.0056 \text{ m/s} = 5.6 \text{ mm/s}$$

$$\tau_\eta = \left(\frac{10^{-6}}{0.1}\right)^{1/2} = (10^{-5})^{1/2} = 0.00316 \text{ s} = 3.2 \text{ ms}$$

**Tiny scales and timescales!**

**Answer:** $\eta = 56$ μm, $u_\eta = 5.6$ mm/s, $\tau_\eta = 3.2$ ms

## Inertial Range

**Range**: $\eta \ll r \ll L$ (between dissipation and energy input)

**Characteristics**:
- **No energy input** (occurs at $\sim L$)
- **No dissipation** (occurs at $\sim \eta$)
- **Pure cascade**: Energy flux constant across scales

**Cascade rate** $\epsilon$ is only relevant parameter (viscosity $\nu$ unimportant, large scales $L$ too).

**Statistical isotropy**: Small scales forget large-scale anisotropy.

## Kolmogorov -5/3 Law

**Energy spectrum**: $E(k)$ where $k = 2\pi/\lambda$ (wavenumber)

**Dimensional argument**: In inertial range, $E$ depends only on $k$ and $\epsilon$.

$$E(k) = C\epsilon^a k^b$$

**Dimensions**: $[E] = L^3T^{-2}$ (energy per wavenumber)

$$L^3T^{-2} = (L^2T^{-3})^a (L^{-1})^b = L^{2a-b}T^{-3a}$$

**Match**:
- Time: $-2 = -3a$ → $a = 2/3$
- Length: $3 = 2a - b$ → $b = 2(2/3) - 3 = -5/3$

$$\boxed{E(k) = C\epsilon^{2/3}k^{-5/3}}$$

where $C \approx 1.5$ (Kolmogorov constant, empirically determined).

**Power law**: Straight line on log-log plot with slope $-5/3$.

## Example 2: Inertial Range Wavenumber

$L = 0.1$ m, $\eta = 50$ μm.

What is wavenumber range for inertial range?

**Solution:**

$$k_L \sim \frac{2\pi}{L} = \frac{2\pi}{0.1} = 62.8 \text{ m}^{-1}$$

$$k_\eta \sim \frac{2\pi}{\eta} = \frac{2\pi}{5 \times 10^{-5}} = 1.26 \times 10^5 \text{ m}^{-1}$$

**Inertial range**: $62.8 < k < 1.26 \times 10^5$ m⁻¹

**Three decades** in wavenumber space!

**Answer:** $60 \lesssim k \lesssim 10^5$ m⁻¹

## Fourier Transform and Spectral Analysis

**Velocity Fourier transform**:

$$\hat{u}_i(k) = \int u_i(x)e^{-ik \cdot x}dx$$

**Energy in mode $k$**:

$$E(k) = \frac{1}{2}\int_{|k'|=k} |\hat{u}(k')|^2 d\Omega_{k'}$$

**Total TKE**:

$$k = \int_0^\infty E(k)dk$$

**Dissipation spectrum**:

$$\epsilon(k) = 2\nu k^2 E(k)$$

**Total dissipation**:

$$\epsilon = \int_0^\infty 2\nu k^2 E(k)dk$$

Peaks at $k \sim 1/\eta$ (Kolmogorov scale).

## Example 3: Spectral Energy Calculation

Spectrum $E(k) = 0.01k^{-5/3}$ for $100 < k < 10,000$ m⁻¹, zero elsewhere.

Estimate total TKE.

**Solution:**

$$k = \int_{100}^{10000} 0.01k^{-5/3}dk$$

$$= 0.01\left[\frac{k^{-2/3}}{-2/3}\right]_{100}^{10000}$$

$$= 0.01 \cdot \left(-\frac{3}{2}\right)\left[(10000)^{-2/3} - (100)^{-2/3}\right]$$

$$= -0.015\left[0.0215 - 0.215\right] = -0.015(-0.1935) = 0.0029 \text{ m}^2\text{/s}^2$$

**Answer:** $k \approx 0.003$ m²/s²

## Taylor Microscale

**Definition** (via dissipation):

$$\epsilon = 15\nu\frac{k}{\lambda^2}$$

Solving:

$$\boxed{\lambda = \sqrt{\frac{15\nu k}{\epsilon}}}$$

(Alternatively: $\lambda = \sqrt{10\nu k/\epsilon}$ depending on definition)

**Physical meaning**: Scale where velocity derivatives matter for dissipation.

**Intermediate**: $\eta < \lambda < L$

**Taylor-scale Reynolds number**:

$$\text{Re}_\lambda = \frac{\sqrt{k}\lambda}{\nu}$$

**Typical**: $\text{Re}_\lambda \sim 100-1000$ for laboratory turbulence, $\sim 10^4$ atmospheric.

## Example 4: Taylor Microscale

$k = 5$ m²/s², $\epsilon = 0.2$ m²/s³, $\nu = 1.5 \times 10^{-5}$ m²/s.

Find $\lambda$ and $\text{Re}_\lambda$.

**Solution:**

$$\lambda = \sqrt{\frac{15(1.5 \times 10^{-5})(5)}{0.2}} = \sqrt{\frac{1.125 \times 10^{-4}}{0.2}} = \sqrt{5.625 \times 10^{-4}}$$

$$= 0.0237 \text{ m} = 23.7 \text{ mm}$$

$$\text{Re}_\lambda = \frac{\sqrt{5}(0.0237)}{1.5 \times 10^{-5}} = \frac{(2.236)(0.0237)}{1.5 \times 10^{-5}}$$

$$= \frac{0.053}{1.5 \times 10^{-5}} = 3533$$

**Moderate $\text{Re}_\lambda$** → well-developed turbulence.

**Answer:** $\lambda = 23.7$ mm, $\text{Re}_\lambda = 3533$

## Integral Scale

**Characteristic size of energy-containing eddies**:

$$L \sim \frac{k^{3/2}}{\epsilon}$$

**Physical**: Large eddies have velocity $\sim \sqrt{k}$, turnover time $\sim L/\sqrt{k}$, dissipation $\sim k/(\text{turnover time}) \sim k^{3/2}/L$.

**Reynolds number**:

$$\text{Re}_L = \frac{UL}{\nu} \sim \frac{\sqrt{k}L}{\nu}$$

## Scale Separation

**Range of scales**:

$$\frac{L}{\eta} = \frac{k^{3/2}/\epsilon}{(\nu^3/\epsilon)^{1/4}} = \frac{k^{3/2}}{\epsilon^{3/4}\nu^{3/4}}\epsilon^{1/4} = \left(\frac{k}{\nu\epsilon}\right)^{3/4}$$

Using $\epsilon \sim k^{3/2}/L$:

$$\frac{L}{\eta} \sim \text{Re}_L^{3/4}$$

**High Re** → large scale separation → wide inertial range!

## Example 5: Scale Separation

$\text{Re}_L = 10^6$ (atmospheric).

Estimate $L/\eta$.

**Solution:**

$$\frac{L}{\eta} \sim (10^6)^{3/4} = 10^{6 \cdot 3/4} = 10^{4.5} = 31,623$$

**Four decades** of scale separation!

**DNS impossible** (would need $N^3 \sim 10^{13}$ grid points).

**Answer:** $L/\eta \sim 3 \times 10^4$

## Correlation Functions

**Two-point correlation**:

$$R_{ij}(r) = \overline{u_i'(x)u_j'(x+r)}$$

**Longitudinal** ($i = j$, $r$ parallel to $u_i$):

$$f(r) = \frac{\overline{u'(x)u'(x+r)}}{\overline{u'^2}}$$

**Integral scale** (from correlation):

$$L = \int_0^\infty f(r)dr$$

**Relation to spectrum** (Fourier pair):

$$E(k) = 4\pi k^2\int_0^\infty f(r)\frac{\sin(kr)}{kr}r^2dr$$

## Example 6: Correlation Length

Correlation $f(r) = e^{-r/L_c}$.

Find integral scale.

**Solution:**

$$L = \int_0^\infty e^{-r/L_c}dr = \left[-L_c e^{-r/L_c}\right]_0^\infty = L_c$$

**Correlation length** = integral scale for exponential decay.

**Answer:** $L = L_c$

## Isotropy Assumption

**Valid for**: Small scales in inertial and dissipation ranges.

**Invalid for**: Large scales (remember mean flow anisotropy), near walls (wall-blocking).

**Test**: Check $\overline{u'^2} \approx \overline{v'^2} \approx \overline{w'^2}$, shear stresses small.

**In Kolmogorov theory**: Small scales **universally isotropic** regardless of large-scale forcing.

## Intermittency

**Observation**: Dissipation not uniformly distributed—concentrated in sheets, filaments.

**Higher-order statistics**: Flatness $F = \overline{u'^4}/(\overline{u'^2})^2 > 3$ (Gaussian would be 3).

**Refined similarity hypothesis**: Corrections to $k^{-5/3}$ at high $k$.

**She-Leveque**: $E(k) \sim k^{-5/3 - \mu}$ where $\mu \sim 0.15$ (intermittency correction).

## Example 7: Flatness Factor

Velocity fluctuations: $\overline{u'^2} = 4$ m²/s², $\overline{u'^4} = 20$ m⁴/s⁴.

Find flatness.

**Solution:**

$$F = \frac{\overline{u'^4}}{(\overline{u'^2})^2} = \frac{20}{(4)^2} = \frac{20}{16} = 1.25$$

$F < 3$ → **sub-Gaussian** (less intermittent than Gaussian).

**Typical turbulence**: $F \approx 3-5$ (super-Gaussian, intermittent).

**Answer:** $F = 1.25$ (sub-Gaussian)

## Dissipation Rate Estimate

**From $k$ and $L$**:

$$\epsilon \sim \frac{k^{3/2}}{L}$$

**From Taylor microscale**:

$$\epsilon = \frac{15\nu k}{\lambda^2}$$

## Example 8: Dissipation from Scales

$k = 10$ m²/s², $L = 0.5$ m.

Estimate $\epsilon$.

**Solution:**

$$\epsilon \sim \frac{k^{3/2}}{L} = \frac{(10)^{3/2}}{0.5} = \frac{31.62}{0.5} = 63.2 \text{ m}^2\text{/s}^3$$

**Order of magnitude** estimate (exact coefficient $O(1)$).

**Answer:** $\epsilon \sim 60$ m²/s³

## Energy Cascade Rate

**Equilibrium**: Production = Dissipation

$$\mathcal{P} = \epsilon$$

**Cascade time** at scale $\ell$:

$$\tau(\ell) \sim \frac{\ell}{u(\ell)}$$

where $u(\ell) \sim (\epsilon\ell)^{1/3}$ (from Kolmogorov).

Thus: $\tau(\ell) \sim (\ell^2/\epsilon)^{1/3}$

**Faster cascade** at smaller scales!

## Example 9: Cascade Timescale

At $\ell = 0.01$ m, $\epsilon = 1$ m²/s³.

Find $\tau(\ell)$.

**Solution:**

$$\tau(\ell) \sim \left(\frac{\ell^2}{\epsilon}\right)^{1/3} = \left(\frac{(0.01)^2}{1}\right)^{1/3} = (10^{-4})^{1/3}$$

$$= 0.0464 \text{ s} = 46 \text{ ms}$$

**Fast turnover** at small scales.

**Answer:** $\tau \sim 46$ ms

---

## Example 10: Spectral Peak Location

Where does dissipation spectrum $\epsilon(k) = 2\nu k^2 E(k)$ peak if $E(k) \sim k^{-5/3}$ and dissipation scale is $\eta$?

**Solution:**

$$\epsilon(k) \sim \nu k^2 \cdot k^{-5/3} = \nu k^{1/3}$$

**Increasing** with $k$ in inertial range!

**Peaks** at $k \sim 1/\eta$ (Kolmogorov scale) where inertial range ends.

**Answer:** Peak at $k_\eta \sim 1/\eta$

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: C** - Kolmogorov length $\eta = (\nu^3/\epsilon)^{1/4}$ depends on **both** viscosity $\nu$ (dissipation mechanism) and dissipation rate $\epsilon$ (energy cascade rate).

**Problem 2: C** - $E(k) = C\epsilon^{2/3}k^{-5/3}$ applies in **inertial range** ($\eta \ll \lambda \ll L$) where energy cascade occurs without input or dissipation, only $\epsilon$ relevant.

**Problem 3: C** - Taylor microscale $\lambda = \sqrt{15\nu k/\epsilon}$ is **intermediate scale** between Kolmogorov $\eta$ (dissipation) and integral $L$ (energy-containing).

**Problem 4: B** - Scale separation: $L/\eta \sim \text{Re}_L^{3/4}$ → high Reynolds number enables wide inertial range with many decades of self-similar cascade.

**Problem 5: B** - $E(k) \propto k^{-5/3}$ is **decreasing** power law → energy concentrated at low $k$ (large scales), cascades to high $k$ (small scales) where dissipated.
