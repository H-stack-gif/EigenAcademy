# Article 6.2: One-Dimensional Isentropic Flow

Variable-area ducts with compressible flow exhibit counterintuitive behavior: **area-velocity relation** $\frac{dA}{A} = (\text{Ma}^2 - 1)\frac{du}{u}$ reveals that subsonic flow ($\text{Ma} < 1$) accelerates in converging sections (like incompressible) but supersonic flow ($\text{Ma} > 1$) **accelerates in diverging sections**—behavior reversal at sonic conditions. This enables **de Laval (converging-diverging) nozzles** to accelerate flow from subsonic to supersonic: converging section brings Ma to 1 at **throat** (minimum area), diverging section continues acceleration supersonically. **Choked flow** occurs when throat reaches Ma = 1, limiting mass flow rate: $\dot{m} = \frac{A^*P_0}{\sqrt{T_0}}\sqrt{\frac{\gamma}{R}}\left(\frac{2}{\gamma+1}\right)^{(\gamma+1)/2(\gamma-1)}$ where $A^*$ is throat area. **Area ratio** $A/A^*$ uniquely determines Mach number (two solutions: subsonic and supersonic branch) via $\frac{A}{A^*} = \frac{1}{\text{Ma}}\left[\frac{2}{\gamma+1}(1 + \frac{\gamma-1}{2}\text{Ma}^2)\right]^{(\gamma+1)/2(\gamma-1)}$.

The physics: **information propagation**  distinguishes regimes. Subsonic flow "knows" about downstream geometry (acoustic waves travel upstream), adjusts smoothly. Supersonic flow cannot receive upstream messages (Ma > 1 means disturbances convect downstream faster than they propagate); only geometry changes work. **Nozzle design** exploits this: rocket nozzles expand combustion products from subsonic chamber through sonic throat to supersonic exhaust, maximizing thrust via pressure expansion. **Critical area $A^*$** represents sonic reference; all properties scaled to critical values where Ma = 1. The **area-Mach relation** requires iterative solution (Newton-Raphson) given $A/A^*$ to find Ma, or direct calculation given Ma to find area ratio—essential for nozzle sizing.

This article derives continuity equation $\rho Au = \dot{m}$ and momentum/energy for variable area, develops **area-velocity relation** from logarithmic differentiation of continuity with isentropic relations, analyzes subsonic vs. supersonic duct behavior, derives de Laval nozzle operating principles, develops **choked flow condition** and maximum mass flow, derives area-Mach number relation $A/A^*(\text{Ma})$ with critical values, discusses back-pressure effects and shock formation in nozzles, treats nozzle performance off-design, and works through extensive examples involving Mach number from area ratio, mass flow calculations, nozzle throat sizing, exit conditions, and shock location determination.

## Conservation Laws in Variable Area Duct

**Continuity**:
$$\boxed{\rho A u = \dot{m} = \text{const}}$$

**Momentum** (frictionless, 1D):
$$P + \rho u^2 = \text{const}$$

**Energy** (adiabatic):
$$h + \frac{u^2}{2} = h_0 = \text{const}$$

**Combine** with $P = \rho RT$, $h = c_pT$ → complete system.

## Area-Velocity Relation Derivation

From continuity: $\rho Au = \text{const}$

Logarithmic differentiation:
$$\frac{d\rho}{\rho} + \frac{dA}{A} + \frac{du}{u} = 0$$

From energy: $c_pT + \frac{u^2}{2} = c_pT_0$

Differentiate: $c_p dT + u \, du = 0$

$$\frac{dT}{T} = -\frac{u \, du}{c_pT}$$

From isentropic $P/\rho^\gamma = \text{const}$:

$$\frac{dP}{P} = \gamma\frac{d\rho}{\rho}$$

Ideal gas: $\frac{dP}{P} = \frac{d\rho}{\rho} + \frac{dT}{T}$

Substitute:
$$\gamma\frac{d\rho}{\rho} = \frac{d\rho}{\rho} + \frac{dT}{T}$$

$$(\gamma - 1)\frac{d\rho}{\rho} = \frac{dT}{T} = -\frac{u \, du}{c_pT}$$

$$\frac{d\rho}{\rho} = -\frac{u \, du}{(\gamma-1)c_pT} = -\frac{u \, du}{\gamma RT} = -\text{Ma}^2\frac{du}{u}$$

(using $c^2 = \gamma RT$ and $\text{Ma} = u/c$)

Back to continuity:
$$-\text{Ma}^2\frac{du}{u} + \frac{dA}{A} + \frac{du}{u} = 0$$

$$\boxed{\frac{dA}{A} = (\text{Ma}^2 - 1)\frac{du}{u}}$$

**KEY RESULT!**

## Example 1: Area Change for Acceleration

Subsonic flow ($\text{Ma} = 0.6$) needs to accelerate ($du > 0$).

What must area do?

**Solution:**

$$\frac{dA}{A} = (0.6^2 - 1)\frac{du}{u} = (0.36 - 1)\frac{du}{u} = -0.64\frac{du}{u}$$

Since $du > 0$: $dA < 0$

**Area must decrease** (converging nozzle).

**Like incompressible**: subsonic behaves "normally."

**Answer:** Converging ($dA < 0$)

---

## Example 2: Supersonic Area Change

Supersonic flow ($\text{Ma} = 2$) accelerates ($du > 0$).

Area change?

**Solution:**

$$\frac{dA}{A} = (4 - 1)\frac{du}{u} = 3\frac{du}{u}$$

Since $du > 0$: $dA > 0$

**Area must increase** (diverging nozzle)!

**Counterintuitive**: Supersonic flow accelerates in expanding section.

**Answer:** Diverging ($dA > 0$)

---

## De Laval Nozzle

**Converging-diverging** nozzle:
1. **Converging** section: Subsonic accelerates, Ma increases toward 1
2. **Throat** ($A = A^*$, minimum area): Ma = 1 (sonic conditions)
3. **Diverging** section: Supersonic accelerates further, Ma increases

**Critical**: Smooth transition through Ma = 1 only at minimum area.

**Applications**: Rocket engines, steam turbines, wind tunnels.

## Choked Flow

**Throat reaches Ma = 1** → mass flow **maximum**.

**Cannot increase** $\dot{m}$ by lowering back pressure further (downstream cannot signal upstream).

**Mass flow** (choked):

At throat ($\text{Ma} = 1$, $A = A^*$):

$$\dot{m} = \rho^* A^* u^* = \rho^* A^* c^*$$

Express in stagnation properties:

$$T^*/T_0 = \frac{2}{\gamma+1}, \quad P^*/P_0 = \left(\frac{2}{\gamma+1}\right)^{\gamma/(\gamma-1)}$$

$$\rho^* = \frac{P^*}{RT^*}, \quad c^* = \sqrt{\gamma RT^*}$$

After algebra:

$$\boxed{\dot{m} = \frac{A^*P_0}{\sqrt{T_0}}\sqrt{\frac{\gamma}{R}}\left(\frac{2}{\gamma+1}\right)^{(\gamma+1)/2(\gamma-1)}}$$

**For air** ($\gamma = 1.4$): Coefficient = 0.0404

## Example 3: Mass Flow Rate Calculation

Nozzle: $A^* = 0.001$ m², $P_0 = 500$ kPa, $T_0 = 600$ K.

Find $\dot{m}$ (air).

**Solution:**

$$\dot{m} = 0.0404 \cdot \frac{A^*P_0}{\sqrt{T_0}} = 0.0404 \cdot \frac{(0.001)(500,000)}{\sqrt{600}}$$

$$= 0.0404 \cdot \frac{500}{24.49} = 0.0404 \cdot 20.42 = 0.825 \text{ kg/s}$$

**Answer:** $\dot{m} = 0.83$ kg/s

---

## Area-Mach Number Relation

From isentropic relations and continuity:

$$\boxed{\frac{A}{A^*} = \frac{1}{\text{Ma}}\left[\frac{2}{\gamma+1}\left(1 + \frac{\gamma-1}{2}\text{Ma}^2\right)\right]^{(\gamma+1)/2(\gamma-1)}}$$

**Key features**:
- At Ma = 1: $A/A^* = 1$ ✓
- Ma < 1: Subsonic branch
- Ma > 1: Supersonic branch
- **Minimum** at Ma = 1

**Two solutions** for given $A/A^* > 1$: one subsonic, one supersonic.

## Example 4: Area Ratio for Ma = 2

Find $A/A^*$ for $\text{Ma} = 2$ (air).

**Solution:**

$$\frac{A}{A^*} = \frac{1}{2}\left[\frac{2}{2.4}\left(1 + 0.2(4)\right)\right]^{3}$$

$$= \frac{1}{2}\left[\frac{2}{2.4}(1.8)\right]^3 = \frac{1}{2}\left[1.5\right]^3$$

$$= \frac{1}{2}(3.375) = 1.688$$

**Exit area 1.69× throat** for Ma = 2 exhaust.

**Answer:** $A/A^* = 1.69$

---

## Solving for Mach Number from Area Ratio

Given $A/A^*$, find Ma: **transcendental**, requires **iteration**.

**Newton-Raphson**:

$$\text{Ma}_{n+1} = \text{Ma}_n - \frac{f(\text{Ma}_n)}{f'(\text{Ma}_n)}$$

where $f(\text{Ma}) = \frac{A(\text{Ma})}{A^*} - \frac{A_{given}}{A^*}$

Alternatively: **tables** or **software**.

## Example 5: Mach Number from Area Ratio

$A/A^* = 2.0$.

Find supersonic Mach number.

**Solution:**

**Iterate** or use table:

Try Ma = 2: $A/A^* = 1.688$ (too low)
Try Ma = 2.5: $A/A^* = 2.637$ (too high)
Try Ma = 2.2: $A/A^* = 2.005$ ✓

**Converges** to Ma ≈ 2.197.

**Answer:** $\text{Ma} \approx 2.20$

## Back Pressure Effects

**Back pressure** $P_b$ (exit pressure) determines nozzle operation:

1. **High $P_b$**: Subsonic throughout
2. **Lower $P_b$**: Sonic at throat, subsonic diverging (converging nozzle behavior)
3. **Design $P_b$**: Isentropic expansion, fully supersonic diverging
4. **Too low $P_b$**: Shock in nozzle, adjust to $P_b$
5. **Very low $P_b$**: Overexpanded, expansion waves at exit

**Shock location** adjusts to match $P_b$ when off-design.

## Example 6: Throat Conditions

Flow reaches $\text{Ma} = 1$ at throat where $P^* = 200$ kPa, $T^* = 400$ K.

Find $P_0$, $T_0$.

**Solution:**

$$T_0 = T^*\left(\frac{\gamma+1}{2}\right) = 400\left(\frac{2.4}{2}\right) = 400(1.2) = 480 \text{ K}$$

$$P_0 = P^*\left(\frac{\gamma+1}{2}\right)^{\gamma/(\gamma-1)} = 200(1.2)^{3.5}$$

$$= 200(1.893) = 378.6 \text{ kPa}$$

**Answer:** $P_0 = 379$ kPa, $T_0 = 480$ K

---

## Nozzle Efficiency

**Ideal**: Isentropic expansion, $P_e = P_b$ at exit.

**Real**: Losses (friction, separation) → lower exit velocity.

**Efficiency**: $\eta_n = \frac{\Delta h_{actual}}{\Delta h_{isentropic}}$

Typically $\eta_n = 0.95-0.99$ for well-designed nozzles.

## Example 7: Exit Velocity

Nozzle expands from $T_0 = 600$ K, $P_0 = 1$ MPa to $P_e = 100$ kPa isentropically.

Find exit velocity (air).

**Solution:**

$$T_e = T_0\left(\frac{P_e}{P_0}\right)^{(\gamma-1)/\gamma} = 600\left(\frac{100}{1000}\right)^{0.2857}$$

$$= 600(0.1)^{0.2857} = 600(0.518) = 310.8 \text{ K}$$

$$u_e = \sqrt{2c_p(T_0 - T_e)} = \sqrt{2(1005)(600 - 310.8)}$$

$$= \sqrt{2(1005)(289.2)} = \sqrt{581,412} = 762.5 \text{ m/s}$$

**Answer:** $u_e = 763$ m/s

---

## Critical Properties

**At Ma = 1** (critical conditions):

$$T^*/T_0 = \frac{2}{\gamma+1}, \quad P^*/P_0 = \left(\frac{2}{\gamma+1}\right)^{\gamma/(\gamma-1)}$$

$$\rho^*/\rho_0 = \left(\frac{2}{\gamma+1}\right)^{1/(\gamma-1)}$$

**For air**:
- $T^*/T_0 = 0.833$
- $P^*/P_0 = 0.528$
- $\rho^*/\rho_0 = 0.634$

## Example 8: Throat to Stagnation Ratio

For $\gamma = 1.4$, verify $P^*/P_0$.

**Solution:**

$$P^*/P_0 = \left(\frac{2}{2.4}\right)^{3.5} = (0.833)^{3.5}$$

$$= 0.833 \times (0.833)^{2.5}$$

Calculate: $(0.833)^{2.5} = e^{2.5\ln(0.833)} = e^{-0.457} = 0.633$

$$P^*/P_0 = 0.833 \times 0.633 = 0.528$$ ✓

**Answer:** $P^*/P_0 = 0.528$ verified

---

## Example 9: Subsonic Branch Area Ratio

For $\text{Ma} = 0.5$, find $A/A^*$.

**Solution:**

$$\frac{A}{A^*} = \frac{1}{0.5}\left[\frac{2}{2.4}(1 + 0.2 \times 0.25)\right]^{3}$$

$$= 2\left[\frac{2}{2.4}(1.05)\right]^3 = 2[(0.833)(1.05)]^3$$

$$= 2[0.875]^3 = 2(0.670) = 1.340$$

**Same $A/A^*$** has supersonic solution at Ma ≈ 1.57.

**Answer:** $A/A^* = 1.34$

---

## Example 10: Rocket Nozzle Sizing

Thrust chamber: $P_0 = 5$ MPa, $T_0 = 3000$ K, $\dot{m} = 100$ kg/s.

Find throat area (assume $\gamma = 1.25$, $R = 400$ J/(kg·K)).

**Solution:**

$$A^* = \frac{\dot{m}\sqrt{T_0}}{P_0}\sqrt{\frac{R}{\gamma}}\left(\frac{\gamma+1}{2}\right)^{-(\gamma+1)/2(\gamma-1)}$$

For $\gamma = 1.25$: Coefficient = $\sqrt{\frac{400}{1.25}}(1.125)^{-4.5} = 17.89 \times 0.583 = 10.43$

$$A^* = \frac{100\sqrt{3000}}{5 \times 10^6} \times 10.43 = \frac{100(54.77)}{5 \times 10^6} \times 10.43$$

$$= \frac{5477}{5 \times 10^6} \times 10.43 = 0.001095 \times 10.43 = 0.01142 \text{ m}^2$$

**Answer:** $A^* = 114$ cm²

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: C** - Area-velocity relation: subsonic ($\text{Ma}^2 - 1 < 0$) → converging ($dA < 0$) gives acceleration ($du > 0$), behaves like incompressible.

**Problem 2: C** - Choked flow: throat reaches **Ma = 1** (sonic), limits mass flow, characteristic of maximum flow condition.

**Problem 3: C** - Supersonic ($\text{Ma}^2 - 1 > 0$) → **diverging** duct ($dA > 0$) causes acceleration ($du > 0$), counterintuitive!

**Problem 4: B** - Choking: **Ma = 1 at throat** limits mass flow, downstream cannot increase $\dot{m}$ (no upstream communication).

**Problem 5: C** - De Laval nozzle: **converging-diverging** accelerates subsonic → sonic (throat) → supersonic, essential for supersonic exhaust.
