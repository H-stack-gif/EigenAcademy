# Article 5.5: Wall-Bounded Turbulence

Walls profoundly modify turbulence structure, imposing no-slip boundary condition and blocking normal velocity fluctuations. **Turbulent boundary layers** exhibit distinct layered structure: **viscous sublayer** ($y^+ < 5$) where molecular viscosity dominates and $u^+ = y^+$ (linear profile), **buffer layer** ($5 < y^+ < 30$) transitioning between viscous and turbulent, **log layer** ($30 < y^+ < 300$) where **logarithmic law of the wall** $u^+ = \frac{1}{\kappa}\ln y^+ + B$ holds (von Kármán constant $\kappa \approx 0.41$, constant $B \approx 5$), and **outer layer** ($y^+ > 300$) where **velocity defect law** $\frac{U_e - u}{u_\tau} = f(\eta)$ with $\eta = y/\delta$ describes wake-like behavior. Wall-normal coordinate scaled as $y^+ = yu_\tau/\nu$ using **friction velocity** $u_\tau = \sqrt{\tau_w/\rho}$ collapses inner-layer profiles; outer layer scales with boundary layer thickness $\delta$.

The **overlap region** where inner and outer scalings simultaneously apply yields logarithmic profile via **matched asymptotic expansions**—a cornerstone result. **Turbulent pipe flow** exhibits similar structure with log layer spanning much of cross-section. **Skin friction coefficient** $C_f = \tau_w/(\frac{1}{2}\rho U^2)$ for turbulent flow scales as $C_f \sim \text{Re}^{-1/4}$ (power law) versus laminar $C_f \sim \text{Re}^{-1}$—turbulence dramatically increases drag but less steeply than Re suggests. **Rough walls** shift log layer downward: $u^+ = \frac{1}{\kappa}\ln y^+ + B - \Delta B$ where $\Delta B(\epsilon^+)$ depends on roughness Reynolds number $\epsilon^+ = \epsilon u_\tau/\nu$, transitioning from hydraulically smooth ($\epsilon^+ < 5$) to fully rough ($\epsilon^+ > 70$) where drag becomes Re-independent.

This article derives wall scaling using $u_\tau$, $\nu$, and $y$ for inner layer, develops **law of the wall** $u^+ = (1/\kappa)\ln y^+ + B$ from overlap matching, discusses buffer layer transitional behavior, presents **velocity defect law** $(U_e - u)/u_\tau = f(y/\delta)$ for outer layer, analyzes turbulent pipe flow and its similarity to boundary layers, derives skin friction correlations $C_f \sim \text{Re}^{-1/4}$, treats rough wall effects including Nikuradse's sand-grain experiments, discusses anisotropy near walls (suppression of $\overline{v'^2}$, enhancement of $\overline{u'^2}$), introduces turbulent structures (streaks, bursts, sweeps, ejections), and works through extensive examples involving $y^+$ calculations, friction velocity determination, log-law application, skin friction prediction, roughness effects, and wall-layer structure identification.

## Wall Coordinate and Friction Velocity

**Friction velocity**:

$$\boxed{u_\tau = \sqrt{\frac{\tau_w}{\rho}}}$$

Units: m/s (velocity scale set by wall shear).

**Wall coordinate**:

$$\boxed{y^+ = \frac{yu_\tau}{\nu}}$$

**Velocity normalized**:

$$\boxed{u^+ = \frac{u}{u_\tau}}$$

**Inner-layer scaling**: All quantities in wall units ($u^+$, $y^+$).

## Viscous Sublayer

**Region**: $y^+ < 5$

**Dominant**: Viscous stress $\tau = \mu\frac{du}{dy}$

Near wall: $\tau \approx \tau_w = \mu\frac{du}{dy}\bigg|_0$

$$\frac{du}{dy} \approx \frac{\tau_w}{\mu}$$

Integrate:

$$u \approx \frac{\tau_w}{\mu}y = \frac{\rho u_\tau^2}{\mu}y = \frac{u_\tau^2}{\nu}y$$

Normalize:

$$\boxed{u^+ = y^+}$$

**Linear profile** in viscous sublayer!

## Example 1: Viscous Sublayer Thickness

$u_\tau = 0.5$ m/s, $\nu = 1.5 \times 10^{-5}$ m²/s.

At what $y$ does viscous sublayer end ($y^+ = 5$)?

**Solution:**

$$y^+ = \frac{yu_\tau}{\nu} = 5$$

$$y = \frac{5\nu}{u_\tau} = \frac{5(1.5 \times 10^{-5})}{0.5} = \frac{7.5 \times 10^{-5}}{0.5}$$

$$= 1.5 \times 10^{-4} \text{ m} = 0.15 \text{ mm}$$

**Very thin layer!**

**Answer:** $y = 0.15$ mm

---

## Log Layer (Law of the Wall)

**Region**: $30 < y^+ < 300$ (varies, can extend to $y^+ \sim 0.2\delta^+$)

**Logarithmic law**:

$$\boxed{u^+ = \frac{1}{\kappa}\ln y^+ + B}$$

**Constants**:
- $\kappa \approx 0.41$ (von Kármán constant)
- $B \approx 5.0$ (smooth walls)

**Derivation**: Overlap matching between inner ($y^+$) and outer ($y/\delta$) scalings.

**Remarkably universal**: Observed in pipes, channels, boundary layers, atmospheric flows.

## Example 2: Velocity in Log Layer

At $y^+ = 100$, find $u^+$ using log law ($\kappa = 0.41$, $B = 5$).

**Solution:**

$$u^+ = \frac{1}{0.41}\ln(100) + 5 = \frac{4.605}{0.41} + 5$$

$$= 11.23 + 5 = 16.23$$

**Velocity**: $u = 16.23u_\tau$

**Answer:** $u^+ = 16.2$

---

## Buffer Layer

**Region**: $5 < y^+ < 30$

**Transition** between viscous and turbulent regimes.

**No simple law**: Neither $u^+ = y^+$ nor log law accurate.

**Empirical**: Power laws like $u^+ = Ay^{+n}$ with $n \approx 0.15-0.2$.

**Important for**: Peak turbulence production, $\overline{u'v'}$ maximum.

## Velocity Defect Law (Outer Layer)

**Region**: $y^+ > 300$ or $y/\delta > 0.1$

**Outer scaling**: $\eta = y/\delta$, velocity defect $(U_e - u)/u_\tau$

$$\boxed{\frac{U_e - u}{u_\tau} = f(\eta)}$$

where $f$ is wake function (depends on pressure gradient).

**Zero pressure gradient**: $f(\eta) = -\frac{1}{\kappa}\ln\eta + C$

**Physical**: Outer region like turbulent wake, independent of viscosity.

## Example 3: Friction Velocity from Wall Shear

Wall shear $\tau_w = 10$ Pa, water ($\rho = 1000$ kg/m³).

Find $u_\tau$.

**Solution:**

$$u_\tau = \sqrt{\frac{\tau_w}{\rho}} = \sqrt{\frac{10}{1000}} = \sqrt{0.01} = 0.1 \text{ m/s}$$

**Answer:** $u_\tau = 0.1$ m/s

---

## Overlap Region and Matching

**Inner solution** (near wall): Depends on $y$, $u_\tau$, $\nu$

$$u_{inner} = u_\tau g(y^+)$$

**Outer solution** (away from wall): Depends on $y$, $u_\tau$, $\delta$, $U_e$

$$u_{outer} = U_e - u_\tau h(\eta)$$

**Overlap** ($y^+ \gg 1$ and $\eta \ll 1$):

Both must match → logarithmic form only possibility!

$$u^+ = \frac{1}{\kappa}\ln y^+ + B = U_e^+ - \frac{1}{\kappa}\ln\eta - C$$

## Turbulent Pipe Flow

**Similar structure**: Viscous sublayer → buffer → log layer → wake (centerline).

**Log layer**: Extends from $y^+ \approx 30$ to $r/R \approx 0.8$.

**Centerline**: Wakefunction correction.

**Mean velocity** (approximate):

$$\overline{u} = u_\tau\left(\frac{1}{\kappa}\ln y^+ + B\right)$$

in log region.

## Example 4: Centerline Velocity in Pipe

Pipe radius $R = 0.05$ m, $u_\tau = 0.4$ m/s, $\nu = 10^{-6}$ m²/s.

Estimate centerline $u_c$.

**Solution:**

At $y = R$: $y^+ = \frac{(0.05)(0.4)}{10^{-6}} = 20,000$

Log law (rough estimate):

$$u_c^+ = \frac{1}{0.41}\ln(20000) + 5 + \text{wake}$$

$$= 2.44(9.90) + 5 + 2 = 24.2 + 7 = 31.2$$

(wake correction $\sim 2$ at centerline)

$$u_c = 31.2(0.4) = 12.5 \text{ m/s}$$

**Answer:** $u_c \approx 12.5$ m/s

## Skin Friction Correlation

**Turbulent**: $C_f = \frac{\tau_w}{\frac{1}{2}\rho U^2}$

**Empirical** (flat plate):

$$\boxed{C_f = \frac{0.059}{\text{Re}_x^{1/5}}}$$

(Schultz-Grunow, valid $10^5 < \text{Re}_x < 10^9$)

**Compare laminar**: $C_f = 0.664/\sqrt{\text{Re}_x}$ (Blasius)

**Turbulent has higher drag but grows slower** with Re.

## Example 5: Turbulent vs. Laminar $C_f$

At $\text{Re}_x = 10^6$, compare turbulent and laminar $C_f$.

**Solution:**

**Laminar**: $C_f = \frac{0.664}{\sqrt{10^6}} = \frac{0.664}{1000} = 0.000664$

**Turbulent**: $C_f = \frac{0.059}{(10^6)^{1/5}} = \frac{0.059}{10^{6/5}} = \frac{0.059}{15.85} = 0.00372$

**Turbulent is $\sim 5.6\times$ higher!**

**Answer:** Laminar: $6.6 \times 10^{-4}$; Turbulent: $3.7 \times 10^{-3}$

## Rough Walls

**Roughness height**: $\epsilon$ (sand-grain equivalent)

**Roughness Reynolds number**: $\epsilon^+ = \frac{\epsilon u_\tau}{\nu}$

**Regimes**:
- **Hydraulically smooth**: $\epsilon^+ < 5$ (roughness in viscous sublayer)
- **Transitionally rough**: $5 < \epsilon^+ < 70$
- **Fully rough**: $\epsilon^+ > 70$ (roughness dominates)

**Rough-wall log law**:

$$u^+ = \frac{1}{\kappa}\ln y^+ + B - \Delta B(\epsilon^+)$$

where $\Delta B$ = roughness function (downward shift).

**Fully rough**: $\Delta B \approx \frac{1}{\kappa}\ln\epsilon^+ - 3.5$

## Example 6: Roughness Classification

$\epsilon = 0.1$ mm, $u_\tau = 0.3$ m/s, $\nu = 10^{-6}$ m²/s.

Is wall smooth, transitional, or rough?

**Solution:**

$$\epsilon^+ = \frac{(0.0001)(0.3)}{10^{-6}} = \frac{3 \times 10^{-5}}{10^{-6}} = 30$$

$5 < \epsilon^+ < 70$ → **Transitionally rough**

**Moderate roughness effect** on drag.

**Answer:** Transitionally rough ($\epsilon^+ = 30$)

---

## Anisotropy Near Walls

**Observation**: $\overline{u'^2} \gg \overline{v'^2}$ near wall.

**Reason**: Wall blocks normal fluctuations ($v' \to 0$ at wall).

**Typical** (at $y^+ \approx 15$):
- $\overline{u'^2}/u_\tau^2 \approx 7$
- $\overline{v'^2}/u_\tau^2 \approx 1$
- $\overline{w'^2}/u_\tau^2 \approx 4$

**Highly anisotropic** contrary to Kolmogorov assumption.

**Eddy viscosity models** (isotropic) struggle near walls.

## Turbulent Structures

**Streaks**: Alternating low/high-speed fluid ($\lambda_z^+ \approx 100$).

**Bursts**: Violent ejection of low-momentum fluid from wall.

**Sweeps**: High-momentum fluid rushing toward wall.

**Quasi-coherent**: Organized structures persist longer than eddy turnover time.

**Reynolds stress production**: Dominated by sweep-ejection cycles.

## Example 7: Streak Spacing

Streaks spaced $\lambda_z^+ = 100$.

If $u_\tau = 0.5$ m/s, $\nu = 1.5 \times 10^{-5}$ m²/s, find physical spacing.

**Solution:**

$$\lambda_z = \frac{\lambda_z^+ \nu}{u_\tau} = \frac{100(1.5 \times 10^{-5})}{0.5} = \frac{1.5 \times 10^{-3}}{0.5}$$

$$= 3 \times 10^{-3} \text{ m} = 3 \text{ mm}$$

**Small-scale structures** in wall layer.

**Answer:** $\lambda_z = 3$ mm

## Drag Reduction

**Riblets**: Streamwise grooves reduce drag $\sim 5-10$%.

**Polymer additives**: Toms effect—small polymer concentration drastically reduces drag.

**Compliant walls**: Flexible surfaces absorb turbulent fluctuations.

**Superhydrophobic**: Air pockets reduce shear.

## Example 8: Log-Law Velocity Prediction

$y = 0.01$ m, $u_\tau = 0.4$ m/s, $\nu = 1.5 \times 10^{-5}$ m²/s.

Predict velocity using log law.

**Solution:**

$$y^+ = \frac{(0.01)(0.4)}{1.5 \times 10^{-5}} = \frac{0.004}{1.5 \times 10^{-5}} = 267$$

$30 < y^+ < 300$ → **in log layer** ✓

$$u^+ = \frac{1}{0.41}\ln(267) + 5 = 2.44(5.59) + 5 = 13.6 + 5 = 18.6$$

$$u = 18.6(0.4) = 7.44 \text{ m/s}$$

**Answer:** $u = 7.4$ m/s

---

## Example 9: Boundary Layer Thickness from $u_\tau$

Freestream $U = 20$ m/s, $u_\tau = 0.6$ m/s.

Estimate $\delta$ using $u_\tau/U \approx 0.04$ (typical turbulent BL).

**Solution:**

$u_\tau$ given. Typical: $C_f = 2(u_\tau/U)^2$

$$C_f = 2\left(\frac{0.6}{20}\right)^2 = 2(0.03)^2 = 0.0018$$

For flat plate: $C_f = 0.059/\text{Re}_x^{1/5}$

But need $x$ or $\delta$ — incomplete problem.

**Alternative**: 

$$U^+ = \frac{20}{0.6} = 33.3$$

From log law extended: $\delta^+ = \frac{\delta u_\tau}{\nu}$ very large ($\sim 10^3-10^5$).

**Need more info** for accurate $\delta$.

**Typical**: $\delta u_\tau/\nu \sim 2000-5000$.

**Answer:** Incomplete data, $\delta \sim 1-10$ cm likely

---

## Example 10: Fully Rough Drag Increase

Smooth wall: $\Delta B = 0$. Rough wall: $\Delta B = 5$.

How does drag change (same $u_\tau$)?

**Solution:**

Log law: $u^+ = \frac{1}{\kappa}\ln y^+ + B - \Delta B$

At same $y^+$: $u^+$ decreases by $\Delta B = 5$.

To maintain same flow rate, must **increase $u_\tau$** (higher wall shear).

**Drag increases**: $\tau_w = \rho u_\tau^2$ increases.

**Quantitative**: Depends on details, but typically $20-50$% drag increase.

**Answer:** Drag increases (higher $u_\tau$ needed)

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: B** - Viscous sublayer ($y^+ < 5$): viscous stress dominates, integration of constant stress gives **linear profile** $u^+ = y^+$.

**Problem 2: B** - Von Kármán constant: $\kappa \approx 0.41$ appears in logarithmic law of the wall $u^+ = \frac{1}{\kappa}\ln y^+ + B$, universal for wall-bounded turbulence.

**Problem 3: B** - Friction velocity: $u_\tau = \sqrt{\tau_w/\rho}$ has units $\sqrt{Pa/(kg/m^3)} = \sqrt{m^2/s^2} = $ **m/s** (velocity scale).

**Problem 4: D** - Turbulent flat plate: $C_f \sim \text{Re}^{-1/5}$ or $\sim \text{Re}^{-1/4}$ depending on correlation, **much weaker** than laminar $\sim \text{Re}^{-1}$.

**Problem 5: B** - Rough walls: $u^+ = \frac{1}{\kappa}\ln y^+ + B - \Delta B$ where $\Delta B > 0$ → log layer shifts **downward** (lower velocity at given $y^+$, higher drag).
