# Article 6.4: Expansion Waves and Prandtl-Meyer Flow


**Supersonic expansion** around convex corners proceeds smoothly via **expansion fan** (Prandtl-Meyer expansion)—continuous, isentropic process unlike compression shocks. The **Prandtl-Meyer function** $\nu(\text{Ma})$ characterizes turning: flow deflection $\Delta\theta = \nu(\text{Ma}_2) - \nu(\text{Ma}_1)$ uniquely determines downstream Mach number from upstream conditions and geometry. **Mach waves** at angle $\mu = \sin^{-1}(1/\text{Ma})$ propagate disturbances; expansion comprises infinite family of Mach waves emanating from corner. **Characteristics**—curves along which disturbances propagate—form mathematical basis: $C^+$ and $C^-$ families with directions $\theta \pm \mu$ carry **Riemann invariants**, differential equations integrated to determine flow field. Unlike shocks (entropy increases), expansions are **reversible** ($\Delta s = 0$), pressure/temperature/density decrease, Mach number increases.

**Maximum turning angle**: As $\text{Ma} \to \infty$, $\nu_{max} = \frac{\pi}{2}(\sqrt{\frac{\gamma+1}{\gamma-1}} - 1) \approx 130.5°$ for air—beyond this, flow expands to vacuum. **Method of characteristics** solves 2D supersonic flow by tracking characteristic lines $C^{\pm}$: along each characteristic, specific combination of flow variables (Riemann invariant) remains constant. **Centered expansion fan**: simple wave where all characteristics emanate from single point (sharp corner); downstream of fan, flow uniform with increased Ma, decreased $P$, $T$, $\rho$. Expansion fans appear on: supersonic aircraft trailing edges, rocket plumes expanding to low pressure, wind tunnel corners. **Nozzle design** exploits method of characteristics to shape walls yielding desired exit conditions while maintaining isentropic acceleration.

This article derives Prandtl-Meyer function $\nu(\text{Ma})$ from isentropic turning relations, introduces Mach angle $\mu = \sin^{-1}(1/\text{Ma})$ and Mach cone, develops centered expansion fan geometry, presents method of characteristics for supersonic flow with $C^{\pm}$ families and compatibility equations, derives Riemann invariants along characteristics, treats maximum turning angle calculation, discusses expansion vs. compression asymmetry (continuous vs. discontinuous), applies to simple wave regions, and works through extensive examples involving Prandtl-Meyer calculations, expansion fan angles, characteristic line construction, nozzle exit conditions, and shock-expansion interactions on airfoils.

## Prandtl-Meyer Function

**For isentropic turning** from $\text{Ma}_1$ to $\text{Ma}_2$ through angle $\Delta\theta$:

$$\boxed{\nu(\text{Ma}) = \sqrt{\frac{\gamma+1}{\gamma-1}}\tan^{-1}\left(\sqrt{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma+1}(\text{Ma}^2-1)}\right) - \tan^{-1}(\sqrt{\text{Ma}^2-1})}$$

**Turning angle**: $\Delta\theta = \nu(\text{Ma}_2) - \nu(\text{Ma}_1)$

**For air** ($\gamma = 1.4$):
$$\nu(\text{Ma}) = \sqrt{6}\tan^{-1}\left(\sqrt{\frac{\text{Ma}^2-1}{6}}\right) - \tan^{-1}(\sqrt{\text{Ma}^2-1})$$

## Example 1: Prandtl-Meyer Function Value

Find $\nu(\text{Ma} = 2)$ for air.

**Solution:**

$$\nu(2) = \sqrt{6}\tan^{-1}\left(\sqrt{\frac{4-1}{6}}\right) - \tan^{-1}(\sqrt{3})$$

$$= 2.449\tan^{-1}(0.707) - \tan^{-1}(1.732)$$

$$= 2.449(35.26°) - 60.0° = 86.35° - 60.0° = 26.35°$$

**Answer:** $\nu = 26.4°$

---

## Mach Angle and Mach Cone

**Mach angle** (angle of Mach wave):
$$\boxed{\mu = \sin^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{\text{Ma}}\right)}$$

**Mach cone**: 3D surface of disturbances, half-angle $\mu$.

**Zone of action**: Downstream of Mach cone.
**Zone of silence**: Upstream (cannot receive signals).

## Example 2: Mach Angle Calculation

$\text{Ma} = 2.5$.

Find Mach angle.

**Solution:**

$$\mu = \sin^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{2.5}\right) = \sin^{-1}(0.4) = 23.6°$$

**Narrow cone** at high Ma.

**As $\text{Ma} \to 1$**: $\mu \to 90°$ (sonic).

**Answer:** $\mu = 23.6°$

---

## Centered Expansion Fan

**Flow around convex corner** (angle $\theta$):

**Upstream**: Uniform at $\text{Ma}_1$

**Fan**: Infinite Mach waves from corner, each turns flow slightly.

**Downstream**: Uniform at $\text{Ma}_2$ where $\nu(\text{Ma}_2) = \nu(\text{Ma}_1) + \theta$

**Fan angles**:
- Leading edge (upstream): $\mu_1 = \sin^{-1}(1/\text{Ma}_1)$
- Trailing edge (downstream): $\mu_2 = \sin^{-1}(1/\text{Ma}_2)$

## Example 3: Expansion Fan Downstream Mach

$\text{Ma}_1 = 2$, corner angle $\theta = 15°$.

Find $\text{Ma}_2$ (air).

**Solution:**

$$\nu_1 = 26.38°$$ (from tables or formula)

$$\nu_2 = \nu_1 + \theta = 26.38° + 15° = 41.38°$$

**From tables**: $\nu = 41.38°$ → $\text{Ma}_2 \approx 2.385$

**Mach number increases** through expansion.

**Answer:** $\text{Ma}_2 \approx 2.39$

---

## Property Changes Through Expansion

**Isentropic relations** apply:

$$\frac{P_2}{P_1} = \left(\frac{T_2}{T_1}\right)^{\gamma/(\gamma-1)}$$

With:
$$\frac{T_2}{T_1} = \frac{1 + \frac{\gamma-1}{2}\text{Ma}_1^2}{1 + \frac{\gamma-1}{2}\text{Ma}_2^2}$$

**Pressure drops**, **temperature drops**, **density drops** through expansion.

## Example 4: Pressure Ratio Through Expansion

From Example 3: $\text{Ma}_1 = 2$, $\text{Ma}_2 = 2.385$.

Find $P_2/P_1$ (air).

**Solution:**

$$\frac{T_2}{T_1} = \frac{1 + 0.2(4)}{1 + 0.2(5.688)} = \frac{1.8}{2.138} = 0.842$$

$$\frac{P_2}{P_1} = (0.842)^{3.5} = 0.596$$

**40% pressure drop** through 15° expansion.

**Answer:** $P_2/P_1 = 0.60$

---

## Maximum Turning Angle

**As $\text{Ma} \to \infty$**:

$$\nu_{max} = \frac{\pi}{2}\left(\sqrt{\frac{\gamma+1}{\gamma-1}} - 1\right)$$

**For air**: $\nu_{max} = \frac{\pi}{2}(\sqrt{6} - 1) = 1.5708(1.449) = 130.45°$

**Beyond** $\nu_{max}$: Expansion to vacuum.

## Example 5: Maximum Expansion Verification

Show $\nu_{max}$ for air is ~130°.

**Solution:**

$$\nu_{max} = 90°(\sqrt{6} - 1) = 90(2.449 - 1) = 90(1.449) = 130.4°$$

**Physical limit**: Cannot turn more than ~130° before vacuum.

**Answer:** $\nu_{max} = 130.4°$ ✓

## Method of Characteristics

**Characteristic lines**: Paths along which disturbances propagate.

**For 2D supersonic flow**:

**$C^+$ characteristics**: $\frac{dy}{dx} = \tan(\theta + \mu)$

**$C^-$ characteristics**: $\frac{dy}{dx} = \tan(\theta - \mu)$

**Compatibility conditions**: Along characteristics, specific variable combinations constant.

**Riemann invariants**:
- Along $C^+$: $\theta + \nu = \text{const}$
- Along $C^-$: $\theta - \nu = \text{const}$

## Example 6: Characteristic Direction

At point where $\text{Ma} = 2.5$, $\theta = 10°$.

Find $C^+$ and $C^-$ slopes.

**Solution:**

$$\mu = \sin^{-1}(1/2.5) = 23.6°$$

$$C^+: \frac{dy}{dx} = \tan(10° + 23.6°) = \tan(33.6°) = 0.663$$

$$C^-: \frac{dy}{dx} = \tan(10° - 23.6°) = \tan(-13.6°) = -0.242$$

**Two characteristic families** cross at each point.

**Answer:** $C^+$: 0.66; $C^-$: -0.24

## Simple Wave Region

**Simple wave**: All $C^+$ (or $C^-$) emanate from single curve/point.

**Centered expansion**: All characteristics from corner → simple wave.

**Within simple wave**: Flow determined by single Riemann invariant.

## Shock-Expansion Theory

**Supersonic airfoil**:
- **Leading edge**: Oblique shock (compression)
- **Trailing edge**: Expansion fan

**Combine**: Shock relations + Prandtl-Meyer → entire flowfield.

**Thin airfoil**: Weak shocks, small expansions → linearize.

## Example 7: Flat Plate Lift via Shock-Expansion

Flat plate at $\alpha = 5°$, $\text{Ma}_\infty = 2.5$.

Estimate $C_L$.

**Solution:**

**Lower surface** (compression): Weak oblique shock.
**Upper surface** (expansion): Prandtl-Meyer fan.

**Approximate** (assuming small angles):
$$\Delta P_{lower} \approx \frac{2\gamma\text{Ma}^2}{\gamma+1}\alpha = \frac{2(1.4)(6.25)}{2.4}(0.0873) = 1.528 \text{ (relative)}$$

$$C_L \approx \frac{4\alpha}{\sqrt{\text{Ma}^2-1}} = \frac{4(0.0873)}{\sqrt{5.25}} = \frac{0.349}{2.291} = 0.152$$

**Ackeret formula** (next article details).

**Answer:** $C_L \approx 0.15$

## Expansion vs. Compression Asymmetry

**Compression** (adverse): Shocks (discontinuous, entropy increase)

**Expansion** (favorable): Continuous fans (isentropic, no entropy change)

**Physical**: Information can spread smoothly in expansion, must concentrate in compression.

## Example 8: Nozzle Exit Design

Design supersonic nozzle exit for $\text{Ma}_e = 3$ from $\text{Ma} = 2$ at point.

Turning angle?

**Solution:**

$$\nu_1 = \nu(2) = 26.38°$$

$$\nu_2 = \nu(3) = \sqrt{6}\tan^{-1}\sqrt{\frac{8}{6}} - \tan^{-1}\sqrt{8}$$

$$= 2.449(47.11°) - 70.53° = 115.4° - 70.53° = 44.87°$$

$$\Delta\theta = 44.87° - 26.38° = 18.49°$$

**Wall turns ~18.5°** to accelerate Ma 2 → 3.

**Answer:** $\theta = 18.5°$

---

## Example 9: Vacuum Expansion

$\text{Ma}_1 = 5$, expand to vacuum.

Total turning angle?

**Solution:**

$$\nu_1 = \nu(5) \approx 76.9°$$ (from tables)

$$\nu_2 = \nu_{max} = 130.45°$$

$$\Delta\theta = 130.45° - 76.9° = 53.55°$$

**~54° turn** to reach vacuum from Ma = 5.

**Answer:** $\theta = 53.6°$

---

## Example 10: Riemann Invariant Check

Along $C^+$ from $(\text{Ma}_1 = 2, \theta_1 = 0°)$ to $(\text{Ma}_2 = ?, \theta_2 = 15°)$.

Find $\text{Ma}_2$.

**Solution:**

**Riemann invariant** (along $C^+$): $\theta + \nu = \text{const}$

$$0° + 26.38° = 15° + \nu_2$$

$$\nu_2 = 26.38° - 15° = 11.38°$$

**From tables**: $\nu = 11.38°$ → $\text{Ma} \approx 1.55$

**Answer:** $\text{Ma}_2 = 1.55$
