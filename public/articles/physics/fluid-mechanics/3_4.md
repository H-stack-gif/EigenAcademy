# Article 3.4: Vorticity Dynamics and Circulation

Vorticity—the curl of velocity $\boldsymbol{\omega} = \nabla \times \mathbf{u}$—measures local rotation of fluid elements. While kinematic in definition, vorticity obeys profound **dynamical laws** derived from the momentum equation. In inviscid flow, the **vorticity equation** reveals that vorticity is **conserved following fluid particles** in 2D flows, and undergoes **vortex stretching** in 3D due to the term $(\boldsymbol{\omega} \cdot \nabla)\mathbf{u}$. This stretching mechanism intensifies vorticity along directions of strain, driving turbulent energy cascade and atmospheric phenomena like tornadoes. **Circulation** $\Gamma = \oint_C \mathbf{u} \cdot d\mathbf{l}$, the line integral of velocity, provides an integral measure of vorticity via Stokes' theorem: $\Gamma = \int_S \boldsymbol{\omega} \cdot d\mathbf{A}$.

**Kelvin's circulation theorem** states that for inviscid, barotropic flow, circulation around a material contour (moving with the fluid) remains constant: $\frac{D\Gamma}{Dt} = 0$. This remarkable result explains why vorticity, once created, persists and moves with the fluid. **Helmholtz vortex theorems** extend this: vortex lines move with the fluid, vortex tube strength is constant along its length, and vortex lines cannot end in the fluid (they form closed loops or terminate at boundaries). These theorems provide powerful constraints on vortex dynamics. The **Biot-Savart law** relates vorticity distribution to velocity field: $\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{r}) = -\frac{1}{4\pi}\int \frac{\boldsymbol{\omega}(\mathbf{r}') \times (\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}')}{|\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}'|^3} dV'$, analogous to magnetic field from current distribution. For point vortices in 2D, this simplifies to explicit velocity formulas governing their motion—vortex pairs travel together, vortex streets exhibit periodic shedding.

This article derives the vorticity equation from Euler/Navier-Stokes, explores 2D and 3D vorticity dynamics separately (2D: no stretching, advection only; 3D: stretching dominates), proves Kelvin's circulation theorem and discusses its assumptions, develops Helmholtz theorems and their physical meaning, derives the Biot-Savart law for velocity from vorticity, analyzes point vortex motion and vortex interactions, treats line and ring vortices, discusses vorticity generation at boundaries, and works through extensive examples involving circulation calculations, vortex tube dynamics, vortex pair motion, vortex stretching estimates, and trailing vortices. The mathematics involves heavy vector calculus (curl, Stokes' theorem, vector identities), material derivatives of line and surface integrals, and integral equations.

## Vorticity Equation (Inviscid)

Start with **Euler equation**:
$$\rho\frac{D\mathbf{u}}{Dt} = -\nabla P + \rho\mathbf{g}$$

Take curl:
$$\nabla \times \left(\frac{D\mathbf{u}}{Dt}\right) = -\nabla \times (\nabla P/\rho) + \nabla \times \mathbf{g}$$

**Note**: $\nabla \times \nabla P = 0$ (curl of gradient is zero).

For $\mathbf{g} = -g\nabla z$: $\nabla \times \mathbf{g} = 0$

Using vector identity:
$$\frac{D\mathbf{u}}{Dt} = \frac{\partial\mathbf{u}}{\partial t} + (\mathbf{u} \cdot \nabla)\mathbf{u} = \frac{\partial\mathbf{u}}{\partial t} + \nabla\left(\frac{\mathbf{u}^2}{2}\right) - \mathbf{u} \times (\nabla \times \mathbf{u})$$

$$= \frac{\partial\mathbf{u}}{\partial t} + \nabla\left(\frac{\mathbf{u}^2}{2}\right) - \mathbf{u} \times \boldsymbol{\omega}$$

Take curl:
$$\nabla \times \frac{D\mathbf{u}}{Dt} = \nabla \times \frac{\partial\mathbf{u}}{\partial t} - \nabla \times (\mathbf{u} \times \boldsymbol{\omega})$$

$$= \frac{\partial\boldsymbol{\omega}}{\partial t} - \nabla \times (\mathbf{u} \times \boldsymbol{\omega})$$

Using identity $\nabla \times (\mathbf{u} \times \boldsymbol{\omega}) = (\boldsymbol{\omega} \cdot \nabla)\mathbf{u} - (\mathbf{u} \cdot \nabla)\boldsymbol{\omega} + \mathbf{u}(\nabla \cdot \boldsymbol{\omega}) - \boldsymbol{\omega}(\nabla \cdot \mathbf{u})$:

For incompressible: $\nabla \cdot \mathbf{u} = 0$, and $\nabla \cdot \boldsymbol{\omega} = 0$ always (divergence of curl is zero).

Result:
$$\boxed{\frac{D\boldsymbol{\omega}}{Dt} = (\boldsymbol{\omega} \cdot \nabla)\mathbf{u}}$$

**Vorticity equation** for inviscid, incompressible flow!

**Physical interpretation**:
- Left side: rate of change of vorticity following fluid
- Right side: **vortex stretching** term (3D effect)

## 2D Vorticity Dynamics

In **2D flow** (no $z$-dependence, $w = 0$):

$$\boldsymbol{\omega} = \omega_z\hat{\mathbf{z}}$$

$$(\boldsymbol{\omega} \cdot \nabla)\mathbf{u} = 0$$ (perpendicular)

Vorticity equation becomes:

$$\boxed{\frac{D\omega_z}{Dt} = 0}$$

**Vorticity is conserved** following fluid particles!

$$\frac{\partial\omega_z}{\partial t} + u\frac{\partial\omega_z}{\partial x} + v\frac{\partial\omega_z}{\partial y} = 0$$

**Advection equation**: Vorticity transported as passive scalar (no generation, no stretching).

## 3D Vorticity: Vortex Stretching

The term $(\boldsymbol{\omega} \cdot \nabla)\mathbf{u}$ represents **vortex stretching**.

**Physical mechanism**: If vortex tube is stretched, conservation of angular momentum requires rotation rate to increase.

**Example**: Fluid element with vorticity $\boldsymbol{\omega}$ aligned with straining flow $\mathbf{u} = (\epsilon x, -\epsilon y/2, -\epsilon z/2)$:

$$(\boldsymbol{\omega} \cdot \nabla)\mathbf{u} = \omega_x\epsilon\hat{\mathbf{x}} - \omega_y\frac{\epsilon}{2}\hat{\mathbf{y}} - \omega_z\frac{\epsilon}{2}\hat{\mathbf{z}}$$

If $\boldsymbol{\omega} = \omega_0\hat{\mathbf{x}}$:
$$\frac{D\omega_x}{Dt} = \epsilon\omega_x$$

Solution: $\omega_x(t) = \omega_0 e^{\epsilon t}$ (exponential growth!)

**Turbulence**: Vortex stretching cascade transfers energy to small scales.

## Circulation

**Definition**:
$$\boxed{\Gamma = \oint_C \mathbf{u} \cdot d\mathbf{l}}$$

By **Stokes' theorem**:
$$\boxed{\Gamma = \int_S \boldsymbol{\omega} \cdot d\mathbf{A}}$$

**Physical meaning**: Net "spin" around curve.

**Units**: m²/s (same as velocity × length)

## Kelvin's Circulation Theorem

For **material curve** $C(t)$ moving with fluid:

$$\boxed{\frac{D\Gamma}{Dt} = 0}$$

for inviscid, barotropic flow.

**Proof**:

$$\frac{D\Gamma}{Dt} = \frac{D}{Dt}\oint_{C(t)} \mathbf{u} \cdot d\mathbf{l}$$

$$= \oint_{C(t)} \frac{D\mathbf{u}}{Dt} \cdot d\mathbf{l} + \oint_{C(t)} \mathbf{u} \cdot \frac{D(d\mathbf{l})}{Dt}$$

From Euler: $\frac{D\mathbf{u}}{Dt} = -\frac{1}{\rho}\nabla P + \mathbf{g}$

For barotropic ($\rho = \rho(P)$): $\frac{1}{\rho}\nabla P = \nabla\left(\int \frac{dP}{\rho}\right)$

$$\oint \frac{D\mathbf{u}}{Dt} \cdot d\mathbf{l} = -\oint \nabla\left(\int\frac{dP}{\rho} - gz\right) \cdot d\mathbf{l} = 0$$

(closed loop)

Second term: $\frac{D(d\mathbf{l})}{Dt} = d(\mathbf{u})$ (velocity difference between nearby points)

$$\oint \mathbf{u} \cdot d(\mathbf{u}) = \oint d\left(\frac{\mathbf{u}^2}{2}\right) = 0$$

Therefore: $\frac{D\Gamma}{Dt} = 0$ ✓

**Consequences**:
- Circulation created = circulation destroyed (by viscosity or baroclinicity)
- Irrotational flow remains irrotational
- Vorticity confined to regions where created

## Helmholtz Vortex Theorems

**Theorem 1**: **Vortex lines** move with the fluid.

(A vortex line is a curve everywhere tangent to $\boldsymbol{\omega}$.)

**Theorem 2**: **Strength of vortex tube** is constant along its length.

Strength = $\int_A \boldsymbol{\omega} \cdot d\mathbf{A}$ over cross-section.

**Proof**: By continuity of vorticity field and Kelvin's theorem.

**Theorem 3**: Vortex lines **cannot end** in the fluid.

They form **closed loops** or extend to boundaries/infinity.

**Physical interpretation**: Like magnetic field lines (from $\nabla \cdot \mathbf{B} = 0$), since $\nabla \cdot \boldsymbol{\omega} = 0$.

## Biot-Savart Law

Velocity field induced by vorticity distribution:

$$\boxed{\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{r}) = -\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_V \frac{\boldsymbol{\omega}(\mathbf{r}') \times (\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}')}{|\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}'|^3} dV'}$$

**Analogous to**: Magnetic field $\mathbf{B}$ from current density $\mathbf{J}$:

$$\mathbf{B}(\mathbf{r}) = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\int \frac{\mathbf{J}(\mathbf{r}') \times (\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}')}{|\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}'|^3} dV'$$

**For line vortex** (filament):

$$\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{r}) = \frac{\Gamma}{4\pi}\int_{\text{line}} \frac{d\mathbf{l}' \times (\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}')}{|\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}'|^3}$$

## Point Vortices (2D)

**Single point vortex** at origin with circulation $\Gamma$:

$$u_\theta = \frac{\Gamma}{2\pi r}, \quad u_r = 0$$

**Velocity at $\mathbf{r}_i$ due to vortex at $\mathbf{r}_j$** (strength $\Gamma_j$):

$$\mathbf{u}_i = \frac{\Gamma_j}{2\pi} \frac{(\mathbf{r}_i - \mathbf{r}_j)^\perp}{|\mathbf{r}_i - \mathbf{r}_j|^2}$$

where $\mathbf{v}^\perp = (-v_y, v_x)$ (90° rotation).

**Two point vortices**: Each induces velocity on the other.

$$\frac{d\mathbf{r}_1}{dt} = \mathbf{u}_1(\mathbf{r}_1) = \frac{\Gamma_2}{2\pi} \frac{(\mathbf{r}_1 - \mathbf{r}_2)^\perp}{|\mathbf{r}_1 - \mathbf{r}_2|^2}$$

**Same-sign vortices** ($\Gamma_1\Gamma_2 > 0$): Rotate around common center.

**Opposite-sign vortices** (vortex pair): Translate together.

## Example 1: Circulation Around Rectangle

Flow $\mathbf{u} = (y, -x, 0)$. Calculate $\Gamma$ around rectangle $(0,0)$ to $(a, b)$.

**Solution:**

**Method 1**: Direct integration (4 sides, tedious).

**Method 2**: Stokes' theorem.

Vorticity: $\omega_z = \frac{\partial v}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial u}{\partial y} = -1 - 1 = -2$

$$\Gamma = \int_S \omega_z \, dA = \int_0^a\int_0^b (-2) \, dy \, dx = -2ab$$

**Answer:** $\Gamma = -2ab$

## Example 2: Kelvin's Theorem Application

Circular material curve initially has $\Gamma = 10$ m²/s. After some time, curve stretched into ellipse.

Find new circulation (inviscid, barotropic).

**Solution:**

By Kelvin's theorem: $\frac{D\Gamma}{Dt} = 0$

$$\Gamma(t) = \Gamma(0) = 10 \text{ m}^2\text{/s}$$

**Circulation conserved** regardless of curve shape!

**Answer:** $\Gamma = 10$ m²/s (unchanged)

## Example 3: Vortex Stretching Rate

Vortex tube with $\omega_z = 100$ s⁻¹ stretched in $z$-direction: $w = \epsilon z$ with $\epsilon = 0.5$ s⁻¹.

Find rate of change of $\omega_z$.

**Solution:**

From vorticity equation:
$$\frac{D\omega_z}{Dt} = \omega_z\frac{\partial w}{\partial z} = \omega_z \epsilon$$

$$\frac{D\omega_z}{Dt} = (100)(0.5) = 50 \text{ s}^{-2}$$

After time $\Delta t = 1$ s:
$$\omega_z(t) \approx \omega_z(0) + \frac{D\omega_z}{Dt}\Delta t = 100 + 50 = 150 \text{ s}^{-1}$$

**Exponential growth** for longer times.

**Answer:** $\frac{D\omega_z}{Dt} = 50$ s⁻²

## Example 4: Vortex Pair Translation

Two point vortices: $\Gamma_1 = +\Gamma$ at $(0, d/2)$, $\Gamma_2 = -\Gamma$ at $(0, -d/2)$.

Find translation velocity.

**Solution:**

Velocity at vortex 1 due to vortex 2:

$$\mathbf{u}_1 = \frac{\Gamma_2}{2\pi}\frac{(\mathbf{r}_1 - \mathbf{r}_2)^\perp}{|\mathbf{r}_1 - \mathbf{r}_2|^2}$$

$$\mathbf{r}_1 - \mathbf{r}_2 = (0, d)$$

$$(\mathbf{r}_1 - \mathbf{r}_2)^\perp = (-d, 0)$$

$$\mathbf{u}_1 = \frac{-\Gamma}{2\pi} \frac{(-d, 0)}{d^2} = \frac{\Gamma}{2\pi d}(1, 0)$$

**Both vortices** translate in $+x$ direction at:

$$U = \frac{\Gamma}{2\pi d}$$

**Answer:** Translation velocity $U = \frac{\Gamma}{2\pi d}$

## Example 5: Vortex Ring

Circular vortex ring radius $R$, circulation $\Gamma$.

Estimate self-induced velocity (approximate).

**Solution:**

By Biot-Savart, each element induces velocity on opposite side.

**Order of magnitude**: Distance $\sim R$, circulation $\Gamma$

$$U \sim \frac{\Gamma}{R}\ln\frac{R}{a}$$

where $a$ is core radius (regularization needed to avoid singularity).

**Kelvin's formula**: $U = \frac{\Gamma}{4\pi R}\left(\ln\frac{8R}{a} - \frac{1}{4}\right)$

For $R/a = 100$: $U \approx \frac{\Gamma}{4\pi R}(4.7) \approx \frac{0.37\Gamma}{R}$

**Answer:** $U \sim \frac{\Gamma}{R}$ (logarithmic corrections)

## Example 6: Trailing Vortices

Aircraft wing (span $b = 30$ m) generates lift $L = 500,000$ N at $U = 100$ m/s (air $\rho = 1.2$ kg/m³).

Estimate trailing vortex circulation.

**Solution:**

From Kutta-Joukowski: $L = \rho U\Gamma b$ (total lift, both vortices)

$$\Gamma = \frac{L}{\rho U b} = \frac{500,000}{(1.2)(100)(30)} = \frac{500,000}{3,600} = 139 \text{ m}^2\text{/s}$$

**Two counter-rotating vortices** (tip vortices) with $\Gamma/2$ each.

**Answer:** $\Gamma \approx 139$ m²/s per vortex pair

## Example 7: Rankine Vortex

Solid body rotation for $r < a$: $u_\theta = \Omega r$

Potential vortex for $r > a$: $u_\theta = \Gamma/(2\pi r)$

Match at $r = a$: Find $\Gamma$ in terms of $\Omega$.

**Solution:**

Continuity of velocity at $r = a$:

$$\Omega a = \frac{\Gamma}{2\pi a}$$

$$\Gamma = 2\pi\Omega a^2$$

**Vorticity**:
- $r < a$: $\omega_z = 2\Omega$ (constant)
- $r > a$: $\omega_z = 0$ (irrotational)

**Circulation** (curve enclosing core):

$$\Gamma = \int_0^a \omega_z \cdot 2\pi r \, dr = 2\Omega \int_0^a 2\pi r \, dr = 2\Omega\pi a^2 = \Gamma$$ ✓

**Answer:** $\Gamma = 2\pi\Omega a^2$

## Example 8: Vortex Sheet

Vortex sheet at $y = 0$ with vorticity distribution $\gamma(x)$ (circulation per unit length).

Induced velocity at $y = h$?

**Solution:**

By Biot-Savart (2D):

$$u(x, h) = \int_{-\infty}^\infty \frac{\gamma(x')}{2\pi} \frac{1}{x - x'} dx'$$

For uniform $\gamma = \gamma_0$: **Diverges** (needs cutoff).

For **localized** vortex sheet: Finite velocity.

**Physical**: Vortex sheets are **unstable** (Kelvin-Helmholtz instability), roll up into discrete vortices.

**Answer:** $u \sim \int \frac{\gamma(x')}{x - x'} dx'$ (principal value integral)

## Example 9: Helmholtz Theorem 2

Vortex tube: cross-section $A_1 = 0.01$ m² with $\omega_1 = 100$ s⁻¹.

Tube stretches to $A_2 = 0.005$ m². Find $\omega_2$.

**Solution:**

**Tube strength** constant:

$$\Gamma = \omega_1 A_1 = \omega_2 A_2$$

$$\omega_2 = \omega_1\frac{A_1}{A_2} = 100 \cdot \frac{0.01}{0.005} = 200 \text{ s}^{-1}$$

**Doubling vorticity** when area halved.

**Answer:** $\omega_2 = 200$ s⁻¹

## Example 10: Line Vortex Velocity

Straight line vortex along $z$-axis with circulation $\Gamma$.

Find velocity at $(x, y, 0)$.

**Solution:**

By symmetry: velocity in azimuthal direction.

$$u_\theta = \frac{\Gamma}{2\pi r}$$

In Cartesian:
$$u = -\frac{\Gamma y}{2\pi(x^2 + y^2)}, \quad v = \frac{\Gamma x}{2\pi(x^2 + y^2)}$$

**Velocity perpendicular** to position vector.

**Answer:** $\mathbf{u} = \frac{\Gamma}{2\pi r^2}(-y, x, 0)$

---

## Example 11: Vorticity Generation at Wall

Fluid initially irrotational flows past wall. Vorticity appears near wall.

Explain mechanism (viscous).

**Solution:**

**No-slip condition**: Fluid velocity matches wall (zero for stationary wall).

**Velocity gradient** near wall: $\frac{\partial u}{\partial y} \neq 0$

**Vorticity**: $\omega_z = \frac{\partial v}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial u}{\partial y}$

For boundary layer: $\omega_z \approx -\frac{\partial u}{\partial y} < 0$ (if $u$ decreases toward wall).

**Viscous diffusion** from wall into fluid generates vorticity.

**Answer:** Viscosity + no-slip → vorticity generation at boundaries

## Example 12: Tornado Vortex

Tornado approximated as Rankine vortex: core radius $a = 100$ m, maximum wind $v_{max} = 100$ m/s at $r = a$.

Estimate circulation and angular velocity in core.

**Solution:**

At $r = a$: $v_{max} = \Omega a = \frac{\Gamma}{2\pi a}$

From $\Omega a = v_{max}$:
$$\Omega = \frac{v_{max}}{a} = \frac{100}{100} = 1 \text{ rad/s}$$

$$\Gamma = 2\pi a v_{max} = 2\pi(100)(100) = 62,832 \text{ m}^2\text{/s}$$

**Huge circulation**!

**Answer:** $\Omega = 1$ rad/s, $\Gamma \approx 63,000$ m²/s
