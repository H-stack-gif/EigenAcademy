# Article 4.1: Exact Solutions to Navier-Stokes Equations

The full Navier-Stokes equations are notoriously difficult—their nonlinearity precludes analytical solution in most cases. Yet for certain highly symmetric geometries and flow conditions, **exact solutions** exist that reveal fundamental physics of viscous flow. These solutions typically exploit symmetry to reduce the problem to one dimension, where the nonlinear convective term $(\mathbf{u} \cdot \nabla)\mathbf{u}$ vanishes or simplifies dramatically. **Couette flow** between parallel plates, **Poiseuille flow** in pipes and channels, **Stokes flow** past spheres at low Reynolds number, and **rotating flows** in cylinders all admit closed-form solutions. These benchmark cases provide physical insight into velocity profiles, shear stress distributions, pressure drops, and force balances, and serve as validation tests for numerical codes.

**Parallel flows**—where velocity has only one non-zero component depending on a single coordinate—form the largest class of exact solutions. The governing equations reduce to ordinary differential equations solvable by elementary integration. **Couette flow** (shear-driven) exhibits linear velocity profiles and constant shear stress, while **Poiseuille flow** (pressure-driven) yields parabolic velocity profiles with zero stress at the centerline and maximum at walls. **Plane Poiseuille flow** (between parallel plates) and **Hagen-Poiseuille flow** (circular pipe) differ quantitatively but share qualitative features. Time-dependent solutions like **Stokes' first problem** (suddenly accelerated plate) reveal diffusive spreading of momentum via the diffusion equation $\frac{\partial u}{\partial t} = \nu\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial y^2}$, with penetration depth $\delta \sim \sqrt{\nu t}$ characteristic of viscous diffusion.

This article catalogs major exact solutions to Navier-Stokes, derives Couette and Poiseuille flows with detailed stress and pressure gradient calculations, treats combined pressure-shear flows, analyzes rotating flows (solid body rotation, free vortex, rotating cylinders and disks), derives Stokes flow around a sphere at low Reynolds number with drag force $F_D = 6\pi\mu aU$, explores similarity solutions including Stokes' first problem for oscillating plates, discusses the role of stream functions in axisymmetric flows, and works through extensive examples involving velocity profiles, shear stress distributions, flow rates, power requirements, force calculations, and transient responses. The mathematics involves **heavy ordinary differential equations**, cylindrical and spherical coordinate systems, linearization techniques for low Reynolds number, integration with boundary conditions, and similarity methods.

## Couette Flow (Shear-Driven)

**Setup**: Two parallel plates separated by distance $h$. Bottom plate at $y = 0$ stationary, top plate at $y = h$ moving at velocity $U$.

**Assumptions**: Steady, 2D, incompressible, fully developed (no $x$-dependence), no pressure gradient.

**Navier-Stokes** ($x$-momentum):
$$0 = \mu\frac{d^2u}{dy^2}$$

**Boundary conditions**: 
- No-slip at bottom: $u(0) = 0$
- No-slip at top: $u(h) = U$

**Solution**: Integrate twice:
$$\frac{d^2u}{dy^2} = 0 \implies \frac{du}{dy} = C_1 \implies u = C_1 y + C_2$$

Apply BCs: $C_2 = 0$, $C_1 = U/h$

$$\boxed{u(y) = \frac{Uy}{h}}$$

**Linear velocity profile** - hallmark of shear-driven flow!

**Shear stress**: 
$$\tau = \mu\frac{du}{dy} = \frac{\mu U}{h}$$ (constant everywhere)

**Force on moving plate** (per unit area): $F/A = \tau = \mu U/h$

**Power required** (per unit area): $P/A = F/A \cdot U = \mu U^2/h$

### Pressure-Driven Couette

**Both** pressure gradient and wall motion:

$$0 = -\frac{dP}{dx} + \mu\frac{d^2u}{dy^2}$$

Let $-dP/dx = G$ (constant pressure gradient).

**BCs**: $u(0) = 0$, $u(h) = U$

**Solution** (superposition):
$$\boxed{u(y) = \frac{Uy}{h} + \frac{G}{2\mu}y(h-y)}$$

First term: Couette (shear)
Second term: Poiseuille (pressure)

## Poiseuille Flow (Pressure-Driven)

### Plane Poiseuille Flow (Channel)

**Setup**: Flow between parallel plates at $y = 0$ and $y = h$, driven by pressure gradient $dP/dx = -G$ (constant $G > 0$).

**Navier-Stokes**:
$$0 = G + \mu\frac{d^2u}{dy^2}$$

**BCs**: $u(0) = 0$, $u(h) = 0$ (no-slip both walls)

**Solution**:
$$\frac{d^2u}{dy^2} = -\frac{G}{\mu}$$

Integrate twice:
$$u = -\frac{G}{2\mu}y^2 + Ay + B$$

Apply BCs: $B = 0$, $A = Gh/(2\mu)$

$$\boxed{u(y) = \frac{G}{2\mu}y(h - y)}$$

**Parabolic profile** with maximum at centerline $y = h/2$:
$$u_{max} = \frac{Gh^2}{8\mu}$$

**Flow rate** (per unit width):
$$Q = \int_0^h u \, dy = \frac{G}{2\mu}\int_0^h y(h-y)dy = \frac{Gh^3}{12\mu}$$

**Average velocity**: $\bar{u} = Q/h = \frac{Gh^2}{12\mu} = \frac{2}{3}u_{max}$

**Shear stress**:
$$\tau = \mu\frac{du}{dy} = \mu\frac{G}{2\mu}(h - 2y) = \frac{G}{2}(h - 2y)$$

At walls: $\tau(0) = Gh/2$, $\tau(h) = -Gh/2$
At centerline: $\tau(h/2) = 0$

### Hagen-Poiseuille Flow (Circular Pipe)

**Setup**: Flow in pipe radius $R$, pressure gradient $dP/dx = -G$.

**Cylindrical coordinates**: $u_z(r)$ only, $u_r = u_\theta = 0$

**Navier-Stokes** ($z$-component):
$$0 = G + \mu\frac{1}{r}\frac{d}{dr}\left(r\frac{du}{dr}\right)$$

**Expand**:
$$\frac{d}{dr}\left(r\frac{du}{dr}\right) = -\frac{Gr}{\mu}$$

Integrate:
$$r\frac{du}{dr} = -\frac{Gr^2}{2\mu} + C_1$$

Require $u$ finite at $r = 0$: $C_1 = 0$

$$\frac{du}{dr} = -\frac{Gr}{2\mu}$$

Integrate:
$$u(r) = -\frac{Gr^2}{4\mu} + C_2$$

BC: $u(R) = 0$ → $C_2 = GR^2/(4\mu)$

$$\boxed{u(r) = \frac{G}{4\mu}(R^2 - r^2)}$$

**Parabolic profile**, maximum at centerline:
$$u_{max} = \frac{GR^2}{4\mu}$$

**Flow rate** (**Poiseuille's law**):
$$Q = \int_0^R u \cdot 2\pi r \, dr = \frac{2\pi G}{4\mu}\int_0^R (R^2 - r^2)r \, dr$$

$$= \frac{\pi G}{2\mu}\left[R^2\frac{r^2}{2} - \frac{r^4}{4}\right]_0^R = \frac{\pi G}{2\mu}\left(\frac{R^4}{2} - \frac{R^4}{4}\right)$$

$$\boxed{Q = \frac{\pi R^4 G}{8\mu} = \frac{\pi R^4}{8\mu}\left(-\frac{dP}{dx}\right)}$$

**Average velocity**: $\bar{u} = Q/(\pi R^2) = \frac{GR^2}{8\mu} = \frac{1}{2}u_{max}$

**Pressure drop over length $L$**:
$$\Delta P = GL = \frac{8\mu \bar{u} L}{R^2}$$

## Stokes Flow Around Sphere (Low Reynolds Number)

For **very low Re** ($\text{Re} = \frac{\rho Ua}{\mu} \ll 1$, where $a$ is sphere radius):

Inertial terms negligible, **linearized Navier-Stokes**:
$$\nabla P = \mu\nabla^2\mathbf{u}$$

with continuity: $\nabla \cdot \mathbf{u} = 0$

**Stokes' solution** (in spherical coordinates, complex derivation):

Velocity field involves stream function $\psi$.

**Key result** - **Stokes' law** for drag:
$$\boxed{F_D = 6\pi\mu a U}$$

**Physical interpretation**:
- Drag proportional to $\mu$ (viscosity)
- Drag proportional to $U$ (linear, not quadratic!)
- Drag proportional to $a$ (size)

**Low Re**: Viscous forces dominate, flow reversible (time-reversible), no separation, no wake.

**Applications**: Sedimentation, microfluidics, blood flow, aerosol dynamics.

## Rotating Flows

### Solid Body Rotation

**Setup**: Fluid rotating as rigid body with angular velocity $\Omega$.

**Velocity**: $u_\theta = \Omega r$ (circumferential only)

**Vorticity**: $\omega_z = \frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial(ru_\theta)}{\partial r} = 2\Omega$ (constant!)

**Pressure distribution** (from radial momentum balance):
$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial r} = \rho\frac{u_\theta^2}{r} = \rho\Omega^2 r$$

Integrate:
$$P = P_0 + \frac{1}{2}\rho\Omega^2 r^2$$

**Parabolic pressure rise** with radius.

### Flow Between Rotating Cylinders

**Inner cylinder** radius $R_i$ rotating at $\Omega_i$, **outer** $R_o$ at $\Omega_o$.

**Velocity**: $u_\theta(r)$ only

**Navier-Stokes** ($\theta$-momentum, steady, axisymmetric):
$$0 = \nu\frac{d}{dr}\left[\frac{1}{r}\frac{d}{dr}(ru_\theta)\right]$$

**Solution form**: $u_\theta = Ar + B/r$

**BCs**: $u_\theta(R_i) = \Omega_i R_i$, $u_\theta(R_o) = \Omega_o R_o$

Solve for $A$, $B$:

$$\boxed{u_\theta(r) = \frac{(\Omega_o R_o^2 - \Omega_i R_i^2)r}{R_o^2 - R_i^2} + \frac{(\Omega_i - \Omega_o)R_i^2 R_o^2}{R_o^2 - R_i^2} \cdot \frac{1}{r}}$$

**Special case** (inner rotating, outer fixed): $\Omega_o = 0$

## Stokes' First Problem (Similarity Solution)

**Setup**: Semi-infinite fluid at rest. At $t = 0$, plate at $y = 0$ suddenly moves at velocity $U$.

**Unsteady Navier-Stokes**:
$$\frac{\partial u}{\partial t} = \nu\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial y^2}$$

**1D diffusion equation** for momentum!

**ICs**: $u(y,0) = 0$ for $y > 0$

**BCs**: $u(0,t) = U$ for $t > 0$, $u(\infty,t) = 0$

**Similarity solution**: $\eta = y/(2\sqrt{\nu t})$

Look for $u = U f(\eta)$:

$$\frac{\partial u}{\partial t} = U f'(\eta) \cdot \frac{\partial\eta}{\partial t} = U f'(\eta) \cdot \left(-\frac{y}{4(\nu t)^{3/2}}\sqrt{\nu}\right) = -U f'(\eta)\frac{\eta}{2t}$$

$$\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial y^2} = U f''(\eta) \cdot \frac{1}{4\nu t}$$

Substitute:
$$-\frac{\eta f'}{2t} = \nu \cdot \frac{f''}{4\nu t}$$

$$\boxed{f'' + 2\eta f' = 0}$$

**Solution**: $f' = Ce^{-\eta^2}$

Integrate: $f = C\int_0^\eta e^{-s^2}ds + D$

Apply BCs: $f(0) = 1$, $f(\infty) = 0$

$$f(\eta) = 1 - \frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_0^\eta e^{-s^2}ds = \text{erfc}(\eta)$$

$$\boxed{u(y,t) = U \cdot \text{erfc}\left(\frac{y}{2\sqrt{\nu t}}\right)}$$

**Penetration depth**: $\delta(t) \sim \sqrt{\nu t}$ (diffusive growth!)

**Wall shear stress**:
$$\tau_w = \mu\frac{\partial u}{\partial y}\bigg|_{y=0} = \frac{\mu U}{\sqrt{\pi\nu t}}$$

Decreases as $1/\sqrt{t}$.

## Example 1: Couette Power Requirement

Plates separated by $h = 2$ mm, oil viscosity $\mu = 0.05$ Pa·s, top plate moves at $U = 3$ m/s.

Find power per unit area.

**Solution:**

Shear stress:
$$\tau = \frac{\mu U}{h} = \frac{(0.05)(3)}{0.002} = 75 \text{ Pa}$$

Power per area:
$$P/A = \tau U = (75)(3) = 225 \text{ W/m}^2$$

**Answer:** $P/A = 225$ W/m²

## Example 2: Plane Poiseuille Flow Rate

Channel height $h = 0.01$ m, pressure gradient $G = 1000$ Pa/m, water $\mu = 0.001$ Pa·s.

Find flow rate per meter width.

**Solution:**

$$Q = \frac{Gh^3}{12\mu} = \frac{(1000)(0.01)^3}{12(0.001)}$$

$$= \frac{1000 \times 10^{-6}}{0.012} = \frac{0.001}{0.012} = 0.0833 \text{ m}^3\text{/s/m}$$

$$= 83.3 \text{ L/s per meter width}$$

**Answer:** $Q = 83.3$ L/(s·m)

---

## Example 3: Hagen-Poiseuille Pressure Drop

Pipe $R = 0.025$ m, $L = 50$ m, flow rate $Q = 0.001$ m³/s, oil $\mu = 0.1$ Pa·s.

Find pressure drop.

**Solution:**

From Poiseuille's law:
$$Q = \frac{\pi R^4 \Delta P}{8\mu L}$$

$$\Delta P = \frac{8\mu LQ}{\pi R^4} = \frac{8(0.1)(50)(0.001)}{\pi(0.025)^4}$$

$$= \frac{0.04}{\pi(3.906 \times 10^{-7})} = \frac{0.04}{1.227 \times 10^{-6}} = 32,600 \text{ Pa}$$

$$= 32.6 \text{ kPa}$$

**Answer:** $\Delta P = 32.6$ kPa

## Example 4: Stokes Drag on Falling Sphere

Water droplet $d = 0.1$ mm in air, $\rho_w = 1000$ kg/m³, find terminal velocity.

Air: $\mu = 1.8 \times 10^{-5}$ Pa·s, $\rho_{air} = 1.2$ kg/m³.

**Solution:**

At terminal velocity: $F_D = W$

$$6\pi\mu aU_t = \rho_w \cdot \frac{4}{3}\pi a^3 \cdot g$$

$$U_t = \frac{2\rho_w a^2 g}{9\mu}$$

$$a = 0.05 \text{ mm} = 5 \times 10^{-5} \text{ m}$$

$$U_t = \frac{2(1000)(5 \times 10^{-5})^2(9.81)}{9(1.8 \times 10^{-5})}$$

$$= \frac{2(1000)(2.5 \times 10^{-9})(9.81)}{1.62 \times 10^{-4}}$$

$$= \frac{4.905 \times 10^{-5}}{1.62 \times 10^{-4}} = 0.303 \text{ m/s}$$

Check Re: $\text{Re} = \frac{\rho_{air} U_t d}{\mu} = \frac{(1.2)(0.303)(10^{-4})}{1.8 \times 10^{-5}} = 2.02$ (marginally low, Stokes reasonable)

**Answer:** $U_t = 0.30$ m/s

## Example 5: Velocity Profile Comparison

Compare $u_{max}/\bar{u}$ for plane and circular Poiseuille flow.

**Solution:**

**Plane**: $u_{max}/\bar{u} = \frac{3}{2} = 1.5$

**Circular**: $u_{max}/\bar{u} = 2$

**Circular pipe** has more peaked profile (velocity gradients confined by circular boundary).

**Answer:** Plane: 1.5, Circular: 2.0

## Example 6: Combined Couette-Poiseuille

Flow with $U = 2$ m/s (top plate), $G = 500$ Pa/m, $h = 0.005$ m, $\mu = 0.01$ Pa·s.

Find location of maximum velocity.

**Solution:**

$$u(y) = \frac{Uy}{h} + \frac{G}{2\mu}y(h-y)$$

$$\frac{du}{dy} = \frac{U}{h} + \frac{G}{2\mu}(h - 2y) = 0$$

$$\frac{2}{0.005} + \frac{500}{2(0.01)}(0.005 - 2y) = 0$$

$$400 + 25000(0.005 - 2y) = 0$$

$$525 = 50000y$$

$$y = 0.0105$$ m

Since $y > h = 0.005$ m, maximum occurs at $y = h$ (top plate).

**Answer:** Maximum at $y = h$ (top plate)

## Example 7: Rotating Cylinder Torque

Inner cylinder $R_i = 0.1$ m rotates at $\Omega = 10$ rad/s, outer $R_o = 0.12$ m fixed.

Length $L = 0.5$ m, oil $\mu = 0.5$ Pa·s.

Find torque on inner cylinder.

**Solution:**

Velocity:
$$u_\theta(r) = \frac{\Omega R_i^2(R_o^2 - r^2)}{r(R_o^2 - R_i^2)}$$

(for $\Omega_o = 0$)

Shear stress at $r = R_i$:
$$\tau = \mu r\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left(\frac{u_\theta}{r}\right)\bigg|_{R_i}$$

After differentiation:
$$\tau = \frac{2\mu\Omega R_i R_o^2}{R_o^2 - R_i^2}$$

$$= \frac{2(0.5)(10)(0.01)(0.0144)}{0.0144 - 0.01} = \frac{0.00144}{0.0044} = 32.7 \text{ Pa}$$

Torque:
$$T = \tau \cdot 2\pi R_i L \cdot R_i = 32.7 \cdot 2\pi(0.1)(0.5)(0.1)$$

$$= 32.7 \cdot 0.0314 = 1.03 \text{ N·m}$$

**Answer:** $T = 1.0$ N·m

## Example 8: Momentum Penetration Depth

Stokes' first problem: plate velocity $U = 1$ m/s, how long for momentum to reach $y = 0.01$ m?

Air $\nu = 1.5 \times 10^{-5}$ m²/s.

**Solution:**

Penetration depth $\delta \sim 2\sqrt{\nu t}$

For $\delta = 0.01$ m:

$$0.01 = 2\sqrt{(1.5 \times 10^{-5})t}$$

$$t = \frac{(0.005)^2}{1.5 \times 10^{-5}} = \frac{2.5 \times 10^{-5}}{1.5 \times 10^{-5}} = 1.67 \text{ s}$$

**Answer:** $t = 1.7$ s

## Example 9: Pipe Flow Scaling

If flow rate doubles in Hagen-Poiseuille, by what factor does pressure drop increase?

**Solution:**

$$\Delta P \propto Q$$

If $Q \to 2Q$: $\Delta P \to 2\Delta P$

**Linear relationship** (laminar flow).

**Contrast turbulent**: $\Delta P \propto Q^2$ (quadratic).

**Answer:** Factor of 2

## Example 10: Shear Stress in Pipe

For Hagen-Poiseuille, find $\tau(r)$.

**Solution:**

$$u(r) = \frac{G}{4\mu}(R^2 - r^2)$$

$$\tau = \mu\frac{du}{dr} = \mu \cdot \frac{G}{4\mu}(-2r) = -\frac{Gr}{2}$$

**Linear** in $r$!

At center ($r = 0$): $\tau = 0$
At wall ($r = R$): $\tau = -GR/2$

**Answer:** $\tau(r) = -\frac{Gr}{2}$

---

## Example 11: Average vs. Maximum Velocity

For plane Poiseuille, if $u_{max} = 0.9$ m/s, find $\bar{u}$ and $Q$ (per meter, $h = 0.02$ m).

**Solution:**

$$\bar{u} = \frac{2}{3}u_{max} = \frac{2}{3}(0.9) = 0.6 \text{ m/s}$$

$$Q = \bar{u} \cdot h = (0.6)(0.02) = 0.012 \text{ m}^3\text{/s per meter}$$

**Answer:** $\bar{u} = 0.6$ m/s, $Q = 12$ L/(s·m)

## Example 12: No-Slip Verification

In Couette flow, what would happen if no-slip violated (slip at wall)?

**Solution:**

**No-slip**: Fundamental boundary condition from molecular interactions.

**If violated**: $u|_{wall} \neq U_{wall}$ → linear profile wouldn't match BCs → different solution.

**Slip length** $\lambda_s$: $u|_{wall} = \lambda_s\frac{du}{dy}|_{wall}$ (rare, nano/microflows, polymer flows)

**Most macroscopic flows**: No-slip essential and experimentally verified.

**Answer:** No-slip is fundamental; violation changes entire solution
