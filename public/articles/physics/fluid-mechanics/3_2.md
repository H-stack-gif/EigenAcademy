# Article 3.2: Potential Flow Theory

When flow is both **inviscid** and **irrotational** ($\nabla \times \mathbf{u} = 0$), a remarkable simplification occurs: velocity can be expressed as the gradient of a scalar **velocity potential** $\phi$, and for incompressible flow, this potential satisfies **Laplace's equation** $\nabla^2\phi = 0$. This transforms the nonlinear Navier-Stokes equations into a linear partial differential equation! The price we pay is physical realism—real flows near surfaces develop boundary layers with vorticity—but potential flow provides accurate solutions for attached flow regions at high Reynolds numbers, and the mathematical elegance enables analytical solutions for complex geometries. **Superposition** of elementary solutions (sources, sinks, doublets, vortices) builds flows past cylinders, airfoils, and other bodies.

The **stream function** $\psi$ for 2D incompressible flow provides a complementary description: constant $\psi$ curves are streamlines, and for irrotational flow, $\psi$ also satisfies Laplace's equation. Together, $\phi$ and $\psi$ form the **complex potential** $w = \phi + i\psi$ (covered in Article 3.3). **Elementary potential flows**—uniform flow, source/sink, doublet, vortex—serve as building blocks. A **source** creates radial outflow from a point; a **sink** draws fluid inward; a **doublet** (source-sink pair in the limit) represents flow past a cylinder; a **vortex** produces circular streamlines with circulation. Superposing these elements generates flows past bodies: source + uniform flow = half-body; doublet + uniform flow = cylinder; doublet + vortex + uniform flow = cylinder with circulation (lift).

This article introduces velocity potential and its properties, derives Laplace's equation for incompressible, irrotational flow, reviews stream function and its relationship to potential, presents elementary potential flow solutions (uniform, source, sink, doublet, vortex), demonstrates superposition to create complex flows, explores flow past cylinder (Rankine oval, half-body), discusses uniqueness of solutions and boundary conditions, and works through extensive examples involving potential calculations, streamline patterns, velocity fields, and flow constructions. The mathematics involves heavy partial differential equations (Laplace's equation), vector calculus, separation of variables in multiple coordinate systems, and boundary value problems.

## Irrotational Flow and Velocity Potential

**Irrotational flow**: $\nabla \times \mathbf{u} = 0$

By vector identity, any irrotational vector field can be written as gradient of a scalar:

$$\boxed{\mathbf{u} = \nabla \phi}$$

where $\phi(\mathbf{r}, t)$ is the **velocity potential**.

**Verification**: $\nabla \times \nabla \phi = 0$ identically (curl of gradient is zero).

**Components**:
$$u = \frac{\partial \phi}{\partial x}, \quad v = \frac{\partial \phi}{\partial y}, \quad w = \frac{\partial \phi}{\partial z}$$

**Physical meaning**: $\phi$ represents "potential" for velocity (like electric potential for field).

## Laplace's Equation

For **incompressible flow**: $\nabla \cdot \mathbf{u} = 0$

Substitute $\mathbf{u} = \nabla \phi$:

$$\nabla \cdot (\nabla \phi) = 0$$

$$\boxed{\nabla^2 \phi = 0}$$

**Laplace's equation** for velocity potential!

**In Cartesian coordinates**:
$$\frac{\partial^2\phi}{\partial x^2} + \frac{\partial^2\phi}{\partial y^2} + \frac{\partial^2\phi}{\partial z^2} = 0$$

**In cylindrical** $(r, \theta, z)$:
$$\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left(r\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial r}\right) + \frac{1}{r^2}\frac{\partial^2\phi}{\partial\theta^2} + \frac{\partial^2\phi}{\partial z^2} = 0$$

**In spherical** $(r, \theta, \phi)$:
$$\frac{1}{r^2}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left(r^2\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial r}\right) + \frac{1}{r^2\sin\theta}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\left(\sin\theta\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial\theta}\right) + \frac{1}{r^2\sin^2\theta}\frac{\partial^2\phi}{\partial\phi^2} = 0$$

**Key property**: Laplace's equation is **linear** → superposition of solutions!

## Stream Function Review (2D)

For **2D incompressible flow**, stream function $\psi(x, y)$ satisfies:

$$u = \frac{\partial\psi}{\partial y}, \quad v = -\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial x}$$

**For irrotational flow**: $\omega_z = 0$

$$\omega_z = \frac{\partial v}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial u}{\partial y} = -\nabla^2\psi = 0$$

$$\boxed{\nabla^2\psi = 0}$$

**Both $\phi$ and $\psi$ satisfy Laplace's equation!**

**Relationship**: Cauchy-Riemann conditions (Article 3.3):
$$\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x} = \frac{\partial\psi}{\partial y}, \quad \frac{\partial\phi}{\partial y} = -\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial x}$$

**Physical meaning**:
- Lines of constant $\phi$ (equipotentials): perpendicular to velocity
- Lines of constant $\psi$ (streamlines): parallel to velocity
- **Orthogonal**: $\nabla\phi \perp \nabla\psi$

## Elementary Solutions

### Uniform Flow

**Potential**: $\phi = Ux$ (flow in $x$-direction)

**Velocity**: $u = \frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x} = U$, $v = 0$

**Stream function**: $\psi = Uy$

**Streamlines**: $y = \text{const}$ (horizontal lines)

### Source (2D)

**Definition**: Fluid emanates radially from point at rate $m$ (strength).

**Potential** (polar): $\boxed{\phi = \frac{m}{2\pi}\ln r}$

**Velocity**: 
$$u_r = \frac{\partial\phi}{\partial r} = \frac{m}{2\pi r}, \quad u_\theta = \frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial\theta} = 0$$

**Stream function**: $\psi = \frac{m\theta}{2\pi}$

**Streamlines**: $\theta = \text{const}$ (radial lines)

**Volumetric flow rate**: $Q = \int u_r \, dl = \int_0^{2\pi} \frac{m}{2\pi r} \cdot r \, d\theta = m$

### Sink

Same as source with $m < 0$ (fluid drawn inward).

### Vortex (2D)

**Definition**: Circular flow around point with circulation $\Gamma$.

**Potential**: $\boxed{\phi = -\frac{\Gamma\theta}{2\pi}}$ (multi-valued!)

**Velocity**:
$$u_r = 0, \quad u_\theta = \frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial\theta} = \frac{\Gamma}{2\pi r}$$

**Stream function**: $\psi = -\frac{\Gamma}{2\pi}\ln r$

**Streamlines**: $r = \text{const}$ (concentric circles)

**Circulation**: $\oint_C \mathbf{u} \cdot d\mathbf{l} = \Gamma$ for any curve enclosing vortex.

### Doublet (2D)

**Definition**: Source-sink pair with separation $\delta \to 0$, strength $m\delta = \mu$ (doublet strength).

**Potential**: $\boxed{\phi = -\frac{\mu\cos\theta}{2\pi r}}$

**Velocity**:
$$u_r = -\frac{\mu\cos\theta}{2\pi r^2}, \quad u_\theta = -\frac{\mu\sin\theta}{2\pi r^2}$$

**Stream function**: $\psi = -\frac{\mu\sin\theta}{2\pi r}$

**Physical interpretation**: Flow around a sphere or cylinder.

## Superposition: Flow Past Cylinder

**Combine**: Uniform flow + Doublet

$$\phi = Ur\cos\theta - \frac{\mu\cos\theta}{2\pi r}$$

Choose $\mu = 2\pi U a^2$ (where $a$ is cylinder radius):

$$\phi = U\left(r + \frac{a^2}{r}\right)\cos\theta$$

**Velocity**:
$$u_r = U\left(1 - \frac{a^2}{r^2}\right)\cos\theta$$

At $r = a$: $u_r = 0$ ✓ (no penetration—boundary condition satisfied!)

**Streamline** $\psi = 0$ gives circle $r = a$ → represents solid cylinder.

**Velocity on surface** ($r = a$):
$$u_\theta = -2U\sin\theta$$

Maximum at $\theta = 90°, 270°$: $|u_\theta| = 2U$

Stagnation points at $\theta = 0°, 180°$: $u = 0$

## Rankine Half-Body

**Combine**: Uniform flow + Source

$$\phi = Ux + \frac{m}{2\pi}\ln r$$

**Stagnation streamline** ($\psi = 0$):

At stagnation point $(x_s, 0)$: $u = 0$

$$U + \frac{m}{2\pi r_s} = 0 \implies r_s = -\frac{m}{2\pi U}$$

(No physical stagnation for source—half-body extends to infinity.)

But, stagnation at $x = -\infty$; body forms closed contour on one side.

## Example 1: Potential for Corner Flow

Flow in corner: $\mathbf{u} = (kx, -ky, 0)$.

Find velocity potential.

**Solution:**

$$u = \frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x} = kx \implies \phi = \frac{kx^2}{2} + f(y)$$

$$v = \frac{\partial\phi}{\partial y} = -ky \implies \frac{\partial f}{\partial y} = -ky$$

$$f(y) = -\frac{ky^2}{2} + C$$

$$\phi = \frac{k}{2}(x^2 - y^2) + C$$

Choose $C = 0$:
$$\boxed{\phi = \frac{k}{2}(x^2 - y^2)}$$

**Equipotentials**: Hyperbolae $x^2 - y^2 = \text{const}$

**Answer:** $\phi = \frac{k}{2}(x^2 - y^2)$

---

## Example 2: Verify Laplace for Source

For source $\phi = \frac{m}{2\pi}\ln r$, verify $\nabla^2\phi = 0$ (except at origin).

**Solution:**

In polar coordinates:
$$\nabla^2\phi = \frac{1}{r}\frac{d}{dr}\left(r\frac{d\phi}{dr}\right) + \frac{1}{r^2}\frac{\partial^2\phi}{\partial\theta^2}$$

$$\frac{d\phi}{dr} = \frac{m}{2\pi r}$$

$$\frac{d}{dr}\left(r\frac{d\phi}{dr}\right) = \frac{d}{dr}\left(\frac{m}{2\pi}\right) = 0$$

$$\frac{\partial^2\phi}{\partial\theta^2} = 0$$

$$\nabla^2\phi = 0$$ ✓ (for $r > 0$)

**Singularity at $r = 0$**: Actually $\nabla^2\phi = m\delta(\mathbf{r})$ (Dirac delta).

**Answer:** $\nabla^2\phi = 0$ for $r > 0$

---

## Example 3: Stream Function for Vortex

For vortex $u_r = 0$, $u_\theta = \Gamma/(2\pi r)$, find $\psi$.

**Solution:**

$$u_r = \frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial\theta} = 0 \implies \psi = f(r)$$

$$u_\theta = -\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial r} = \frac{\Gamma}{2\pi r}$$

$$\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial r} = -\frac{\Gamma}{2\pi r}$$

Integrate:
$$\psi = -\frac{\Gamma}{2\pi}\ln r + C$$

Choose $C = 0$:
$$\boxed{\psi = -\frac{\Gamma}{2\pi}\ln r}$$

**Streamlines**: $r = \text{const}$ (circles)

**Answer:** $\psi = -\frac{\Gamma}{2\pi}\ln r$

---

## Example 4: Superposed Source and Sink

Source at $(−a, 0)$ with strength $m$, sink at $(a, 0)$ with strength $−m$.

Find potential.

**Solution:**

$$\phi = \frac{m}{2\pi}\ln r_1 - \frac{m}{2\pi}\ln r_2 = \frac{m}{2\pi}\ln\frac{r_1}{r_2}$$

where $r_1 = \sqrt{(x+a)^2 + y^2}$, $r_2 = \sqrt{(x-a)^2 + y^2}$

**Streamlines**: Curves of constant $r_1/r_2$ (circles).

**Physical interpretation**: Flow around obstacle (Rankine oval when combined with uniform flow).

**Answer:** $\phi = \frac{m}{2\pi}\ln\frac{r_1}{r_2}$

---

## Example 5: Velocity on Cylinder Surface

For flow past cylinder $\phi = U(r + a^2/r)\cos\theta$, find velocity at $\theta = 60°$ on surface.

**Solution:**

At $r = a$:

$$u_r = U\left(1 - \frac{a^2}{a^2}\right)\cos 60° = 0$$ ✓

$$u_\theta = -\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial\theta}\bigg|_{r=a} \cdot \frac{1}{a}$$

$$= -U\left(a + \frac{a^2}{a}\right)(-\sin 60°) \cdot \frac{1}{a}$$

$$= 2U\sin 60° = 2U \cdot \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = \sqrt{3}U = 1.732U$$

**Answer:** $u_\theta = \sqrt{3}U \approx 1.73U$

---

## Example 6: Pressure on Cylinder

Using result from Example 5, find pressure coefficient at $\theta = 60°$.

**Solution:**

Bernoulli:
$$C_P = 1 - \left(\frac{u}{U}\right)^2 = 1 - (1.732)^2 = 1 - 3 = -2$$

**Negative**: Suction (pressure below freestream).

At $\theta = 90°$: $u = 2U$ → $C_P = 1 - 4 = -3$ (maximum suction)

At $\theta = 0°, 180°$: $u = 0$ → $C_P = 1$ (stagnation)

**Answer:** $C_P = -2$

---

## Example 7: Rankine Oval

Combine uniform flow $U$ with source $m$ at $(-a, 0)$ and sink $-m$ at $(a, 0)$.

Find length and width of oval.

**Solution:**

**Stagnation points**: Where $u = 0$

On $x$-axis: uniform flow opposes source/sink radial flow.

At front stagnation: $U = \frac{m}{2\pi x_s}$ (approximately, for $x_s \gg a$)

$$x_s \approx \frac{m}{2\pi U}$$

**Length**: $L \approx 2x_s = \frac{m}{\pi U}$

**Width**: From streamline $\psi = 0$ perpendicular to $x$-axis.

**Maximum width** $h$: (complex calculation, typically $h \sim \sqrt{ma/U}$)

**Answer:** Length $\sim m/(\pi U)$; Width $\sim \sqrt{ma/U}$

---

## Example 8: Doublet Formation Limit

Source strength $m$ at $(−\delta/2, 0)$, sink $−m$ at $(\delta/2, 0)$.

Take limit $\delta \to 0$, $m \to \infty$ with $m\delta = \mu$ fixed.

**Solution:**

Potential:
$$\phi = \frac{m}{2\pi}\ln r_1 - \frac{m}{2\pi}\ln r_2 = \frac{m}{2\pi}\ln\frac{r_1}{r_2}$$

For small $\delta$:
$$\frac{r_1}{r_2} \approx 1 + \frac{\delta\cos\theta}{r}$$

$$\ln\frac{r_1}{r_2} \approx \frac{\delta\cos\theta}{r}$$

$$\phi \approx \frac{m\delta\cos\theta}{2\pi r} = \frac{\mu\cos\theta}{2\pi r}$$

Standard result (proper geometry):
$$\phi_{\text{doublet}} = -\frac{\mu\cos\theta}{2\pi r}$$

**Answer:** $\phi = -\frac{\mu\cos\theta}{2\pi r}$ (doublet)

---

## Example 9: Orthogonality of $\phi$ and $\psi$

Show that curves $\phi = C_1$ and $\psi = C_2$ are orthogonal.

**Solution:**

Gradients:
$$\nabla\phi = \left(\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x}, \frac{\partial\phi}{\partial y}\right) = (u, v)$$

$$\nabla\psi = \left(\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial x}, \frac{\partial\psi}{\partial y}\right) = (-v, u)$$

Dot product:
$$\nabla\phi \cdot \nabla\psi = u(-v) + v(u) = -uv + vu = 0$$ ✓

**Orthogonal gradients** → perpendicular level curves.

**Answer:** Orthogonal by construction

---

## Example 10: 3D Source

Spherically symmetric source: $u_r = Q/(4\pi r^2)$, $u_\theta = u_\phi = 0$.

Find potential.

**Solution:**

$$u_r = \frac{\partial\phi}{\partial r} = \frac{Q}{4\pi r^2}$$

Integrate:
$$\phi = -\frac{Q}{4\pi r} + C$$

Choose $C = 0$:
$$\boxed{\phi = -\frac{Q}{4\pi r}}$$

**Verify Laplace**: $\nabla^2\phi = 0$ for $r > 0$ ✓

**Singularity**: $\nabla^2\phi = Q\delta^3(\mathbf{r})$ at origin.

**Answer:** $\phi = -\frac{Q}{4\pi r}$

---

## Example 11: Flow Rate Through Surface

For source $m$, calculate flow through circle $r = R$ (2D, per unit depth).

**Solution:**

$$Q = \int_0^{2\pi} u_r \cdot R \, d\theta = \int_0^{2\pi} \frac{m}{2\pi R} \cdot R \, d\theta$$

$$= \frac{m}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} d\theta = \frac{m}{2\pi} \cdot 2\pi = m$$

**Independent of $R$**: Source strength $m$ is volumetric flow rate!

**Answer:** $Q = m$ (per unit depth)

---

## Example 12: Harmonic Function Properties

Show that if $\phi_1$ and $\phi_2$ satisfy Laplace, so does $\phi = c_1\phi_1 + c_2\phi_2$.

**Solution:**

$$\nabla^2\phi = \nabla^2(c_1\phi_1 + c_2\phi_2)$$

$$= c_1\nabla^2\phi_1 + c_2\nabla^2\phi_2$$

$$= c_1(0) + c_2(0) = 0$$ ✓

**Linearity of Laplace operator** → superposition principle.

**Answer:** Linear combinations satisfy Laplace

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: B** - Velocity potential $\mathbf{u} = \nabla\phi$ exists when $\nabla \times \mathbf{u} = 0$ (irrotational).

**Problem 2: B** - Incompressible + irrotational: $\nabla \cdot (\nabla\phi) = \nabla^2\phi = 0$ (Laplace's equation).

**Problem 3: C** - 2D source: $\phi = \frac{m}{2\pi}\ln r$ (logarithmic).

**Problem 4: B** - Gradients $\nabla\phi$ and $\nabla\psi$ are orthogonal → level curves perpendicular.

**Problem 5: C** - Doublet is limiting case of source-sink pair as separation → 0 with fixed strength × distance.
