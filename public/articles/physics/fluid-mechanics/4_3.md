# Article 4.3: Boundary Layer Separation and Drag

When adverse pressure gradients decelerate boundary layer flow, the low-momentum fluid near the wall can reverse direction—**boundary layer separation**—fundamentally altering aerodynamic characteristics. The **separation criterion** $\left(\frac{\partial u}{\partial y}\right)_{y=0} = 0$ marks the point where wall shear stress vanishes and reverse flow begins. Beyond separation, the boundary layer approximation breaks down: a large recirculating wake forms, pressure recovery fails, and **pressure drag** (form drag) dominates. This phenomenon explains why bluff bodies like cylinders and spheres experience drag coefficients $C_D \sim 1$ while streamlined airfoils achieve $C_D \sim 0.01$—a factor of 100 difference. The **drag crisis** on spheres near $\text{Re} \sim 3 \times 10^5$, where transition to turbulent boundary layer delays separation and dramatically reduces drag, demonstrates the complex interplay between transition, separation, and drag.

**Adverse pressure gradients** $\frac{dP}{dx} > 0$ arise when flow decelerates (e.g., rear of bluff body, diffuser). From boundary layer momentum equation, positive $\frac{dP}{dx}$ acts against flow, strongest effect near wall where velocity already low. **Separation point** advances upstream as adverse gradient strengthens. **Turbulent boundary layers** resist separation better than laminar—fuller velocity profiles mean more momentum near wall. **Streamlining** (gradual body tapering) minimizes adverse gradients, delays/prevents separation, dramatically reduces pressure drag. **Total drag** decomposes: $D = D_f + D_p$ where friction drag $D_f = \int_S \tau_w dA$ and pressure drag $D_p = \int_S (P - P_\infty)\cos\theta \, dA$.

This article develops separation criterion from boundary layer theory, analyzes adverse pressure gradient effects on velocity profiles using Falkner-Skan solutions with $\beta < 0$, derives pressure and friction drag components via surface integration, introduces drag coefficient $C_D = D/(\frac{1}{2}\rho U^2 A)$ and typical values for various geometries (flat plate, cylinder, sphere, streamlined bodies), explains drag crisis physics including laminar-turbulent transition effects, discusses streamlining principles and form drag reduction strategies, treats practical separation control methods (vortex generators, surface roughness, suction/blowing), analyzes d'Alembert's paradox resolution via viscosity, and works through extensive examples involving separation prediction from Falkner-Skan, drag calculations for various bodies, drag crisis quantification, streamlining effectiveness comparisons, and golf ball dimple physics.

## Separation Criterion

From boundary layer momentum equation near wall ($y \to 0$):

$$u\frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + v\frac{\partial u}{\partial y} = U\frac{dU}{dx} + \nu\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial y^2}$$

At wall: $u(x,0) = v(x,0) = 0$

$$\boxed{0 = U\frac{dU}{dx} + \nu\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial y^2}\bigg|_{y=0}}$$

For **favorable gradient** ($\frac{dU}{dx} > 0$): $\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial y^2}|_0 < 0$ → concave profile

For **adverse gradient** ($\frac{dU}{dx} < 0$): $\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial y^2}|_0 > 0$ → inflection point appears

**At separation**: Wall shear stress vanishes

$$\boxed{\tau_w = \mu\frac{\partial u}{\partial y}\bigg|_{y=0} = 0}$$

**Beyond separation**: Reverse flow ($u < 0$ near wall), BL approximation invalid.

## Adverse Pressure Gradient Effects

From Falkner-Skan with $\beta < 0$ (adverse gradient):

**Velocity profiles** become inflected as $\beta \to \beta_{sep} \approx -0.0904$.

**Wall shear decreases**: $f''(0)$ decreases with more negative $\beta$.

**Examples**:
- $\beta = 0$: $f''(0) = 0.332$ (Blasius)
- $\beta = -0.05$: $f''(0) \approx 0.22$
- $\beta = -0.0904$: $f''(0) = 0$ (separation!)

**Physical mechanism**: Adverse gradient decelerates flow → low-momentum near-wall fluid stagnates → reverse flow.

## Drag Decomposition

**Total drag force**:

$$D = D_f + D_p$$

**Friction drag** (viscous shear):
$$D_f = \int_S \tau_w \, dA = \int_S \mu\frac{\partial u}{\partial n}\bigg|_w dA$$

**Pressure drag** (form drag):
$$D_p = \int_S (P - P_\infty)(\mathbf{n} \cdot \mathbf{e}_D) \, dA$$

For 2D body:
$$D_p = \int_0^{2\pi} (P(\theta) - P_\infty)\cos\theta \cdot dA$$

## Drag Coefficient

$$\boxed{C_D = \frac{D}{\frac{1}{2}\rho U^2 A}}$$

where $A$ = reference area (frontal for bluff, planform for airfoils).

**Typical values**:
- Flat plate perpendicular to flow: $C_D \approx 2.0$
- Circular cylinder: $C_D \approx 1.2$ (subcritical, $10^4 < \text{Re} < 10^5$)
- Sphere: $C_D \approx 0.47$ (subcritical, $10^3 < \text{Re} < 2 \times 10^5$)
- Streamlined body (3:1 ellipse): $C_D \approx 0.05$
- Flat plate parallel (friction only): $C_D = 1.33/\sqrt{\text{Re}_L}$

## Drag Crisis on Sphere

**Subcritical** ($\text{Re} < 3 \times 10^5$):
- Laminar boundary layer
- Separates at $\theta \approx 80°$ from front stagnation
- Wide wake → high pressure drag
- $C_D \approx 0.47$

**Critical** ($\text{Re} \approx 3-4 \times 10^5$):
- Transition to turbulent BL
- **Drag crisis**: $C_D$ drops dramatically
- Turbulent BL separates later ($\theta \approx 120°$)
- Narrower wake → lower pressure drag
- $C_D \approx 0.1$

**Supercritical** ($\text{Re} > 4 \times 10^5$):
- Fully turbulent BL
- $C_D \approx 0.2$ (slightly rises after crisis minimum)

**Golf ball dimples**: Trip BL to turbulent at lower Re → achieve drag crisis at playable speeds → longer flight!

## Streamlining

**Goal**: Minimize separation → reduce pressure drag.

**Method**: Gradual taper → small adverse pressure gradients.

**Example**: Streamlined body (length/diameter = 3-4):
- $C_D \approx 0.04-0.08$ (mostly friction)
- **Factor 10-20 lower** than bluff cylinder

**Trade-off**: Longer body → more surface area → more friction drag.

**Optimal**: Balance pressure and friction contributions.

## d'Alembert's Paradox Resolution

**Inviscid potential flow** around closed body:
$$D_{inviscid} = 0$$ (d'Alembert's paradox)

**Real flows with viscosity**:
- Boundary layer forms
- Separation creates wake
- Pressure doesn't recover on rear
- **$D \neq 0$**!

**Viscosity causes drag** (directly via friction, indirectly via separation and pressure drag).

## Example 1: Separation from Falkner-Skan

For $\beta = -0.09$, is separation expected?

**Solution:**

Critical value: $\beta_{sep} \approx -0.0904$

Since $\beta = -0.09 > -0.0904$: **marginally attached**.

**Very close to separation** → $f''(0) \approx 0.01$ (very small wall shear).

Any small disturbance or slight change in geometry → separation.

**Answer:** Marginally attached, nearly separated

## Example 2: Drag on Cylinder

Cylinder $D = 0.1$ m, $L = 1$ m, $U = 10$ m/s (air).

Find drag force.

**Solution:**

$$\text{Re}_D = \frac{UD}{\nu} = \frac{(10)(0.1)}{1.5 \times 10^{-5}} = 6.67 \times 10^4$$

Subcritical: $C_D \approx 1.2$

Frontal area: $A = DL = (0.1)(1) = 0.1$ m²

$$D = C_D \cdot \frac{1}{2}\rho U^2 A = (1.2) \cdot \frac{1}{2}(1.2)(100)(0.1)$$

$$= 1.2 \cdot 6 = 7.2 \text{ N}$$

**Answer:** $D = 7.2$ N

## Example 3: Drag Crisis Demonstration

Smooth sphere vs. rough sphere, $D = 0.2$ m, $U = 15$ m/s.

**Solution:**

$$\text{Re} = \frac{(15)(0.2)}{1.5 \times 10^{-5}} = 2 \times 10^5$$

**Smooth** (subcritical): $C_D \approx 0.47$

$$D_{smooth} = (0.47) \cdot \frac{1}{2}(1.2)(225)\pi(0.1)^2 = 0.47 \cdot 4.24 = 1.99 \text{ N}$$

**Rough** (trips to turbulent): $C_D \approx 0.15$ (premature drag crisis)

$$D_{rough} = (0.15) \cdot 4.24 = 0.64 \text{ N}$$

**Roughness reduces drag** by factor of 3!

**Answer:** Rough sphere has 1/3 the drag

---

## Example 4: Streamlined vs. Bluff Drag Comparison

Cylinder $C_D = 1.2$ vs. 4:1 streamlined body $C_D = 0.05$ (same frontal area, same speed).

**Solution:**

$$\frac{D_{cylinder}}{D_{stream}} = \frac{C_D^{cyl}}{C_D^{stream}} = \frac{1.2}{0.05} = 24$$

**Streamlining reduces drag by factor of 24!**

**Cost**: Streamlined body longer → more material, more internal volume.

**Answer:** Streamlining gives 24× drag reduction

## Example 5: Pressure vs. Friction Drag on Sphere

Subcritical sphere: total $C_D = 0.47$. Estimate $C_{D,f}$ and $C_{D,p}$.

**Solution:**

**Friction drag**: Integrated BL skin friction
- Laminar BL over front
- $C_{D,f} \approx 0.05$ (estimate from flat plate scaling)

**Pressure drag**: Remainder
$$C_{D,p} = C_D - C_{D,f} = 0.47 - 0.05 = 0.42$$

**Pressure drag dominates** (~90% of total) due to large separated wake.

**Answer:** $C_{D,p} \approx 0.42$ (90%), $C_{D,f} \approx 0.05$ (10%)

## Example 6: Golf Ball Dimples Effectiveness

Smooth ball: critical Re $\sim 3 \times 10^5$
Dimpled: critical Re $\sim 5 \times 10^4$

Drive speed $U = 50$ m/s, $d = 0.043$ m.

**Solution:**

$$\text{Re} = \frac{(50)(0.043)}{1.5 \times 10^{-5}} = 1.43 \times 10^5$$

**Smooth**: Subcritical ($< 3 \times 10^5$) → $C_D \approx 0.5$ → short flight

**Dimpled**: Supercritical ($> 5 \times 10^4$) → $C_D \approx 0.25$ → **2× range**!

**Answer:** Dimples halve drag, double range

## Example 7: Cylinder Wake Width

Cylinder $D = 0.05$ m separates at $\theta = 80°$.

Estimate wake width downstream.

**Solution:**

Separation angle $\theta_s = 80°$ from front.

Wake forms from separation points on both sides.

**Wake width** $\approx 2D\sin(180° - \theta_s) = 2D\sin(100°)$

$$= 2(0.05)\sin(100°) = 0.1(0.985) = 0.0985 \text{ m} \approx 2D$$

**Wide wake** ≈ 2 diameters.

**Answer:** Wake width $\approx 0.10$ m (2$D$)

## Example 8: Flat Plate Parallel vs. Perpendicular Drag

Plate $L = 1$ m, $W = 0.5$ m, $U = 20$ m/s.

Compare drag parallel vs. perpendicular to flow.

**Solution:**

**Parallel** (friction only):
$$\text{Re}_L = \frac{(20)(1)}{1.5 \times 10^{-5}} = 1.33 \times 10^6$$

$$C_D = \frac{1.328}{\sqrt{\text{Re}_L}} = \frac{1.328}{\sqrt{1.33 \times 10^6}} = 0.00115$$

$$A = LW = 0.5 \text{ m}^2$$

$$D_{par} = (0.00115) \cdot \frac{1}{2}(1.2)(400)(0.5) \cdot 2 = 0.276 \text{ N}$$ (both sides)

**Perpendicular** (pressure):
$$C_D \approx 2.0$$, $A = 0.5$ m²

$$D_{perp} = (2.0) \cdot \frac{1}{2}(1.2)(400)(0.5) = 240 \text{ N}$$

**Ratio**: $D_{perp}/D_{par} = 240/0.276 = 870$!

**Answer:** Perpendicular drag 870× parallel

## Example 9: Separation Control with Vortex Generators

Vortex generators energize BL, delay separation.

If separation moves from $x = 0.5$ m to $x = 0.7$ m, estimate drag reduction on body length $L = 1$ m.

**Solution:**

**Without VG**: Wake from $x = 0.5$ m → large pressure drag over 50% of body.

**With VG**: Wake from $x = 0.7$ m → pressure drag over 30% of body.

**Rough estimate**: Pressure drag proportional to wake length
$$\frac{D_{with}}{D_{without}} \approx \frac{0.3}{0.5} = 0.6$$

**40% drag reduction** (typical VG effectiveness).

**Cost**: Small VG friction drag (~2-5% addition) ≪ pressure drag saved.

**Answer:** ≈40% drag reduction

## Example 10: Adverse Gradient Velocity Profile

Falkner-Skan $\beta = -0.05$ vs. Blasius $\beta = 0$.

Compare velocity at $\eta = 1$.

**Solution:**

From numerical solutions:

**Blasius**: $f'(1) \approx 0.33$

**$\beta = -0.05$**: $f'(1) \approx 0.28$

**Adverse gradient** → **slower velocity** at same $\eta$ (BL thickened, velocity deficit increased).

**Profile shape**: More inflected for adverse case.

**Answer:** Adverse case has $u/U = 0.28$ vs. 0.33 (Blasius)

## Example 11: Power to Overcome Drag

Car: $C_D = 0.3$, frontal area $A = 2$ m², speed $U = 30$ m/s.

**Solution:**

$$D = (0.3) \cdot \frac{1}{2}(1.2)(900)(2) = 0.3 \cdot 1080 = 324 \text{ N}$$

$$P = D \cdot U = (324)(30) = 9720 \text{ W} = 9.7 \text{ kW}$$

**At highway speed** (100 km/h), ~10 kW just for drag!

**Scales as $U^3$**: Doubling speed → 8× power.

**Answer:** $P = 9.7$ kW

## Example 12: d'Alembert Paradox Numerical Check

Inviscid flow past cylinder gives zero drag.

Real cylinder $C_D = 1.2$. Quantify paradox.

**Solution:**

**Inviscid theory**: $D = 0$

**Real flow**: $D = C_D \cdot \frac{1}{2}\rho U^2 A$

**Discrepancy**: Infinite! ($D_{real}/D_{inviscid} = \infty$)

**Resolution**: Viscosity → BL → separation → wake → pressure drag.

Even "small" viscosity ($\nu = 10^{-5}$ m²/s) has **dominant effect** on drag via separation.

**Answer:** Inviscid theory completely fails for drag prediction

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: B** - Separation criterion: wall shear stress vanishes → $\tau_w = \mu\frac{\partial u}{\partial y}|_{y=0} = 0$.

**Problem 2: B** - Drag crisis: BL transitions laminar→turbulent → turbulent BL has more momentum near wall → resists separation better → separates later ($\theta \approx 120°$ vs. 80°) → narrower wake → lower drag.

**Problem 3: B** - Streamlining reduces adverse pressure gradients → delays/prevents separation → reduces pressure drag (form drag). May slightly increase friction drag due to more surface area.

**Problem 4: B** - Subcritical sphere/cylinder: large separated wake → low pressure on rear → pressure drag ~90% of total. Friction drag only ~10%.

**Problem 5: B** - d'Alembert's paradox: potential (inviscid) flow theory predicts zero drag on any closed body. Resolved by viscosity causing BL separation and wake.
