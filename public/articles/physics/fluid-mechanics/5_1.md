# Article 5.1: Instability and Transition to Turbulence

The transition from smooth laminar flow to chaotic turbulence represents one of the most profound phenomena in fluid mechanics—a fundamental shift in flow character triggered by **instability**. Small disturbances, always present in real flows, can grow exponentially when Reynolds number exceeds critical thresholds, ultimately destroying the orderly laminar state. **Linear stability theory** provides the mathematical framework: decompose flow into base state plus infinitesimal perturbations $\mathbf{u} = \mathbf{U}(y) + \epsilon\hat{\mathbf{u}}(y)e^{i(\alpha x - \omega t)}$, linearize Navier-Stokes, obtain eigenvalue problem for growth rate $\omega_i = \text{Im}(\omega)$. For parallel shear flows, the **Orr-Sommerfeld equation**—a fourth-order ODE with complex eigenvalues—governs viscous stability. **Rayleigh's inflection point theorem** provides elegant inviscid criterion: parallel flow $U(y)$ unstable only if velocity profile has inflection point where $U'' = 0$. **Tollmien-Schlichting waves** in boundary layers, predicted theoretically and confirmed experimentally, demonstrate viscous instability leading to transition.

The **route to turbulence** follows characteristic stages: laminar base flow → linear instability growth → nonlinear saturation → secondary instabilities → breakdown to turbulence. **Critical Reynolds numbers** vary dramatically: $\text{Re}_{crit} \sim 2300$ for pipes, $\sim 5 \times 10^5$ for flat plate boundary layers, $\sim 1700$ for Rayleigh-Bénard convection. **Transition mechanisms** include natural transition (amplification of environmental disturbances), bypass transition (large disturbances skip linear regime), and separation-induced transition. Understanding these processes enables flow control strategies—tripping boundary layers to turbulent state delays separation on airfoils; suppressing instabilities maintains laminar flow to reduce drag.

This article develops linear stability theory via **normal mode analysis**, derives the **Orr-Sommerfeld equation** from linearized Navier-Stokes with detailed steps, introduces eigenvalue problems and numerical solution methods, proves **Rayleigh's inflection point criterion** for inviscid instability, analyzes Tollmien-Schlichting waves in boundary layers including neutral stability curves, discusses **Fjørtoft's theorem** (refinement of Rayleigh), treats transition mechanisms and critical Reynolds numbers for various geometries, explores route to turbulence and cascade of instabilities, and works through extensive examples involving eigenvalue calculations, growth rate determination, neutral curve construction, critical Reynolds number estimation, and physical interpretation of instability mechanisms.

## Linear Stability Theory Framework

**Base flow**: Steady parallel shear flow $\mathbf{U} = (U(y), 0, 0)$

**Perturbation**: Infinitesimal disturbance
$$\mathbf{u} = \mathbf{U} + \epsilon\mathbf{u}'(x,y,z,t)$$

where $\epsilon \ll 1$.

**Normal mode assumption**: 
$$u'_i(x,y,z,t) = \hat{u}_i(y)e^{i(\alpha x + \beta z - \omega t)}$$

where:
- $\alpha$ = streamwise wavenumber (real)
- $\beta$ = spanwise wavenumber (real)
- $\omega = \omega_r + i\omega_i$ = complex frequency
- $\hat{u}_i(y)$ = amplitude functions

**Growth rate**: $\omega_i = \text{Im}(\omega)$
- $\omega_i > 0$: Unstable (disturbance grows exponentially)
- $\omega_i < 0$: Stable (disturbance decays)
- $\omega_i = 0$: Neutral (marginal stability)

**Phase speed**: $c = \omega/\alpha$ (complex)

**Physical disturbance**:
$$u' = \text{Re}[\hat{u}(y)e^{i(\alpha x - \omega t)}] = |\hat{u}(y)|e^{\omega_i t}\cos(\alpha x - \omega_r t + \phi)$$

**Temporal stability**: Given $\alpha$ (real), find $\omega$ (complex) → predict growth in time at fixed location.

**Spatial stability**: Given $\omega$ (real), find $\alpha$ (complex) → predict growth in space for wave propagating downstream.

## Orr-Sommerfeld Equation Derivation

**Linearized continuity** (2D, incompressible):
$$\frac{\partial u'}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial v'}{\partial y} = 0$$

Define **stream function**: $u' = \frac{\partial\psi'}{\partial y}$, $v' = -\frac{\partial\psi'}{\partial x}$

For normal mode: $\psi' = \hat{\phi}(y)e^{i(\alpha x - \omega t)}$

Thus:
$$u' = \hat{\phi}'(y)e^{i(\alpha x - \omega t)}$$
$$v' = -i\alpha\hat{\phi}(y)e^{i(\alpha x - \omega t)}$$

**Linearized Navier-Stokes** ($y$-momentum eliminated via vorticity):

After substitution and linearization:

$$\boxed{(\alpha^2 - D^2)^2\hat{\phi} = i\alpha\text{Re}\left[(U - c)(\alpha^2 - D^2)\hat{\phi} - U''\hat{\phi}\right]}$$

where $D = \frac{d}{dy}$ and $c = \omega/\alpha$.

**Boundary conditions**:
- No-slip at walls: $\hat{\phi} = \hat{\phi}' = 0$ at $y = 0, h$

**Fourth-order ODE** with complex eigenvalue $c$ (or $\omega$).

**Orr-Sommerfeld equation** is **THE** equation for viscous stability!

## Example 1: Normal Mode Growth Rate

Disturbance $u' = 0.01\cos(x - 5t)e^{0.1t}$.

Find wavenumber, frequency, growth rate, phase speed.

**Solution:**

Compare to $u' = A e^{\omega_i t}\cos(\alpha x - \omega_r t)$:

$$\alpha = 1 \text{ (wavenumber)}$$
$$\omega_r = 5 \text{ (real frequency)}$$
$$\omega_i = 0.1 \text{ (growth rate)}$$

$$c = \frac{\omega_r}{\alpha} = \frac{5}{1} = 5 \text{ (phase speed)}$$

**Unstable** since $\omega_i > 0$ (grows exponentially).

**Amplitude doubles** in time $t = \ln(2)/\omega_i = 6.93$ time units.

**Answer:** $\alpha = 1$, $\omega_r = 5$, $\omega_i = 0.1$, $c = 5$; unstable

## Rayleigh's Inflection Point Theorem

For **inviscid** parallel flow ($\text{Re} \to \infty$), Orr-Sommerfeld reduces to **Rayleigh equation**:

$$(U - c)(\hat{\phi}'' - \alpha^2\hat{\phi}) - U''\hat{\phi} = 0$$

**Rayleigh's theorem**: Necessary condition for instability is existence of **inflection point** where $U'' = 0$.

**Proof sketch**:
1. Multiply Rayleigh equation by $\hat{\phi}^*$ (complex conjugate), integrate across channel
2. Apply boundary conditions
3. Separate real and imaginary parts
4. If $U'' \neq 0$ everywhere, show $\omega_i = 0$ (neutral or stable)
5. Therefore, instability requires $U'' = 0$ somewhere

**Physical interpretation**: Inflection point → vorticity gradient changes sign → potential for wave amplification via Reynolds stress working against mean shear.

## Example 2: Inflection Point Check

Profile $U(y) = \sin(y)$ for $0 < y < \pi$.

Is flow potentially unstable (inviscid)?

**Solution:**

$$U'(y) = \cos(y)$$
$$U''(y) = -\sin(y)$$

$U'' = 0$ only at boundaries for this profile within $(0,\pi)$.

But: For $0 < y < \pi$, $\sin(y) > 0$, so $U'' < 0$ everywhere in interior.

**No inflection point** in interior → **stable** by Rayleigh's criterion.

**Answer:** Stable (no interior inflection point)

## Fjørtoft's Theorem (Refinement)

**Fjørtoft**: For instability, not only must inflection point exist, but also:

$$U''(U - U_s) < 0$$

somewhere, where $U_s = U(y_s)$ is velocity at inflection point $y_s$.

**Stronger criterion** than Rayleigh's—rules out some profiles with inflection points.

## Tollmien-Schlichting Waves

In **boundary layers**, viscosity plays dual role:
- Stabilizing at small scales (dissipation)
- Destabilizing via production of disturbance energy from mean flow

**Tollmien-Schlichting (T-S) waves**: Unstable modes in Blasius boundary layer.

**Characteristics**:
- Two-dimensional waves ($\beta = 0$)
- Frequency $\omega \sim 10^{-4} U/\delta$ (low frequency)
- Wavelength $\lambda \sim 10\delta$
- Growth rates small but finite

**Neutral stability curve**: $\text{Re}_\delta$ vs. $\alpha\delta$ dividing stable/unstable regions.

**Lower branch**: $\text{Re}_\delta \approx 520$ (onset)
**Upper branch**: $\text{Re}_\delta \approx 30,000$ (viscous damping dominates)

Between branches: **unstable** region where T-S waves amplify.

## Example 3: T-S Wave Frequency

Blasius boundary layer: $U = 10$ m/s, $\delta = 0.005$ m.

Estimate T-S wave frequency.

**Solution:**

$$f \sim \frac{U}{10\delta} = \frac{10}{10(0.005)} = \frac{10}{0.05} = 200 \text{ Hz}$$

**Low frequency** (acoustic waves would be kHz).

**Wavelength**: $\lambda \sim 10\delta = 0.05$ m = 5 cm

**Answer:** $f \sim 200$ Hz

## Critical Reynolds Numbers

**Different geometries**, different critical values:

**Pipe flow** (Poiseuille):
$$\text{Re}_{crit} \approx 2300$$

(But can maintain laminar to $\text{Re} \sim 10^5$ with extreme care—**subcritical** instability)

**Flat plate boundary layer**:
$$\text{Re}_{x,crit} \approx 5 \times 10^5$$

(Natural transition, depends on freestream turbulence)

**Channel flow** (plane Poiseuille):
$$\text{Re}_{crit} \approx 5772$$

(Linear theory critical Re; actual transition lower due to subcritical mechanisms)

**Rayleigh-Bénard convection**:
$$\text{Ra}_{crit} \approx 1708$$

(Rayleigh number, thermal instability)

## Example 4: Transition Location on Flat Plate

Plate at $U = 20$ m/s (air, $\nu = 1.5 \times 10^{-5}$ m²/s).

Where does natural transition occur?

**Solution:**

$$\text{Re}_{x,crit} = \frac{U x_{trans}}{\nu} = 5 \times 10^5$$

$$x_{trans} = \frac{5 \times 10^5 \cdot \nu}{U} = \frac{5 \times 10^5 (1.5 \times 10^{-5})}{20}$$

$$= \frac{7.5}{20} = 0.375 \text{ m} = 37.5 \text{ cm}$$

**Transition** at $x \approx 38$ cm from leading edge.

**Downstream**: Turbulent boundary layer.

**Answer:** $x_{trans} = 37.5$ cm

## Transition Mechanisms

### Natural Transition

**Sequence**:
1. Environmental disturbances enter boundary layer
2. T-S waves amplify exponentially (linear growth)
3. Nonlinear effects saturate
4. Three-dimensional secondary instabilities
5. Breakdown to turbulent spots
6. Spots merge → fully turbulent

**e^N method**: Integrate growth rate along streamline
$$N = \int_{x_0}^x -\alpha\omega_i \, dx$$

Transition when $N \approx 9-10$ (9 e-foldings).

### Bypass Transition

**Large disturbances** ($>1\%$ freestream turbulence) skip linear T-S stage.

**Direct** breakdown to turbulence via transient growth mechanisms.

**Much earlier** transition than natural.

### Separation-Induced Transition

**Adverse pressure gradient** → separation → shear layer highly unstable → rapid transition in separated region.

**Reattachment** as turbulent boundary layer.

## Example 5: e^N Transition Prediction

If $N = 9$ for transition and maximum $\omega_i = 0.01$ sustained over $\Delta x = 0.5$ m:

Will transition occur?

**Solution:**

Approximate (constant $\omega_i$):
$$N \approx \alpha\omega_i\Delta x$$

Need to estimate $\alpha$. For T-S waves, $\alpha \sim 1/\delta$. If $\delta \sim 0.005$ m:

$$\alpha \sim 200 \text{ m}^{-1}$$

$$N \approx (200)(0.01)(0.5) = 1$$

$N = 1 < 9$ → **No transition** yet.

**Need more distance** or higher growth rates.

**Answer:** No transition ($N = 1 \ll 9$)

## Subcritical vs. Supercritical

**Supercritical** (e.g., Rayleigh-Bénard):
- Below $\text{Re}_{crit}$: stable to infinitesimal disturbances
- Above $\text{Re}_{crit}$: unstable, smooth transition

**Subcritical** (e.g., pipe flow):
- Linearly stable for all Re
- **Finite-amplitude** disturbances trigger turbulence below linear $\text{Re}_{crit}$
- Bistability: laminar and turbulent coexist

**Pipe flow paradox**: Linear theory predicts stability, but turbulence observed at $\text{Re} \sim 2300$.

**Resolution**: Transient growth of non-normal modes, then subcritical nonlinear instability.

## Example 6: Subcritical Instability in Pipe

Pipe flow at $\text{Re} = 3000$ (above empirical $\text{Re}_{crit} = 2300$).

Is flow stable or unstable?

**Solution:**

**Linear stability theory**: Stable (no exponentially growing modes).

**Experiment**: **Turbulent** (finite disturbances trigger transition).

**Subcritical instability**: Nonlinear mechanisms allow turbulence despite linear stability.

**Answer:** Linearly stable, but turbulent in practice (subcritical)

## Orr-Sommerfeld Eigenvalue Problem

**Numerical solution** required (no closed form except special cases).

**Methods**:
1. **Shooting method**: Integrate from boundary, adjust $c$ until boundary condition satisfied
2. **Spectral methods**: Expand $\hat{\phi}$ in Chebyshev polynomials, convert to matrix eigenvalue problem
3. **Finite differences**: Discretize ODE, solve generalized eigenvalue problem

**Output**: Eigenvalue spectrum $c_n$ (multiple modes).

Most unstable mode: largest $\text{Im}(c)$ (fastest growth).

## Example 7: Interpreting Eigenvalue

Orr-Sommerfeld gives $c = 0.4 + 0.01i$ for $\alpha = 1$.

Find $\omega$ and interpret.

**Solution:**

$$\omega = \alpha c = (1)(0.4 + 0.01i) = 0.4 + 0.01i$$

$$\omega_r = 0.4, \quad \omega_i = 0.01$$

**Phase speed**: $c_r = \omega_r/\alpha = 0.4$

**Growth rate**: $\omega_i = 0.01 > 0$ → **unstable**

Disturbance grows as $e^{0.01t}$, propagates at speed $0.4$.

**Answer:** $\omega = 0.4 + 0.01i$; unstable, $c = 0.4$

## Neutral Stability Curves

**Neutral curve**: Locus of $(\alpha, \text{Re})$ where $\omega_i = 0$.

**Typical shape**:
- **Lower branch**: Onset of instability (increasing Re)
- **Upper branch**: Restabilization (high Re, viscous damping)
- **Most unstable**: Peak growth rate between branches

**For Blasius**:
- Lower branch: $\text{Re}_{\delta^*} \approx 520$
- Upper branch: $\text{Re}_{\delta^*} \approx 30,000$

Within curve: **unstable** band.

## Example 8: Neutral Curve Reading

At $\alpha = 0.3$, neutral curve shows $\text{Re}_{lower} = 1000$, $\text{Re}_{upper} = 10,000$.

At $\text{Re} = 5000$, is $\alpha = 0.3$ mode stable?

**Solution:**

$$\text{Re}_{lower} < 5000 < \text{Re}_{upper}$$

**Inside unstable band** → **unstable**.

$\alpha = 0.3$ mode grows at $\text{Re} = 5000$.

**Answer:** Unstable (within neutral curve)

## Physical Mechanisms of Instability

**Shear**: Velocity gradients $dU/dy$ provide energy source via Reynolds stress $-\rho\overline{u'v'}$ working against mean shear.

**Stratification**: Buoyancy forces (Rayleigh-Bénard) or density gradients.

**Centrifugal**: Rotating flows (Taylor-Couette instability).

**Viscosity**: Dual role—dissipates energy but also mediates instability through critical layers.

**Reynolds stress production**:
$$\mathcal{P} = -\overline{u'v'}\frac{dU}{dy}$$

If $\mathcal{P} > 0$: Disturbances extract energy from mean flow → growth.

## Example 9: Reynolds Stress Production Sign

Mean flow $U = y^2$, fluctuations give $\overline{u'v'} = -0.01$ at $y = 1$.

Does disturbance extract energy?

**Solution:**

$$\frac{dU}{dy} = 2y \bigg|_{y=1} = 2$$

$$\mathcal{P} = -(-0.01)(2) = 0.02 > 0$$

**Positive production** → disturbances **extract energy** from mean flow → **growth**.

**Answer:** Yes, energy extraction (P > 0)

## Asymptotic Analysis at Large Re

For $\text{Re} \to \infty$, Orr-Sommerfeld develops **triple deck structure**:

1. **Viscous wall layer**: $y \sim \text{Re}^{-5/8}$
2. **Main layer**: Inviscid Rayleigh equation
3. **Upper deck**: Potential flow

**Matching** between layers determines growth rates.

**Result**: $\omega_i \sim \text{Re}^{-1/2}$ (small growth rates at high Re).

## Example 10: Growth Rate Scaling

If $\omega_i = 0.01$ at $\text{Re} = 10^4$, estimate $\omega_i$ at $\text{Re} = 4 \times 10^4$ (asymptotic regime).

**Solution:**

$$\omega_i \propto \text{Re}^{-1/2}$$

$$\frac{\omega_{i,2}}{\omega_{i,1}} = \sqrt{\frac{\text{Re}_1}{\text{Re}_2}} = \sqrt{\frac{10^4}{4 \times 10^4}} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{4}} = \frac{1}{2}$$

$$\omega_{i,2} = \frac{0.01}{2} = 0.005$$

**Growth rate decreases** with increasing Re (viscous damping weakens but inertia stabilizes).

**Answer:** $\omega_i \approx 0.005$

---

## Route to Turbulence

**Stages**:
1. **Laminar**: Smooth, predictable
2. **Linear instability**: Exponential growth of small disturbances
3. **Nonlinear saturation**: Amplitude limits, secondary flows develop
4. **Secondary instabilities**: 3D breakdown, turbulent spots
5. **Fully turbulent**: Chaotic, broad spectrum

**Cascade of instabilities**: Primary instability → secondary → tertiary → ... → turbulence

## Example 11: Turbulent Spot Growth

Turbulent spot created at $x = 0.5$ m, grows at half-angle $\theta = 10°$.

At $x = 1$ m, what is spot width?

**Solution:**

Downstream distance: $\Delta x = 1 - 0.5 = 0.5$ m

Spot width: $W = 2\Delta x \tan\theta = 2(0.5)\tan(10°)$

$$= 1.0(0.176) = 0.176 \text{ m} = 17.6 \text{ cm}$$

**Wedge-shaped** spots grow as they convect.

**Answer:** $W = 17.6$ cm
