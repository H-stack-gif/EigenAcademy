# Article 5.3: Turbulence Modeling

The closure problem demands **turbulence models**—empirical or semi-empirical relations closing RANS equations by expressing Reynolds stresses in terms of mean flow quantities. The hierarchy ranges from **zero-equation mixing length models** $\tau_{ij}^R \sim \ell_m^2|\overline{S}|\overline{S}_{ij}$ (algebraic, cheap but limited) to **two-equation models** like $k$-$\epsilon$ (transport PDEs for turbulent kinetic energy $k$ and dissipation rate $\epsilon$, widely used in CFD) to **Reynolds Stress Models (RSM)** solving full transport equations for each $\overline{u_i'u_j'}$ (most accurate but expensive). The **eddy viscosity concept** $\tau_{ij}^R = 2\mu_t\overline{S}_{ij} - \frac{2}{3}\rho k\delta_{ij}$ analogizes turbulent stresses to viscous stresses via turbulent viscosity $\mu_t$—but unlike molecular $\mu$, $\mu_t$ is **not a fluid property**, depending on local turbulence and flow history.

**$k$-$\epsilon$ model** solves transport equations for $k = \frac{1}{2}\overline{u_i'u_i'}$ and $\epsilon = \nu\overline{(\partial u_i'/\partial x_j)^2}$, computing eddy viscosity as $\mu_t = C_\mu\rho k^2/\epsilon$ where $C_\mu \approx 0.09$. Production term $P_k = -\overline{u_i'u_j'}\frac{\partial \overline{u_i}}{\partial x_j} = \mu_t|\overline{S}|^2$ (via eddy viscosity) provides energy source; dissipation $\epsilon$ removes energy. Empirical constants $C_{\epsilon 1}, C_{\epsilon 2}, \sigma_k, \sigma_\epsilon$ calibrated from experiments. **Large Eddy Simulation (LES)** resolves large energy-containing eddies directly, models subgrid scales via filtering—computationally intensive but captures unsteady large-scale structures. **Direct Numerical Simulation (DNS)** resolves all scales from integral $L$ to Kolmogorov $\eta$—requires grid resolution $N^3 \sim (\text{Re})^{9/4}$, feasible only for low-moderate Re.

This article introduces mixing length model $\ell_m$ and Prandtl's formulation, develops Spalart-Allmaras one-equation model (transport equation for $\tilde{\nu}$), derives **$k$-$\epsilon$ model equations** with detailed production/dissipation terms and empirical constants ($C_{\epsilon 1} = 1.44$, $C_{\epsilon 2} = 1.92$, etc.), discusses eddy viscosity $\mu_t = C_\mu\rho k^2/\epsilon$ and its physical basis, treats wall functions and near-wall modeling challenges, introduces RSM transport equations for $\overline{u_i'u_j'}$, covers LES methodology including filtering and subgrid-scale models (Smagorinsky), mentions DNS requirements and limitations, and works through extensive examples involving $\mu_t$ calculations, $k$-$\epsilon$ solutions, model comparison, wall function application, LES grid resolution, and practical CFD implications.

## Mixing Length Model (Zero-Equation)

**Prandtl's mixing length**: $\ell_m$ = distance fluid parcel travels before mixing.

**Eddy viscosity**: 
$$\boxed{\mu_t = \rho\ell_m^2\left|\frac{d\overline{u}}{dy}\right|}$$

**Reynolds stress**:
$$\tau_{12}^R = -\rho\overline{u'v'} = \mu_t\frac{d\overline{u}}{dy} = \rho\ell_m^2\left(\frac{d\overline{u}}{dy}\right)^2$$

**Specification**: Need $\ell_m(y)$ (empirical).

**For boundary layers**: $\ell_m = \kappa y$ near wall (von Kármán constant $\kappa \approx 0.41$).

**Limitations**: Algebraic (no history), fails for separated flows, arbitrary $\ell_m$ specification.

## Example 1: Mixing Length Near Wall

Boundary layer with $\frac{d\overline{u}}{dy} = 200$ s⁻¹ at $y = 0.01$ m, $\ell_m = 0.41y$.

Find $\mu_t$ (air, $\rho = 1.2$ kg/m³).

**Solution:**

$$\ell_m = 0.41(0.01) = 0.0041 \text{ m}$$

$$\mu_t = \rho\ell_m^2\left|\frac{d\overline{u}}{dy}\right| = (1.2)(0.0041)^2(200)$$

$$= (1.2)(1.68 \times 10^{-5})(200) = 0.00403 \text{ Pa·s}$$

**Much larger** than molecular $\mu_{air} \approx 1.8 \times 10^{-5}$ Pa·s!

**Answer:** $\mu_t = 4.0 \times 10^{-3}$ Pa·s

## Spalart-Allmaras Model (One-Equation)

**Transport equation** for modified eddy viscosity $\tilde{\nu}$:

$$\frac{D\tilde{\nu}}{Dt} = c_{b1}(1 - f_{t2})\tilde{S}\tilde{\nu} + \frac{1}{\sigma}\nabla \cdot [(\nu + \tilde{\nu})\nabla\tilde{\nu}] - c_{w1}f_w\left(\frac{\tilde{\nu}}{d}\right)^2 + f_{t2}c_{b2}\frac{1}{\sigma}(\nabla\tilde{\nu})^2$$

**Eddy viscosity**: $\mu_t = \rho\tilde{\nu}f_{v1}$

**Complex damping functions** ($f_{v1}, f_{t2}, f_w$) near walls.

**Advantages**: One equation, good for aerospace applications.

**Disadvantages**: Many empirical functions, tuned for specific flows.

## k-ε Model (Two-Equation)

**Most widely used** in industrial CFD.

**Turbulent kinetic energy equation**:

$$\boxed{\frac{Dk}{Dt} = \frac{\partial}{\partial x_j}\left[\left(\nu + \frac{\nu_t}{\sigma_k}\right)\frac{\partial k}{\partial x_j}\right] + P_k - \epsilon}$$

where:
- $\nu_t = \mu_t/\rho$
- $P_k = \mu_t|\overline{S}|^2$ (production, using eddy viscosity)
- $\sigma_k = 1.0$ (turbulent Prandtl number for $k$ diffusion)

**Dissipation equation**:

$$\boxed{\frac{D\epsilon}{Dt} = \frac{\partial}{\partial x_j}\left[\left(\nu + \frac{\nu_t}{\sigma_\epsilon}\right)\frac{\partial \epsilon}{\partial x_j}\right] + C_{\epsilon 1}\frac{\epsilon}{k}P_k - C_{\epsilon 2}\frac{\epsilon^2}{k}}$$

**Empirical constants**:
- $C_\mu = 0.09$
- $C_{\epsilon 1} = 1.44$
- $C_{\epsilon 2} = 1.92$
- $\sigma_k = 1.0$
- $\sigma_\epsilon = 1.3$

**Eddy viscosity**:
$$\boxed{\mu_t = C_\mu\rho\frac{k^2}{\epsilon}}$$

## Example 2: Eddy Viscosity from k-ε

Turbulence with $k = 2$ m²/s², $\epsilon = 0.5$ m²/s³, $\rho = 1000$ kg/m³.

Find $\mu_t$.

**Solution:**

$$\mu_t = C_\mu\rho\frac{k^2}{\epsilon} = (0.09)(1000)\frac{(2)^2}{0.5}$$

$$= (0.09)(1000)(8) = 720 \text{ Pa·s}$$

**Very high turbulent viscosity** (compared to water $\mu \approx 0.001$ Pa·s)!

**Answer:** $\mu_t = 720$ Pa·s

## Eddy Viscosity Physical Basis

**Analogy to molecular viscosity**: $\tau = \mu\frac{du}{dy}$ becomes $\tau^R = \mu_t\frac{d\overline{u}}{dy}$

**But**: $\mu$ is fluid property (thermodynamic), $\mu_t$ is **flow property** (depends on turbulence level, geometry, history).

**Dimensional analysis**: $\mu_t \sim \rho \times \text{velocity scale} \times \text{length scale}$

Using $k$ and $\epsilon$:
- Velocity scale: $\sqrt{k}$
- Length scale: $k^{3/2}/\epsilon$
- Thus: $\mu_t \sim \rho\sqrt{k} \cdot \frac{k^{3/2}}{\epsilon} = \rho\frac{k^2}{\epsilon}$

Explains $\mu_t = C_\mu\rho k^2/\epsilon$ structure!

## Production Term

In $k$-$\epsilon$ model:

$$P_k = -\overline{u_i'u_j'}\frac{\partial \overline{u_i}}{\partial x_j}$$

Using eddy viscosity hypothesis: $\tau_{ij}^R = 2\mu_t\overline{S}_{ij} - \frac{2}{3}\rho k\delta_{ij}$

Leads to:
$$\boxed{P_k = \mu_t\overline{S}_{ij}\overline{S}_{ij} = \mu_t|\overline{S}|^2}$$

where $|\overline{S}| = \sqrt{2\overline{S}_{ij}\overline{S}_{ij}}$ (magnitude of mean strain rate).

## Example 3: Production Calculation

Flow with $\mu_t = 100$ Pa·s, mean shear rate $|\overline{S}| = 10$ s⁻¹.

Find $P_k$.

**Solution:**

$$P_k = \mu_t|\overline{S}|^2 = (100)(10)^2 = 10,000 \text{ Pa/s} = 10,000 \text{ W/m}^3$$

(Units check: Pa·s⁻² = kg/(m·s³) = W/m³)

**Large production** → TKE increasing rapidly.

**Answer:** $P_k = 10$ kW/m³

---

## Dissipation Equation Physical Interpretation

$$\frac{D\epsilon}{Dt} = \ldots + C_{\epsilon 1}\frac{\epsilon}{k}P_k - C_{\epsilon 2}\frac{\epsilon^2}{k}$$

**Production of $\epsilon$**: Proportional to $P_k$ (production of $k$ increases small-scale activity → more dissipation).

**Destruction of $\epsilon$**: $-C_{\epsilon 2}\epsilon^2/k$ (cascade depletes $\epsilon$).

**Balance**: $\epsilon \sim P_k$ in equilibrium (dissipation = production).

## Wall Functions

**Near-wall problem**: High gradients, requires fine mesh.

**Wall functions**: Bridge viscous sublayer, impose log-law at first grid point.

**Assumes**: $y^+ = \frac{yu_\tau}{\nu} > 30$ (in log layer).

**Provides**: $\overline{u}(y)$, $k(y)$, $\epsilon(y)$ from wall distance.

**Avoids**: Resolving $y^+ < 1$ (expensive).

## Example 4: Wall Function Application

First grid point at $y = 0.001$ m, $u_\tau = 0.5$ m/s, $\nu = 10^{-5}$ m²/s.

Is $y^+$ suitable for wall function?

**Solution:**

$$y^+ = \frac{yu_\tau}{\nu} = \frac{(0.001)(0.5)}{10^{-5}} = \frac{5 \times 10^{-4}}{10^{-5}} = 50$$

$30 < y^+ < 300$ → **yes, in log layer** → wall function applicable.

**Answer:** Yes, $y^+ = 50$ (log layer)

## Reynolds Stress Models (RSM)

**Full transport equations** for each $\overline{u_i'u_j'}$ component (6 in 3D):

$$\frac{D\overline{u_i'u_j'}}{Dt} = P_{ij} + \Pi_{ij} - \epsilon_{ij} + D_{ij}$$

**Production**: $P_{ij} = -\overline{u_i'u_k'}\frac{\partial \overline{u_j}}{\partial x_k} - \overline{u_j'u_k'}\frac{\partial \overline{u_i}}{\partial x_k}$

**Pressure-strain**: $\Pi_{ij} = \overline{\frac{P'}{\rho}\left(\frac{\partial u_i'}{\partial x_j} + \frac{\partial u_j'}{\partial x_i}\right)}$ (redistributes energy, modeled)

**Dissipation**: $\epsilon_{ij} \approx \frac{2}{3}\epsilon\delta_{ij}$ (isotropic assumption)

**Advantages**: Captures anisotropy, complex flows.

**Disadvantages**: 7 equations (6 stresses + $\epsilon$), expensive, still requires models for $\Pi_{ij}$.

## Example 5: RSM vs. k-ε Equations

Compare equation counts for 3D turbulent flow.

**Solution:**

**$k$-$\epsilon$**:
- Mean: 4 (continuity + 3 momentum)
- Turbulence: 2 ($k$, $\epsilon$)
- **Total: 6 equations**

**RSM**:
- Mean: 4
- Reynolds stresses: 6 ($\overline{u'u'}$, $\overline{v'v'}$, $\overline{w'w'}$, $\overline{u'v'}$, $\overline{u'w'}$, $\overline{v'w'}$)
- Dissipation: 1 ($\epsilon$)
- **Total: 11 equations**

**RSM** nearly doubles computational cost!

**Answer:** $k$-$\epsilon$: 6; RSM: 11

## Large Eddy Simulation (LES)

**Philosophy**: Resolve large scales (anisotropic, geometry-dependent), model small scales (isotropic, universal).

**Filtering**: $\overline{u}_i(x) = \int G(x - x')u_i(x')dx'$ separates resolved from subgrid.

**Filtered N-S**:
$$\frac{\partial \overline{u}_i}{\partial t} + \overline{u}_j\frac{\partial \overline{u}_i}{\partial x_j} = -\frac{1}{\rho}\frac{\partial \overline{P}}{\partial x_i} + \nu\nabla^2\overline{u}_i - \frac{\partial \tau_{ij}^{SGS}}{\partial x_j}$$

**Subgrid-scale stress**: $\tau_{ij}^{SGS} = \overline{u_i u_j} - \overline{u}_i\overline{u}_j$ (modeled)

**Smagorinsky model**: $\tau_{ij}^{SGS} - \frac{1}{3}\tau_{kk}^{SGS}\delta_{ij} = -2\nu_{SGS}\overline{S}_{ij}$

where $\nu_{SGS} = (C_S\Delta)^2|\overline{S}|$, $\Delta$ = filter width (grid size).

## Example 6: LES Filter Width

Grid spacing $\Delta x = \Delta y = \Delta z = 0.01$ m.

What is filter width $\Delta$?

**Solution:**

$$\Delta = (\Delta x \Delta y \Delta z)^{1/3} = (0.01^3)^{1/3} = 0.01 \text{ m}$$

**Resolves scales** $> 0.01$ m, models scales $< 0.01$ m.

**Requires**: Domain integral scale $L \gg \Delta \gg \eta$ (Kolmogorov).

**Answer:** $\Delta = 0.01$ m

---

## Direct Numerical Simulation (DNS)

**Resolves all scales**: No modeling, direct solution of full N-S.

**Grid resolution**: $\Delta x \sim \eta = (\nu^3/\epsilon)^{1/4}$

**Total points**: $N^3 \sim (L/\eta)^3 = \text{Re}^{9/4}$

**Timestep**: $\Delta t \sim \tau_\eta = (\nu/\epsilon)^{1/2}$

**Total cost**: $\propto \text{Re}^3$ (very expensive!).

**Feasible**: $\text{Re} \lesssim 10^4$ (channel flow), $\text{Re} \sim 100$ (complex geometry).

**Use**: Research, fundamental turbulence physics, validation.

## Example 7: DNS Grid Requirement

Turbulence with $L = 0.1$ m, $\eta = 0.0001$ m.

Estimate grid points needed for DNS.

**Solution:**

$$N \sim \frac{L}{\eta} = \frac{0.1}{0.0001} = 1000$$

Total points: $N^3 = 10^9$ (1 billion grid points!)

**Modern supercomputers**: Feasible but expensive.

**Answer:** $\sim 10^9$ grid points

---

## Model Comparison

**Mixing length**:
- Pros: Simple, fast
- Cons: No history, fails for complex flows

**$k$-$\epsilon$**:
- Pros: Robust, widely validated, moderate cost
- Cons: Isotropic assumption, poor near walls

**RSM**:
- Pros: Anisotropic, best for complex flows
- Cons: Expensive, stability issues

**LES**:
- Pros: Captures unsteady structures, good accuracy
- Cons: Very expensive, requires fine mesh

**DNS**:
- Pros: No modeling, exact (within numerics)
- Cons: Prohibitively expensive for high Re

## Example 8: Model Selection

Steady flow around car body, moderate Re, industrial timeline.

Which model?

**Solution:**

**Requirements**: 3D geometry (complex), steady RANS acceptable, cost-effective.

**Choice**: **$k$-$\epsilon$ model**
- Proven for external aerodynamics
- Reasonable accuracy for drag/lift
- Fast enough for design iterations

**Not LES** (too expensive for design), **not DNS** (impossible).

**Answer:** $k$-$\epsilon$ model

## Turbulent Prandtl Number

**Heat transfer**: $\overline{u_i'\theta'} = -\alpha_t\frac{\partial \overline{\theta}}{\partial x_i}$

**Turbulent Prandtl number**: $\text{Pr}_t = \frac{\nu_t}{\alpha_t}$

**Typically**: $\text{Pr}_t \approx 0.9$ (turbulent mixing of momentum vs. heat similar).

**In models**: Often set constant for simplicity.

## Example 9: Turbulent Heat Flux

$\nu_t = 0.01$ m²/s, $\text{Pr}_t = 0.9$, temperature gradient $\frac{\partial \overline{T}}{\partial y} = -100$ K/m.

Find turbulent heat flux $\overline{v'T'}$.

**Solution:**

$$\alpha_t = \frac{\nu_t}{\text{Pr}_t} = \frac{0.01}{0.9} = 0.0111 \text{ m}^2\text{/s}$$

$$\overline{v'T'} = -\alpha_t\frac{\partial \overline{T}}{\partial y} = -(0.0111)(-100) = 1.11 \text{ K·m/s}$$

**Upward heat flux** (toward cold region).

**Answer:** $\overline{v'T'} = 1.11$ K·m/s

---

## Example 10: Equilibrium k-ε

In equilibrium turbulence, production = dissipation.

If $P_k = 50$ W/m³, what is $\epsilon$?

**Solution:**

$$\epsilon = P_k = 50 \text{ m}^2\text{/s}^3$$

**Balance**: Energy input (production) equals energy removal (dissipation).

**Typical** in developed turbulent flows away from boundaries.

**Answer:** $\epsilon = 50$ m²/s³

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: B** - Eddy viscosity $\mu_t$ is **flow property** (depends on turbulence level, mean shear, geometry), unlike molecular $\mu$ (thermodynamic fluid property).

**Problem 2: C** - $k$-$\epsilon$ model: two-equation model solving transport PDEs for turbulent kinetic energy $k$ and dissipation rate $\epsilon$, then computing $\mu_t = C_\mu\rho k^2/\epsilon$.

**Problem 3: C** - LES resolves **large scales** (energy-containing eddies) on computational grid, models **subgrid scales** (small eddies) via SGS models like Smagorinsky.

**Problem 4: D** - DNS requires resolving Kolmogorov scale $\eta$ → grid points $N^3 \sim (L/\eta)^3 \sim \text{Re}^{9/4}$ (extremely expensive scaling!).

**Problem 5: B** - Prandtl mixing length near wall: $\ell_m = \kappa y$ where $\kappa \approx 0.41$ (von Kármán constant), scales linearly with distance from wall.
