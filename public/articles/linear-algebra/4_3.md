# Linear Algebra Topic 4.3: Geometric Interpretation

Numbers aren't just abstract quantities—they measure things. The determinant measures **how much a linear transformation stretches or compresses space**. A determinant of 2 means volumes double; a determinant of 0.5 means volumes halve. A negative determinant means the transformation reverses orientation, like flipping space inside-out.

This geometric perspective transforms determinants from mysterious formulas into intuitive concepts. In two dimensions, the determinant gives the signed area of the parallelogram formed by column vectors. In three dimensions, it's the signed volume of a parallelepiped. For a linear transformation, it's the factor by which all volumes scale.

These geometric ideas have profound applications. In calculus, Jacobian determinants appear in change-of-variables formulas for multiple integrals. In differential equations, the determinant of the coefficient matrix determines system behavior. In physics, determinants appear in cross products, moments of inertia, and quantum mechanics. Understanding the geometry makes these applications transparent.

## Area and Volume Interpretation

For $2 \times 2$ matrix $ A = \begin{bmatrix} \vec{a}_1 & \vec{a}_2 \end{bmatrix}$ with column vectors $\vec{a}_1, \vec{a}_2$:

$$ |\det(A)| = \text{area of parallelogram spanned by } \vec{a}_1 \text{ and } \vec{a}_2$$

For $3 \times 3$ matrix $A = [\vec{a}_1 \ \vec{a}_2 \ \vec{a}_3]$:

$$ |\det(A)| = \text{volume of parallelepiped spanned by } \vec{a}_1, \vec{a}_2, \vec{a}_3$$

The **sign** of the determinant indicates **orientation**:
- $\det(A) > 0$: transformation preserves orientation
- $\det(A) < 0$: transformation reverses orientation
- $\det(A) = 0$: transformation collapses dimension (vectors are coplanar)

### Example 1: Area of Parallelogram

Find the area of the parallelogram formed by vectors $ \vec{v}_1 = \begin{bmatrix} 3 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$ and $ \vec{v}_2 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix}$.

**Solution:**

Form matrix with these as columns:
$$ A = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{bmatrix}$$

$$ \det(A) = (3)(2) - (1)(1) = 6 - 1 = 5$$

**Answer:** Area = $|\det(A)| = |5| = 5$ square units

### Example 2: Collinear Vectors

Find the area of the parallelogram formed by $ \vec{v}_1 = \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$ and $ \vec{v}_2 = \begin{bmatrix} 6 \\ 3 \end{bmatrix}$.

**Solution:**

$$ \det\begin{bmatrix} 2 & 6 \\ 1 & 3 \end{bmatrix} = (2)(3) - (6)(1) = 6 - 6 = 0$$

**Answer:** Area = 0. The vectors are collinear ($\vec{v}_2 = 3\vec{v}_1$), so they don't span a 2D region.

### Example 3: Volume of Parallelepiped

Find the volume of the parallelepiped spanned by:
$$ \vec{v}_1 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}, \quad \vec{v}_2 = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 2 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}, \quad \vec{v}_3 = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ 3 \end{bmatrix}$$

**Solution:**

$$ A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 3 \end{bmatrix}$$

Diagonal matrix: $\det(A) = (1)(2)(3) = 6$

**Answer:** Volume = 6 cubic units. (This is a rectangular box with dimensions $1 \times 2 \times 3$.)

### Example 4: Coplanar Vectors

Determine if vectors $ \vec{v}_1 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \\ 3 \end{bmatrix}$, $ \vec{v}_2 = \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 4 \\ 6 \end{bmatrix}$, $ \vec{v}_3 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$ are coplanar.

**Solution:**

Vectors are coplanar iff they don't span a 3D region, i.e., iff $\det(A) = 0$.

$$ A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 1 \\ 2 & 4 & 0 \\ 3 & 6 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

Notice column 2 is twice column 1, so $\det(A) = 0$.

**Answer:** Yes, coplanar. Volume = 0 means they lie in a plane.

## Linear Transformations and Scaling Factors

If $T: \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}^n$ is a linear transformation with standard matrix $A$, then $\det(A)$ is the **volume scaling factor**.

For any region $R$ in $\mathbb{R}^n$:
$$ \text{Volume}(T(R)) = |\det(A)| \cdot \text{Volume}(R)$$

### Example 5: Scaling Transformation

A linear transformation doubles the $x$-coordinate and triples the $y$-coordinate. By what factor does it scale areas?

**Solution:**

The transformation has matrix $ A = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 3 \end{bmatrix}$.

$$ \det(A) = (2)(3) = 6$$

**Answer:** Areas are scaled by factor 6. A unit square becomes a $2 \times 3$ rectangle with area 6.

### Example 6: Rotation Preserves Area

Show that rotation by angle $\theta$ preserves areas.

**Solution:**

Rotation matrix: $ R_{\theta} = \begin{bmatrix} \cos\theta & -\sin\theta \\ \sin\theta & \cos\theta \end{bmatrix}$

$$ \det(R_{\theta}) = \cos^2\theta - (-\sin\theta)(\sin\theta) = \cos^2\theta + \sin^2\theta = 1$$

Since $|\det(R_{\theta})| = 1$, areas are preserved.

**Answer:** Rotation scales areas by factor 1 (preserves them). Rotations are **isometries**.

### Example 7: Reflection Changes Orientation

Find the determinant of reflection across the $x$-axis and interpret.

**Solution:**

Reflection matrix: $ A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{bmatrix}$

$$ \det(A) = (1)(-1) = -1$$

**Answer:** $\det = -1$ means areas are preserved ($|-1| = 1$) but orientation is reversed (negative sign). Reflection flips the plane.

### Example 8: Projection Collapses Dimension

What is the determinant of projection onto the $x$-axis in $\mathbb{R}^2$?

**Solution:**

Projection matrix: $ P = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$

$$ \det(P) = (1)(0) = 0$$

**Answer:** $\det = 0$ means all 2D areas collapse to zero (projected onto a 1D line). Dimension is lost.

## Cross Product and Determinants

In $\mathbb{R}^3$, the cross product $\vec{u} \times \vec{v}$ can be computed using a determinant:

$$ \vec{u} \times \vec{v} = \begin{vmatrix} \vec{i} & \vec{j} & \vec{k} \\ u_1 & u_2 & u_3 \\ v_1 & v_2 & v_3 \end{vmatrix}$$

where $\vec{i}, \vec{j}, \vec{k}$ are unit vectors.

The magnitude $\|\vec{u} \times \vec{v}\|$ equals the area of the parallelogram spanned by $\vec{u}$ and $\vec{v}$.

### Example 9: Cross Product via Determinant

Find $ \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \\ 3 \end{bmatrix} \times \begin{bmatrix} 4 \\ 5 \\ 6 \end{bmatrix}$.

**Solution:**

$$ \vec{u} \times \vec{v} = \begin{vmatrix} \vec{i} & \vec{j} & \vec{k} \\ 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 4 & 5 & 6 \end{vmatrix}$$

Expand along row 1:
$$ = \vec{i}\begin{vmatrix} 2 & 3 \\ 5 & 6 \end{vmatrix} - \vec{j}\begin{vmatrix} 1 & 3 \\ 4 & 6 \end{vmatrix} + \vec{k}\begin{vmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 4 & 5 \end{vmatrix}$$

$$ = \vec{i}(12-15) - \vec{j}(6-12) + \vec{k}(5-8)$$

$$ = -3\vec{i} + 6\vec{j} - 3\vec{k}$$

**Answer:** $ \begin{bmatrix} -3 \\ 6 \\ -3 \end{bmatrix}$

### Example 10: Area via Cross Product

Find the area of the parallelogram spanned by $ \vec{u} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}$ and $ \vec{v} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}$ in $\mathbb{R}^3$.

**Solution:**

$$ \vec{u} \times \vec{v} = \begin{vmatrix} \vec{i} & \vec{j} & \vec{k} \\ 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 1 & 1 & 0 \end{vmatrix} = \vec{i}(0) - \vec{j}(0) + \vec{k}(1) = \vec{k}$$

$$ \|\vec{u} \times \vec{v}\| = \|\vec{k}\| = 1$$

**Answer:** Area = 1 square unit.

## Cramer's Rule

**Cramer's Rule** solves $A\vec{x} = \vec{b}$ when $A$ is invertible:

$$ x_i = \frac{\det(A_i(\vec{b}))}{\det(A)}$$

where $A_i(\vec{b})$ is matrix $A$ with column $i$ replaced by $\vec{b}$.

**Note**: Inefficient for large systems (use row reduction instead), but useful theoretically.

### Example 11: Cramer's Rule for $2 \times 2$

Solve $ \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 3 & 4 \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 5 \\ 6 \end{bmatrix}$ using Cramer's Rule.

**Solution:**

$$ \det(A) = \begin{vmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 3 & 4 \end{vmatrix} = 8 - 3 = 5$$

$$ A_1(\vec{b}) = \begin{bmatrix} 5 & 1 \\ 6 & 4 \end{bmatrix}, \quad \det(A_1(\vec{b})) = 20 - 6 = 14$$

$$ A_2(\vec{b}) = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 5 \\ 3 & 6 \end{bmatrix}, \quad \det(A_2(\vec{b})) = 12 - 15 = -3$$

$$ x = \frac{14}{5}, \quad y = \frac{-3}{5}$$

**Answer:** $ \vec{x} = \begin{bmatrix} 14/5 \\ -3/5 \end{bmatrix}$

### Example 12: When Cramer's Rule Fails

Why can't we use Cramer's Rule on $ \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 4 \\ 1 & 2 \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 6 \\ 3 \end{bmatrix}$?

**Solution:**

$$ \det(A) = (2)(2) - (4)(1) = 4 - 4 = 0$$

Cramer's Rule requires $\det(A) \neq 0$ (division by determinant).

Since $\det(A) = 0$, the matrix is singular and Cramer's Rule doesn't apply.

**Answer:** Can't use Cramer's Rule when $\det(A) = 0$. (This system actually has infinitely many solutions.)

### Example 13: Application - Triangle Area

Find the area of the triangle with vertices $(1, 2)$, $(3, 5)$, $(4, 1)$.

**Solution:**

Triangle area = $\frac{1}{2} \times$ (parallelogram area formed by vectors from one vertex to the other two)

Vectors from $(1,2)$ to other vertices:
$$ \vec{v}_1 = \begin{bmatrix} 3-1 \\ 5-2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 3 \end{bmatrix}, \quad \vec{v}_2 = \begin{bmatrix} 4-1 \\ 1-2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 3 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix}$$

$$ \det\begin{bmatrix} 2 & 3 \\ 3 & -1 \end{bmatrix} = (2)(-1) - (3)(3) = -2 - 9 = -11$$

Triangle area $ = \frac{1}{2}|-11| = \frac{11}{2}$

**Answer:** Area = $\frac{11}{2}$ square units

## Practice Problems

**Problem 1:** The determinant of a $3 \times 3$ matrix $A$ is 8. What is the volume of the parallelepiped formed by the column vectors of $A$?

A) 2
B) 4
C) 8
D) 64
E) 24

**Problem 2:** If a linear transformation $T: \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}^2$ has determinant $-3$, what happens to areas?

A) Multiplied by 3, orientation preserved
B) Multiplied by 3, orientation reversed
C) Multiplied by -3, orientation preserved
D) Multiplied by 9
E) Divided by 3

**Problem 3:** Which transformation preserves both area and orientation in $\mathbb{R}^2$?

A) Reflection across $y = x$
B) Scaling by factor 2
C) Projection onto $x$-axis
D) Rotation by $45°$
E) Shear transformation with $\det = -1$

**Problem 4:** The area of the parallelogram formed by $ \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$ and $ \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 3 \end{bmatrix}$ is:

A) 3
B) 5
C) 6
D) 7
E) 11

**Problem 5:** If three vectors in $\mathbb{R}^3$ have determinant 0, what can we conclude?

A) They are linearly independent
B) They span all of $\mathbb{R}^3$
C) They are coplanar
D) They form a basis
E) At least one is the zero vector

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: C**
$|\det(A)|$ = volume of parallelepiped
$|8| = 8$
Determinant directly gives the volume (taking absolute value).

**Problem 2: B**
$|\det(T)| = |-3| = 3$ gives area scaling factor
Negative determinant means orientation is reversed
Areas are multiplied by 3, orientation is reversed.

**Problem 3: D**
Rotation has $\det = 1 > 0$
$|\det| = 1$ preserves area
Positive determinant preserves orientation
(A) has $\det = -1$ (reverses orientation)
(B) has $\det = 4$ (changes area)
(C) has $\det = 0$ (collapses dimension)

**Problem 4: B**
$ \det\begin{bmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 3 \end{bmatrix} = (2)(3) - (1)(1) = 6 - 1 = 5$
Area = $|5| = 5$

**Problem 5: C**
If $\det = 0$, the vectors don't span a 3D region
They lie in a plane (coplanar)
Could be in a line or plane, but definitely not spanning all of $\mathbb{R}^3$
(A), (B), (D) all require $\det \neq 0$
(E) is possible but not necessary (could just be linearly dependent without being zero)
