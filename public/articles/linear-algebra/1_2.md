# Linear Algebra Topic 1.2: Row Reduction and Echelon Forms

You're staring at a system of four equations in five variables. Maybe you could use substitution—eliminate one variable, then another, then another. But which equation should you start with? Which variable should you eliminate first? One wrong choice and you're drowning in fractions and algebraic chaos.

There's a better way. The **row reduction algorithm** provides a systematic, foolproof method for solving any linear system. No guessing, no lucky choices—just a clear sequence of operations that always works. Better yet, the algorithm reveals the structure of solutions: whether they exist, whether they're unique, and if not, how to express the infinite solution set.

This algorithmic approach does more than solve systems. It's the foundation for nearly everything in linear algebra. Understanding row reduction means understanding how to find bases, compute inverses, determine linear independence, and analyze linear transformations. Master this topic, and you've mastered the most fundamental computational tool in the entire course.

## Elementary Row Operations

We can manipulate the rows of an augmented matrix without changing the solution set using three **elementary row operations**:

1. **Row swap**: Exchange two rows (notation: $R_i \leftrightarrow R_j$)
2. **Row scaling**: Multiply a row by a nonzero constant (notation: $cR_i \to R_i$)
3. **Row addition**: Add a multiple of one row to another (notation: $R_i + cR_j \to R_i$)

These operations preserve solutions because they're just performing the same algebraic manipulations we'd do to the equations themselves.

### Example 1: Elementary Row Operations

Perform the indicated operations on $ \left[\begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & 2 & -1 & 4 \\ 2 & -1 & 3 & 1 \\ -1 & 3 & 2 & 5 \end{array}\right]$:

a) $R_1 \leftrightarrow R_2$
b) $3R_3 \to R_3$
c) $R_2 - 2R_1 \to R_2$

**Solution:**

a) Swap rows 1 and 2:
$$ \left[\begin{array}{ccc|c} 2 & -1 & 3 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 & -1 & 4 \\ -1 & 3 & 2 & 5 \end{array}\right]$$

b) Starting from the original matrix, multiply row 3 by 3:
$$ \left[\begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & 2 & -1 & 4 \\ 2 & -1 & 3 & 1 \\ -3 & 9 & 6 & 15 \end{array}\right]$$

c) Starting from the original matrix, replace row 2 with row 2 minus twice row 1:
New row 2: $(2, -1, 3, 1) - 2(1, 2, -1, 4) = (0, -5, 5, -7)$

**Answer:** $ \left[\begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & 2 & -1 & 4 \\ 0 & -5 & 5 & -7 \\ -1 & 3 & 2 & 5 \end{array}\right]$

## Row Echelon Form (REF)

A matrix is in **row echelon form** if:

1. All nonzero rows are above any rows of all zeros
2. The **leading entry** (first nonzero number from the left, also called a **pivot**) of each nonzero row is to the right of the leading entry of the row above it
3. All entries below a leading entry are zero

**Example of REF:**
$$ \left[\begin{array}{cccc|c} 2 & -1 & 3 & 1 & 5 \\ 0 & 3 & -2 & 4 & 2 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 5 & -1 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \end{array}\right]$$

The pivots are 2, 3, and 5 (in positions (1,1), (2,2), and (3,4)).

**Reduced Row Echelon Form (RREF)** adds two more requirements:

4. Each leading entry is 1
5. Each leading entry is the only nonzero entry in its column

**Example of RREF:**
$$ \left[\begin{array}{cccc|c} 1 & 0 & 2 & 0 & 3 \\ 0 & 1 & -1 & 0 & 4 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 & -2 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \end{array}\right]$$

**Key fact**: Every matrix has a unique RREF, but many possible REFs.

### Example 2: Identifying Echelon Forms

Determine whether each matrix is in REF, RREF, both, or neither:

a) $ \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 0 & 1 & 4 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$

b) $ \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$

c) $ \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 2 \end{bmatrix}$

**Solution:**

a) REF but not RREF. Pivots (all 1's) are in correct staircase pattern, zeros below pivots. However, columns 2 and 3 have nonzero entries above their pivots (the 2, 3, 4), violating RREF requirement.

b) Both REF and RREF. This is the $3 \times 3$ identity matrix—all pivots are 1, in staircase pattern, and each is the only nonzero entry in its column.

c) Neither. The second row is all zeros but appears before the third nonzero row, violating REF requirement 1.

**Answer:** (a) REF only, (b) Both, (c) Neither

## The Row Reduction Algorithm

Here's the systematic procedure to reduce any matrix to REF, then to RREF:

**Forward phase (to REF):**

1. Start with the leftmost nonzero column (pivot column)
2. If necessary, swap rows to get a nonzero entry at the top of this column
3. Use row operations to create zeros below this pivot
4. Move to the next row and next pivot column; repeat until in REF

**Backward phase (REF to RREF):**

5. Starting from the rightmost pivot, scale each pivot row so the pivot becomes 1
6. Use row operations to create zeros above each pivot
7. Move leftward through pivots until complete

Let's see this in action.

### Example 3: Row Reduction to REF

Reduce to row echelon form:
$$ \left[\begin{array}{ccc|c} 0 & 3 & -6 & 6 \\ 3 & -9 & 12 & -9 \\ -2 & 6 & -8 & 6 \end{array}\right]$$

**Solution:**

The leftmost nonzero column is column 1. We need a nonzero entry in position (1,1):

$R_1 \leftrightarrow R_2$:
$$ \left[\begin{array}{ccc|c} 3 & -9 & 12 & -9 \\ 0 & 3 & -6 & 6 \\ -2 & 6 & -8 & 6 \end{array}\right]$$

Create zero in position (3,1):

$R_3 + \frac{2}{3}R_1 \to R_3$:
New row 3: $(-2, 6, -8, 6) + \frac{2}{3}(3, -9, 12, -9) = (-2, 6, -8, 6) + (2, -6, 8, -6) = (0, 0, 0, 0)$

$$ \left[\begin{array}{ccc|c} 3 & -9 & 12 & -9 \\ 0 & 3 & -6 & 6 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \end{array}\right]$$

This is in REF.

**Answer:** $ \left[\begin{array}{ccc|c} 3 & -9 & 12 & -9 \\ 0 & 3 & -6 & 6 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \end{array}\right]$

### Example 4: Continuing to RREF

Continue Example 3 to RREF.

**Solution:**

Starting from: $ \left[\begin{array}{ccc|c} 3 & -9 & 12 & -9 \\ 0 & 3 & -6 & 6 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \end{array}\right]$

Scale pivots to 1:

$\frac{1}{3}R_1 \to R_1$ and $\frac{1}{3}R_2 \to R_2$:
$$ \left[\begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & -3 & 4 & -3 \\ 0 & 1 & -2 & 2 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \end{array}\right]$$

Create zero above the second pivot (position (2,2)):

$R_1 + 3R_2 \to R_1$:
New row 1: $(1, -3, 4, -3) + 3(0, 1, -2, 2) = (1, 0, -2, 3)$

**Answer:** $ \left[\begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & 0 & -2 & 3 \\ 0 & 1 & -2 & 2 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \end{array}\right]$

### Example 5: Complete Row Reduction

Reduce to RREF and solve the system:
$$ \left[\begin{array}{cccc|c} 1 & 2 & 0 & 1 & 3 \\ 2 & 4 & 1 & 3 & 8 \\ 1 & 2 & 1 & 2 & 6 \end{array}\right]$$

**Solution:**

Already have a 1 in position (1,1). Create zeros below:

$R_2 - 2R_1 \to R_2$: $(2, 4, 1, 3, 8) - 2(1, 2, 0, 1, 3) = (0, 0, 1, 1, 2)$

$R_3 - R_1 \to R_3$: $(1, 2, 1, 2, 6) - (1, 2, 0, 1, 3) = (0, 0, 1, 1, 3)$

$$ \left[\begin{array}{cccc|c} 1 & 2 & 0 & 1 & 3 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 & 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 & 1 & 3 \end{array}\right]$$

Next pivot is in position (2,3). Create zero below:

$R_3 - R_2 \to R_3$: $(0, 0, 1, 1, 3) - (0, 0, 1, 1, 2) = (0, 0, 0, 0, 1)$

$$ \left[\begin{array}{cccc|c} 1 & 2 & 0 & 1 & 3 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 & 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 \end{array}\right]$$

The third row represents $0 = 1$, which is impossible.

**Answer:** The system is inconsistent (no solution). We can stop here—no need to complete RREF when we've found a contradiction.

### Example 6: System with Free Variables

Reduce to RREF and describe solutions:
$$ \left[\begin{array}{cccc|c} 1 & 3 & 0 & -1 & 2 \\ 2 & 6 & 1 & 1 & 7 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 & 3 & 4 \end{array}\right]$$

**Solution:**

$R_2 - 2R_1 \to R_2$: $(2, 6, 1, 1, 7) - 2(1, 3, 0, -1, 2) = (0, 0, 1, 3, 3)$

$$ \left[\begin{array}{cccc|c} 1 & 3 & 0 & -1 & 2 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 & 3 & 3 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 & 3 & 4 \end{array}\right]$$

$R_3 - R_2 \to R_3$: $(0, 0, 1, 3, 4) - (0, 0, 1, 3, 3) = (0, 0, 0, 0, 1)$

We get $0 = 1$ in row 3.

**Answer:** Inconsistent system (no solution).

### Example 7: Consistent System with Infinitely Many Solutions

Reduce to RREF and find general solution:
$$ \left[\begin{array}{ccc|c} 2 & -1 & 3 & 5 \\ 4 & -2 & 7 & 12 \\ -2 & 1 & -2 & -3 \end{array}\right]$$

**Solution:**

$\frac{1}{2}R_1 \to R_1$:
$$ \left[\begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & -\frac{1}{2} & \frac{3}{2} & \frac{5}{2} \\ 4 & -2 & 7 & 12 \\ -2 & 1 & -2 & -3 \end{array}\right]$$

$R_2 - 4R_1 \to R_2$ and $R_3 + 2R_1 \to R_3$:
$$ \left[\begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & -\frac{1}{2} & \frac{3}{2} & \frac{5}{2} \\ 0 & 0 & 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 & 2 \end{array}\right]$$

$R_3 - R_2 \to R_3$:
$$ \left[\begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & -\frac{1}{2} & \frac{3}{2} & \frac{5}{2} \\ 0 & 0 & 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \end{array}\right]$$

Create zero above pivot in position (2,3):

$R_1 - \frac{3}{2}R_2 \to R_1$:
$$ \left[\begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & -\frac{1}{2} & 0 & -\frac{1}{2} \\ 0 & 0 & 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \end{array}\right]$$

Reading as equations:
- $x_1 - \frac{1}{2}x_2 = -\frac{1}{2}$, so $x_1 = -\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{2}x_2$
- $x_3 = 2$

Variable $x_2$ is free (no pivot in column 2). Let $x_2 = t$:

**Answer:** $ \begin{bmatrix} x_1 \\ x_2 \\ x_3 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{2}t \\ t \\ 2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -\frac{1}{2} \\ 0 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix} + t\begin{bmatrix} \frac{1}{2} \\ 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}$ for any real $t$.

## Pivot Positions and Pivot Columns

A **pivot position** in a matrix is a location that corresponds to a leading 1 in the RREF.

A **pivot column** contains a pivot position.

**Key observations:**
- Basic variables correspond to pivot columns
- Free variables correspond to non-pivot columns
- Number of free variables = (number of columns) - (number of pivots)

### Example 8: Counting Pivots and Free Variables

For the RREF matrix:
$$ \left[\begin{array}{ccccc|c} 1 & 0 & 2 & 0 & -1 & 3 \\ 0 & 1 & -1 & 0 & 3 & 2 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 & 2 & -1 \end{array}\right]$$

Identify pivot columns and free variables.

**Solution:**

Pivot columns: 1, 2, and 4 (containing leading 1's)
Basic variables: $x_1, x_2, x_4$
Free variables: $x_3, x_5$ (columns 3 and 5 have no pivots)

Number of free variables: $5 - 3 = 2$

**Answer:** Pivot columns are 1, 2, 4. Free variables are $x_3$ and $x_5$.

## Existence and Uniqueness Theorem

For system $A\vec{x} = \vec{b}$ with augmented matrix $[A|\vec{b}]$:

**Existence**: The system is consistent if and only if the rightmost column of $[A|\vec{b}]$ is not a pivot column (i.e., no row of form $[0 \ 0 \ \cdots \ 0 \ | \ b]$ with $b \neq 0$ in RREF).

**Uniqueness**: If consistent, the solution is unique if and only if there are no free variables (i.e., every column of $A$ is a pivot column).

### Example 9: Analyzing Consistency and Uniqueness

Without solving, determine if the system is consistent, and if so, whether the solution is unique:

a) RREF: $ \left[\begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & 0 & 3 & 2 \\ 0 & 1 & -1 & 5 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \end{array}\right]$

b) RREF: $ \left[\begin{array}{cc|c} 1 & 0 & 4 \\ 0 & 1 & -2 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{array}\right]$

c) RREF: $ \left[\begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & 0 & 0 & 3 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 & -1 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 & 2 \end{array}\right]$

**Solution:**

a) No pivot in rightmost column (consistent). But column 3 of the coefficient matrix has no pivot, so $x_3$ is free. Infinitely many solutions.

b) The rightmost column has a pivot (third row reads $0 = 1$). Inconsistent—no solution.

c) No pivot in rightmost column (consistent). All three columns of coefficient matrix have pivots (no free variables). Unique solution.

**Answer:** (a) Consistent, infinitely many solutions; (b) Inconsistent; (c) Consistent, unique solution

## Solution Sets of Homogeneous Systems

For homogeneous system $A\vec{x} = \vec{0}$:

- Always consistent (trivial solution $\vec{x} = \vec{0}$ always exists)
- Has nontrivial solutions if and only if there are free variables
- Solution set forms a subspace of $\mathbb{R}^n$ (we'll explore this more in Unit 2)

### Example 10: Homogeneous System Solution Set

Find the general solution of the homogeneous system:
$$ \begin{cases}
x_1 - 3x_2 + 2x_3 = 0 \\
2x_1 - 6x_2 + 4x_3 = 0
\end{cases}$$

**Solution:**

Augmented matrix: $ \left[\begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & -3 & 2 & 0 \\ 2 & -6 & 4 & 0 \end{array}\right]$

$R_2 - 2R_1 \to R_2$:
$$ \left[\begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & -3 & 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \end{array}\right]$$

Already in RREF. From $x_1 - 3x_2 + 2x_3 = 0$: $x_1 = 3x_2 - 2x_3$

Free variables: $x_2 = s$, $x_3 = t$

$$ \begin{bmatrix} x_1 \\ x_2 \\ x_3 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 3s - 2t \\ s \\ t \end{bmatrix} = s\begin{bmatrix} 3 \\ 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} + t\begin{bmatrix} -2 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

**Answer:** All solutions have form $ s\begin{bmatrix} 3 \\ 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} + t\begin{bmatrix} -2 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$ for any real $s, t$.

## Rank of a Matrix

The **rank** of matrix $A$ is the number of pivot positions in any echelon form of $A$.

Equivalently: rank$(A)$ = number of leading 1's in RREF of $A$.

**Properties:**
- rank$(A) \leq \min(m, n)$ for an $m \times n$ matrix
- If $A$ is $n \times n$ and rank$(A) = n$, then $A$ is invertible (we'll see why in Topic 1.4)
- For system $A\vec{x} = \vec{b}$: number of free variables = $n - \text{rank}(A)$

### Example 11: Computing Rank

Find the rank of $ A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 \\ 2 & 4 & 6 & 8 \\ 1 & 3 & 5 & 7 \end{bmatrix}$

**Solution:**

Row reduce:
$R_2 - 2R_1 \to R_2$ and $R_3 - R_1 \to R_3$:
$$ \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 2 & 3 \end{bmatrix}$$

$R_2 \leftrightarrow R_3$:
$$ \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 \\ 0 & 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$$

Two pivot positions (in columns 1 and 2).

**Answer:** rank$(A) = 2$

### Example 12: Application - Checking Linear Independence

The columns of matrix $A$ are linearly independent if and only if $A\vec{x} = \vec{0}$ has only the trivial solution. Use this to check if the vectors are linearly independent:

$$ \vec{v}_1 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \\ 3 \end{bmatrix}, \ \vec{v}_2 = \begin{bmatrix} 4 \\ 5 \\ 6 \end{bmatrix}, \ \vec{v}_3 = \begin{bmatrix} 7 \\ 8 \\ 9 \end{bmatrix}$$

**Solution:**

Form matrix with these as columns:
$$ A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 4 & 7 \\ 2 & 5 & 8 \\ 3 & 6 & 9 \end{bmatrix}$$

Row reduce to check if $A\vec{x} = \vec{0}$ has only trivial solution:

$R_2 - 2R_1 \to R_2$ and $R_3 - 3R_1 \to R_3$:
$$ \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 4 & 7 \\ 0 & -3 & -6 \\ 0 & -6 & -12 \end{bmatrix}$$

$R_3 - 2R_2 \to R_3$:
$$ \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 4 & 7 \\ 0 & -3 & -6 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$$

Only two pivots for three variables means one free variable exists.

**Answer:** Linearly dependent (nontrivial solutions exist). In fact, $\vec{v}_3 = 2\vec{v}_2 - \vec{v}_1$.

### Example 13: Network Flow Problem

In a network, flow in must equal flow out at each node. Set up and solve for the flows:

[Imagine a network with 3 nodes where $f_1$ flows in, splits at a junction, and $f_2, f_3$ flow out]

Node A: $f_1 = f_2 + f_3$
Node B: $f_2 = f_4$  
Node C: $f_3 + f_4 = 10$

**Solution:**

Rearrange to standard form:
$$ \begin{cases}
f_1 - f_2 - f_3 = 0 \\
f_2 - f_4 = 0 \\
f_3 + f_4 = 10
\end{cases}$$

Augmented matrix:
$$ \left[\begin{array}{cccc|c} 1 & -1 & -1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 & -1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 & 1 & 10 \end{array}\right]$$

$R_1 + R_2 \to R_1$:
$$ \left[\begin{array}{cccc|c} 1 & 0 & -1 & -1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 & -1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 & 1 & 10 \end{array}\right]$$

$R_1 + R_3 \to R_1$:
$$ \left[\begin{array}{cccc|c} 1 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 10 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 & -1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 & 1 & 10 \end{array}\right]$$

Reading solutions: $f_1 = 10$, $f_2 = f_4$, $f_3 = 10 - f_4$

Let $f_4 = t$ (free variable):

**Answer:** $f_1 = 10$, $f_2 = t$, $f_3 = 10 - t$, $f_4 = t$ for $0 \leq t \leq 10$ (physical constraint).

A) The system has a unique solution
B) The system has infinitely many solutions

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: B**
Check requirements: pivots are 1 (yes), zeros below pivots (yes), zeros above pivots (yes), pivots in staircase pattern (yes).
(A) has 2 above first pivot in column 1
(C) has pivot 2 instead of 1 in position (2,2)
(D) pivots not in staircase pattern (first pivot in column 2, not column 1)
(E) has nonzero entry above the third pivot

**Problem 2: D**
Consistent (no pivot in rightmost column).
Variable $x_3$ is free (column 3 has no pivot).
Free variables mean infinitely many solutions.

**Problem 3: B**
All rows are multiples of the first row.
Row reduction gives only one pivot.
Rank = 1.

**Problem 4: C**
Second row represents $0x_1 + 0x_2 + 0x_3 = 5$, i.e., $0 = 5$.
This is impossible.
System is inconsistent.

**Problem 5: C**
Number of free variables = (total variables) - (pivot columns) = 5 - 3 = 2.
Need 2 parameters (one for each free variable).
