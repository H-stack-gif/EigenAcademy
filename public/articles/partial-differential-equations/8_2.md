# Partial Differential Equations Topic 8.2: Eigenfunction Expansions

The power of Sturm-Liouville theory lies in **eigenfunction expansions**: any "reasonable" function can be written as an infinite series of eigenfunctions. This generalizes Fourier series—where the eigenfunctions happen to be sines and cosines—to arbitrary Sturm-Liouville problems. Given eigenfunctions $\{\phi_n\}$, we expand $f(x) = \sum c_n\phi_n(x)$ where coefficients come from orthogonality: $c_n = \langle f, \phi_n\rangle / \|\phi_n\|^2$.

**Completeness** is the key property: the eigenfunctions form a basis for the function space. This means every function can be approximated arbitrarily well by finite sums, and the infinite series converges (in appropriate sense) to the function. Completeness isn't obvious—it's a deep theorem requiring careful analysis—but once established, it provides a systematic method for solving PDEs.

This article explores eigenfunction expansions systematically. We'll compute expansions for various Sturm-Liouville problems, understand convergence (pointwise, uniform, $L^2$), use expansions to solve inhomogeneous ODEs and PDEs, and discover how different boundary conditions lead to different expansion bases. These techniques complete our toolkit for solving linear PDEs.

## Computing Eigenfunction Expansions

### Example 1: Standard Fourier Sine Series

Expand $f(x) = x$ on $[0, \pi]$ using $\sin(nx)$.

**Solution:**

**Eigenfunctions**: $\phi_n = \sin(nx)$ from $y'' + \lambda y = 0$, $y(0) = y(\pi) = 0$

**Weight**: $w(x) = 1$

**Orthogonality**: $\int_0^{\pi} \sin(mx)\sin(nx) dx = \begin{cases} \pi/2 & m = n \\ 0 & m \neq n \end{cases}$

**Coefficients**:
$$c_n = \frac{\langle f, \phi_n\rangle}{\|\phi_n\|^2} = \frac{\int_0^{\pi} x\sin(nx) dx}{\pi/2}$$

Integration by parts:
$$\int_0^{\pi} x\sin(nx) dx = \left[-\frac{x\cos(nx)}{n}\right]_0^{\pi} + \frac{1}{n}\int_0^{\pi}\cos(nx) dx = -\frac{\pi\cos(n\pi)}{n} = \frac{(-1)^{n+1}\pi}{n}$$

$$c_n = \frac{2(-1)^{n+1}}{n}$$

$$x = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{2(-1)^{n+1}}{n}\sin(nx)$$

**Answer:** $x = 2\sum \frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{n}\sin(nx)$

### Example 2: Cosine Series with Neumann BCs

Expand $f(x) = x$ on $[0, L]$ using $\cos(n\pi x/L)$.

**Solution:**

**Problem**: $y'' + \lambda y = 0$, $y'(0) = y'(L) = 0$

**Eigenvalues**: $\lambda_0 = 0$, $\lambda_n = (n\pi/L)^2$ for $n \geq 1$

**Eigenfunctions**: $\phi_0 = 1$, $\phi_n = \cos(n\pi x/L)$

**Note**: $\phi_0 = 1$ from $\lambda_0 = 0$!

$$\|\phi_0\|^2 = \int_0^L 1^2 dx = L$$
$$\|\phi_n\|^2 = \int_0^L \cos^2(n\pi x/L) dx = L/2$$

**Coefficients**:
$$c_0 = \frac{1}{L}\int_0^L x \, dx = \frac{L}{2}$$

$$c_n = \frac{2}{L}\int_0^L x\cos(n\pi x/L) dx = \frac{2L}{(n\pi)^2}[\cos(n\pi) - 1] = \begin{cases} -\frac{4L}{(n\pi)^2} & n \text{ odd} \\ 0 & n \text{ even} \end{cases}$$

$$x = \frac{L}{2} - \frac{4L}{\pi^2}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{\cos((2k+1)\pi x/L)}{(2k+1)^2}$$

**Answer:** $x = L/2 - \frac{4L}{\pi^2}\sum \frac{\cos[(2k+1)\pi x/L]}{(2k+1)^2}$

### Example 3: Bessel Function Expansion

Expand $f(r) = 1$ using Bessel functions $J_0(\alpha_{0n}r)$ on $[0, 1]$.

**Solution:**

**Problem**: $\frac{d}{dr}\left[r\frac{dy}{dr}\right] + \lambda r y = 0$, $y(0)$ bounded, $y(1) = 0$

**Eigenvalues**: $\lambda_n = \alpha_{0n}^2$ where $J_0(\alpha_{0n}) = 0$

**Eigenfunctions**: $\phi_n(r) = J_0(\alpha_{0n}r)$

**Weight**: $w(r) = r$ (from Sturm-Liouville form!)

**Orthogonality**: $\int_0^1 rJ_0(\alpha_{0m}r)J_0(\alpha_{0n}r) dr = \begin{cases} \frac{1}{2}[J_1(\alpha_{0n})]^2 & m = n \\ 0 & m \neq n \end{cases}$

**Coefficients**:
$$c_n = \frac{\int_0^1 r \cdot 1 \cdot J_0(\alpha_{0n}r) dr}{\frac{1}{2}[J_1(\alpha_{0n})]^2}$$

Using $\int_0^1 rJ_0(\alpha r) dr = \frac{1}{\alpha}J_1(\alpha)$:

$$c_n = \frac{2J_1(\alpha_{0n})/\alpha_{0n}}{J_1^2(\alpha_{0n})} = \frac{2}{\alpha_{0n}J_1(\alpha_{0n})}$$

$$1 = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{2}{\alpha_{0n}J_1(\alpha_{0n})}J_0(\alpha_{0n}r)$$

**Answer:** $1 = 2\sum \frac{J_0(\alpha_{0n}r)}{\alpha_{0n}J_1(\alpha_{0n})}$

### Example 4: Mixed Boundary Conditions

Expand using eigenfunctions from $y'' + \lambda y = 0$, $y(0) = 0$, $y'(L) = 0$.

**Solution:**

From Unit 8.1 Example 10:
$$\lambda_n = \left[\frac{(2n+1)\pi}{2L}\right]^2$$
$$\phi_n(x) = \sin\left(\frac{(2n+1)\pi x}{2L}\right)$$

**Orthogonality**: $\int_0^L \phi_m\phi_n dx = \begin{cases} L/2 & m = n \\ 0 & m \neq n \end{cases}$

**Expansion**: $f(x) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} c_n\sin\left(\frac{(2n+1)\pi x}{2L}\right)$

$$c_n = \frac{2}{L}\int_0^L f(x)\sin\left(\frac{(2n+1)\pi x}{2L}\right) dx$$

**Different from** regular sine series! Odd harmonics only, but different scaling.

**Answer:** Expansion uses $\sin[(2n+1)\pi x/(2L)]$, not $\sin(n\pi x/L)$

## Convergence of Expansions

### Example 5: Pointwise Convergence

When does $\sum c_n\phi_n(x)$ converge to $f(x)$ pointwise?

**Solution:**

**Sufficient conditions** (similar to Fourier series):
- $f$ piecewise continuous
- $f$ has piecewise continuous derivative
- At discontinuities: converges to $\frac{f(x^-) + f(x^+)}{2}$

**Rate depends on smoothness**:
- $f$ discontinuous: $c_n \sim 1/n$ (slow)
- $f$ continuous, $f'$ discontinuous: $c_n \sim 1/n^2$ (faster)
- $f$ smooth: $c_n$ decays exponentially (very fast)

**Answer:** Converges pointwise under Dirichlet-like conditions

### Example 6: Uniform Convergence

For uniform convergence throughout $[a, b]$:

**Solution:**

**Theorem**: If $f$ and $f'$ continuous, and BCs satisfied, then eigenfunction expansion converges **uniformly**.

**Weierstrass M-test**: If $|c_n\phi_n(x)| \leq M_n$ with $\sum M_n < \infty$, series converges uniformly.

For smooth $f$: $c_n$ decays fast enough.

**Consequence**: Can differentiate term-by-term if $f$ smooth enough.

**Answer:** Uniform convergence requires $f$ continuous with continuous derivative

### Example 7: Mean-Square ($L^2$) Convergence

**Most general** notion: $\|f - \sum_{n=1}^N c_n\phi_n\|^2 \to 0$ as $N \to \infty$.

**Solution:**

**Theorem** (Completeness): For any $f$ with $\int_a^b w(x)f^2(x) dx < \infty$:

$$\lim_{N\to\infty} \int_a^b w(x)\left[f(x) - \sum_{n=1}^N c_n\phi_n(x)\right]^2 dx = 0$$

**Parseval's identity**:
$$\int_a^b wf^2 dx = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} c_n^2 \|\phi_n\|^2$$

**Weakest requirement**: $f$ need not be continuous!

**Most important** for PDEs: energy convergence.

**Answer:** $L^2$ convergence holds for any square-integrable $f$

## Solving Inhomogeneous Problems

### Example 8: Inhomogeneous ODE

Solve $-y'' = f(x)$ on $[0, L]$ with $y(0) = y(L) = 0$.

**Solution:**

**Expand** $f$ and $y$ in eigenfunctions $\sin(n\pi x/L)$:
$$f(x) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} f_n\sin(n\pi x/L)$$
$$y(x) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} y_n\sin(n\pi x/L)$$

**Substitute** into ODE:
$$-\sum y_n \left[-\frac{n^2\pi^2}{L^2}\right]\sin(n\pi x/L) = \sum f_n\sin(n\pi x/L)$$

$$\frac{n^2\pi^2}{L^2}y_n = f_n$$

$$y_n = \frac{L^2}{n^2\pi^2}f_n$$

**Solution**:
$$y(x) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{L^2}{n^2\pi^2}f_n\sin(n\pi x/L)$$

where $f_n = \frac{2}{L}\int_0^L f(x)\sin(n\pi x/L) dx$

**Answer:** $y = \sum \frac{L^2}{n^2\pi^2}f_n\sin(n\pi x/L)$

### Example 9: Specific Source

For $f(x) = x$ in Example 8:

**Solution:**

From Example 1: $f_n = 2(-1)^{n+1}/n$

$$y_n = \frac{L^2}{n^2\pi^2} \cdot \frac{2(-1)^{n+1}}{n} = \frac{2L^2(-1)^{n+1}}{n^3\pi^2}$$

$$y(x) = \frac{2L^2}{\pi^2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{n^3}\sin(n\pi x/L)$$

**Check**: $y(0) = 0$ ✓, $y(L) = 0$ ✓

**Physical**: Solution to Poisson equation $-y'' = x$ (linear source).

**Answer:** $y = \frac{2L^2}{\pi^2}\sum \frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{n^3}\sin(n\pi x/L)$

### Example 10: Time-Dependent PDE

Heat equation $u_t = ku_{xx}$ with $u(0,t) = u(L,t) = 0$, $u(x,0) = f(x)$.

**Solution:**

**Expand** in eigenfunctions:
$$u(x, t) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} T_n(t)\sin(n\pi x/L)$$

**Substitute**:
$$\sum T_n'(t)\sin(n\pi x/L) = -k\sum T_n(t)\frac{n^2\pi^2}{L^2}\sin(n\pi x/L)$$

**Each mode evolves independently**:
$$T_n'(t) = -k\frac{n^2\pi^2}{L^2}T_n(t)$$

$$T_n(t) = T_n(0)e^{-kn^2\pi^2 t/L^2}$$

**Initial condition**: $T_n(0) = f_n = \frac{2}{L}\int_0^L f(x)\sin(n\pi x/L) dx$

$$u(x, t) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} f_n e^{-kn^2\pi^2 t/L^2}\sin(n\pi x/L)$$

**Answer:** $u = \sum f_n e^{-kn^2\pi^2 t/L^2}\sin(n\pi x/L)$

## Multi-Dimensional Expansions

### Example 11: 2D Rectangle

Laplace's equation on rectangle $[0, a] \times [0, b]$ with $u = 0$ on three sides, $u(x, b) = f(x)$.

**Solution:**

**Separation**: $u(x, y) = X(x)Y(y)$

**Eigenfunctions in $x$**: $X_n = \sin(n\pi x/a)$, $\lambda_n = (n\pi/a)^2$

**For $Y$**: $Y'' - (n\pi/a)^2 Y = 0$ gives $Y = \sinh(n\pi y/a)$

**General solution**:
$$u(x, y) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} B_n\sin(n\pi x/a)\sinh(n\pi y/a)$$

**Match $u(x, b) = f(x)$**:
$$\sum B_n\sin(n\pi x/a)\sinh(n\pi b/a) = f(x)$$

$$B_n = \frac{2}{a\sinh(n\pi b/a)}\int_0^a f(x)\sin(n\pi x/a) dx$$

**Answer:** Double expansion in $x$ and $y$ eigenfunctions

### Example 12: Circular Disk

Laplace on disk: $u_{rr} + \frac{1}{r}u_r + \frac{1}{r^2}u_{\theta\theta} = 0$ with $u(1, \theta) = f(\theta)$.

**Solution:**

**Separation**: $u = R(r)\Theta(\theta)$

**Angular eigenfunctions**: $\Theta_n = \cos(n\theta)$ and $\sin(n\theta)$, $n = 0, 1, 2, \ldots$

**Radial solutions**: $R_n = r^n$ (bounded at origin)

**General**:
$$u(r, \theta) = \frac{a_0}{2} + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} r^n[a_n\cos(n\theta) + b_n\sin(n\theta)]$$

**Boundary**: Standard Fourier series for $f(\theta)$ scaled by $r^n$.

**Answer:** Expansion in Fourier modes $r^n\cos(n\theta)$, $r^n\sin(n\theta)$

### Example 13: Sturm-Liouville in Each Variable

For general domain, separate in each coordinate.

**Solution:**

**Example**: $u_{xx} + u_{yy} = 0$ on $[0, a] \times [0, b]$ with appropriate BCs.

**$x$-eigenfunctions**: $X_m(x)$ from Sturm-Liouville in $x$

**$y$-eigenfunctions**: $Y_n(y)$ from Sturm-Liouville in $y$

**Product eigenfunctions**: $\phi_{mn}(x, y) = X_m(x)Y_n(y)$

**Double sum**:
$$u(x, y) = \sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} c_{mn}X_m(x)Y_n(y)$$

**Orthogonality**: $\langle\phi_{mn}, \phi_{pq}\rangle = 0$ unless $m=p$ and $n=q$.

**Answer:** Separate variables, expand in products of 1D eigenfunctions

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: B**
Orthogonality gives coefficients
$c_n = \frac{\langle f, \phi_n\rangle}{\|\phi_n\|^2} = \frac{\int_a^b wf\phi_n dx}{\int_a^b w\phi_n^2 dx}$
Projection onto eigenfunction $\phi_n$
Generalizes Fourier coefficients
Weight $w(x)$ from Sturm-Liouville problem

**Problem 2: B**
Smooth $f$ → fast decay of $c_n$ → fast convergence
Discontinuous $f$: $c_n \sim 1/n$ (slow)
$f$ continuous, $f'$ discontinuous: $c_n \sim 1/n^2$
Infinitely smooth: exponential decay
Integration by parts relates smoothness to coefficient decay

**Problem 3: B**
Mean-square (energy) convergence
$\int w(f - S_N)^2 dx \to 0$ as $N \to \infty$
Weakest/most general convergence
Doesn't require pointwise convergence
Parseval: $\int wf^2 = \sum c_n^2\|\phi_n\|^2$

**Problem 4: C**
Expand both in eigenfunctions
$f = \sum f_n\phi_n$, $y = \sum y_n\phi_n$
Substitute into ODE
Eigenfunctions satisfy $L\phi_n = -\lambda_n\phi_n$
Get algebraic relation: $\lambda_n y_n = f_n$

**Problem 5: A**
Separation of variables: $u = X(x)Y(y)$
Products of 1D eigenfunctions
Double sum: $\sum_m\sum_n c_{mn}X_m(x)Y_n(y)$
Orthogonal in 2D: $\langle X_mY_n, X_pY_q\rangle = 0$ unless $m=p, n=q$
Extends to higher dimensions
