# Partial Differential Equations Topic 6.1: Derivation and Applications

Hold a copper plate at different temperatures along its edges and wait. Eventually, the temperature settles into a stable pattern—no longer changing with time, but varying from point to point across the plate. This **steady-state** distribution satisfies **Laplace's equation** $\nabla^2 u = 0$, the most important elliptic PDE in mathematics and physics. When there are internal heat sources, the temperature satisfies **Poisson's equation** $\nabla^2 u = f$, a natural generalization.

Laplace's equation appears everywhere: steady-state temperature, electrostatic potential, gravitational potential, fluid flow, elastic membranes. Its solutions—**harmonic functions**—possess remarkable properties: they're infinitely smooth, they equal their average over any sphere, and they attain maximum and minimum values only on boundaries. These properties make Laplace's equation fundamentally different from the heat and wave equations.

This article derives Laplace's and Poisson's equations from physical principles and explores their applications. We'll see how steady states emerge as long-time limits of diffusion processes, understand why these equations are elliptic (no preferred direction), and discover the physical meaning of harmonic functions. These equations govern equilibrium configurations—the final resting states toward which dynamic systems evolve.

## Derivation from Heat Equation

### Example 1: Steady-State Temperature

Start with heat equation: $u_t = k\nabla^2 u$

What happens as $t \to \infty$?

**Solution:**

At equilibrium (steady state): $u_t = 0$

Heat equation becomes:
$$k\nabla^2 u = 0$$

$$\nabla^2 u = 0$$

This is **Laplace's equation**.

**Physical meaning**: No net heat flow—every point receives as much heat as it loses.

**Answer:** Steady-state temperature satisfies Laplace's equation

### Example 2: Laplace's Equation in Different Dimensions

Write $\nabla^2 u = 0$ explicitly in 1D, 2D, and 3D.

**Solution:**

**1D** (not interesting—linear solutions):
$$u_{xx} = 0 \implies u(x) = Ax + B$$

**2D** (rectangles, circles):
$$u_{xx} + u_{yy} = 0$$

**3D** (spheres, boxes):
$$u_{xx} + u_{yy} + u_{zz} = 0$$

**Coordinate-free form**: $\nabla^2 u = \text{div}(\text{grad } u) = 0$

**Answer:** 2D: $u_{xx} + u_{yy} = 0$; 3D: $u_{xx} + u_{yy} + u_{zz} = 0$

### Example 3: Poisson's Equation with Sources

If there are heat sources $f(x,y)$ (watts per unit volume):

**Solution:**

Heat equation with sources:
$$u_t = k\nabla^2 u + f$$

At steady state:
$$k\nabla^2 u + f = 0$$

$$\nabla^2 u = -\frac{f}{k}$$

Defining $g = -f/k$:
$$\nabla^2 u = g$$

This is **Poisson's equation**.

**Physical meaning**: Sources create non-zero divergence of heat flux.

**Answer:** Steady state with sources gives Poisson's equation $\nabla^2 u = f$

## Elliptic Nature

### Example 4: Classification

For $Au_{xx} + Bu_{xy} + Cu_{yy} = F$, discriminant is $\Delta = B^2 - 4AC$.

Classify Laplace's equation.

**Solution:**

Laplace: $u_{xx} + u_{yy} = 0$

$A = 1$, $B = 0$, $C = 1$

$$\Delta = 0^2 - 4(1)(1) = -4 < 0$$

**Elliptic** (like ellipse equation $x^2/a^2 + y^2/b^2 = 1$).

**Physical meaning**: No preferred direction—isotropic.

**Answer:** Laplace's equation is elliptic ($\Delta < 0$)

### Example 5: No Real Characteristics

For elliptic PDEs, characteristic curves are complex.

**Solution:**

Characteristics satisfy: $A(dy/dx)^2 - B(dy/dx) + C = 0$

For Laplace: $(dy/dx)^2 + 1 = 0$

$$dy/dx = \pm i$$

**Complex characteristics** mean information doesn't propagate along specific curves.

**Contrast parabolic/hyperbolic**: Real characteristics carry information.

**Answer:** Complex characteristics; no directional propagation

### Example 6: Domain of Dependence

For Laplace's equation on bounded domain, where does solution depend on data?

**Solution:**

Solution at ANY interior point depends on data at ALL boundary points.

**Non-local**: Change boundary value anywhere affects solution everywhere.

**Contrast wave equation**: Local domain of dependence (finite speed).

**Physical**: Steady state "knows about" entire boundary simultaneously.

**Answer:** Solution depends on entire boundary (non-local)

## Electrostatics Application

### Example 7: Electrostatic Potential

Electric field $\vec{E}$ relates to potential $\phi$ by:
$$\vec{E} = -\nabla \phi$$

In free space (no charges):

**Solution:**

Gauss's law: $\text{div } \vec{E} = \rho/\epsilon_0$ where $\rho$ is charge density.

In free space: $\rho = 0$

$$\text{div}(-\nabla \phi) = 0$$

$$-\nabla^2 \phi = 0$$

$$\nabla^2 \phi = 0$$

**Laplace's equation** for electrostatic potential!

**Answer:** Potential in charge-free region satisfies Laplace's equation

### Example 8: Poisson's Equation with Charges

With charge density $\rho(x,y,z)$:

**Solution:**

Gauss's law: $\text{div } \vec{E} = \rho/\epsilon_0$

$$-\nabla^2 \phi = \rho/\epsilon_0$$

$$\nabla^2 \phi = -\frac{\rho}{\epsilon_0}$$

**Poisson's equation** with source term $-\rho/\epsilon_0$.

**Example**: Point charge at origin: $\rho = q\delta(\vec{r})$

Solution: $\phi = \frac{q}{4\pi\epsilon_0 r}$ (Coulomb potential)

**Answer:** With charges, potential satisfies Poisson's equation

### Example 9: Capacitor

Two parallel plates at $y = 0$ (voltage $V_0$) and $y = d$ (voltage $0$).

Find potential between plates.

**Solution:**

**PDE**: $\nabla^2 \phi = 0$ (no charges between plates)

**Boundary conditions**: $\phi(x, 0, z) = V_0$, $\phi(x, d, z) = 0$

By symmetry, $\phi$ depends only on $y$:
$$\frac{d^2\phi}{dy^2} = 0$$

$$\phi(y) = Ay + B$$

From BCs: $B = V_0$, $Ad + V_0 = 0$

$$\phi(y) = V_0\left(1 - \frac{y}{d}\right)$$

**Linear** potential between plates.

**Answer:** $\phi(y) = V_0(1 - y/d)$

## Gravity and Potential Theory

### Example 10: Gravitational Potential

Gravitational field $\vec{g} = -\nabla \Phi$ where $\Phi$ is gravitational potential.

**Solution:**

Gauss's law for gravity: $\text{div } \vec{g} = -4\pi G \rho_m$

where $\rho_m$ is mass density, $G$ is gravitational constant.

$$-\nabla^2 \Phi = -4\pi G \rho_m$$

$$\nabla^2 \Phi = 4\pi G \rho_m$$

**Poisson's equation** with source $4\pi G \rho_m$.

In empty space: $\nabla^2 \Phi = 0$ (Laplace).

**Answer:** Gravitational potential satisfies Poisson's equation

### Example 11: Point Mass

For point mass $M$ at origin:

**Solution:**

$$\nabla^2 \Phi = 4\pi G M \delta(\vec{r})$$

Solution (by spherical symmetry):
$$\Phi(r) = -\frac{GM}{r}$$

Check: $\nabla^2(1/r) = -4\pi\delta(\vec{r})$ (important identity!)

**Gravitational potential** decreases as $1/r$.

**Answer:** $\Phi = -GM/r$

## Fluid Flow and Complex Analysis

### Example 12: Incompressible, Irrotational Flow

For 2D fluid flow with velocity $\vec{v} = (u, v)$:

**Solution:**

**Incompressible**: $\text{div } \vec{v} = u_x + v_y = 0$

**Irrotational**: $\text{curl } \vec{v} = v_x - u_y = 0$

Define **stream function** $\psi$ and **velocity potential** $\phi$:
$$u = \psi_y = \phi_x, \quad v = -\psi_x = \phi_y$$

Taking derivatives:
$$\nabla^2 \psi = \psi_{xx} + \psi_{yy} = -v_x - u_y = 0$$
$$\nabla^2 \phi = \phi_{xx} + \phi_{yy} = u_x + v_y = 0$$

Both $\psi$ and $\phi$ satisfy **Laplace's equation**!

**Connection to complex analysis**: $f(z) = \phi + i\psi$ is analytic.

**Answer:** Stream function and velocity potential both satisfy Laplace's equation

### Example 13: Harmonic Functions

Functions satisfying $\nabla^2 u = 0$ are called **harmonic**.

**Solution:**

**Examples of harmonic functions**:
- Linear: $u = ax + by + c$
- Logarithmic (2D): $u = \ln r$ where $r = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$
- Inverse distance (3D): $u = 1/r$ where $r = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2 + z^2}$
- Real/imaginary parts of analytic functions (2D)

**Properties**:
- Infinitely differentiable ($C^\infty$) in interior
- Mean value property
- Maximum principle (max/min on boundary)

**Answer:** Harmonic functions are smooth solutions to Laplace's equation

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: B**
Laplace's equation in 2D: $u_{xx} + u_{yy} = 0$
In 3D: $u_{xx} + u_{yy} + u_{zz} = 0$
Coordinate-free: $\nabla^2 u = 0$
Second derivatives in all spatial directions
Time-independent (steady state)

**Problem 2: C**
Elliptic: $B^2 - 4AC < 0$ (negative discriminant)
Example: $u_{xx} + u_{yy} = 0$ has $A=1, B=0, C=1$, so $\Delta = -4 < 0$
Parabolic: $\Delta = 0$ (e.g., heat equation)
Hyperbolic: $\Delta > 0$ (e.g., wave equation)
No real characteristics for elliptic

**Problem 3: C**
Steady state: $u_t = 0$
Heat equation: $u_t = k\nabla^2 u$
At steady state: $k\nabla^2 u = 0$
Therefore: $\nabla^2 u = 0$ (Laplace's equation)
No time dependence

**Problem 4: B**
Electric field: $\vec{E} = -\nabla \phi$
Gauss's law with $\rho = 0$: $\nabla \cdot \vec{E} = 0$
Substituting: $-\nabla^2 \phi = 0$
Laplace's equation: $\nabla^2 \phi = 0$
With charges: Poisson's equation $\nabla^2 \phi = -\rho/\epsilon_0$

**Problem 5: B**
Elliptic PDEs are boundary value problems
No time derivative → no initial conditions needed
Solution determined entirely by boundary conditions
Contrast: wave/heat equations need initial conditions
Non-local: depends on entire boundary
