# Partial Differential Equations Topic 5.1: Derivation and Physical Interpretation

Pluck a guitar string and watch it vibrate—the string oscillates up and down, with different points moving at different times. Unlike heat diffusion where temperature smooths out, wave motion preserves and propagates disturbances. A pulse traveling along a stretched rope, sound waves in air, light waves in space—all are governed by the **wave equation**, the fundamental PDE describing oscillatory phenomena.

The wave equation $u_{tt} = c^2u_{xx}$ emerges from Newton's second law applied to a continuous medium. A small segment of vibrating string experiences restoring force from tension pulling it back toward equilibrium. The key insight: acceleration (second time derivative) equals restoring force (proportional to curvature, the second spatial derivative). The constant $c$ is wave speed, determined by tension and mass density—tight strings with low mass vibrate faster.

This article derives the wave equation from first principles and explores what distinguishes it from the heat equation. We'll see why waves have **finite propagation speed** (disturbances travel along characteristics), why energy is conserved (no dissipation), and how boundary conditions determine standing wave patterns. Understanding the physics makes solving wave problems intuitive: waves don't disappear—they bounce, interfere, and persist indefinitely.

## Derivation from Newton's Second Law

Consider elastic string stretched along $x$-axis with tension $T$, mass per unit length $\rho$, displaced vertically by $u(x,t)$.

### Example 1: Force Balance on String Element

For small element $[x, x + \Delta x]$, find net vertical force.

**Solution:**

**Tension forces** at endpoints pull on element:
- At $x$: tension $T$ at angle $\theta(x)$, vertical component $T\sin\theta \approx T\tan\theta = Tu_x(x,t)$
- At $x + \Delta x$: tension $T$ at angle $\theta(x + \Delta x)$, vertical component $Tu_x(x + \Delta x, t)$

**Net vertical force**:
$$F = T[u_x(x + \Delta x, t) - u_x(x, t)] \approx T u_{xx}(x,t) \Delta x$$

(Small angle approximation: $\sin\theta \approx \tan\theta = u_x$ for small slopes)

**Answer:** Net force $F \approx T u_{xx} \Delta x$

### Example 2: Newton's Second Law

Mass of element: $m = \rho \Delta x$

Acceleration: $a = u_{tt}$

Apply $F = ma$:

**Solution:**

$$T u_{xx} \Delta x = \rho \Delta x \cdot u_{tt}$$

Divide by $\rho \Delta x$:
$$\frac{T}{\rho} u_{xx} = u_{tt}$$

Define **wave speed**: $c^2 = \frac{T}{\rho}$ (tension divided by mass density)

**Wave equation**:
$$u_{tt} = c^2 u_{xx}$$

**Answer:** $u_{tt} = c^2 u_{xx}$ where $c = \sqrt{T/\rho}$

### Example 3: Dimensions of Wave Speed

Verify that $c = \sqrt{T/\rho}$ has dimensions of velocity.

**Solution:**

$[T]$ = force = $MLT^{-2}$

$[\rho]$ = mass per length = $ML^{-1}$

$$[c^2] = \frac{[T]}{[\rho]} = \frac{MLT^{-2}}{ML^{-1}} = L^2T^{-2}$$

$$[c] = LT^{-1}$$ (velocity) ✓

**Physical meaning**: Higher tension or lower mass → faster wave propagation.

**Answer:** $[c] = LT^{-1}$ (velocity units)

### Example 4: Typical Wave Speeds

Guitar string: $T \approx 70$ N, $\rho \approx 10^{-3}$ kg/m

**Solution:**

$$c = \sqrt{\frac{70}{10^{-3}}} = \sqrt{70000} \approx 265 \text{ m/s}$$

**Sound in air** (different derivation): $c \approx 343$ m/s

**Light in vacuum**: $c = 3 \times 10^8$ m/s

**Seismic waves**: $c \approx 5-8$ km/s

**Answer:** Guitar string: $\sim 250$ m/s; varies widely by medium

## Comparison with Heat Equation

### Example 5: Heat vs. Wave Equations

Compare $u_t = ku_{xx}$ (heat) with $u_{tt} = c^2u_{xx}$ (wave).

**Solution:**

**Heat equation** ($u_t = ku_{xx}$):
- First-order in time (one initial condition: $u(x,0)$)
- Diffusion: smooths out variations
- Irreversible (increases entropy)
- Infinite propagation speed

**Wave equation** ($u_{tt} = c^2u_{xx}$):
- Second-order in time (two initial conditions: $u(x,0)$ and $u_t(x,0)$)
- Oscillation: preserves variations
- Reversible (time-symmetric)
- Finite propagation speed $c$

**Answer:** Heat dissipates, wave oscillates; fundamentally different physics

### Example 6: Initial Conditions

Why does wave equation need two initial conditions?

**Solution:**

Wave equation is second-order in $t$: $u_{tt} = c^2u_{xx}$

Analogous to $y'' = f(t)$ (second-order ODE) needing $y(0)$ and $y'(0)$.

**Physical meaning**:
- $u(x, 0) = f(x)$ specifies initial **displacement**
- $u_t(x, 0) = g(x)$ specifies initial **velocity**

String's future motion depends on both where it is and how fast it's moving.

**Answer:** Second-order in time requires position and velocity initially

### Example 7: Time Reversibility

If $u(x,t)$ satisfies wave equation, does $u(x,-t)$ also satisfy it?

**Solution:**

Let $v(x,t) = u(x,-t)$.

$$v_t = -u_t(x,-t), \quad v_{tt} = u_{tt}(x,-t)$$
$$v_{xx} = u_{xx}(x,-t)$$

If $u_{tt} = c^2u_{xx}$:
$$v_{tt} = u_{tt}(x,-t) = c^2u_{xx}(x,-t) = c^2v_{xx}$$ ✓

**Physical meaning**: Can "run the movie backward" for waves (not for heat!).

**Answer:** Yes, wave equation is time-reversible

## Boundary Conditions

### Example 8: Fixed End

String fixed at $x = 0$: what boundary condition?

**Solution:**

**Fixed end**: $u(0, t) = 0$ (Dirichlet)

String cannot move at fixed point.

**Physical**: End tied to wall or held by hand.

**Answer:** $u(0,t) = 0$

### Example 9: Free End

String end at $x = L$ is free to move vertically but constrained horizontally. What condition?

**Solution:**

At free end, no vertical force:
$$T u_x(L, t) = 0 \implies u_x(L, t) = 0$$ (Neumann)

**Physical**: String attached to frictionless ring on vertical rod—can slide up/down but not sideways.

**Answer:** $u_x(L,t) = 0$

### Example 10: Complete Problem Statement

State complete initial-boundary value problem for vibrating string on $[0, L]$ with fixed ends.

**Solution:**

**PDE**: $u_{tt} = c^2u_{xx}$ for $0 < x < L$, $t > 0$

**Boundary conditions**: 
- $u(0, t) = 0$ (left end fixed)
- $u(L, t) = 0$ (right end fixed)

**Initial conditions**:
- $u(x, 0) = f(x)$ (initial displacement)
- $u_t(x, 0) = g(x)$ (initial velocity)

**Answer:** PDE + 2 boundary conditions + 2 initial conditions (well-posed)

## Physical Interpretation

### Example 11: Propagation Speed

A pulse travels along string. How far does it move in time $\Delta t$?

**Solution:**

Wave speed is $c$, so in time $\Delta t$:
$$\Delta x = c \Delta t$$

**Characteristic curves**: $x \pm ct = \text{const}$

Information propagates along these curves at speed $c$.

**Contrast heat equation**: information spreads infinitely fast (though exponentially weakly).

**Answer:** Pulse travels distance $c\Delta t$ (finite propagation speed)

### Example 12: Domain of Dependence

For wave equation, solution $u(x_0, t_0)$ depends on what initial data?

**Solution:**

Characteristics through $(x_0, t_0)$ going backward to $t = 0$:
- $x - ct = x_0 - ct_0$ hits $t = 0$ at $x = x_0 - ct_0$
- $x + ct = x_0 + ct_0$ hits $t = 0$ at $x = x_0 + ct_0$

**Domain of dependence**: interval $[x_0 - ct_0, x_0 + ct_0]$

Solution at $(x_0, t_0)$ depends ONLY on initial data in this interval.

**Answer:** Solution depends on initial data in $[x_0 - ct_0, x_0 + ct_0]$

### Example 13: Standing vs. Traveling Waves

Distinguish standing waves and traveling waves.

**Solution:**

**Traveling wave**: $u = f(x - ct)$ or $u = g(x + ct)$
- Pulse moves without changing shape
- Energy propagates
- Example: plucking string once

**Standing wave**: $u = A\sin(kx)\cos(\omega t)$
- Pattern fixed in space, oscillates in time
- Nodes (zero displacement) stay fixed
- Example: vibrating guitar string at resonance

Decomposition: any solution is sum of left- and right-traveling waves.

**Answer:** Traveling waves move; standing waves oscillate in place

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: C**
Wave speed: $c = \sqrt{T/\rho}$
$T$ = tension (force), $\rho$ = linear mass density
Higher tension → faster waves
Higher mass → slower waves
Example: tight guitar string (high $T$) vibrates fast
Independent of frequency/wavelength (non-dispersive)

**Problem 2: B**
$u_{tt} = c^2u_{xx}$ (second derivative in time)
Requires two initial conditions: $u(x,0)$ and $u_t(x,0)$
Position and velocity needed
Contrast heat equation: $u_t = ku_{xx}$ (first-order in time)
Like $F = ma$ (second-order ODE)

**Problem 3: A**
Fixed end: $u = 0$ (Dirichlet condition)
String cannot move at attachment point
Example: guitar string at bridge and nut
Free end: $u_x = 0$ (Neumann)
Determines allowed frequencies (resonance)

**Problem 4: B**
If $u(x,t)$ solves $u_{tt} = c^2u_{xx}$, then $u(x,-t)$ also solves it
Can "play movie backward"
Consequence of time-symmetric PDE
Contrast heat equation (irreversible)
Physical: mechanical systems are reversible

**Problem 5: C**
Solution at $(x_0,t_0)$ depends on $[x_0 - ct_0, x_0 + ct_0]$
Interval length: $2ct_0$ (grows linearly with $t_0$)
Finite propagation speed $c$
Characteristics: $x \pm ct = \text{const}$
Contrast heat equation (infinite domain of dependence)
