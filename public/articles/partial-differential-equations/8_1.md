# Partial Differential Equations Topic 8.1: Sturm-Liouville Theory Fundamentals

Throughout this course, we've encountered the same pattern repeatedly: solve a differential equation, find eigenfunctions, expand solutions as series. The heat equation gave us $\sin(n\pi x/L)$, Laplace's equation in polar coordinates gave us $r^n\cos(n\theta)$, and Fourier series gave us trigonometric functions. These aren't coincidences—they're all examples of **Sturm-Liouville problems**, a unified theory explaining why eigenfunction methods work so universally.

A **Sturm-Liouville problem** is an eigenvalue problem of the form $\frac{d}{dx}[p(x)y'] + [q(x) + \lambda w(x)]y = 0$ with appropriate boundary conditions. This framework encompasses virtually all the eigenvalue problems we've seen. **Sturm-Liouville theory** guarantees that eigenfunctions form a complete orthogonal basis, that eigenvalues are real and can be ordered, and that eigenfunctions have predictable nodal properties.

This article develops Sturm-Liouville theory from the ground up. We'll derive the canonical form, understand the role of the weight function $w(x)$, prove orthogonality of eigenfunctions, explore different types of boundary conditions, and discover why this theory is so powerful. These results provide the theoretical foundation for separation of variables and eigenfunction expansions.

## Sturm-Liouville Form

### Example 1: Standard Sturm-Liouville Problem

**General form**:
$$\frac{d}{dx}\left[p(x)\frac{dy}{dx}\right] + [q(x) + \lambda w(x)]y = 0$$

on interval $[a, b]$ with boundary conditions.

**Solution:**

**Functions**:
- $p(x) > 0$: coefficient function
- $q(x)$: potential function
- $w(x) > 0$: weight function
- $\lambda$: eigenvalue parameter

**Boundary conditions** (various types):
- Dirichlet: $y(a) = 0$, $y(b) = 0$
- Neumann: $y'(a) = 0$, $y'(b) = 0$
- Robin: $\alpha y(a) + \beta y'(a) = 0$, etc.
- Periodic: $y(a) = y(b)$, $y'(a) = y'(b)$

**Answer:** $\frac{d}{dx}[p(x)y'] + [q(x) + \lambda w(x)]y = 0$ with BCs

### Example 2: Converting to Sturm-Liouville Form

Convert $y'' + \lambda y = 0$ to Sturm-Liouville form.

**Solution:**

Already in form! 

$$\frac{d}{dx}[1 \cdot y'] + [0 + \lambda \cdot 1]y = 0$$

**Identify**: $p(x) = 1$, $q(x) = 0$, $w(x) = 1$

**Simplest case**: Constant coefficients.

With $y(0) = y(L) = 0$:
- Eigenvalues: $\lambda_n = (n\pi/L)^2$
- Eigenfunctions: $y_n = \sin(n\pi x/L)$

**Answer:** Already Sturm-Liouville with $p=1$, $q=0$, $w=1$

### Example 3: Bessel's Equation

$$x^2y'' + xy' + (x^2 - n^2)y = 0$$

Convert to Sturm-Liouville form.

**Solution:**

Rewrite:
$$xy'' + y' + \left(x - \frac{n^2}{x}\right)y = 0$$

Multiply by $1/x$:
$$y'' + \frac{1}{x}y' + \left(1 - \frac{n^2}{x^2}\right)y = 0$$

Factor derivative:
$$\frac{d}{dx}\left[x\frac{dy}{dx}\right] + \left(x - \frac{n^2}{x}\right)y = 0$$

With eigenvalue $\lambda$:
$$\frac{d}{dx}\left[x\frac{dy}{dx}\right] + \left[\frac{-n^2}{x} + \lambda x\right]y = 0$$

**Sturm-Liouville form**: $p(x) = x$, $q(x) = -n^2/x$, $w(x) = x$

**Answer:** $p=x$, $q=-n^2/x$, $w=x$

### Example 4: Legendre's Equation

$$(1-x^2)y'' - 2xy' + n(n+1)y = 0$$

**Solution:**

Rewrite:
$$\frac{d}{dx}[(1-x^2)y'] + n(n+1)y = 0$$

Check:
$$\frac{d}{dx}[(1-x^2)y'] = -2xy' + (1-x^2)y''$$

Yes! Already in form.

**Sturm-Liouville**: $p(x) = 1-x^2$, $q(x) = 0$, $w(x) = 1$, $\lambda = n(n+1)$

**Domain**: $[-1, 1]$ where $p(-1) = p(1) = 0$ (singular endpoints)

**Answer:** $p=1-x^2$, $q=0$, $w=1$ (singular at endpoints)

## Orthogonality Theory

### Example 5: Inner Product with Weight

Define inner product:
$$\langle f, g \rangle = \int_a^b f(x)g(x)w(x) \, dx$$

**Solution:**

**Weight function** $w(x) > 0$ generalizes standard inner product.

**Why weighted?** Natural for Sturm-Liouville problems.

**Orthogonality**: $\langle y_m, y_n \rangle = 0$ for $m \neq n$

**Norm**: $\|y_n\|^2 = \int_a^b y_n^2(x)w(x) \, dx$

**Examples**:
- Standard: $w(x) = 1$
- Bessel: $w(x) = x$
- Chebyshev: $w(x) = 1/\sqrt{1-x^2}$

**Answer:** Weighted inner product $\langle f,g\rangle = \int fgw \, dx$

### Example 6: Proof of Orthogonality

Show eigenfunctions are orthogonal.

**Solution:**

Let $y_m$ and $y_n$ be eigenfunctions with eigenvalues $\lambda_m \neq \lambda_n$.

$$\frac{d}{dx}[py_m'] + (q + \lambda_m w)y_m = 0$$
$$\frac{d}{dx}[py_n'] + (q + \lambda_n w)y_n = 0$$

Multiply first by $y_n$, second by $y_m$, subtract:
$$y_n\frac{d}{dx}[py_m'] - y_m\frac{d}{dx}[py_n'] + (\lambda_m - \lambda_n)wy_my_n = 0$$

Left side is exact derivative:
$$\frac{d}{dx}[p(y_ny_m' - y_my_n')] = (\lambda_n - \lambda_m)wy_my_n$$

Integrate over $[a, b]$:
$$\left[p(y_ny_m' - y_my_n')\right]_a^b = (\lambda_n - \lambda_m)\int_a^b wy_my_n \, dx$$

**Boundary terms vanish** for separated BCs!

$$(\lambda_n - \lambda_m)\int_a^b wy_my_n \, dx = 0$$

Since $\lambda_n \neq \lambda_m$: $\int wy_my_n = 0$

**Answer:** $\langle y_m, y_n \rangle = 0$ for $m \neq n$

### Example 7: Reality of Eigenvalues

Show eigenvalues are real for self-adjoint problems.

**Solution:**

Suppose $\lambda$ is complex eigenvalue with eigenfunction $y$.

Take complex conjugate of Sturm-Liouville equation:
$$\frac{d}{dx}[p\bar{y}'] + (q + \bar{\lambda} w)\bar{y} = 0$$

From Example 6 technique (using $y$ and $\bar{y}$):
$$(\lambda - \bar{\lambda})\int_a^b w|y|^2 dx = 0$$

Since $\int w|y|^2 > 0$ (non-trivial $y$):
$$\lambda = \bar{\lambda}$$

Therefore $\lambda$ is real!

**Answer:** All eigenvalues are real for self-adjoint S-L problems

## Types of Boundary Conditions

### Example 8: Separated Boundary Conditions

Most common: conditions at $a$ and $b$ are independent.

**Solution:**

**At $x = a$**: $\alpha_1 y(a) + \alpha_2 y'(a) = 0$

**At $x = b$**: $\beta_1 y(b) + \beta_2 y'(b) = 0$

**Examples**:
- Dirichlet: $y(a) = y(b) = 0$
- Neumann: $y'(a) = y'(b) = 0$
- Robin: $y'(a) - hy(a) = 0$, $y'(b) + hy(b) = 0$

**Self-adjoint**: Boundary terms in integration by parts vanish.

**Guarantee orthogonality** of eigenfunctions.

**Answer:** Separated BCs ensure self-adjointness and orthogonality

### Example 9: Periodic Boundary Conditions

$$y(a) = y(b), \quad y'(a) = y'(b)$$

**Solution:**

**Not separated** but still self-adjoint!

Boundary term in integration by parts:
$$\left[p(y_ny_m' - y_my_n')\right]_a^b = p(b)[y_n(b)y_m'(b) - y_m(b)y_n'(b)] - p(a)[y_n(a)y_m'(a) - y_m(a)y_n'(a)]$$

If $p(a) = p(b)$ and $y, y'$ periodic: terms cancel!

**Example**: Fourier series on $[0, 2\pi]$ with $p \equiv 1$.

**Answer:** Periodic BCs self-adjoint if $p(a) = p(b)$

### Example 10: Mixed Boundary Conditions

$y(0) = 0$, $y'(L) = 0$ (one Dirichlet, one Neumann)

**Solution:**

For $y'' + \lambda y = 0$:

$$y(x) = A\sin(\sqrt{\lambda}x) + B\cos(\sqrt{\lambda}x)$$

From $y(0) = 0$: $B = 0$

From $y'(L) = 0$: $\sqrt{\lambda}\cos(\sqrt{\lambda}L) = 0$

$$\sqrt{\lambda}L = (n + 1/2)\pi$$

$$\lambda_n = \left[\frac{(2n+1)\pi}{2L}\right]^2, \quad n = 0, 1, 2, \ldots$$

**Eigenfunctions**: $y_n = \sin\left(\frac{(2n+1)\pi x}{2L}\right)$

**Different spectrum** from Dirichlet-Dirichlet!

**Answer:** $\lambda_n = [(2n+1)\pi/(2L)]^2$, eigenfunctions $\sin[(2n+1)\pi x/(2L)]$

## Sturm Comparison and Oscillation Theorems

### Example 11: Ordering of Eigenvalues

Eigenvalues can be ordered: $\lambda_1 < \lambda_2 < \lambda_3 < \cdots$

**Solution:**

**Theorem**: For regular Sturm-Liouville problem with separated BCs:
- Infinitely many eigenvalues
- $\lambda_1 < \lambda_2 < \lambda_3 < \cdots \to \infty$
- $n$-th eigenfunction has exactly $n-1$ zeros in $(a, b)$

**Example**: $y'' + \lambda y = 0$ on $[0, L]$, $y(0) = y(L) = 0$
- $\lambda_1 = (\pi/L)^2$: $\sin(\pi x/L)$ has 0 interior zeros
- $\lambda_2 = (2\pi/L)^2$: $\sin(2\pi x/L)$ has 1 zero at $x = L/2$
- $\lambda_n = (n\pi/L)^2$: $\sin(n\pi x/L)$ has $n-1$ zeros

**Answer:** $\lambda_1 < \lambda_2 < \cdots$, $n$-th eigenfunction has $n-1$ nodes

### Example 12: Rayleigh Quotient

Characterize eigenvalues variationally.

**Solution:**

**Rayleigh quotient**:
$$R[y] = \frac{-\int_a^b [py'^2 - qy^2] dx}{\int_a^b wy^2 dx}$$

**Minimum principle**: $\lambda_1 = \min R[y]$ over admissible $y$.

The minimizer is $y_1$ (first eigenfunction).

**Physical**: Lowest eigenvalue minimizes "energy" functional.

**Example**: Vibrating string—fundamental mode has lowest frequency.

**Answer:** First eigenvalue minimizes Rayleigh quotient

### Example 13: Singular Sturm-Liouville Problems

What if $p(a) = 0$ or interval is infinite?

**Solution:**

**Singular** if:
- $p(a) = 0$ or $p(b) = 0$ (coefficient vanishes)
- Interval is $[a, \infty)$ or $(-\infty, b)$ or $(-\infty, \infty)$
- $w(x)$ or $q(x)$ unbounded

**Examples**:
- Bessel: $p(0) = 0$
- Legendre: $p(\pm 1) = 0$
- Hermite: interval $(-\infty, \infty)$, $w(x) = e^{-x^2}$

**Boundary conditions**: Require boundedness at singular points.

**Spectrum**: May have continuous spectrum in addition to discrete.

**Answer:** Singular problems have modified boundary conditions and possibly continuous spectrum

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: B**
Standard Sturm-Liouville form: $(py')' + (q + \lambda w)y = 0$
Also written: $\frac{d}{dx}[p(x)y'] + [q(x) + \lambda w(x)]y = 0$
$p(x) > 0$: coefficient function
$w(x) > 0$: weight function
$q(x)$: potential; $\lambda$: eigenvalue parameter

**Problem 2: B**
Orthogonality: $\int_a^b y_m(x)y_n(x)w(x) dx = 0$ for $m \neq n$
Weight function $w(x)$ from S-L equation
Different problems → different weights
Bessel: $w(x) = x$; Legendre: $w(x) = 1$
Weighted $L^2$ space

**Problem 3: C**
Regular S-L with separated BCs:
All eigenvalues real (from self-adjointness)
Can be ordered: $\lambda_1 < \lambda_2 < \lambda_3 < \cdots$
$\lambda_n \to \infty$ as $n \to \infty$
Smallest eigenvalue $\lambda_1$ often most important
Complex eigenvalues impossible for self-adjoint

**Problem 4: B**
Oscillation theorem: $n$-th eigenfunction has $n-1$ zeros in $(a,b)$
First eigenfunction: no interior zeros
Second: one zero; third: two zeros
Example: $\sin(n\pi x/L)$ has $n-1$ zeros in $(0,L)$
Nodes separate as $n$ increases

**Problem 5: B**
Rayleigh quotient: $R[y] = \frac{-\int[py'^2 - qy^2]dx}{\int wy^2 dx}$
Minimum over admissible functions gives $\lambda_1$
Minimizer is first eigenfunction $y_1$
Variational characterization of eigenvalues
Useful for approximations and bounds
