# Partial Differential Equations Topic 1.2: Classification of Second-Order PDEs

The three classical PDEs—heat, wave, and Laplace's equations—seem very different physically, yet they share a common mathematical structure. All are second-order linear PDEs, but they belong to different **types**: the heat equation is parabolic, the wave equation is hyperbolic, and Laplace's equation is elliptic. This classification isn't just abstract mathematics—it determines the equation's physical character, what boundary and initial conditions are appropriate, and which solution techniques work.

Parabolic equations describe diffusion processes where information spreads infinitely fast (heat instantly affects the entire domain, though weakly). Hyperbolic equations describe wave propagation with finite speed (disturbances travel along characteristics). Elliptic equations describe equilibrium states where the solution at each point depends on the entire boundary (no time evolution, purely spatial).

Understanding PDE classification lets you predict solution behavior before solving. It tells you whether solutions will smooth out discontinuities (parabolic), propagate them along characteristics (hyperbolic), or be infinitely smooth everywhere (elliptic). This article develops the classification theory for second-order PDEs and connects mathematical type to physical behavior.

## General Second-Order Linear PDE

The most general second-order linear PDE in two variables is:

$$Au_{xx} + Bu_{xy} + Cu_{yy} + Du_x + Eu_y + Fu = G$$

where $A, B, C, D, E, F, G$ can be functions of $x$ and $y$ (but not $u$).

**Classification at a point $(x_0, y_0)$**: Compute discriminant

$$\Delta = B^2 - 4AC$$

- **Elliptic** if $\Delta < 0$ (like $x^2 + y^2 = r^2$, an ellipse)
- **Parabolic** if $\Delta = 0$ (like $y = x^2$, a parabola)
- **Hyperbolic** if $\Delta > 0$ (like $x^2 - y^2 = c$, a hyperbola)

### Example 1: Classifying Laplace's Equation

Classify $u_{xx} + u_{yy} = 0$.

**Solution:**

$$A = 1, \quad B = 0, \quad C = 1$$

$$\Delta = 0^2 - 4(1)(1) = -4 < 0$$

**Answer:** Elliptic everywhere

### Example 2: Classifying the Heat Equation

The heat equation is often written $u_t = ku_{xx}$. To classify, rewrite with all terms on one side:

$$ku_{xx} - u_t = 0$$

Treating $x$ and $t$ as the two variables:

**Solution:**

$$A = k, \quad B = 0, \quad C = 0$$ (no $u_{tt}$ term)

$$\Delta = 0^2 - 4(k)(0) = 0$$

**Answer:** Parabolic everywhere

### Example 3: Classifying the Wave Equation

Classify $u_{tt} - c^2u_{xx} = 0$.

**Solution:**

Rearrange: $-c^2u_{xx} + u_{tt} = 0$

With $x, t$ as variables:
$$A = -c^2, \quad B = 0, \quad C = 1$$

$$\Delta = 0^2 - 4(-c^2)(1) = 4c^2 > 0$$

**Answer:** Hyperbolic everywhere

### Example 4: Variable Coefficients

Classify $x^2u_{xx} + y^2u_{yy} = 0$.

**Solution:**

$$A = x^2, \quad B = 0, \quad C = y^2$$

$$\Delta = 0 - 4(x^2)(y^2) = -4x^2y^2$$

For $(x, y) \neq (0, 0)$: $\Delta < 0$ (elliptic)

At origin: $\Delta = 0$ (degenerate)

**Answer:** Elliptic except at origin

### Example 5: Mixed Derivative Term

Classify $u_{xx} - 2u_{xy} + u_{yy} = 0$.

**Solution:**

$$A = 1, \quad B = -2, \quad C = 1$$

$$\Delta = (-2)^2 - 4(1)(1) = 4 - 4 = 0$$

**Answer:** Parabolic everywhere

## Canonical Forms

Each PDE type can be transformed to a simpler **canonical form** by change of variables:

**Elliptic**: $u_{\xi\xi} + u_{\eta\eta} = \cdots$ (Laplace-like)

**Parabolic**: $u_{\eta} = u_{\xi\xi} + \cdots$ (heat-like)

**Hyperbolic**: $u_{\xi\eta} = \cdots$ or $u_{\xi\xi} - u_{\eta\eta} = \cdots$ (wave-like)

### Example 6: Canonical Form of Hyperbolic PDE

The canonical form $u_{\xi\eta} = 0$ has general solution:

**Solution:**

Integrate with respect to $\xi$:
$$u_\eta = f(\eta)$$ (arbitrary function of $\eta$ only)

Integrate with respect to $\eta$:
$$u(\xi, \eta) = F(\eta) + G(\xi)$$

where $F' = f$ and $G$ is arbitrary.

**Answer:** $u(\xi, \eta) = F(\eta) + G(\xi)$ (sum of functions of single variables)

### Example 7: Transforming to Canonical Form

For $u_{xx} - u_{yy} = 0$, use transformation $\xi = x + y$, $\eta = x - y$.

**Solution:**

Chain rule:
$$u_x = u_\xi + u_\eta$$
$$u_y = u_\xi - u_\eta$$

$$u_{xx} = (u_\xi + u_\eta)_x = u_{\xi\xi} + 2u_{\xi\eta} + u_{\eta\eta}$$
$$u_{yy} = (u_\xi - u_\eta)_y = u_{\xi\xi} - 2u_{\xi\eta} + u_{\eta\eta}$$

$$u_{xx} - u_{yy} = 4u_{\xi\eta} = 0$$

Canonical form: $u_{\xi\eta} = 0$

**Answer:** Transforms to $u_{\xi\eta} = 0$

## Physical Interpretation of Classification

### Example 8: Elliptic PDEs

Elliptic PDEs like $\nabla^2 u = 0$ describe:

- Steady-state temperature (equilibrium)
- Electric potential in free space
- Incompressible fluid flow

**Key property**: Solutions are **infinitely smooth** (analytic) in interior if data is smooth.

**Answer:** Describe equilibrium; smooth solutions

### Example 9: Parabolic PDEs

Parabolic PDEs like $u_t = ku_{xx}$ describe:

- Heat diffusion
- Concentration spreading
- Option pricing (Black-Scholes)

**Key property**: **Infinite propagation speed** (information spreads instantly, though exponentially weakly).

**Answer:** Describe diffusion; smooth discontinuities instantly

### Example 10: Hyperbolic PDEs

Hyperbolic PDEs like $u_{tt} = c^2u_{xx}$ describe:

- Wave propagation
- Sound and light
- Vibrating strings

**Key property**: **Finite propagation speed** $c$ (causality preserved; disturbances travel along characteristics).

**Answer:** Describe waves; finite propagation speed

## Characteristic Curves

For hyperbolic PDEs, **characteristics** are curves along which information propagates.

For $Au_{xx} + Bu_{xy} + Cu_{yy} = 0$, characteristics satisfy:

$$A\left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right)^2 - B\frac{dy}{dx} + C = 0$$

### Example 11: Characteristics of Wave Equation

Find characteristics of $u_{tt} - c^2u_{xx} = 0$.

**Solution:**

With $A = -c^2$, $B = 0$, $C = 1$ (treating $t$ like $y$):

$$-c^2\left(\frac{dt}{dx}\right)^2 + 1 = 0$$

$$\left(\frac{dt}{dx}\right)^2 = \frac{1}{c^2}$$

$$\frac{dt}{dx} = \pm\frac{1}{c}$$

Integrating: $t = \pm\frac{x}{c} + \text{const}$

Characteristics: $x - ct = \text{const}$ and $x + ct = \text{const}$

**Answer:** Two families of straight lines with slopes $\pm c$

### Example 12: Domain of Dependence

For wave equation with initial data at $t = 0$, the solution $u(x_0, t_0)$ depends only on data in the interval $[x_0 - ct_0, x_0 + ct_0]$.

Why?

**Solution:**

Information propagates at speed $c$ along characteristics.

From point $(x_0, t_0)$, trace characteristics backward:
- $x - ct = x_0 - ct_0$ (left characteristic)
- $x + ct = x_0 + ct_0$ (right characteristic)

These intersect $t = 0$ at $x_0 - ct_0$ and $x_0 + ct_0$.

**Answer:** Finite propagation speed limits domain of dependence

### Example 13: Elliptic vs. Hyperbolic

Why does Laplace's equation (elliptic) have no characteristics, while wave equation (hyperbolic) has two families?

**Solution:**

For characteristics: $A\left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right)^2 - B\frac{dy}{dx} + C = 0$

**Laplace**: $1 \cdot \left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right)^2 + 1 = 0$

No real solutions (would need $\frac{dy}{dx} = \pm i$).

**Wave**: Has real characteristic slopes $\pm c$.

**Physical meaning**: 
- Elliptic: solution at each point depends on all boundaries (no preferred direction)
- Hyperbolic: information travels along characteristics (causal structure)

**Answer:** Elliptic has no real characteristics (information from all directions); hyperbolic has real characteristics (directional propagation)

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: B**
Discriminant: $\Delta = B^2 - 4AC$
Analogous to quadratic formula discriminant
Determines PDE type:
- $\Delta < 0$: elliptic
- $\Delta = 0$: parabolic
- $\Delta > 0$: hyperbolic

**Problem 2: B**
$A = 1, B = 4, C = 4$
$\Delta = 4^2 - 4(1)(4) = 16 - 16 = 0$
Parabolic (discriminant = 0)
Can be factored: $(u_x + 2u_y)_x + 2(u_x + 2u_y)_y = 0$

**Problem 3: C**
Elliptic PDEs: $\Delta < 0$
Laplace's equation: $\nabla^2 u = 0$
Steady-state (no time dependence)
Electric potential, gravitational field, equilibrium temperature
Solutions depend on all boundaries

**Problem 4: C**
Wave equation: $u_{tt} = c^2u_{xx}$ (hyperbolic, $\Delta > 0$)
Two characteristic families: $x \pm ct = \text{const}$
Information propagates along characteristics
Finite propagation speed $c$
Contrast with elliptic (no real characteristics)

**Problem 5: B**
Parabolic PDEs (heat equation): infinite propagation speed
Any temperature change instantly affects entire domain
Effects exponentially small at distance
Physical: diffusion has no maximum speed
Mathematical: smooths discontinuities immediately
