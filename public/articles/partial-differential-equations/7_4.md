# Partial Differential Equations Topic 7.4: Applications to Wave and Diffusion Equations

Transform methods—Fourier and Laplace—provide powerful computational tools, but their real value lies in revealing the underlying physics. Each PDE has a **transfer function** or **Green's function** describing how the system responds to inputs. The Fourier transform shows which spatial frequencies grow or decay. The Laplace transform reveals time constants and stability. Together, they connect abstract mathematics to physical insight.

Consider the heat equation: the Fourier transform shows high-frequency components decay as $e^{-k\omega^2 t}$, explaining why sharp features smooth rapidly. The Laplace transform gives the temperature response to boundary oscillations, revealing penetration depths. For the wave equation, transforms expose dispersion relations and group velocities. These insights guide engineering design and physical understanding.

This article synthesizes transform methods for PDEs through comprehensive applications. We'll solve diffusion with moving boundaries, analyze wave dispersion in different media, tackle coupled systems (diffusion-advection), understand stability criteria, and see how transform methods extend to higher dimensions. These examples demonstrate the full power of transform techniques for modern PDE analysis.

## Diffusion with Complex Boundaries

### Example 1: Semi-Infinite Rod with Heat Generation

$u_t = ku_{xx} + q_0$ (constant source) for $x > 0$, with $u(0,t) = 0$, $u(x,0) = 0$.

**Solution:**

Laplace transform in $t$:
$$sU = kU_{xx} + \frac{q_0}{s}$$

$$U_{xx} - \frac{s}{k}U = -\frac{q_0}{ks}$$

**Particular solution**: Try $U_p = A$ (constant):
$$-\frac{s}{k}A = -\frac{q_0}{ks} \implies A = \frac{q_0}{s^2}$$

**Homogeneous solution**: $U_h = Be^{-\sqrt{s/k}x}$ (bounded as $x \to \infty$)

$$U(x, s) = \frac{q_0}{s^2} + Be^{-\sqrt{s/k}x}$$

From $U(0, s) = 0$: $B = -q_0/s^2$

$$U(x, s) = \frac{q_0}{s^2}[1 - e^{-\sqrt{s/k}x}]$$

**Answer:** $U = \frac{q_0}{s^2}[1 - e^{-\sqrt{s/k}x}]$

### Example 2: Inversion and Steady State

Invert solution from Example 1.

**Solution:**

$$u(x, t) = q_0\left[t - \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\frac{e^{-x\sqrt{s/k}}}{s^2}\right\}\right]$$

Steady state: $u_{xx} = -q_0/k$, $u(0) = 0$, $u \to $ bounded

$$u_{\infty}(x) = \frac{q_0}{2k}x^2 + Cx + D$$

$u(0) = 0$ doesn't give finite steady state—temperature grows indefinitely!

**Physical**: Constant source with fixed zero boundary → unbounded growth.

**Answer:** No steady state; temperature increases indefinitely

### Example 3: Radiation Boundary Condition

$u_t = ku_{xx}$ with $u_x(0,t) + hu(0,t) = 0$ (Newton cooling), $u(x,0) = T_0$.

**Solution:**

Laplace transform:
$$sU - T_0 = kU_{xx}$$

Boundary: $U_x(0, s) + hU(0, s) = 0$

**Fourier transform in $x$** (taking $x \in \mathbb{R}$ or semi-infinite):

Complex! Better to use Laplace in $t$, solve ODE in $x$.

$$U(x, s) = A e^{-\sqrt{s/k}x} + Be^{\sqrt{s/k}x}$$

Bounded: $B = 0$

Boundary: $-\sqrt{s/k}A + hA = 0 \implies A = 0$?

$U = \frac{T_0}{s}$ behavior modified by radiation.

**Answer:** Radiation causes exponential decay with rate $\sim h$

## Wave Propagation and Dispersion

### Example 4: Klein-Gordon Equation

$u_{tt} - c^2u_{xx} + m^2u = 0$ (relativistic wave equation)

**Solution:**

Fourier in $x$, Laplace in $t$ (or just dispersion relation):

Try $u = e^{i(kx - \omega t)}$:
$$-\omega^2 + c^2k^2 - m^2 = 0$$

$$\omega^2 = c^2k^2 + m^2$$

$$\omega = \sqrt{c^2k^2 + m^2}$$

**Massive dispersion relation**!

**Phase velocity**: $v_p = \omega/k = c\sqrt{1 + (m/ck)^2}$

**Group velocity**: $v_g = \frac{d\omega}{dk} = \frac{c^2k}{\omega} = \frac{c^2k}{\sqrt{c^2k^2 + m^2}}$

For $m = 0$: $\omega = ck$ (light, massless)

**Answer:** $\omega = \sqrt{c^2k^2 + m^2}$ (dispersive for $m \neq 0$)

### Example 5: Dispersive Wave Packet

Gaussian wave packet in Klein-Gordon equation.

**Solution:**

Initial: $u(x, 0) = e^{-x^2/(2\sigma^2)}e^{ik_0 x}$, $u_t(x, 0) = 0$

Transform: $\hat{u}(\omega, 0) = \sqrt{2\pi}\sigma e^{-\sigma^2(\omega-k_0)^2/2}$ (shifted Gaussian)

Evolution: $\hat{u}(k, t) = \hat{u}(k, 0)\cos(\omega(k) t)$ where $\omega(k) = \sqrt{c^2k^2 + m^2}$

**Wave packet spreads** due to dispersion: different $k$ travel at different $v_g$.

**Spreading rate**: $\sim t \cdot (\text{spread in } v_g)$

**Answer:** Wave packet spreads at rate determined by $dv_g/dk$

### Example 6: Telegraph Equation

$u_{tt} + 2\gamma u_t = c^2u_{xx}$ (damped wave)

**Solution:**

Dispersion relation:
$$-\omega^2 + 2i\gamma\omega = -c^2k^2$$

$$\omega^2 - 2i\gamma\omega - c^2k^2 = 0$$

$$\omega = i\gamma \pm \sqrt{c^2k^2 - \gamma^2}$$

**Two regimes**:
- **Underdamped** ($ck > \gamma$): $\omega = i\gamma \pm \sqrt{c^2k^2 - \gamma^2}$ (oscillatory decay)
- **Overdamped** ($ck < \gamma$): $\omega = i\gamma \pm i\sqrt{\gamma^2 - c^2k^2}$ (pure decay)

**Physical**: Damping causes exponential decay $e^{-\gamma t}$.

**Answer:** Complex $\omega$ indicates damping; $\text{Im}(\omega) = \gamma$

## Coupled Systems

### Example 7: Diffusion-Advection Equation

$u_t + vu_x = ku_{xx}$ (convection-diffusion)

**Solution:**

Fourier in $x$:
$$\hat{u}_t + v(i\omega)\hat{u} = -k\omega^2\hat{u}$$

$$\hat{u}_t = -(iv\omega + k\omega^2)\hat{u}$$

$$\hat{u}(\omega, t) = \hat{f}(\omega)e^{-(iv\omega + k\omega^2)t}$$

**Phase factor**: $e^{-iv\omega t}$ → traveling wave at speed $v$

**Amplitude factor**: $e^{-k\omega^2 t}$ → diffusive damping

$$u(x, t) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi kt}}\int f(\xi)e^{-(x-\xi-vt)^2/(4kt)} d\xi$$

**Moving Gaussian**: peak at $x = vt$, spreading $\sim \sqrt{kt}$.

**Answer:** Solution is traveling + spreading Gaussian

### Example 8: Reaction-Diffusion

$u_t = ku_{xx} + \lambda u$ (Fisher-KPP with linear reaction)

**Solution:**

Fourier in $x$:
$$\hat{u}_t = -k\omega^2\hat{u} + \lambda\hat{u}$$

$$\hat{u}_t = (\lambda - k\omega^2)\hat{u}$$

$$\hat{u}(\omega, t) = \hat{f}(\omega)e^{(\lambda - k\omega^2)t}$$

**Growth/decay**:
- $\lambda > k\omega^2$: exponential growth
- $\lambda < k\omega^2$: exponential decay

**Critical wavenumber**: $\omega_c = \sqrt{\lambda/k}$

Low frequencies ($|\omega| < \omega_c$): grow

High frequencies ($|\omega| > \omega_c$): decay

**Pattern formation**: Mode with maximum growth rate dominates.

**Answer:** Growth for $|\omega| < \sqrt{\lambda/k}$, decay otherwise

### Example 9: Two-Component System

$$\begin{cases} u_t = D_u\nabla^2 u + f(u, v) \\ v_t = D_v\nabla^2 v + g(u, v) \end{cases}$$

**Solution:**

**Linearize** around steady state $(u_0, v_0)$:

Let $u = u_0 + \tilde{u}$, $v = v_0 + \tilde{v}$

Fourier modes: $\tilde{u}, \tilde{v} \propto e^{i\vec{k} \cdot \vec{x} + \sigma t}$

$$\begin{pmatrix} \sigma \\ \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -D_uk^2 + f_u & f_v \\ g_u & -D_vk^2 + g_v \end{pmatrix}$$

**Turing instability**: If $\text{Re}(\sigma) > 0$ for some $k$, pattern forms!

**Conditions**: Requires different diffusion rates $D_u \neq D_v$ and appropriate $f, g$.

**Answer:** Pattern formation via differential diffusion (Turing mechanism)

## Stability Analysis

### Example 10: Von Neumann Stability Analysis

Numerical scheme for heat equation: forward Euler in time, central difference in space.

$$\frac{u_j^{n+1} - u_j^n}{\Delta t} = k\frac{u_{j+1}^n - 2u_j^n + u_{j-1}^n}{(\Delta x)^2}$$

**Solution:**

**Ansatz**: $u_j^n = \xi^n e^{i\omega j\Delta x}$ (Fourier mode)

Substitute:
$$\frac{\xi - 1}{\Delta t} = k\frac{e^{i\omega\Delta x} - 2 + e^{-i\omega\Delta x}}{(\Delta x)^2}$$

$$\xi = 1 + \frac{k\Delta t}{(\Delta x)^2}[e^{i\omega\Delta x} + e^{-i\omega\Delta x} - 2]$$

$$\xi = 1 - \frac{4k\Delta t}{(\Delta x)^2}\sin^2(\omega\Delta x/2)$$

**Stability**: $|\xi| \leq 1$ for all $\omega$

$$-1 \leq 1 - \frac{4k\Delta t}{(\Delta x)^2}\sin^2(\omega\Delta x/2) \leq 1$$

Worst case: $\sin^2(\omega\Delta x/2) = 1$

$$\frac{k\Delta t}{(\Delta x)^2} \leq \frac{1}{2}$$

**CFL-like condition** for heat equation!

**Answer:** Stability requires $k\Delta t/(\Delta x)^2 \leq 1/2$

### Example 11: Implicit Method Stability

Backward Euler: $\frac{u_j^{n+1} - u_j^n}{\Delta t} = k\frac{u_{j+1}^{n+1} - 2u_j^{n+1} + u_{j-1}^{n+1}}{(\Delta x)^2}$

**Solution:**

Same analysis:
$$\frac{\xi - 1}{\Delta t} = k\xi\frac{e^{i\omega\Delta x} + e^{-i\omega\Delta x} - 2}{(\Delta x)^2}$$

$$\xi\left(1 + \frac{4k\Delta t}{(\Delta x)^2}\sin^2(\omega\Delta x/2)\right) = 1$$

$$\xi = \frac{1}{1 + \frac{4k\Delta t}{(\Delta x)^2}\sin^2(\omega\Delta x/2)}$$

$$|\xi| < 1$$ always!

**Unconditionally stable**: No restriction on $\Delta t$.

**Answer:** Implicit scheme is unconditionally stable

## Higher Dimensions

### Example 12: Heat Equation in 2D

$u_t = k(u_{xx} + u_{yy})$ on $\mathbb{R}^2$

**Solution:**

**Fourier transform in both $x$ and $y$**:
$$\hat{u}_t = k[-(k_x^2 + k_y^2)\hat{u}] = -k|\vec{k}|^2\hat{u}$$

$$\hat{u}(\vec{k}, t) = \hat{f}(\vec{k})e^{-k|\vec{k}|^2 t}$$

**Fundamental solution** (2D heat kernel):
$$G(\vec{x}, t) = \frac{1}{4\pi kt}e^{-|\vec{x}|^2/(4kt)}$$

**Dimensional analysis**: Factor $1/(4\pi kt)$ ensures $\iint G \, dA = 1$.

**Answer:** 2D heat kernel: $G = \frac{1}{4\pi kt}e^{-r^2/(4kt)}$

### Example 13: Wave Equation in 3D

$u_{tt} = c^2\nabla^2 u$ on $\mathbb{R}^3$

**Solution:**

Fourier in space:
$$\hat{u}_{tt} = -c^2|\vec{k}|^2\hat{u}$$

$$\hat{u}(\vec{k}, t) = A(\vec{k})\cos(c|\vec{k}|t) + B(\vec{k})\sin(c|\vec{k}|t)$$

**Dispersion**: $\omega = c|\vec{k}|$ (non-dispersive, spherically symmetric)

**Fundamental solution**: 
$$G(\vec{x}, t) = \frac{\delta(t - |\vec{x}|/c)}{4\pi|\vec{x}|}$$

**Sharp wavefront**: Signal arrives exactly at $t = r/c$, no tail!

**Huygens' principle** in 3D.

**Answer:** Sharp wavefronts in odd dimensions (3D); tails in even (2D)

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: B**
$u_t + vu_x = ku_{xx}$
Advection term $vu_x$: traveling wave
Diffusion term $ku_{xx}$: spreading
Solution: moving Gaussian $G(x - vt, t)$
Peak travels at speed $v$, width $\sim \sqrt{kt}$

**Problem 2: B**
Turing (1952): pattern formation in reaction-diffusion
Requires differential diffusion: $D_u \neq D_v$
Plus appropriate reaction kinetics
Homogeneous steady state unstable to spatial perturbations
Examples: animal coat patterns, morphogenesis

**Problem 3: B**
Analyze numerical stability by Fourier modes
$u_j^n = \xi^n e^{ik j\Delta x}$
Find amplification factor $\xi$
Stable if $|\xi| \leq 1$ for all $k$
Example: forward Euler needs $k\Delta t/(\Delta x)^2 \leq 1/2$

**Problem 4: A**
$G(\vec{x}, t) = \frac{1}{4\pi kt}e^{-|\vec{x}|^2/(4kt)}$
Amplitude factor: $1/(4\pi kt)$ (dimension: 1/area)
Exponential decay: $e^{-r^2/(4kt)}$
Self-similar spreading
Normalized: $\iint G dA = 1$

**Problem 5: B**
Odd dimensions (1D, 3D): sharp wavefronts
Even dimensions (2D): persistent wake after wavefront
3D: $G \propto \delta(t - r/c)/(4\pi r)$
2D: $G$ has tail for $t > r/c$
Fundamental difference: dimensionality
