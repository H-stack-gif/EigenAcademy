# Partial Differential Equations Topic 3.2: Fourier Sine and Cosine Series

A string vibrates between two fixed points—only sine waves fit the boundary conditions. Heat diffuses through an insulated rod—only cosine waves satisfy zero-flux ends. The shape of the domain and boundary conditions determine which trigonometric functions are appropriate. **Half-range Fourier series**—sine-only or cosine-only expansions—arise naturally when solving PDEs on finite intervals.

The key insight: by extending a function defined on $[0, L]$ either oddly or evenly to $[-L, L]$, we can represent it using only sines or only cosines. An **odd extension** produces a sine series (all $a_n = 0$), while an **even extension** produces a cosine series (all $b_n = 0$). These aren't just mathematical tricks—they correspond directly to physical boundary conditions in PDE problems.

This article develops sine and cosine series systematically. We'll learn when to use each type, how to compute coefficients, understand the connection to boundary conditions, and see why certain PDEs naturally select one type over the other. Mastering half-range expansions is essential for separation of variables in the heat and wave equations.

## Even and Odd Functions

### Example 1: Even Function Properties

A function is **even** if $f(-x) = f(x)$.

**Solution:**

**Examples:** $\cos(x)$, $x^2$, $|x|$, $x^4 + 1$

**Properties:**
- Graph symmetric about $y$-axis
- $\int_{-a}^{a} f(x) \, dx = 2\int_{0}^{a} f(x) \, dx$
- Product of two even functions is even
- Sum of even functions is even

**Answer:** Even functions symmetric about vertical axis

### Example 2: Odd Function Properties

A function is **odd** if $f(-x) = -f(x)$.

**Solution:**

**Examples:** $\sin(x)$, $x$, $x^3$, $x^5 - x$

**Properties:**
- Graph has origin symmetry (180° rotation)
- $\int_{-a}^{a} f(x) \, dx = 0$ (cancellation)
- Product of odd and even is odd
- Product of two odd functions is even

**Answer:** Odd functions have origin symmetry

### Example 3: Product Rules

Classify products:

**Solution:**

- **Even × Even = Even**: $\cos x \cdot x^2$
- **Odd × Odd = Even**: $\sin x \cdot x$
- **Even × Odd = Odd**: $\cos x \cdot x$

**Integral implications:**
$$\int_{-L}^{L} (\text{odd}) \, dx = 0$$
$$\int_{-L}^{L} (\text{even}) \, dx = 2\int_{0}^{L} (\text{even}) \, dx$$

**Answer:** Even-even → even; odd-odd → even; even-odd → odd

## Fourier Cosine Series

For even function on $[-L, L]$:

$$f(x) = \frac{a_0}{2} + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n\cos\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right)$$

### Example 4: Why Only Cosines for Even Functions?

If $f(x)$ is even, why are all $b_n = 0$?

**Solution:**

$$b_n = \frac{1}{L}\int_{-L}^{L} f(x)\sin(n\pi x/L) \, dx$$

$f(x)$ is even, $\sin(n\pi x/L)$ is odd.

Product is **odd**, so integral over symmetric interval is zero.

Therefore all $b_n = 0$.

**Answer:** Even function times odd function integrates to zero

### Example 5: Cosine Series for $f(x) = x^2$ on $[0, \pi]$

Extend $f$ evenly to $[-\pi, \pi]$ and find cosine series.

**Solution:**

Even extension: $f(x) = x^2$ on $[-\pi, \pi]$ (already even).

$$a_0 = \frac{2}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi} x^2 \, dx = \frac{2}{\pi} \cdot \frac{\pi^3}{3} = \frac{2\pi^2}{3}$$

$$a_n = \frac{2}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi} x^2\cos(nx) \, dx$$

Integration by parts twice:
$$= \frac{2}{\pi}\left[\frac{x^2\sin(nx)}{n}\Big|_0^{\pi} - \frac{2x}{n}\int_0^{\pi} \sin(nx) \, dx\right]$$

$$= \frac{2}{\pi}\left[0 + \frac{2x\cos(nx)}{n^2}\Big|_0^{\pi} - \frac{2}{n^2}\int_0^{\pi} \cos(nx) \, dx\right]$$

$$= \frac{2}{\pi}\left[\frac{2\pi\cos(n\pi)}{n^2} - 0\right] = \frac{4\cos(n\pi)}{n^2} = \frac{4(-1)^n}{n^2}$$

Fourier cosine series:
$$x^2 = \frac{\pi^2}{3} + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{4(-1)^n}{n^2}\cos(nx)$$

**Answer:** $x^2 = \frac{\pi^2}{3} + 4\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n}{n^2}\cos(nx)$

### Example 6: Cosine Series for Constant

Find cosine series for $f(x) = 1$ on $[0, L]$.

**Solution:**

$$a_0 = \frac{2}{L}\int_{0}^{L} 1 \, dx = 2$$

$$a_n = \frac{2}{L}\int_{0}^{L} \cos(n\pi x/L) \, dx = \frac{2}{L} \cdot \frac{L}{n\pi}\sin(n\pi x/L)\Big|_0^{L} = 0$$

Series: $f(x) = 1$ (constant is its own cosine series).

**Answer:** $f(x) = 1$ (only $a_0$ term survives)

## Fourier Sine Series

For odd function on $[-L, L]$:

$$f(x) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} b_n\sin\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right)$$

### Example 7: Why Only Sines for Odd Functions?

If $f(x)$ is odd, why are all $a_n = 0$?

**Solution:**

$$a_n = \frac{1}{L}\int_{-L}^{L} f(x)\cos(n\pi x/L) \, dx$$

$f(x)$ is odd, $\cos(n\pi x/L)$ is even.

Product is **odd**, integral is zero.

Therefore all $a_n = 0$ (including $a_0$).

**Answer:** Odd function times even function integrates to zero

### Example 8: Sine Series for $f(x) = x$ on $[0, \pi]$

Extend $f$ oddly and find sine series.

**Solution:**

Odd extension: $f(x) = \begin{cases} x & 0 \leq x \leq \pi \\ -x & -\pi \leq x < 0 \end{cases}$

$$b_n = \frac{2}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi} x\sin(nx) \, dx$$

Integration by parts:
$$= \frac{2}{\pi}\left[-\frac{x\cos(nx)}{n}\Big|_0^{\pi} + \frac{1}{n}\int_0^{\pi} \cos(nx) \, dx\right]$$

$$= \frac{2}{\pi}\left[-\frac{\pi\cos(n\pi)}{n} + \frac{\sin(nx)}{n^2}\Big|_0^{\pi}\right]$$

$$= \frac{2}{\pi}\left[-\frac{\pi(-1)^n}{n} + 0\right] = \frac{-2(-1)^n}{n} = \frac{2(-1)^{n+1}}{n}$$

Fourier sine series:
$$x = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{2(-1)^{n+1}}{n}\sin(nx) = 2\left[\sin x - \frac{\sin 2x}{2} + \frac{\sin 3x}{3} - \cdots\right]$$

**Answer:** $x = 2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{n}\sin(nx)$

### Example 9: Sine Series for $f(x) = 1$ on $[0, L]$

Find sine series for constant function.

**Solution:**

Odd extension creates square wave.

$$b_n = \frac{2}{L}\int_{0}^{L} 1 \cdot \sin(n\pi x/L) \, dx$$

$$= \frac{2}{L} \cdot \left[-\frac{L\cos(n\pi x/L)}{n\pi}\right]_0^{L}$$

$$= \frac{2}{n\pi}[1 - \cos(n\pi)] = \frac{2}{n\pi}[1 - (-1)^n]$$

$$= \begin{cases} \frac{4}{n\pi} & n \text{ odd} \\ 0 & n \text{ even} \end{cases}$$

Series:
$$1 = \frac{4}{\pi}\left[\sin(\pi x/L) + \frac{\sin(3\pi x/L)}{3} + \frac{\sin(5\pi x/L)}{5} + \cdots\right]$$

**Answer:** $1 = \frac{4}{\pi}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{\sin((2k+1)\pi x/L)}{2k+1}$

## Connection to Boundary Conditions

### Example 10: Fixed-End String (Dirichlet)

String with $u(0, t) = u(L, t) = 0$: which series?

**Solution:**

Boundary conditions: $u = 0$ at both ends.

Sine functions satisfy: $\sin(0) = \sin(n\pi) = 0$.

Use **Fourier sine series**:
$$u(x, t) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} b_n(t)\sin(n\pi x/L)$$

**Answer:** Dirichlet (fixed-end) → sine series

### Example 11: Insulated-End Rod (Neumann)

Heat equation with $u_x(0, t) = u_x(L, t) = 0$: which series?

**Solution:**

Boundary conditions: $u_x = 0$ at both ends (no heat flux).

Cosine functions satisfy: $\frac{d}{dx}\cos(n\pi x/L)\Big|_{x=0,L} = 0$.

Use **Fourier cosine series**:
$$u(x, t) = \frac{a_0(t)}{2} + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n(t)\cos(n\pi x/L)$$

**Answer:** Neumann (insulated) → cosine series

### Example 12: Why Extension Matters

For $f(x) = x$ on $[0, 1]$, compare sine vs. cosine series at boundaries.

**Solution:**

**Sine series**: Forces $f(0) = f(1) = 0$ (odd extension).

**Cosine series**: Allows $f(0) = 0$, $f'(1) = 0$ (even extension).

**At $x = 0$**:
- Sine series: sum starts at 0
- Cosine series: $a_0/2 + a_1 + a_2 + \cdots$ (nonzero)

**Physical meaning**: Extension type encodes boundary conditions.

**Answer:** Sine series for fixed ends; cosine for insulated ends

### Example 13: Mixed Boundary Conditions

One end fixed, other free: which series?

**Solution:**

**Neither sine nor cosine alone works!**

Need generalized series with:
- $u(0, t) = 0$ (fixed)
- $u_x(L, t) = 0$ (free)

Eigenfunctions: $\sin(\lambda_n x)$ where $\lambda_n$ satisfy $\lambda_n L = (n - 1/2)\pi$.

More complex than standard sine/cosine series.

**Answer:** Mixed boundary conditions require modified eigenfunctions

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: B**
Even function: $f(-x) = f(x)$
All sine coefficients: $b_n = \int f(x)\sin(nx) dx = 0$
Product of even and odd is odd
Only cosine terms survive
Series: $f(x) = \frac{a_0}{2} + \sum a_n\cos(nx)$

**Problem 2: B**
Boundary conditions: $u(0) = u(L) = 0$
Sine functions: $\sin(n\pi \cdot 0/L) = 0$, $\sin(n\pi \cdot L/L) = \sin(n\pi) = 0$
Satisfy both boundary conditions naturally
Eigenfunctions for vibrating string
Example: guitar string modes

**Problem 3: B**
Odd function: $f(-x) = -f(x)$
Split integral: $\int_{-L}^{L} f = \int_{-L}^{0} f + \int_{0}^{L} f$
Substitute $x \to -x$ in first integral: $\int_{-L}^{0} f(x) dx = -\int_{0}^{L} f(x) dx$
Sum cancels: total integral is zero
Geometrically: equal areas above/below axis

**Problem 4: B**
Neumann boundary: $u_x = 0$ (no flux)
Cosine derivative: $\frac{d}{dx}\cos(n\pi x/L) = -\frac{n\pi}{L}\sin(n\pi x/L)$
At $x = 0$: $\sin(0) = 0$ ✓
At $x = L$: $\sin(n\pi) = 0$ ✓
Cosine series satisfies insulated boundaries
Example: insulated rod

**Problem 5: B**
$f(x) = 1$ constant, odd extension is square wave
$b_n = \frac{2}{n\pi}[1 - (-1)^n]$
For $n$ even: $(-1)^n = 1$, so $b_n = 0$
For $n$ odd: $(-1)^n = -1$, so $b_n = \frac{4}{n\pi}$
Only odd harmonics (half-wave symmetry)
