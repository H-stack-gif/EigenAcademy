# Partial Differential Equations Topic 4.2: Separation of Variables

The heat equation $u_t = ku_{xx}$ on a finite rod with fixed boundary temperatures seems impossibly complicated—a PDE in two variables with boundary and initial conditions. But there's an elegant trick: **separation of variables** assumes the solution has special form $u(x,t) = X(x)T(t)$, a product of functions depending on only one variable each. This seemingly restrictive assumption transforms the PDE into two ordinary differential equations, which we can solve using familiar ODE techniques.

The method works because the heat equation with homogeneous boundary conditions admits infinitely many product solutions—one for each integer $n$. These **normal modes** or **eigenfunctions** have different shapes ($\sin(n\pi x/L)$) and different decay rates (exponential with rate $n^2$). Higher modes (more wiggles) decay faster, while the slowest mode dominates long-term behavior. Any initial temperature distribution can be built from these building blocks using **Fourier series**, making the general solution a superposition of normal modes.

This article develops the separation of variables method systematically. We'll derive the eigenvalue problem, find normal modes, understand decay rates, and see why high-frequency components vanish quickly while low-frequency components persist. The technique reveals the deep structure underlying heat diffusion: an infinite hierarchy of exponentially decaying oscillations.

## The Method: Separating Variables

For $u_t = ku_{xx}$ with $u(0,t) = u(L,t) = 0$, assume:

$$u(x, t) = X(x)T(t)$$

**Goal**: Convert PDE into two ODEs.

### Example 1: Substituting Product Solution

Substitute $u = XT$ into $u_t = ku_{xx}$.

**Solution:**

$$u_t = X(x) T'(t)$$
$$u_{xx} = X''(x) T(t)$$

Heat equation:
$$XT' = kX''T$$

Divide both sides by $kXT$ (assuming $X, T \neq 0$):
$$\frac{T'}{kT} = \frac{X''}{X}$$

**Key observation**: Left side depends only on $t$, right side only on $x$. Both must equal same constant.

**Answer:** $\frac{T'}{kT} = \frac{X''}{X} = -\lambda$ (separation constant)

### Example 2: The Two ODEs

From $\frac{T'}{kT} = \frac{X''}{X} = -\lambda$:

**Solution:**

**Spatial ODE**: $X'' = -\lambda X$ or $X'' + \lambda X = 0$

**Temporal ODE**: $T' = -\lambda kT$ or $T' + \lambda kT = 0$

Now apply boundary conditions to $X(x)$.

**Answer:** $X'' + \lambda X = 0$ and $T' + \lambda kT = 0$

### Example 3: Boundary Conditions on $X$

From $u(0, t) = X(0)T(t) = 0$ and $u(L, t) = X(L)T(t) = 0$:

**Solution:**

Since $T(t) \not\equiv 0$ (otherwise $u \equiv 0$, trivial):

$$X(0) = 0, \quad X(L) = 0$$

This is a **Sturm-Liouville eigenvalue problem**:
- Find $\lambda$ and $X$ such that $X'' + \lambda X = 0$ with $X(0) = X(L) = 0$

**Answer:** $X(0) = 0$, $X(L) = 0$ (homogeneous boundary conditions)

## The Eigenvalue Problem

Solve $X'' + \lambda X = 0$ with $X(0) = X(L) = 0$.

Need to consider cases based on sign of $\lambda$.

### Example 4: Case $\lambda < 0$

Let $\lambda = -\mu^2$ where $\mu > 0$.

**Solution:**

$$X'' - \mu^2 X = 0$$

General solution: $X = c_1 e^{\mu x} + c_2 e^{-\mu x}$ (or $c_1\cosh(\mu x) + c_2\sinh(\mu x)$)

Boundary conditions:
- $X(0) = c_1 + c_2 = 0 \implies c_2 = -c_1$
- $X(L) = c_1(e^{\mu L} - e^{-\mu L}) = 0$

Since $e^{\mu L} \neq e^{-\mu L}$ for $\mu > 0$: $c_1 = 0$, thus $c_2 = 0$.

Only trivial solution $X \equiv 0$.

**Answer:** $\lambda < 0$ gives only trivial solution (not eigenvalue)

### Example 5: Case $\lambda = 0$

**Solution:**

$$X'' = 0$$

General solution: $X = c_1 x + c_2$

Boundary conditions:
- $X(0) = c_2 = 0$
- $X(L) = c_1 L = 0 \implies c_1 = 0$

Only trivial solution.

**Answer:** $\lambda = 0$ gives only trivial solution (not eigenvalue)

### Example 6: Case $\lambda > 0$

Let $\lambda = \mu^2$ where $\mu > 0$.

**Solution:**

$$X'' + \mu^2 X = 0$$

General solution: $X = c_1\cos(\mu x) + c_2\sin(\mu x)$

Boundary conditions:
- $X(0) = c_1 = 0$
- $X(L) = c_2\sin(\mu L) = 0$

For nontrivial solution ($c_2 \neq 0$): $\sin(\mu L) = 0$

Therefore: $\mu L = n\pi$ where $n = 1, 2, 3, \ldots$

$$\mu_n = \frac{n\pi}{L}$$

**Eigenvalues**: $\lambda_n = \mu_n^2 = \frac{n^2\pi^2}{L^2}$

**Eigenfunctions**: $X_n(x) = \sin\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right)$

**Answer:** $\lambda_n = \frac{n^2\pi^2}{L^2}$, $X_n = \sin(n\pi x/L)$ for $n = 1, 2, 3, \ldots$

### Example 7: Time-Dependent Solutions

For each eigenvalue $\lambda_n = \frac{n^2\pi^2}{L^2}$, solve $T' + \lambda_n kT = 0$.

**Solution:**

$$T' = -\lambda_n kT = -\frac{n^2\pi^2 k}{L^2}T$$

$$T_n(t) = c_n \exp\left(-\frac{n^2\pi^2 k}{L^2}t\right)$$

**Answer:** $T_n(t) = \exp(-n^2\pi^2 kt/L^2)$ (exponential decay)

### Example 8: Normal Modes

Combining spatial and temporal parts:

$$u_n(x, t) = \sin\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right) \exp\left(-\frac{n^2\pi^2 kt}{L^2}\right)$$

These are **normal modes** or **fundamental solutions**.

Verify $u_1$ satisfies heat equation.

**Solution:**

For $n = 1$:
$$u_1 = \sin(\pi x/L)e^{-\pi^2 kt/L^2}$$

$$(u_1)_t = \sin(\pi x/L) \cdot \left(-\frac{\pi^2 k}{L^2}\right)e^{-\pi^2 kt/L^2}$$

$$(u_1)_x = \frac{\pi}{L}\cos(\pi x/L)e^{-\pi^2 kt/L^2}$$

$$(u_1)_{xx} = -\frac{\pi^2}{L^2}\sin(\pi x/L)e^{-\pi^2 kt/L^2}$$

$$k(u_1)_{xx} = -\frac{\pi^2 k}{L^2}\sin(\pi x/L)e^{-\pi^2 kt/L^2} = (u_1)_t$$ ✓

**Answer:** Each $u_n$ satisfies heat equation and boundary conditions

## Properties of Normal Modes

### Example 9: Decay Rates

Compare decay rates of $u_1$ and $u_{10}$.

**Solution:**

**Mode $n = 1$**: decays as $e^{-\pi^2 kt/L^2}$

**Mode $n = 10$**: decays as $e^{-100\pi^2 kt/L^2}$

Higher modes decay $n^2$ times faster!

**Physical meaning**: Rapid oscillations (high $n$) smooth out quickly; slow variations persist.

After time $t \sim L^2/(k\pi^2)$, only lowest mode remains.

**Answer:** Mode $n$ decays as $e^{-n^2\pi^2 kt/L^2}$; higher modes vanish faster

### Example 10: Characteristic Time Scale

What is the **diffusion time** for rod of length $L$?

**Solution:**

From slowest mode ($n = 1$): $t_{\text{diff}} \sim \frac{L^2}{k\pi^2} \approx \frac{L^2}{k}$

This is time for significant decay of fundamental mode.

**Scaling**: $t \propto L^2$ (doubling length quadruples diffusion time)

**Answer:** $t_{\text{diff}} \sim L^2/k$

### Example 11: Shape of Normal Modes

Sketch $X_1$, $X_2$, $X_3$ for $L = 1$.

**Solution:**

$$X_1(x) = \sin(\pi x)$$ (one half-period, one "hump")

$$X_2(x) = \sin(2\pi x)$$ (two half-periods, two "humps")

$$X_3(x) = \sin(3\pi x)$$ (three half-periods, three "humps")

Each mode has $n$ zero-crossings in $(0, L)$.

**Answer:** Mode $n$ has $n$ spatial oscillations

### Example 12: Energy in Modes

Define energy: $E_n(t) = \int_0^L u_n^2 \, dx$

How does it decay?

**Solution:**

$$u_n = \sin(n\pi x/L)e^{-n^2\pi^2 kt/L^2}$$

$$E_n(t) = e^{-2n^2\pi^2 kt/L^2} \int_0^L \sin^2(n\pi x/L) \, dx$$

The integral is constant ($L/2$), so:

$$E_n(t) = E_n(0) \cdot e^{-2n^2\pi^2 kt/L^2}$$

Energy decays twice as fast as amplitude (since $E \propto u^2$).

**Answer:** $E_n(t) = E_n(0) \exp(-2n^2\pi^2 kt/L^2)$

### Example 13: Long-Time Behavior

For general solution $u = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} c_n u_n$, what survives as $t \to \infty$?

**Solution:**

Higher modes decay faster, so after sufficient time:

$$u(x, t) \approx c_1 \sin(\pi x/L) e^{-\pi^2 kt/L^2}$$

Only fundamental mode remains (all higher modes exponentially small).

**Physical**: Initial wiggles smooth out; only the overall "shape" ($n=1$) persists.

**Answer:** As $t \to \infty$, only fundamental mode $n = 1$ survives

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: B**
Separation of variables: $u(x,t) = X(x)T(t)$ (product)
Substituting into PDE gives two ODEs
$\frac{T'}{kT} = \frac{X''}{X} = -\lambda$ (must equal constant)
Not sum (wouldn't separate), not ratio
Product form enables separation

**Problem 2: B**
Eigenvalue problem: $X'' + \lambda X = 0$ with $X(0) = X(L) = 0$
Solutions: $X_n = \sin(n\pi x/L)$
Eigenvalues: $\lambda_n = (n\pi/L)^2$ for $n = 1, 2, 3, \ldots$
Determines decay rate: $e^{-\lambda_n kt}$
Higher $n$ → larger $\lambda_n$ → faster decay

**Problem 3: B**
Decay rate: $e^{-n^2\pi^2 kt/L^2}$
Higher $n$ → exponent more negative → faster decay
Mode $n = 10$ decays 100× faster than $n = 1$
Physical: rapid oscillations smooth quickly
Long-term: only lowest modes remain

**Problem 4: A**
$X_1(x) = \sin(\pi x/L)$: one half-period on $[0,L]$
Zero at endpoints $x = 0, L$ only (boundary conditions)
No interior zeros
Mode $n$ has $n-1$ interior zeros
Fundamental = simplest spatial pattern

**Problem 5: B**
Diffusion time: $t \sim L^2/k$
From fundamental mode decay rate
Doubling length quadruples time
Characteristic distance: $\sqrt{kt}$
Random walk: $\langle x^2 \rangle \sim 2kt$
