# Partial Differential Equations Topic 2.4: Conservation Laws

When water flows through a pipe, the amount entering any section equals the amount leaving plus any accumulation inside—this is **conservation of mass**. Traffic on a highway behaves similarly: cars entering a section equal cars leaving plus cars accumulating. These conservation principles lead to PDEs of the form $u_t + f(u)_x = 0$, called **conservation laws**. They govern everything from fluid dynamics to traffic flow to gas dynamics.

Conservation laws have a special property: they can develop **discontinuous solutions** (shocks) even from smooth initial data. A traffic light turning green creates a shock wave propagating backward through stopped cars. A breaking ocean wave forms a sharp discontinuity. These shocks require a new mathematical framework—**weak solutions**—since classical derivatives don't exist at discontinuities. Understanding when and where shocks form, and how they propagate, is essential for modeling real-world phenomena.

This article explores conservation laws and shock waves. We'll derive conservation laws from physical principles, understand the Rankine-Hugoniot condition governing shock speed, construct entropy conditions to select physical shocks, and see examples from traffic flow and gas dynamics. Conservation laws reveal how mathematics must adapt when physical reality produces discontinuities that classical analysis cannot handle.

## Conservation Laws: Physical Derivation

A **conservation law** has the form:

$$\frac{\partial u}{\partial t} + \frac{\partial f(u)}{\partial x} = 0$$

or: $u_t + f(u)_x = 0$

where $u$ is conserved quantity (density) and $f(u)$ is flux.

### Example 1: Deriving Conservation Form

For traffic with density $\rho$ and velocity $v(\rho)$, derive conservation law.

**Solution:**

**Conservation of cars**: Rate of change in segment $[x_1, x_2]$ equals net flux through boundaries.

$$\frac{d}{dt}\int_{x_1}^{x_2} \rho(x, t) \, dx = \rho(x_1, t)v(x_1, t) - \rho(x_2, t)v(x_2, t)$$

$$= -\int_{x_1}^{x_2} \frac{\partial}{\partial x}[\rho v] \, dx$$

Since this holds for any interval:
$$\rho_t + (\rho v)_x = 0$$

This is conservation form with $u = \rho$, $f(u) = \rho v(\rho)$.

**Answer:** $\rho_t + (\rho v)_x = 0$

### Example 2: Burgers' Equation as Conservation Law

Show that $u_t + uu_x = 0$ is a conservation law.

**Solution:**

$$u_t + uu_x = u_t + \frac{1}{2}(u^2)_x = 0$$

Using $(u^2)_x = 2uu_x$:
$$u_t + \frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left(\frac{u^2}{2}\right) = 0$$

Conservation form with flux $f(u) = u^2/2$.

**Answer:** Conservation law with $f(u) = u^2/2$

### Example 3: Traffic Flow Model

For $v(\rho) = v_{\max}(1 - \rho/\rho_{\max})$, write the flux function.

**Solution:**

Flux: $f(\rho) = \rho v(\rho) = \rho v_{\max}\left(1 - \frac{\rho}{\rho_{\max}}\right)$

$$f(\rho) = v_{\max}\left(\rho - \frac{\rho^2}{\rho_{\max}}\right)$$

**Maximum flux**: $\frac{df}{d\rho} = v_{\max}\left(1 - \frac{2\rho}{\rho_{\max}}\right) = 0$

At $\rho = \rho_{\max}/2$ (half maximum density).

**Answer:** $f(\rho) = v_{\max}\rho(1 - \rho/\rho_{\max})$; max at $\rho = \rho_{\max}/2$

## Weak Solutions and Shocks

When solution develops discontinuity, classical derivatives undefined. Need **weak solutions**.

### Example 4: Jump Discontinuity

For $u_t + f(u)_x = 0$, if $u$ has jump at $x = s(t)$ separating $u = u_L$ (left) from $u = u_R$ (right), what is shock speed?

**Solution:**

**Rankine-Hugoniot condition**:

$$s = \frac{f(u_R) - f(u_L)}{u_R - u_L}$$

This ensures conservation across shock.

**Derivation**: Integrate conservation law across shock; jump in $u$ balanced by jump in flux.

**Answer:** Shock speed $s = \frac{\Delta f}{\Delta u}$ (Rankine-Hugoniot)

### Example 5: Burgers' Shock Speed

For $u_t + (u^2/2)_x = 0$ with shock from $u_L = 2$ to $u_R = 1$:

**Solution:**

$f(u) = u^2/2$

$$s = \frac{f(u_R) - f(u_L)}{u_R - u_L} = \frac{1/2 - 2}{1 - 2} = \frac{-3/2}{-1} = \frac{3}{2}$$

Shock travels at speed $3/2$ (average of left and right states).

**Answer:** Shock speed $s = 3/2$

### Example 6: Traffic Shock

For traffic model with $\rho_L = 0.8\rho_{\max}$ behind shock and $\rho_R = 0.2\rho_{\max}$ ahead:

If $f(\rho) = v_{\max}\rho(1 - \rho/\rho_{\max})$, find shock speed.

**Solution:**

For $\rho_L = 0.8$, $\rho_R = 0.2$ (dropping $\rho_{\max}$ for simplicity with $v_{\max} = 1$):

$f(0.8) = 0.8(1-0.8) = 0.16$
$f(0.2) = 0.2(1-0.2) = 0.16$

Fluxes equal → stationary shock!

**Answer:** $s = 0$ (stationary shock when fluxes balance)

### Example 7: Entropy Condition

Not all weak solutions are physical. **Lax entropy condition**: Characteristics must enter shock from both sides.

For $u_t + (u^2/2)_x = 0$, which shock is physical: $u_L = 1, u_R = 2$ or $u_L = 2, u_R = 1$?

**Solution:**

Characteristic speed: $\frac{dx}{dt} = f'(u) = u$

**Case 1**: $u_L = 1, u_R = 2$
- Left characteristics have speed 1
- Right characteristics have speed 2
- Characteristics **diverge** from shock (rarefaction, not shock)

**Case 2**: $u_L = 2, u_R = 1$
- Left characteristics have speed 2
- Right characteristics have speed 1
- Characteristics **converge** into shock ✓

**Answer:** $u_L = 2, u_R = 1$ satisfies entropy (characteristics enter shock)

### Example 8: Shock Formation Time

For $u_t + uu_x = 0$ with $u(x, 0) = -x$, we found shock forms at $t = 1$ (Example 5, Article 2.2).

Find the shock position for $t > 1$.

**Solution:**

At $t = 1$: all characteristics meet at origin.

For $t > 1$: shock separates left state from right state.

By symmetry and Rankine-Hugoniot, shock stays at $x = 0$.

States: $u_L = $ (from $x_0 < 0$), $u_R = $ (from $x_0 > 0$)



**Answer 1**: Shock at $x = 0$ for $t \geq 1$ (by symmetry)

**Answer 2**: Shock remains at origin $x = 0$ for $t \geq 1$

## Riemann Problem

The **Riemann problem** is conservation law with piecewise constant initial data:

$$u(x, 0) = \begin{cases} u_L & x < 0 \\ u_R & x > 0 \end{cases}$$

Solution is **self-similar**: $u(x, t) = U(x/t)$.

### Example 9: Riemann Problem - Shock Case

Solve $u_t + (u^2/2)_x = 0$ with $u_L = 2$, $u_R = 1$.

**Solution:**

Entropy condition satisfied ($u_L > u_R$) → shock solution.

Shock speed: $s = \frac{f(u_R) - f(u_L)}{u_R - u_L} = \frac{1/2 - 2}{1 - 2} = 3/2$

Solution:
$$u(x, t) = \begin{cases} 2 & x < 3t/2 \\ 1 & x > 3t/2 \end{cases}$$

**Answer:** Shock at $x = 3t/2$ separating $u = 2$ (left) and $u = 1$ (right)

### Example 10: Riemann Problem - Rarefaction Case

Solve $u_t + (u^2/2)_x = 0$ with $u_L = 1$, $u_R = 2$.

**Solution:**

$u_L < u_R$ → characteristics diverge → rarefaction wave.

Rarefaction fan: $u = x/t$ in region $t < x < 2t$.

Full solution:
$$u(x, t) = \begin{cases} 1 & x < t \\ x/t & t < x < 2t \\ 2 & x > 2t \end{cases}$$

**Answer:** Rarefaction fan connecting $u = 1$ to $u = 2$

### Example 11: Traffic Light Problem

Traffic light at $x = 0$ turns green at $t = 0$. Initially $\rho = \rho_{\max}$ for $x < 0$ (stopped) and $\rho = 0$ for $x > 0$ (empty).

For $f(\rho) = \rho(1 - \rho)$, describe solution.

**Solution:**

$f'(\rho) = 1 - 2\rho$:
- At $\rho = 1$: $f'(1) = -1$ (characteristics move left)
- At $\rho = 0$: $f'(0) = 1$ (characteristics move right)

Characteristics diverge → **rarefaction**.

Rarefaction fan: $\rho = x/t$ for $-t < x < t$.

**Answer:** Rarefaction wave; traffic accelerates smoothly from stopped to free flow

### Example 12: Shock Overtaking Rarefaction

Can a shock catch up to a rarefaction?

**Solution:**

Generally no—rarefaction spreads characteristics apart, shock would need faster speed than all characteristics.

But in complex flows (systems of conservation laws), interactions possible.

For scalar conservation law: shock and rarefaction don't interact (separated by constant states).

**Answer:** For scalar laws, shocks and rarefactions remain separate

### Example 13: Weak vs. Strong Shocks

In gas dynamics, weak shocks satisfy $u_R \approx u_L$ (small jump).

Strong shocks have large $|u_R - u_L|$.

For Burgers', compare $u_L = 2, u_R = 1.9$ vs. $u_L = 2, u_R = 0$.

**Solution:**

**Weak shock**: $s = \frac{(1.9)^2/2 - 2}{1.9 - 2} = \frac{1.805 - 2}{-0.1} = 1.95 \approx 2$

**Strong shock**: $s = \frac{0 - 2}{0 - 2} = 1$

Weak shock travels at nearly $u_L = 2$.
Strong shock travels at average of left and right.

**Answer:** Weak shocks move at approximately $u_L$; strong shocks at average

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: B**
Conservation form: $u_t + f(u)_x = 0$
$u$ = conserved density
$f(u)$ = flux function
Integral form conserves total amount
Example: $u_t + (u^2/2)_x = 0$ (Burgers')

**Problem 2: C**
Rankine-Hugoniot: $s = \frac{f(u_R) - f(u_L)}{u_R - u_L}$
Determines shock propagation speed
Ensures conservation across discontinuity
Derived from integral conservation law
Not sufficient alone (need entropy condition)

**Problem 3: B**
$f(u) = u^2/2$
$s = \frac{(1)^2/2 - (3)^2/2}{1 - 3} = \frac{1/2 - 9/2}{-2} = \frac{-4}{-2} = 2$
Shock speed is average: $(3 + 1)/2 = 2$
Characteristics converge at this speed

**Problem 4: B**
Lax entropy condition: characteristics enter shock
Physical shocks compress information
Rarefactions spread information apart
Without entropy, multiple weak solutions
Entropy selects physical solution

**Problem 5: B**
Shock = discontinuous jump (e.g., from jam to free flow)
Propagates backward through traffic
Example: light turning green
Smooth waves are rarefactions
Conservation law models both
