# Partial Differential Equations Topic 2.3: General First-Order PDEs

Beyond linear and quasilinear PDEs lies the realm of **general first-order PDEs**: $F(x, y, u, u_x, u_y) = 0$. Here derivatives can appear in nonlinear combinations—squares, products, inside transcendental functions. These fully nonlinear equations arise in geometric optics (eikonal equation), optimal control (Hamilton-Jacobi equation), and differential geometry (minimal surfaces). The method of characteristics still applies, but solutions become more intricate: we seek surfaces rather than functions along curves.

General first-order PDEs introduce new phenomena. A single PDE can have infinitely many solutions—a **complete integral** containing two arbitrary constants generates a family of solutions. The **general solution** involves arbitrary functions and emerges from envelopes of families of surfaces. Understanding these solutions requires geometric visualization: we're not just finding functions, but understanding how surfaces fold and wrap in three-dimensional space.

This article explores general first-order PDEs through the classical Charpit method. We'll construct complete integrals, understand envelopes and singular solutions, and see how geometric insights reveal solution structure. These techniques, developed by Lagrange, Charpit, and Hamilton in the 1800s, remain essential for understanding nonlinear wave propagation and geometric PDEs.

## General First-Order PDEs

**General form**: $F(x, y, u, p, q) = 0$ where $p = u_x$, $q = u_y$.

**Key difference from quasilinear**: Can have terms like $p^2$, $pq$, $\sin p$, etc.

### Example 1: Identifying General PDEs

Classify:

(a) $u_x^2 + u_y^2 = 1$ (eikonal equation)

(b) $u_x + u_y = u$

(c) $u_x u_y = u$

**Solution:**

(a) **General/fully nonlinear**: $p^2 + q^2 = 1$ (nonlinear in derivatives)

(b) **Linear**: $p + q = u$

(c) **General/fully nonlinear**: $pq = u$ (product of derivatives)

**Answer:** (a) and (c) general; (b) linear

### Example 2: Complete Integral

A **complete integral** of $F(x, y, u, p, q) = 0$ is a solution containing two arbitrary constants $a, b$:

$$u = \phi(x, y, a, b)$$

For $p^2 + q^2 = 1$, verify that $u = ax + by + c$ (with $a^2 + b^2 = 1$) is a complete integral.

**Solution:**

$$u = ax + by + c$$
$$p = u_x = a, \quad q = u_y = b$$

Substitute: $a^2 + b^2 = 1$ ✓

This is satisfied if we impose $a^2 + b^2 = 1$ (constraint on parameters).

For each choice of $a, b$ (with $a^2 + b^2 = 1$) and $c$, we get a plane solution.

**Answer:** Family of planes with unit normal vector $(a, b, -1)/\sqrt{a^2 + b^2 + 1}$

### Example 3: Geometric Meaning

What do the planes $u = ax + by + c$ (with $a^2 + b^2 = 1$) represent geometrically?

**Solution:**

These are planes whose gradient has magnitude 1:
$$|\nabla u| = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2} = 1$$

**Physical interpretation** (eikonal equation): 
- Surfaces of constant phase in wave propagation
- Wavefronts traveling at unit speed
- Level curves perpendicular to rays

**Answer:** Planes with unit gradient (constant-speed wavefronts)

### Example 4: Clairaut's Equation

The PDE $u = xu_x + yu_y + f(u_x, u_y)$ is **Clairaut's equation**.

Show that $u = ax + by + f(a, b)$ is a complete integral.

**Solution:**

Let $u = ax + by + f(a, b)$ where $a, b$ are constants.

Then $u_x = a$, $u_y = b$.

Check PDE:
$$\text{RHS} = x(a) + y(b) + f(a, b) = ax + by + f(a, b) = u = \text{LHS}$$ ✓

**Answer:** Complete integral with two parameters $a, b$

## Charpit's Method

For general $F(x, y, u, p, q) = 0$, **Charpit's method** finds complete integrals using auxiliary equations:

$$\frac{dx}{F_p} = \frac{dy}{F_q} = \frac{du}{pF_p + qF_q} = \frac{dp}{-F_x - pF_u} = \frac{dq}{-F_y - qF_u}$$

These are the **Charpit equations** (characteristic strip equations).

### Example 5: Applying Charpit's Method

For $F = p + q - u = 0$, find the Charpit equations.

**Solution:**

$F_p = 1$, $F_q = 1$, $F_x = 0$, $F_y = 0$, $F_u = -1$

Charpit equations:
$$\frac{dx}{1} = \frac{dy}{1} = \frac{du}{p + q} = \frac{dp}{-0 - p(-1)} = \frac{dq}{-0 - q(-1)}$$

Simplify:
$$\frac{dx}{1} = \frac{dy}{1} = \frac{du}{p+q} = \frac{dp}{p} = \frac{dq}{q}$$

From $\frac{dp}{p} = \frac{dq}{q}$: $\frac{dp}{p} = \frac{dq}{q} \implies \frac{q}{p} = a$ (constant)

So $q = ap$.

**Answer:** $q = ap$ is first integral of Charpit equations

### Example 6: Complete Integral from Charpit

Using $q = ap$ from Example 5, substitute into $p + q = u$:

**Solution:**

$p + ap = u \implies p(1 + a) = u$

So $p = \frac{u}{1+a}$ and $q = \frac{au}{1+a}$.

From $p = u_x$ and $q = u_y$:
$$u_x = \frac{u}{1+a}, \quad u_y = \frac{au}{1+a}$$

First equation: $\frac{du}{u} = \frac{dx}{1+a} \implies \ln u = \frac{x}{1+a} + f(y)$

Second equation determines $f(y)$.

Complete integral: $u = \exp\left(\frac{x + ay}{1+a} + b\right)$

**Answer:** $u = c e^{(x+ay)/(1+a)}$ (two-parameter family)


### Example 7: Singular Solutions

The **envelope** of a family of surfaces can give additional solutions.

For $u = ax + by + c$ with $a^2 + b^2 = 1$, eliminate $a$ to find envelope.

**Solution:**

We treat $u = ax + by + c$ with the constraint $a^2 + b^2 = 1$ as a one-parameter family.
Let $b = \sqrt{1 - a^2}$ (choosing one branch). Then the family becomes
$$
F(x,y,u,a) = u - ax - b(a), y - c = 0.
$$

Envelope condition:
$$
\frac{\partial F}{\partial a} = 0.
$$

Compute
$$
\frac{db}{da} = -\frac{a}{b}, \qquad
\frac{\partial F}{\partial a} = -x - \frac{db}{da},y
= -x + \frac{a}{b}y = 0.
$$

Thus
$$
\frac{a}{b} = \frac{x}{y}.
$$

Use the constraint $a^2 + b^2 = 1$:
$$
a^2 = \frac{x^2}{x^2 + y^2},
\qquad
b^2 = \frac{y^2}{x^2 + y^2}.
$$

Substitute into $u = ax + by + c$:
$$
u = \frac{x^2 + y^2}{\sqrt{x^2 + y^2}} + c
= \sqrt{x^2 + y^2} + c.
$$

The opposite branch gives the negative sign.

**Answer:**
The envelope (singular solution) is
$$
u = \pm\sqrt{x^2 + y^2} + c.
$$


### Example 8: Geometric Optics

The **eikonal equation** $|\nabla u|^2 = n^2(x, y)$ describes wavefronts in medium with refractive index $n$.

For $n = 1$ (homogeneous), show complete integral is $u = \mathbf{n} \cdot \mathbf{r} + c$ where $|\mathbf{n}| = 1$.

**Solution:**

Let $\mathbf{n} = (a, b)$ with $a^2 + b^2 = 1$.

$$u = ax + by + c$$
$$\nabla u = (a, b)$$
$$|\nabla u|^2 = a^2 + b^2 = 1 = n^2$$ ✓

Each choice of unit vector $\mathbf{n}$ gives plane wave in that direction.

**Answer:** Plane waves in all directions form complete integral

## Hamilton-Jacobi Theory

General first-order PDEs connect deeply to classical mechanics through **Hamilton-Jacobi theory**.

### Example 9: Hamilton-Jacobi Equation

In mechanics, Hamilton's principal function $S(q, t)$ satisfies:

$$\frac{\partial S}{\partial t} + H\left(q, \frac{\partial S}{\partial q}\right) = 0$$

where $H$ is the Hamiltonian.

For free particle $H = \frac{p^2}{2m}$, write the PDE.

**Solution:**

$$S_t + \frac{1}{2m}(S_q)^2 = 0$$

This is a general first-order PDE (nonlinear in $S_q$).

**Answer:** $S_t + \frac{(S_q)^2}{2m} = 0$ (Hamilton-Jacobi for free particle)

### Example 10: Separation of Variables

For time-independent Hamiltonian, try $S(q, t) = W(q) - Et$:

**Solution:**

$$S_t = -E$$
$$S_q = W'(q)$$

Substitute:
$$-E + H(q, W'(q)) = 0$$
$$H(q, W'(q)) = E$$

This is an ODE for $W(q)$ (energy constant $E$).

**Answer:** Reduces to ODE: $H(q, W') = E$

### Example 11: Complete Integral in Mechanics

For harmonic oscillator $H = \frac{p^2}{2m} + \frac{1}{2}kq^2$:

$$\frac{(W')^2}{2m} + \frac{kq^2}{2} = E$$

Solve for $W$.

**Solution:**

$$(W')^2 = 2m\left(E - \frac{kq^2}{2}\right)$$

$$W' = \sqrt{2mE - mkq^2}$$

$$W = \int \sqrt{2mE - mkq^2} \, dq$$

This integral gives $W$ in terms of $E$ (parameter).

Complete integral: $S = W(q, E) - Et$

**Answer:** $S$ with arbitrary constant $E$ (energy) is complete integral

### Example 12: Envelopes and Caustics

In optics, the **caustic** is the envelope of light rays.

For family of lines $y = ax - a^2$ (parameter $a$), find envelope.

**Solution:**

Envelope conditions:
$$f(x, y, a) = y - ax + a^2 = 0$$
$$\frac{\partial f}{\partial a} = -x + 2a = 0 \implies a = \frac{x}{2}$$

Substitute back:
$$y = \frac{x}{2} \cdot x - \left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^2 = \frac{x^2}{2} - \frac{x^2}{4} = \frac{x^2}{4}$$

**Answer:** Envelope is parabola $y = x^2/4$ (caustic curve)

### Example 13: Minimal Surface Equation

The minimal surface equation (surface of minimum area) is:

$$(1 + q^2)p^2 - 2pq \cdot pq + (1 + p^2)q^2 = 0$$

For first-order approximation (small slopes):

$$p^2 + q^2 = 0$$

This has only trivial real solution $p = q = 0$ ($u = \text{const}$, a plane).

For genuine minimal surfaces, need second-order PDE.

**Answer:** First-order approximation gives planes; true minimal surfaces need second-order equation

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: C**
Complete integral: solution with two arbitrary constants
Form: $u = \phi(x, y, a, b)$
Generates two-parameter family of surfaces
Not general solution (which has arbitrary functions)
Foundation for building general solution

**Problem 2: C**
$p^2 + q^2 = 1$ where $p = u_x, q = u_y$
Nonlinear in derivatives (squared terms)
Fully nonlinear (not quasilinear)
Eikonal equation from geometric optics
Describes wavefronts with unit speed

**Problem 3: C**
Charpit equations: characteristic strip method
For general $F(x, y, u, p, q) = 0$
System of five ODEs
Reduces PDE to ODEs along characteristics
Named after Paul Charpit (1784)

**Problem 4: B**
Envelope of family can satisfy PDE
Called singular solution
Different from complete integral or general solution
Example: caustics in optics
Found by eliminating parameter with envelope condition

**Problem 5: C**
Hamilton-Jacobi equation: $S_t + H(q, S_q) = 0$
$S$ is action function
Connects classical mechanics to wave theory
Bridge to quantum mechanics (Schrödinger equation)
Used in optimal control theory
