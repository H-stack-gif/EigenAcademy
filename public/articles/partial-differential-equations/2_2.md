# Partial Differential Equations Topic 2.2: Quasilinear Equations

The transport equation $u_t + cu_x = 0$ has constant wave speed $c$—every part of the wave travels at the same velocity. But what if the wave speed depends on the wave height? In shallow water, taller waves travel faster, leading to the equation $u_t + uu_x = 0$ where wave speed equals wave height $u$. This is a **quasilinear PDE**: linear in derivatives but with coefficients depending on $u$ itself.

Quasilinear equations are fundamentally more complex than linear ones. Wave speed varying with amplitude means different parts of a wave travel at different speeds. Tall parts overtake short parts, causing the wave to steepen and eventually break. This **wave breaking** creates shocks—discontinuities in the solution that can't be smoothed out. The method of characteristics still works, but now characteristics can cross, signaling the formation of shocks.

This article extends the method of characteristics to quasilinear first-order PDEs. We'll solve equations where coefficients depend on $u$, understand what happens when characteristics intersect, and see how smooth initial data can evolve into discontinuous shocks. This is your first encounter with truly nonlinear behavior in PDEs—where solutions have fundamentally different character than their initial conditions.

## Quasilinear First-Order PDEs

A **quasilinear PDE** has the form:

$$a(x, y, u)u_x + b(x, y, u)u_y = c(x, y, u)$$

Coefficients $a, b, c$ depend on $u$, but derivatives appear linearly.

**Key difference from linear**: Characteristics depend on solution $u$ itself.

### Example 1: Identifying Quasilinear PDEs

Classify each PDE:

(a) $u_x + uu_y = 0$

(b) $u_x^2 + u_y^2 = 1$

(c) $u_x + yu_y = u^2$

**Solution:**

(a) **Quasilinear**: coefficient of $u_y$ is $u$, but $u_x, u_y$ appear linearly

(b) **Fully nonlinear**: $u_x^2$ is nonlinear in derivative

(c) **Quasilinear**: derivatives linear, right side depends on $u$

**Answer:** (a) and (c) quasilinear; (b) fully nonlinear

### Example 2: Inviscid Burgers' Equation

The equation $u_t + uu_x = 0$ is inviscid Burgers' equation.

What are the characteristic equations?

**Solution:**

For $u_t + uu_x = 0$:
$$\frac{dt}{1} = \frac{dx}{u} = \frac{du}{0}$$

From $\frac{du}{0}$: $du = 0 \implies u = \text{const along characteristics}$

From $\frac{dx}{dt} = u$: $x = ut + x_0$

**Key insight**: Since $u$ is constant on each characteristic, that characteristic is a straight line with slope $u$.

**Answer:** $u = c$, $x = ct + x_0$ (straight lines, but slope depends on which characteristic)

### Example 3: Simple Wave Solution

Solve $u_t + uu_x = 0$ with $u(x, 0) = 2$ for $x < 0$ and $u(x, 0) = 1$ for $x > 0$.

**Solution:**

For $x_0 < 0$: $u = 2$ along characteristic $x = 2t + x_0$

For $x_0 > 0$: $u = 1$ along characteristic $x = t + x_0$

**At time $t$**:
- Characteristics from $x_0 < 0$ reach points $x = 2t + x_0$ (with $x_0 < 0$)
- Characteristics from $x_0 > 0$ reach points $x = t + x_0$ (with $x_0 > 0$)

Region $x < 2t$: covered by fast characteristics, so $u = 2$
Region $x > t$: covered by slow characteristics, so $u = 1$
Region $t < x < 2t$: **rarefaction fan** (characteristics fan out)

**Answer:** $u = 2$ for $x < t$; $u = 1$ for $x > 2t$; expanding wave between

### Example 4: Initial Value Problem

Solve $u_t + uu_x = 0$ with $u(x, 0) = x$.

**Solution:**

Characteristic through $(x_0, 0)$ has $u = x_0$ (constant).

This characteristic is the line: $x = x_0 t + x_0 = x_0(1 + t)$

Solve for $x_0$: $x_0 = \frac{x}{1 + t}$

Since $u = x_0$: $u(x, t) = \frac{x}{1 + t}$

**Verification**: 
$$u_t = -\frac{x}{(1+t)^2}, \quad u_x = \frac{1}{1+t}$$
$$u_t + uu_x = -\frac{x}{(1+t)^2} + \frac{x}{1+t} \cdot \frac{1}{1+t} = 0$$ ✓

**Answer:** $u(x, t) = \frac{x}{1 + t}$

## Implicit Solutions and Breaking Time

When characteristics intersect, the implicit solution becomes multivalued—signaling shock formation.

### Example 5: Shock Formation

For $u_t + uu_x = 0$ with $u(x, 0) = -x$, when do characteristics first intersect?

**Solution:**

Characteristic through $(x_0, 0)$: $u = -x_0$ (constant)

Line: $x = -x_0 t + x_0 = x_0(1 - t)$

Two characteristics through $(x_1, 0)$ and $(x_2, 0)$ intersect when:
$$x_1(1 - t) = x_2(1 - t)$$

For $x_1 \neq x_2$: $1 - t = 0 \implies t = 1$

**Physical meaning**: Faster characteristics (from larger $x_0$ where $u = -x_0$ is more negative, thus faster) overtake slower ones.

**Answer:** Breaking time $t_b = 1$

### Example 6: Implicit Solution

For Example 5, the implicit solution is $u = -\frac{x}{1-t}$. Verify this satisfies the PDE for $t < 1$.

**Solution:**

$$u_t = -x \cdot \frac{1}{(1-t)^2} = \frac{x}{(1-t)^2}$$

$$u_x = -\frac{1}{1-t}$$

$$u_t + uu_x = \frac{x}{(1-t)^2} + \left(-\frac{x}{1-t}\right)\left(-\frac{1}{1-t}\right) = \frac{x}{(1-t)^2} - \frac{x}{(1-t)^2} = 0$$ ✓

But at $t = 1$: $u \to \pm\infty$ (blows up!)

**Answer:** Valid for $t < 1$; breaks down at $t = 1$

### Example 7: Traffic Flow Model

Traffic density $\rho(x, t)$ satisfies $\rho_t + (\rho v(\rho))_x = 0$ where $v(\rho) = v_{\max}(1 - \rho/\rho_{\max})$ is velocity.

Assuming $v(\rho) = 1 - \rho$, simplify the PDE.

**Solution:**

$$\rho_t + (\rho(1-\rho))_x = 0$$
$$\rho_t + ((1-\rho)\rho_x - \rho\rho_x) = 0$$
$$\rho_t + (1 - 2\rho)\rho_x = 0$$

This is quasilinear: $\rho_t + c(\rho)\rho_x = 0$ with $c(\rho) = 1 - 2\rho$.

**Characteristics**: $\frac{dx}{dt} = 1 - 2\rho$

High density ($\rho$ large): $c < 0$ (backward characteristics—traffic jams propagate backward!)

**Answer:** $\rho_t + (1 - 2\rho)\rho_x = 0$ (quasilinear conservation law)

### Example 8: Method of Characteristics for General Case

For $a(x, t, u)u_x + b(x, t, u)u_t = c(x, t, u)$, write the characteristic equations.

**Solution:**

$$\frac{dx}{a(x, t, u)} = \frac{dt}{b(x, t, u)} = \frac{du}{c(x, t, u)}$$

These are three coupled ODEs for $x(s), t(s), u(s)$.

Unlike linear case, $u$ affects the characteristic path.

**Answer:** $\frac{dx}{ds} = a$, $\frac{dt}{ds} = b$, $\frac{du}{ds} = c$ (parametric ODEs)

### Example 9: Solving Quasilinear PDE

Solve $u_x + uu_y = y$ with $u(x, 0) = x$.

**Solution:**

Characteristic equations:
$$\frac{dx}{1} = \frac{dy}{u} = \frac{du}{y}$$

Along characteristic starting at $(x_0, 0)$, we have $u(x_0, 0) = x_0$.

From $\frac{dy}{u} = \frac{du}{y}$:
$$u \, du = y \, dy$$

Integrate: $\frac{u^2}{2} = \frac{y^2}{2} + C$

At $y = 0$: $\frac{x_0^2}{2} = 0 + C$, so $C = \frac{x_0^2}{2}$

Therefore: $u^2 = y^2 + x_0^2$

Since $u(x_0, 0) = x_0 > 0$, take positive root: $u = \sqrt{y^2 + x_0^2}$

From $\frac{dx}{1} = \frac{dy}{u}$: $\frac{dx}{dy} = \frac{1}{u} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{y^2 + x_0^2}}$

Integrate: $x = \int_0^y \frac{d\eta}{\sqrt{\eta^2 + x_0^2}} = \sinh^{-1}(y/x_0) + x_0$

(This gives $x$ implicitly in terms of $y$ and $x_0$)

**Answer:** $u = \sqrt{y^2 + x_0^2}$ where $x_0$ satisfies $x = \sinh^{-1}(y/x_0) + x_0$

## Rarefaction Waves

When fast characteristics fall behind slow ones, a **rarefaction wave** forms.

### Example 10: Rarefaction Structure

For $u_t + uu_x = 0$ with $u(x, 0) = -1$ for $x < 0$ and $u(x, 0) = 1$ for $x > 0$, describe the solution.

**Solution:**

From $x_0 < 0$: characteristics $x = -t + x_0$ (slope $-1$)
From $x_0 > 0$: characteristics $x = t + x_0$ (slope $1$)

These **spread apart**, leaving a fan region $-t < x < t$.

In the fan: characteristics emanate from origin with slopes varying continuously from $-1$ to $1$.

**Similarity solution** in fan: $u = x/t$ (straight line from origin has constant $u$)

Full solution:
$$u(x, t) = \begin{cases} -1 & x < -t \\ x/t & -t < x < t \\ 1 & x > t \end{cases}$$

**Answer:** Rarefaction fan $u = x/t$ in expanding region

### Example 11: Verification of Rarefaction

Verify $u = x/t$ satisfies $u_t + uu_x = 0$ in the fan region.

**Solution:**

$$u_t = \frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right) = -\frac{x}{t^2}$$

$$u_x = \frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right) = \frac{1}{t}$$

$$u_t + uu_x = -\frac{x}{t^2} + \frac{x}{t} \cdot \frac{1}{t} = -\frac{x}{t^2} + \frac{x}{t^2} = 0$$ ✓

**Answer:** Verified in fan region

### Example 12: Physical Interpretation

In shallow water waves, why do tall regions catch up to short regions?

**Solution:**

Wave speed $c = \sqrt{gh}$ where $h$ is depth.

For $u_t + \sqrt{u} \, u_x = 0$ (approximately):

Taller waves ($u$ larger) have $\sqrt{u}$ larger, so they travel faster.

Fast parts overtake slow parts → steepening → breaking.

**Answer:** Wave speed increases with amplitude, causing steepening

### Example 13: Critical Time

For $u_t + uu_x = 0$ with $u(x, 0) = 1/(1 + x^2)$, estimate when shocks might form.

**Solution:**

Characteristic slope: $\frac{dx}{dt} = u = \frac{1}{1 + x_0^2}$

Characteristics steepen where $\frac{du_0}{dx_0} < 0$ (faster characteristics behind slower ones).

$$\frac{d}{dx_0}\left[\frac{1}{1+x_0^2}\right] = -\frac{2x_0}{(1+x_0^2)^2}$$

This is negative for $x_0 > 0$, positive for $x_0 < 0$.

**Breaking condition**: $1 + t \frac{du_0}{dx_0} = 0$

At $x_0 = 1$: $\frac{du_0}{dx_0} = -\frac{2}{4} = -\frac{1}{2}$

Breaking time: $t_b = \frac{1}{1/2} = 2$

**Answer:** Shock forms around $t \approx 2$

**Problem 4:** For $u_t + uu_x = 0$ with $u(x,0) = x$, the solution at $t = 2$ is:

A) $u = x/3$
B) $u = x/2$
C) $u = x$
D) $u = 2x$

**Problem 5:** Rarefaction waves occur when:

A) Fast characteristics overtake slow ones
B) Slow characteristics overtake fast ones
C) Characteristics spread apart
D) All characteristics parallel
E) $u$ is constant

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: B**
Quasilinear: derivatives appear linearly
But coefficients $a(x,y,u), b(x,y,u), c(x,y,u)$ depend on $u$
Example: $u_t + uu_x = 0$ (coefficient of $u_x$ is $u$)
Contrast linear (coefficients independent of $u$)
Contrast fully nonlinear ($u_x^2$ type terms)

**Problem 2: C**
Characteristic equations: $\frac{dx}{u} = \frac{dt}{1} = \frac{du}{0}$
From $du = 0$: $u = \text{const}$ along characteristics
Each characteristic carries fixed value of $u$
Wave speed $= u$ constant on that characteristic
Characteristics are straight lines: $x = ut + x_0$

**Problem 3: C**
Crossing characteristics → multivalued solution
Breakdown of classical solution
Signals shock formation
Need weak solutions (discontinuities)
Occurs when $1 + t \frac{du_0}{dx_0} = 0$

**Problem 4: A**
From Example 4: $u(x,t) = \frac{x}{1+t}$
At $t = 2$: $u(x,2) = \frac{x}{1+2} = \frac{x}{3}$
Amplitude decays as $1/(1+t)$
Solution stays smooth (no shock for this IC)
Rarefaction wave expanding

**Problem 5: C**
Rarefaction: characteristics fan out
Slow characteristics ahead of fast ones
Creates expanding region
Solution continuous but non-smooth (kink)
Example: $u = x/t$ in fan
Contrast shock (characteristics converge)
