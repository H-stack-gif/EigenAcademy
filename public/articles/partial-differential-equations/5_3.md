# Partial Differential Equations Topic 5.3: Separation of Variables

A guitar string fixed at both ends vibrates in characteristic patterns—the fundamental mode has one antinode, the second harmonic has two, the third has three. These **standing waves** are the normal modes of the string, natural vibration patterns that oscillate sinusoidally in time at specific frequencies. Any vibration of the string can be decomposed into these building blocks, just as any sound is a combination of pure tones.

The method of **separation of variables** finds these normal modes by assuming product solutions $u(x,t) = X(x)T(t)$. Substituting into the wave equation yields two ODEs: one for spatial shape (eigenvalue problem) and one for temporal oscillation. The boundary conditions determine allowed wavelengths, which determine allowed frequencies. These **eigenfrequencies** $\omega_n = n\pi c/L$ are the harmonics—integer multiples of the fundamental frequency.

This article develops standing wave solutions for finite strings. We'll find normal modes, understand their frequencies and shapes, construct general solutions via Fourier series, and see how initial conditions determine which modes are excited. The mathematics reveals why musical instruments produce harmonic overtones and why resonance occurs at specific frequencies.

## Separation of Variables

For $u_{tt} = c^2u_{xx}$ on $0 \leq x \leq L$ with $u(0,t) = u(L,t) = 0$, assume $u = X(x)T(t)$.

### Example 1: Separating the Equation

Substitute $u = XT$ into wave equation.

**Solution:**

$$u_{tt} = X(x)T''(t)$$
$$u_{xx} = X''(x)T(t)$$

Wave equation:
$$XT'' = c^2X''T$$

Divide by $c^2XT$:
$$\frac{T''}{c^2T} = \frac{X''}{X}$$

Left side depends only on $t$, right side only on $x$ → both equal constant.

$$\frac{T''}{c^2T} = \frac{X''}{X} = -\lambda$$

**Answer:** Two ODEs: $X'' + \lambda X = 0$ and $T'' + \lambda c^2T = 0$

### Example 2: Spatial Eigenvalue Problem

With $u(0,t) = u(L,t) = 0$: what conditions on $X$?

**Solution:**

$u(0,t) = X(0)T(t) = 0$ for all $t$

Since $T \not\equiv 0$: $X(0) = 0$

Similarly: $X(L) = 0$

**Sturm-Liouville problem**: Find $\lambda$ and $X$ such that
$$X'' + \lambda X = 0, \quad X(0) = X(L) = 0$$

(Same eigenvalue problem as heat equation!)

**Answer:** $X(0) = 0$, $X(L) = 0$

### Example 3: Eigenvalues and Eigenfunctions

Solve eigenvalue problem from Example 2.

**Solution:**

For $\lambda > 0$: Let $\lambda = \mu^2$.

$$X = c_1\cos(\mu x) + c_2\sin(\mu x)$$

$X(0) = 0$: $c_1 = 0$

$X(L) = 0$: $c_2\sin(\mu L) = 0$

Nontrivial solution: $\mu L = n\pi$, $n = 1, 2, 3, \ldots$

**Eigenvalues**: $\lambda_n = \left(\frac{n\pi}{L}\right)^2$

**Eigenfunctions**: $X_n(x) = \sin\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right)$

**Answer:** $\lambda_n = (n\pi/L)^2$, $X_n = \sin(n\pi x/L)$

### Example 4: Temporal Solutions

For each $\lambda_n$, solve $T'' + \lambda_n c^2T = 0$.

**Solution:**

$$T'' + \omega_n^2 T = 0$$

where $\omega_n = c\sqrt{\lambda_n} = \frac{n\pi c}{L}$ (angular frequency)

General solution:
$$T_n(t) = A_n\cos(\omega_n t) + B_n\sin(\omega_n t)$$

**Alternative form**:
$$T_n(t) = C_n\cos(\omega_n t + \phi_n)$$

**Answer:** $T_n(t) = A_n\cos(n\pi ct/L) + B_n\sin(n\pi ct/L)$

### Example 5: Normal Modes

Combining spatial and temporal parts:

$$u_n(x, t) = \sin\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right)\left[A_n\cos\left(\frac{n\pi ct}{L}\right) + B_n\sin\left(\frac{n\pi ct}{L}\right)\right]$$

These are **normal modes** or **standing waves**.

Verify $u_1$ satisfies wave equation.

**Solution:**

For $n = 1$:
$$u_1 = \sin(\pi x/L)[A_1\cos(\pi ct/L) + B_1\sin(\pi ct/L)]$$

$$(u_1)_{tt} = \sin(\pi x/L) \cdot \left(-\frac{\pi^2c^2}{L^2}\right)[A_1\cos(\pi ct/L) + B_1\sin(\pi ct/L)]$$

$$(u_1)_{xx} = \left(-\frac{\pi^2}{L^2}\right)\sin(\pi x/L)[A_1\cos(\pi ct/L) + B_1\sin(\pi ct/L)]$$

$$c^2(u_1)_{xx} = -\frac{\pi^2c^2}{L^2}\sin(\pi x/L)[...] = (u_1)_{tt}$$ ✓

**Answer:** Each normal mode satisfies wave equation and boundary conditions

## Properties of Normal Modes

### Example 6: Frequencies and Harmonics

The frequencies are $f_n = \frac{\omega_n}{2\pi} = \frac{nc}{2L}$.

For guitar string with $L = 0.65$ m, $c = 400$ m/s:

**Solution:**

$$f_1 = \frac{1 \cdot 400}{2 \cdot 0.65} \approx 308 \text{ Hz}$$ (fundamental)

$$f_2 = 2f_1 \approx 616 \text{ Hz}$$ (first overtone)

$$f_3 = 3f_1 \approx 924 \text{ Hz}$$ (second overtone)

These are **harmonics**: integer multiples of fundamental.

**Answer:** $f_n = nf_1$ where $f_1 = c/(2L)$

### Example 7: Spatial Structure

Mode $n$ has how many nodes (zero-crossings) in $(0, L)$?

**Solution:**

$$X_n(x) = \sin(n\pi x/L)$$

Zeros at $x = 0, L/n, 2L/n, \ldots, (n-1)L/n, L$

Interior nodes: $n - 1$

**Antinodes** (maxima/minima): $n$ total

**Answer:** Mode $n$ has $n-1$ interior nodes and $n$ antinodes

### Example 8: Temporal Oscillation

Each normal mode oscillates with period $T_n = 2\pi/\omega_n = 2L/(nc)$.

**Solution:**

$$T_n = \frac{2L}{nc}$$

Higher modes oscillate faster (shorter period).

Fundamental: $T_1 = 2L/c$

**Physical meaning**: Time for wave to travel length $2L$ (down and back).

**Answer:** Period $T_n = 2L/(nc)$; higher $n$ → shorter period

## General Solution by Superposition

By linearity, general solution is:

$$u(x, t) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \sin\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right)\left[A_n\cos\left(\frac{n\pi ct}{L}\right) + B_n\sin\left(\frac{n\pi ct}{L}\right)\right]$$

### Example 9: Matching Initial Displacement

At $t = 0$: $u(x, 0) = f(x) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} A_n\sin(n\pi x/L)$

How to find $A_n$?

**Solution:**

This is Fourier sine series:
$$A_n = \frac{2}{L}\int_0^L f(x)\sin(n\pi x/L) \, dx$$

(Same as heat equation!)

**Answer:** $A_n = \frac{2}{L}\int_0^L f(x)\sin(n\pi x/L) \, dx$

### Example 10: Matching Initial Velocity

At $t = 0$: $u_t(x, 0) = g(x)$

**Solution:**

$$u_t = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \sin(n\pi x/L) \cdot \frac{n\pi c}{L}\left[-A_n\sin(n\pi ct/L) + B_n\cos(n\pi ct/L)\right]$$

At $t = 0$:
$$g(x) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} B_n \cdot \frac{n\pi c}{L} \sin(n\pi x/L)$$

$$B_n = \frac{2}{n\pi c}\int_0^L g(x)\sin(n\pi x/L) \, dx$$

**Answer:** $B_n = \frac{2}{n\pi c}\int_0^L g(x)\sin(n\pi x/L) \, dx$

### Example 11: Released from Rest

If $g(x) = 0$ (zero initial velocity), then all $B_n = 0$:

$$u(x, t) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} A_n \sin(n\pi x/L)\cos(n\pi ct/L)$$

**Physical meaning**: Pure standing waves (no traveling wave character).

**Answer:** $u = \sum A_n \sin(n\pi x/L)\cos(n\pi ct/L)$

### Example 12: Specific Example

String with $L = \pi$, $c = 1$, initial conditions $f(x) = \sin x$, $g(x) = 0$.

**Solution:**

Initial displacement already in normal mode form: $n = 1$ mode only.

$$A_1 = 1, \quad A_n = 0 \text{ for } n \geq 2$$
$$B_n = 0 \text{ for all } n$$

Solution:
$$u(x, t) = \sin x \cos t$$

**Physical**: String oscillates in fundamental mode at frequency $\omega_1 = 1$.

**Answer:** $u(x,t) = \sin x \cos t$ (pure fundamental mode)

### Example 13: Energy in Modes

Define energy in mode $n$:
$$E_n = \int_0^L [u_{n,t}^2 + c^2u_{n,x}^2] \, dx$$

**Solution:**

For $u_n = \sin(n\pi x/L)\cos(n\pi ct/L + \phi_n)$:

Kinetic energy: $\propto n^2$ (frequency squared)
Potential energy: $\propto n^2$ (curvature squared)

Total energy per mode: $E_n \propto n^2 A_n^2$ (conserved in time)

By orthogonality: $E_{\text{total}} = \sum E_n$

**Answer:** Energy conserved; partitioned independently among modes

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: B**
Product ansatz: $u(x,t) = X(x)T(t)$
Separates PDE into two ODEs
$\frac{T''}{c^2T} = \frac{X''}{X} = -\lambda$
Spatial: $X'' + \lambda X = 0$
Temporal: $T'' + \lambda c^2T = 0$

**Problem 2: B**
Fundamental frequency: $f_1 = \frac{c}{2L}$
Wavelength: $\lambda_1 = 2L$ (half wavelength fits in string)
Angular frequency: $\omega_1 = \frac{\pi c}{L}$
Period: $T_1 = \frac{2L}{c}$ (round trip time)
Example: $L = 0.5$ m, $c = 400$ m/s → $f_1 = 400$ Hz

**Problem 3: C**
$X_n(x) = \sin(n\pi x/L)$ has zeros at $x = kL/n$
Total zeros: endpoints + interior
Interior nodes: $n - 1$
Mode 1: no interior nodes (fundamental)
Mode 2: one interior node (octave)

**Problem 4: B**
Harmonics: $f_n = nf_1$ for $n = 1, 2, 3, \ldots$
Integer multiples of fundamental
Musical intervals: $f_2 = 2f_1$ (octave), $f_3 = 3f_1$ (fifth above octave)
Consequence of boundary conditions
Non-integer harmonics for other systems (bells, drums)

**Problem 5: C**
Wave equation conserves energy
$E = \int [u_t^2 + c^2u_x^2] \, dx = \text{const}$
Energy sloshes between kinetic and potential
Contrast heat equation (energy decays)
No dissipation in ideal string
