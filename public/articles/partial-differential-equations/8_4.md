# Partial Differential Equations Topic 8.4: Green's Functions for PDEs

Green's functions extend powerfully to partial differential equations, where they become **fundamental solutions**—responses to point sources in space and time. For the heat equation, the Green's function is the temperature field from an instantaneous point heat source. For the wave equation, it's the field from an impulsive point disturbance. For Laplace's equation, it's the potential from a point charge. These fundamental solutions encode all the physics of the PDE.

Computing Green's functions for PDEs is more challenging than for ODEs because they depend on multiple variables and must satisfy boundary conditions on surfaces. The method of images constructs Green's functions by placing sources outside the domain to cancel boundary values. Eigenfunction expansions work when the domain has nice geometry. For infinite domains, Fourier or Laplace transforms reduce the PDE to an ODE whose Green's function we already know.

This article develops Green's functions for PDEs comprehensively. We'll find fundamental solutions for heat, wave, and Laplace equations on infinite domains, use the method of images for half-spaces, employ eigenfunction expansions for bounded domains, and understand physical interpretations. These techniques complete our mastery of linear PDE solution methods.

## Fundamental Solutions

### Example 1: Heat Equation in 1D

Find fundamental solution to $u_t = ku_{xx}$ on $(-\infty, \infty)$.

**Solution:**

**Green's function**: $G(x, t; \xi, \tau)$ satisfies
$$G_t = kG_{xx}$$
with $G(x, \tau; \xi, \tau) = \delta(x - \xi)$

**Fourier transform in $x$**:
$$\hat{G}_t = -k\omega^2\hat{G}$$

$$\hat{G}(\omega, t; \xi, \tau) = \hat{G}(\omega, \tau)e^{-k\omega^2(t-\tau)}$$

At $t = \tau$: $\hat{G} = e^{-i\omega\xi}$ (transform of $\delta(x-\xi)$)

$$\hat{G} = e^{-i\omega\xi}e^{-k\omega^2(t-\tau)}$$

**Inverse transform**:
$$G(x, t; \xi, \tau) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi k(t-\tau)}}e^{-(x-\xi)^2/[4k(t-\tau)]}$$

for $t > \tau$.

**Heat kernel!** Response to point source at $(xi, \tau)$.

**Answer:** $G = \frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi k(t-\tau)}}e^{-(x-\xi)^2/[4k(t-\tau)]}$ for $t > \tau$

### Example 2: Using Heat Kernel

Solve $u_t = ku_{xx}$ with $u(x, 0) = f(x)$.

**Solution:**

**Superposition**:
$$u(x, t) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} G(x, t; \xi, 0)f(\xi) d\xi$$

$$= \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi kt}}e^{-(x-\xi)^2/(4kt)}f(\xi) d\xi$$

**Convolution** with heat kernel.

**Answer:** $u = \int G(x,t;\xi,0)f(\xi) d\xi$

### Example 3: Wave Equation in 1D

Fundamental solution for $u_{tt} = c^2u_{xx}$.

**Solution:**

**Green's function** satisfies wave equation with
$$G(x, 0; \xi, 0) = 0, \quad G_t(x, 0; \xi, 0) = \delta(x - \xi)$$

**D'Alembert formula** with impulse initial velocity:
$$G(x, t; \xi, 0) = \frac{1}{2c}[H(x - \xi + ct) - H(x - \xi - ct)]$$

$$= \begin{cases} \frac{1}{2c} & |x - \xi| < ct \\ 0 & |x - \xi| > ct \end{cases}$$

**Traveling rectangular pulse** spreading at speed $c$.

**Answer:** $G = \frac{1}{2c}$ for $|x-\xi| < ct$, zero otherwise

### Example 4: Wave Equation in 3D

Most important: $u_{tt} = c^2\nabla^2 u$ in 3D.

**Solution:**

**Fourier-Laplace transform** (or spherical symmetry):

$$G(\vec{x}, t; \vec{\xi}, 0) = \frac{\delta(t - |\vec{x}-\vec{\xi}|/c)}{4\pi|\vec{x} - \vec{\xi}|}$$

**Sharp wavefront** at $t = r/c$ where $r = |\vec{x} - \vec{\xi}|$.

**Huygens' principle**: Disturbance arrives precisely at $t = r/c$, no tail!

**Physical**: This is why sound is sharp in 3D.

**Contrast 2D**: Wave equation in 2D has diffuse tail (no Huygens').

**Answer:** $G = \frac{\delta(t-r/c)}{4\pi r}$ (sharp spherical wave)

### Example 5: Laplace's Equation in 3D

Fundamental solution (Coulomb potential).

**Solution:**

For $\nabla^2 G = \delta(\vec{x} - \vec{\xi})$:

**Spherical symmetry**: $G$ depends only on $r = |\vec{x} - \vec{\xi}|$.

Away from origin: $\nabla^2 G = \frac{1}{r^2}\frac{d}{dr}\left(r^2\frac{dG}{dr}\right) = 0$

$$r^2G' = C$$

$$G = -\frac{C}{r} + D$$

**Integrate over sphere**: $\int_{\text{sphere}} \nabla^2 G dV = \int_{\text{sphere}} \delta dV = 1$

By divergence theorem: $\int \nabla G \cdot d\vec{S} = 4\pi r^2 G'(r) = 1$

$$G'(r) = \frac{1}{4\pi r^2}$$

$$G(r) = -\frac{1}{4\pi r}$$

**Coulomb/Newton potential** (up to sign/constants).

**Answer:** $G(\vec{x},\vec{\xi}) = -\frac{1}{4\pi|\vec{x}-\vec{\xi}|}$

## Method of Images

### Example 6: Laplace on Half-Space

Solve $\nabla^2 u = 0$ for $z > 0$ with $u(x, y, 0) = f(x, y)$.

**Solution:**

**Need Green's function** with $G = 0$ on $z = 0$ plane.

**Method of images**: Place source at $\vec{\xi} = (\xi, \eta, \zeta)$ and **image source** at $\vec{\xi}' = (\xi, \eta, -\zeta)$ (mirror).

$$G = -\frac{1}{4\pi|\vec{x} - \vec{\xi}|} + \frac{1}{4\pi|\vec{x} - \vec{\xi}'|}$$

**On $z = 0$ plane**: $|\vec{x} - \vec{\xi}| = |\vec{x} - \vec{\xi}'|$, so $G = 0$ ✓

**For $z > 0$**: First term is physical source, second cancels on boundary.

**Answer:** $G = -\frac{1}{4\pi r} + \frac{1}{4\pi r'}$ where $r' = |\vec{x}-\vec{\xi}'|$

### Example 7: Heat Equation on Half-Line

$u_t = ku_{xx}$ for $x > 0$ with $u(0, t) = 0$, $u(x, 0) = f(x)$.

**Solution:**

**Green's function with image**:
$$G(x, t; \xi, \tau) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi k(t-\tau)}}\left[e^{-(x-\xi)^2/[4k(t-\tau)]} - e^{-(x+\xi)^2/[4k(t-\tau)]}\right]$$

**Check**: At $x = 0$: terms cancel → $G(0, t) = 0$ ✓

**Solution**:
$$u(x, t) = \int_0^{\infty} G(x, t; \xi, 0)f(\xi) d\xi$$

**Physical**: Image source creates exactly the right cancellation.

**Answer:** $G = G_{\text{free}} - G_{\text{image}}$ (sources at $\pm\xi$)

### Example 8: Multiple Images

Laplace on $[0, a] \times [0, b]$ rectangle (in 2D).

**Solution:**

**Four walls**: Need images to cancel on all boundaries.

**Infinite array** of images at positions:
$$(\xi_{mn}, \eta_{mn}) = (2ma \pm \xi, 2nb \pm \eta)$$

for all integers $m, n$.

$$G = \sum_{m,n} \pm \frac{\ln r_{mn}}{2\pi}$$

where $r_{mn} = |(\vec{x} - \vec{\xi}_{mn})|$ and signs alternate.

**Complicated** but systematic!

**Answer:** Infinite lattice of image sources

## Eigenfunction Expansion Method

### Example 9: Laplace on Disk

$\nabla^2 u = f$ inside disk $r < a$ with $u(a, \theta) = 0$.

**Solution:**

**Eigenfunctions** from Laplace eigenvalue problem:

$$\nabla^2\phi + \lambda\phi = 0, \quad \phi(a, \theta) = 0$$

In polar: $\phi_{nm}(r, \theta) = J_n(\alpha_{nm}r/a)[\cos(n\theta), \sin(n\theta)]$

where $J_n(\alpha_{nm}) = 0$.

**Green's function**:
$$G(r, \theta; \rho, \phi) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty} \frac{\phi_{nm}(r,\theta)\phi_{nm}(\rho,\phi)}{\lambda_{nm}\|\phi_{nm}\|^2}$$

**Solution**:
$$u = \int_0^a\int_0^{2\pi} G(r,\theta;\rho,\phi)f(\rho,\phi)\rho \, d\rho \, d\phi$$

**Answer:** Double sum over Bessel function products

### Example 10: Poisson Kernel for Disk

Specific case: $\nabla^2 u = 0$ with $u(a, \theta) = f(\theta)$.

**Solution:**

Green's function for Laplace leads to **Poisson kernel**:

$$u(r, \theta) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} \frac{a^2 - r^2}{a^2 - 2ar\cos(\theta - \phi) + r^2}f(\phi) d\phi$$

**Kernel**:
$$P(r, \theta; \phi) = \frac{a^2 - r^2}{a^2 - 2ar\cos(\theta-\phi) + r^2}$$

**Properties**: $P \geq 0$, $\int P d\phi = 2\pi$, concentrates as $r \to a$.

**Answer:** Poisson kernel expresses harmonic function via boundary values

### Example 11: Heat Equation on Finite Interval

$u_t = ku_{xx}$ on $[0, L]$ with $u(0,t) = u(L,t) = 0$, $u(x,0) = f(x)$.

**Solution:**

**Eigenfunctions**: $\phi_n = \sin(n\pi x/L)$, $\lambda_n = (n\pi/L)^2$

**Green's function** (space-time):
$$G(x, t; \xi, \tau) = \frac{2}{L}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} e^{-k(n\pi/L)^2(t-\tau)}\sin(n\pi x/L)\sin(n\pi\xi/L)$$

**Solution**:
$$u(x, t) = \int_0^L G(x, t; \xi, 0)f(\xi) d\xi$$

**Each mode decays** exponentially with rate $k(n\pi/L)^2$.

**Answer:** $G = \frac{2}{L}\sum e^{-k\lambda_n(t-\tau)}\sin(n\pi x/L)\sin(n\pi\xi/L)$

## Higher Dimensions and Advanced Topics

### Example 12: Heat Equation in 3D

Fundamental solution for $u_t = k\nabla^2 u$ in $\mathbb{R}^3$.

**Solution:**

**Product of 1D kernels**:
$$G(\vec{x}, t; \vec{\xi}, \tau) = \frac{1}{[4\pi k(t-\tau)]^{3/2}}e^{-|\vec{x}-\vec{\xi}|^2/[4k(t-\tau)]}$$

**Gaussian in all three directions**.

**Normalization**: $\int_{\mathbb{R}^3} G d^3x = 1$ (conserves initial data).

**Spreading**: Variance $\sigma^2(t) = 2dk(t-\tau)$ where $d = 3$ is dimension.

**Answer:** $G = \frac{1}{[4\pi k(t-\tau)]^{3/2}}e^{-r^2/[4k(t-\tau)]}$

### Example 13: Retarded Green's Function

For wave equation, **causal** Green's function propagates forward in time.

**Solution:**

**Retarded**: $G = 0$ for $t < \tau$ (no signal before source).

**Advanced**: $G = 0$ for $t > \tau$ (acausal, unphysical).

**Physical applications** always use retarded Green's function.

**Example** (3D wave):
$$G_{\text{ret}} = \frac{\delta(t - \tau - r/c)}{4\pi r}$$

Signal arrives at time $\tau + r/c$ (speed $c$).

**Causality** built into formulation.

**Answer:** Retarded $G$ has support only for $t > \tau$

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: C**
Heat kernel: $G(x,t;\xi,\tau) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi k(t-\tau)}}e^{-(x-\xi)^2/[4k(t-\tau)]}$
Fundamental solution for $u_t = ku_{xx}$
Gaussian spreading with time
Variance: $\sigma^2 = 2k(t-\tau)$
Normalization: $\int G dx = 1$

**Problem 2: B**
Method of images: place imaginary sources outside domain
Chosen to satisfy boundary conditions
Example: half-space—mirror source across boundary
Can require infinite array (rectangular domains)
Alternative to eigenfunction expansion

**Problem 3: B**
3D: $G = \frac{\delta(t-r/c)}{4\pi r}$ (sharp front)
Huygens' principle in odd dimensions (1D, 3D)
2D has persistent tail (no Huygens)
Even dimensions: wake behind wavefront
Sharp signals in 3D (sound, light)

**Problem 4: A**
Poisson kernel: $u(r,\theta) = \int P(r,\theta;\phi)f(\phi) d\phi$
Expresses interior harmonic function via boundary
Kernel: $P = \frac{a^2-r^2}{a^2-2ar\cos(\theta-\phi)+r^2}$
$u$ equals weighted average of boundary values
Weights concentrate near $\theta = \phi$ as $r \to a$

**Problem 5: B**
Retarded: causality—effect after cause
$G = 0$ for $t < \tau$ (no signal before source)
Physical systems use retarded $G$
Advanced $G$ (acausal): $G = 0$ for $t > \tau$
Wave equation: signal arrives at $t = \tau + r/c$
