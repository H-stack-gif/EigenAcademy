# Partial Differential Equations Topic 7.1: Fourier Transform Fundamentals

Fourier series work beautifully for periodic functions on finite intervals—they express functions as sums of discrete frequencies. But what about functions on the entire real line, like a single pulse traveling along an infinite string? The **Fourier transform** extends Fourier analysis to non-periodic functions, replacing the discrete sum with a continuous integral over all frequencies. This transforms time-domain signals into frequency-domain representations.

The Fourier transform $\hat{f}(\omega) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(x)e^{-i\omega x} dx$ decomposes a function into its frequency components. Each frequency $\omega$ contributes with amplitude $\hat{f}(\omega)$. The **inverse transform** reconstructs the original function: $f(x) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \hat{f}(\omega)e^{i\omega x} d\omega$. This duality between space and frequency is fundamental to signal processing, quantum mechanics, and PDE theory.

This article develops the Fourier transform systematically. We'll define forward and inverse transforms, compute transforms of standard functions, discover key properties (linearity, shift theorem, scaling), derive the crucial convolution theorem, and understand Parseval's identity. These tools will unlock solutions to PDEs on infinite domains.

## Definition and Basic Properties

### Example 1: Fourier Transform Definition

For function $f(x)$ on $(-\infty, \infty)$:

**Solution:**

**Fourier transform**:
$$\hat{f}(\omega) = \mathcal{F}\{f\}(\omega) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(x)e^{-i\omega x} \, dx$$

**Inverse Fourier transform**:
$$f(x) = \mathcal{F}^{-1}\{\hat{f}\}(x) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \hat{f}(\omega)e^{i\omega x} \, d\omega$$

**Variables**:
- $x$: spatial variable (or time)
- $\omega$: frequency variable (angular frequency)

**Convention note**: Some texts use $2\pi$ factor differently; we follow physics convention.

**Answer:** $\hat{f}(\omega) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(x)e^{-i\omega x} dx$

### Example 2: Transform of Gaussian

Compute Fourier transform of $f(x) = e^{-ax^2}$ where $a > 0$.

**Solution:**

$$\hat{f}(\omega) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-ax^2}e^{-i\omega x} \, dx$$

Complete the square in exponent:
$$-ax^2 - i\omega x = -a\left(x + \frac{i\omega}{2a}\right)^2 - \frac{\omega^2}{4a}$$

$$\hat{f}(\omega) = e^{-\omega^2/(4a)}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-a(x + i\omega/(2a))^2} \, dx$$

By contour integration (or standard result):
$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-az^2} \, dz = \sqrt{\frac{\pi}{a}}$$

$$\hat{f}(\omega) = \sqrt{\frac{\pi}{a}} e^{-\omega^2/(4a)}$$

**Remarkable**: Gaussian transforms to Gaussian!

**Answer:** $\hat{f}(\omega) = \sqrt{\pi/a} \, e^{-\omega^2/(4a)}$

### Example 3: Transform of Rectangular Pulse

$$f(x) = \begin{cases} 1 & |x| < a \\ 0 & |x| > a \end{cases}$$

**Solution:**

$$\hat{f}(\omega) = \int_{-a}^{a} e^{-i\omega x} \, dx = \left[-\frac{e^{-i\omega x}}{i\omega}\right]_{-a}^{a}$$

$$= \frac{e^{i\omega a} - e^{-i\omega a}}{i\omega} = \frac{2\sin(\omega a)}{\omega}$$

Define **sinc function**: $\text{sinc}(x) = \frac{\sin x}{x}$

$$\hat{f}(\omega) = 2a \, \text{sinc}(\omega a)$$

**Physical**: Rectangular pulse in space → sinc in frequency.

**Answer:** $\hat{f}(\omega) = \frac{2\sin(\omega a)}{\omega}$

### Example 4: Transform of Exponential Decay

For $a > 0$:
$$f(x) = \begin{cases} e^{-ax} & x > 0 \\ 0 & x < 0 \end{cases}$$

**Solution:**

$$\hat{f}(\omega) = \int_{0}^{\infty} e^{-ax}e^{-i\omega x} \, dx = \int_{0}^{\infty} e^{-(a+i\omega)x} \, dx$$

$$= \left[-\frac{e^{-(a+i\omega)x}}{a+i\omega}\right]_0^{\infty} = \frac{1}{a+i\omega}$$

**Rationalize**:
$$\hat{f}(\omega) = \frac{1}{a+i\omega} \cdot \frac{a-i\omega}{a-i\omega} = \frac{a-i\omega}{a^2+\omega^2}$$

**Real and imaginary parts**:
- Real: $\frac{a}{a^2+\omega^2}$ (Lorentzian)
- Imaginary: $\frac{-\omega}{a^2+\omega^2}$

**Answer:** $\hat{f}(\omega) = \frac{1}{a+i\omega}$

## Properties of Fourier Transform

### Example 5: Linearity

If $\mathcal{F}\{f\} = \hat{f}$ and $\mathcal{F}\{g\} = \hat{g}$:

**Solution:**

$$\mathcal{F}\{af + bg\} = a\hat{f} + b\hat{g}$$

**Proof**:
$$\mathcal{F}\{af + bg\} = \int (af + bg)e^{-i\omega x} dx = a\int f e^{-i\omega x} dx + b\int g e^{-i\omega x} dx$$

**Answer:** Transform is linear operator

### Example 6: Shift Theorem (Translation)

If $\mathcal{F}\{f(x)\} = \hat{f}(\omega)$, find $\mathcal{F}\{f(x-a)\}$.

**Solution:**

$$\mathcal{F}\{f(x-a)\} = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(x-a)e^{-i\omega x} \, dx$$

Substitute $u = x - a$:
$$= \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(u)e^{-i\omega(u+a)} \, du = e^{-i\omega a}\int f(u)e^{-i\omega u} \, du$$

$$= e^{-i\omega a}\hat{f}(\omega)$$

**Physical**: Spatial shift → phase shift in frequency.

**Answer:** $\mathcal{F}\{f(x-a)\} = e^{-i\omega a}\hat{f}(\omega)$

### Example 7: Scaling Property

Find $\mathcal{F}\{f(ax)\}$ where $a > 0$.

**Solution:**

$$\mathcal{F}\{f(ax)\} = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(ax)e^{-i\omega x} \, dx$$

Substitute $u = ax$, $dx = du/a$:
$$= \frac{1}{a}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(u)e^{-i(\omega/a)u} \, du = \frac{1}{a}\hat{f}(\omega/a)$$

**Interpretation**: Compression in space ($a > 1$) → expansion in frequency.

**Uncertainty principle connection**: Narrow pulse → wide spectrum.

**Answer:** $\mathcal{F}\{f(ax)\} = \frac{1}{a}\hat{f}(\omega/a)$

### Example 8: Transform of Derivative

Find $\mathcal{F}\{f'(x)\}$.

**Solution:**

$$\mathcal{F}\{f'\} = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f'(x)e^{-i\omega x} \, dx$$

Integration by parts:
$$= \left[f(x)e^{-i\omega x}\right]_{-\infty}^{\infty} + i\omega\int f(x)e^{-i\omega x} \, dx$$

Assuming $f \to 0$ as $|x| \to \infty$:
$$= i\omega \hat{f}(\omega)$$

**General**: $\mathcal{F}\{f^{(n)}\} = (i\omega)^n\hat{f}(\omega)$

**Key insight**: Differentiation in space ↔ multiplication by $i\omega$ in frequency.

**Answer:** $\mathcal{F}\{f'\} = i\omega\hat{f}(\omega)$

### Example 9: Transform of Multiplication by $x$

Find $\mathcal{F}\{xf(x)\}$.

**Solution:**

Note: $\frac{d}{d\omega}e^{-i\omega x} = -ixe^{-i\omega x}$

$$\frac{d\hat{f}}{d\omega} = \frac{d}{d\omega}\int f(x)e^{-i\omega x} dx = \int f(x)(-ix)e^{-i\omega x} dx = -i\mathcal{F}\{xf\}$$

$$\mathcal{F}\{xf(x)\} = i\frac{d\hat{f}}{d\omega}$$

**Duality**: Multiplication by $x$ ↔ differentiation in frequency.

**Answer:** $\mathcal{F}\{xf\} = i\hat{f}'(\omega)$

## Convolution Theorem

### Example 10: Convolution Definition

**Convolution** of $f$ and $g$:
$$(f * g)(x) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(x-y)g(y) \, dy$$

**Solution:**

**Properties**:
- Commutative: $f * g = g * f$
- Associative: $f * (g * h) = (f * g) * h$
- Distributive: $f * (g + h) = f * g + f * h$

**Physical meaning**: Output of linear system with impulse response $g$ to input $f$.

**Answer:** $(f*g)(x) = \int f(x-y)g(y) dy$

### Example 11: Convolution Theorem

**Theorem**: $\mathcal{F}\{f * g\} = \hat{f}(\omega)\hat{g}(\omega)$

**Solution:**

$$\mathcal{F}\{f*g\} = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \left[\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(x-y)g(y) dy\right] e^{-i\omega x} dx$$

Change order of integration:
$$= \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} g(y)\left[\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(x-y)e^{-i\omega x} dx\right] dy$$

Substitute $u = x - y$:
$$= \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} g(y)e^{-i\omega y}\left[\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(u)e^{-i\omega u} du\right] dy$$

$$= \hat{f}(\omega)\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} g(y)e^{-i\omega y} dy = \hat{f}(\omega)\hat{g}(\omega)$$

**Key result**: Convolution in space ↔ multiplication in frequency.

**Answer:** $\mathcal{F}\{f*g\} = \hat{f} \cdot \hat{g}$

### Example 12: Application of Convolution Theorem

Heat kernel: $G(x,t) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi kt}}e^{-x^2/(4kt)}$

Solution: $u(x,t) = (G * f)(x)$ where $f$ is initial condition.

**Solution:**

In Fourier space:
$$\hat{u}(\omega, t) = \hat{G}(\omega, t)\hat{f}(\omega)$$

Gaussian transform: $\hat{G}(\omega, t) = e^{-k\omega^2 t}$

$$\hat{u}(\omega, t) = e^{-k\omega^2 t}\hat{f}(\omega)$$

**Physical**: Each frequency component decays exponentially as $e^{-k\omega^2 t}$.

High frequencies ($|\omega|$ large) decay faster!

**Answer:** Convolution with heat kernel = multiplication by $e^{-k\omega^2 t}$ in frequency

## Parseval's Identity

### Example 13: Energy in Fourier Space

**Parseval's identity**:
$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} |f(x)|^2 \, dx = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} |\hat{f}(\omega)|^2 \, d\omega$$

**Solution:**

**Physical interpretation**:
- Left side: Total energy in spatial domain
- Right side: Total energy in frequency domain

**Energy is conserved** under Fourier transform!

**Proof sketch**: Use inverse transform and properties of complex exponentials.

**Application**: Signal processing—total power same in time and frequency.

**Answer:** Energy conserved: $\int |f|^2 = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int |\hat{f}|^2$

$\mathcal{F}\{e^{-ax^2}\} = \sqrt{\pi/a} \, e^{-\omega^2/(4a)}$
Gaussian transforms to Gaussian
Self-similar under Fourier transform
Width in space inversely related to width in frequency
Uncertainty principle: $\Delta x \cdot \Delta \omega \sim 1$

**Problem 2: B**
$\mathcal{F}\{f'(x)\} = i\omega\hat{f}(\omega)$
Differentiation → multiplication by $i\omega$
Higher derivatives: $\mathcal{F}\{f^{(n)}\} = (i\omega)^n\hat{f}$
Crucial for solving differential equations
Turns derivatives into algebra

**Problem 3: C**
Convolution theorem: $\mathcal{F}\{f*g\} = \hat{f}(\omega) \cdot \hat{g}(\omega)$
Convolution in space ↔ multiplication in frequency
Powerful tool for solving PDEs
Linear systems: output = input × transfer function
Efficient computation via FFT
