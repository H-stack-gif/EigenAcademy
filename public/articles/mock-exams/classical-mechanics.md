## Free Response Solutions

### FRQ 1 Solutions

**(a)** In the rotating frame rotating with angular velocity $\omega$:

Position in lab frame: $\mathbf{r} = r(\cos(\omega t + \theta_0)\hat{\mathbf{x}} + \sin(\omega t + \theta_0)\hat{\mathbf{y}})$

In rotating frame with $\theta = 0$ (particle at fixed angle in rotating frame):

**Velocity in lab frame:**
$$v^2 = \dot{r}^2 + r^2\omega^2$$

**Kinetic energy:**
$$T = \frac{1}{2}m(\dot{r}^2 + r^2\omega^2)$$

**Potential energy:** Spring plus centrifugal potential
$$V = \frac{1}{2}kr^2 - \frac{1}{2}m\omega^2r^2$$

The centrifugal potential is $V_{cf} = -\frac{1}{2}m\omega^2r^2$.

**(b)** **Lagrangian:**
$$L = T - V = \frac{1}{2}m\dot{r}^2 + \frac{1}{2}m\omega^2r^2 - \frac{1}{2}kr^2$$

$$L = \frac{1}{2}m\dot{r}^2 - \frac{1}{2}(k - m\omega^2)r^2$$

**Euler-Lagrange equation:**
$$\frac{\partial L}{\partial r} = -(k - m\omega^2)r$$

$$\frac{d}{dt}\frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot{r}} = m\ddot{r}$$

$$-(k - m\omega^2)r - m\ddot{r} = 0$$

$$m\ddot{r} + (k - m\omega^2)r = 0$$

$$\ddot{r} + \left(\frac{k}{m} - \omega^2\right)r = 0$$

**(c)** **Equilibrium:** Setting $\ddot{r} = 0$ and $\dot{r} = 0$ in the equation:

From part (b), equilibrium requires force balance. For the equation $\ddot{r} + (k/m - \omega^2)r = 0$:

If $k/m - \omega^2 > 0$: Only equilibrium is $r_0 = 0$ (center)

If $k/m - \omega^2 = 0$: Critical case, $\omega = \sqrt{k/m}$

If $k/m - \omega^2 < 0$: System is unstable; particle flies outward

For stable equilibrium at $r_0 = 0$, need:
$$\omega < \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}$$

For $\omega > \sqrt{k/m}$, there is no stable equilibrium—centrifugal force exceeds spring restoring force.

**(d)** For small oscillations about $r_0 = 0$, the equation from part (b) already has the form:
$$\ddot{r} + \omega_{osc}^2 r = 0$$

where
$$\omega_{osc}^2 = \frac{k}{m} - \omega^2$$

$$\omega_{osc} = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m} - \omega^2}$$

**Physical interpretation:** As $\omega$ increases, $\omega_{osc}$ decreases. When $\omega \to \sqrt{k/m}$, the oscillation frequency goes to zero (critical case). For $\omega > \sqrt{k/m}$, the system becomes unstable.

**Alternative interpretation:** The effective spring constant is $k_{eff} = k - m\omega^2$. Rotation weakens the effective restoring force.

### FRQ 2 Solutions

**(a)** **Center of mass:** $\mathbf{R}_{CM} = \frac{m_1\mathbf{r}_1 + m_2\mathbf{r}_2}{m_1 + m_2}$

**Relative coordinate:** $\mathbf{r} = \mathbf{r}_1 - \mathbf{r}_2$

Express individual positions:
$$\mathbf{r}_1 = \mathbf{R}_{CM} + \frac{m_2}{m_1+m_2}\mathbf{r}$$

$$\mathbf{r}_2 = \mathbf{R}_{CM} - \frac{m_1}{m_1+m_2}\mathbf{r}$$

**Kinetic energy:**
$$T = \frac{1}{2}m_1\dot{\mathbf{r}}_1^2 + \frac{1}{2}m_2\dot{\mathbf{r}}_2^2 = \frac{1}{2}(m_1+m_2)\dot{\mathbf{R}}_{CM}^2 + \frac{1}{2}\mu\dot{\mathbf{r}}^2$$

where **reduced mass:** $\mu = \frac{m_1m_2}{m_1+m_2}$

**Potential:** $V = U(r) = -k/r$ (depends only on relative coordinate)

**Lagrangian separates:**
$$L = \frac{1}{2}M\dot{\mathbf{R}}_{CM}^2 + \left[\frac{1}{2}\mu\dot{\mathbf{r}}^2 + \frac{k}{r}\right]$$

where $M = m_1 + m_2$.

**(b)** In polar coordinates $(r, \theta)$:

**Kinetic energy:**
$$T = \frac{1}{2}\mu(\dot{r}^2 + r^2\dot{\theta}^2)$$

**Lagrangian:**
$$L = \frac{1}{2}\mu\dot{r}^2 + \frac{1}{2}\mu r^2\dot{\theta}^2 + \frac{k}{r}$$

**Cyclic coordinate:** $\theta$ doesn't appear in $L$ (only $\dot{\theta}$ does)

**Conserved quantity:** Conjugate momentum
$$p_\theta = \frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot{\theta}} = \mu r^2\dot{\theta} = L$$

This is angular momentum!

**Equation of motion for $r$:**

$$\frac{\partial L}{\partial r} = \mu r\dot{\theta}^2 - \frac{k}{r^2}$$

$$\frac{d}{dt}\frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot{r}} = \mu\ddot{r}$$

$$\mu r\dot{\theta}^2 - \frac{k}{r^2} - \mu\ddot{r} = 0$$

$$\mu\ddot{r} = \mu r\dot{\theta}^2 - \frac{k}{r^2}$$

Using $L = \mu r^2\dot{\theta}$:
$$\mu\ddot{r} = \frac{L^2}{\mu r^3} - \frac{k}{r^2}$$

**(c)** **Effective potential:**
$$U_{eff}(r) = -\frac{k}{r} + \frac{L^2}{2\mu r^2}$$

**Analysis:**
- As $r \to 0$: $U_{eff} \to +\infty$ (centrifugal barrier dominates)
- As $r \to \infty$: $U_{eff} \to 0^-$ (approaches zero from below)

**Minimum:** Set $dU_{eff}/dr = 0$:
$$\frac{dU_{eff}}{dr} = \frac{k}{r^2} - \frac{L^2}{\mu r^3} = 0$$

$$r_0 = \frac{L^2}{\mu k}$$

**Value at minimum:**
$$U_{eff}(r_0) = -\frac{k}{L^2/(\mu k)} + \frac{L^2}{2\mu(L^2/(\mu k))^2} = -\frac{\mu k^2}{L^2} + \frac{\mu k^2}{2L^2} = -\frac{\mu k^2}{2L^2}$$

**Sketch:** Should show:
- Vertical asymptote at $r = 0$
- Minimum at $r_0$
- Approaches 0 from below as $r \to \infty$

**(d)** **Circular orbit condition:** At $r_0$, must have $\ddot{r} = 0$ and $\dot{r} = 0$.

From effective potential minimum (part c): $r_0 = \frac{L^2}{\mu k}$

**Energy:** For circular orbit at $r_0$:
$$E = \frac{1}{2}\mu\dot{r}^2 + U_{eff}(r_0)$$

Since $\dot{r} = 0$ for circular orbit:
$$E = U_{eff}(r_0) = -\frac{\mu k^2}{2L^2}$$

Using $r_0 = L^2/(\mu k)$, we can write $L^2 = \mu k r_0$:
$$E = -\frac{\mu k^2}{2\mu k r_0} = -\frac{k}{2r_0}$$

**Verification:** Total energy equals
$$E = T + V = \frac{1}{2}\mu v^2 - \frac{k}{r_0}$$

For circular orbit: $\mu v^2/r_0 = k/r_0^2$, so $\mu v^2 = k/r_0$

$$E = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{k}{r_0} - \frac{k}{r_0} = -\frac{k}{2r_0}$$ ✓

### FRQ 3 Solutions

**(a)** **Kinetic energy:**
$$T = \frac{1}{2}m\dot{x}_1^2 + \frac{1}{2}m\dot{x}_2^2 + \frac{1}{2}m\dot{x}_3^2$$

**Matrix form:**
$$T = \frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix} \dot{x}_1 & \dot{x}_2 & \dot{x}_3 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} m & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & m & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & m \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} \dot{x}_1 \\ \dot{x}_2 \\ \dot{x}_3 \end{pmatrix}$$

$$\mathbf{M} = m\mathbf{I} = m\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$$

**Potential energy:** Four springs, each contributes $\frac{1}{2}k(\Delta x)^2$

- Spring 1 (wall to $m_1$): $\frac{1}{2}kx_1^2$
- Spring 2 ($m_1$ to $m_2$): $\frac{1}{2}k(x_2-x_1)^2$
- Spring 3 ($m_2$ to $m_3$): $\frac{1}{2}k(x_3-x_2)^2$
- Spring 4 ($m_3$ to wall): $\frac{1}{2}kx_3^2$

$$V = \frac{1}{2}k[x_1^2 + (x_2-x_1)^2 + (x_3-x_2)^2 + x_3^2]$$

Expand:
$$V = \frac{1}{2}k[2x_1^2 - 2x_1x_2 + 2x_2^2 - 2x_2x_3 + 2x_3^2]$$

$$= k[x_1^2 - x_1x_2 + x_2^2 - x_2x_3 + x_3^2]$$

**Matrix form:**
$$V = \frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix} x_1 & x_2 & x_3 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 2k & -k & 0 \\ -k & 2k & -k \\ 0 & -k & 2k \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} x_1 \\ x_2 \\ x_3 \end{pmatrix}$$

$$\mathbf{K} = k\begin{pmatrix} 2 & -1 & 0 \\ -1 & 2 & -1 \\ 0 & -1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$$

**(b)** **Equation of motion:**
$$\mathbf{M}\ddot{\mathbf{x}} + \mathbf{K}\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{0}$$

$$m\mathbf{I}\ddot{\mathbf{x}} + k\begin{pmatrix} 2 & -1 & 0 \\ -1 & 2 & -1 \\ 0 & -1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{0}$$

**Normal mode ansatz:** $\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{a}e^{i\omega t}$

$$-\omega^2 m\mathbf{a} + k\mathbf{K}_0\mathbf{a} = \mathbf{0}$$

where $\mathbf{K}_0 = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & -1 & 0 \\ -1 & 2 & -1 \\ 0 & -1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$

**Characteristic equation:**
$$\det(\mathbf{K}_0 - \lambda\mathbf{I}) = 0$$

where $\lambda = \omega^2 m/k$.

$$\det\begin{pmatrix} 2-\lambda & -1 & 0 \\ -1 & 2-\lambda & -1 \\ 0 & -1 & 2-\lambda \end{pmatrix} = 0$$

Expand determinant:
$$(2-\lambda)[(2-\lambda)^2 - 1] + 1[-(2-\lambda)] = 0$$

$$(2-\lambda)[(2-\lambda)^2 - 1] - (2-\lambda) = 0$$

$$(2-\lambda)[(2-\lambda)^2 - 1 - 1] = 0$$

$$(2-\lambda)[(2-\lambda)^2 - 2] = 0$$

Solutions: $\lambda = 2$ or $(2-\lambda)^2 = 2$

$\lambda_1 = 2 - \sqrt{2}$, $\lambda_2 = 2$, $\lambda_3 = 2 + \sqrt{2}$

**Frequencies:**
$$\omega_1 = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}(2-\sqrt{2})} = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}(2-\sqrt{2})^{1/2}$$

$$\omega_2 = \sqrt{\frac{2k}{m}}$$

$$\omega_3 = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}(2+\sqrt{2})} = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}(2+\sqrt{2})^{1/2}$$

**(c)** Lowest frequency: $\omega_1^2 = k(2-\sqrt{2})/m$, so $\lambda_1 = 2 - \sqrt{2}$.

**Eigenvector:** Solve $(\mathbf{K}_0 - \lambda_1\mathbf{I})\mathbf{a} = \mathbf{0}$:

$$\begin{pmatrix} \sqrt{2} & -1 & 0 \\ -1 & \sqrt{2} & -1 \\ 0 & -1 & \sqrt{2} \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} a_1 \\ a_2 \\ a_3 \end{pmatrix} = \mathbf{0}$$

From first row: $\sqrt{2}a_1 - a_2 = 0 \implies a_2 = \sqrt{2}a_1$

From second row: $-a_1 + \sqrt{2}a_2 - a_3 = 0$

$$a_3 = -a_1 + \sqrt{2}(\sqrt{2}a_1) = -a_1 + 2a_1 = a_1$$

So $\mathbf{a}^{(1)} \propto \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ \sqrt{2} \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}$

**Physical description:** All three masses move in the same direction, with the middle mass having larger amplitude ($\sqrt{2}$ times the end masses). This is the lowest frequency "sloshing" mode where all masses move together with a smooth pattern.

**(d)** **Orthogonality condition:**
$$(\mathbf{a}^{(i)})^T\mathbf{M}\mathbf{a}^{(j)} = 0 \quad \text{for } i \neq j$$
