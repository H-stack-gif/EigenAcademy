# Complex Analysis Topic 4.2: Taylor Series

Every smooth real function has a Taylor series, but it might not equal the function—$e^{-1/x^2}$ at the origin is the classic counterexample where the Taylor series is identically zero while the function is positive. Complex analysis is radically different: if $f$ is analytic at $z_0$, then $f$ **equals** its Taylor series in a disk around $z_0$. There's no distinction between "having a Taylor series" and "being equal to it." This is the rigidity of analytic functions: near any point, an analytic function is exactly a power series, and knowing all derivatives at one point determines the function throughout a disk.

The Taylor series $f(z) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{f^{(n)}(z_0)}{n!}(z-z_0)^n$ converges in the largest disk centered at $z_0$ containing no singularities of $f$. The radius of convergence equals the distance from $z_0$ to the nearest singularity—a profound connection between local series expansions and global analytic structure. Computing Taylor series gives explicit formulas for functions, enables approximation, and reveals properties like zeros and growth rates.

This article develops Taylor series for complex analytic functions. We'll prove that analytic functions equal their Taylor series, compute Taylor expansions using derivatives and known series, find radii of convergence geometrically, manipulate series to find new expansions, and use Taylor series to analyze function behavior. You'll master finding Taylor series for standard functions, determining convergence regions, and using series representations to compute and approximate function values.

## Definition and Existence

**Taylor Series**: If $f$ is analytic at $z_0$, the Taylor series of $f$ centered at $z_0$ is:

$$f(z) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} a_n(z - z_0)^n$$

where
$$a_n = \frac{f^{(n)}(z_0)}{n!}$$

**Key Theorem**: If $f$ is analytic in $|z - z_0| < R$, then $f$ equals its Taylor series in that disk.

**Radius of convergence**: $R$ equals distance from $z_0$ to nearest singularity.

### Example 1: Taylor Series from Definition

Find the Taylor series of $f(z) = e^z$ centered at $z_0 = 0$.

**Solution:**

$$f(z) = e^z, \quad f'(z) = e^z, \quad f''(z) = e^z, \ldots, \quad f^{(n)}(z) = e^z$$

At $z_0 = 0$:
$$f^{(n)}(0) = e^0 = 1$$ for all $n$

$$a_n = \frac{f^{(n)}(0)}{n!} = \frac{1}{n!}$$

$$e^z = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{z^n}{n!}$$

Radius: $R = \infty$ (no singularities)

**Answer:** $e^z = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{z^n}{n!}$, converges for all $z$

### Example 2: Taylor Series of Sine

Find the Taylor series of $\sin z$ centered at $z = 0$.

**Solution:**

$$f(z) = \sin z$$
$$f'(z) = \cos z, \quad f''(z) = -\sin z, \quad f'''(z) = -\cos z, \quad f^{(4)}(z) = \sin z$$

At $z = 0$:
$$f(0) = 0, \quad f'(0) = 1, \quad f''(0) = 0, \quad f'''(0) = -1, \quad f^{(4)}(0) = 0$$

Pattern: $f^{(2k)}(0) = 0$ and $f^{(2k+1)}(0) = (-1)^k$

$$\sin z = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^k}{(2k+1)!}z^{2k+1} = z - \frac{z^3}{3!} + \frac{z^5}{5!} - \frac{z^7}{7!} + \cdots$$

**Answer:** $\sin z = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^k z^{2k+1}}{(2k+1)!}$, converges for all $z$

### Example 3: Taylor Series at Non-Zero Point

Find the Taylor series of $f(z) = \frac{1}{z}$ centered at $z_0 = 1$.

**Solution:**

$$f(z) = \frac{1}{z}, \quad f'(z) = -\frac{1}{z^2}, \quad f''(z) = \frac{2}{z^3}, \quad f^{(n)}(z) = \frac{(-1)^n n!}{z^{n+1}}$$

At $z_0 = 1$:
$$f^{(n)}(1) = (-1)^n n!$$

$$a_n = \frac{f^{(n)}(1)}{n!} = (-1)^n$$

$$\frac{1}{z} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} (-1)^n(z-1)^n$$

Radius: Distance from $z_0 = 1$ to nearest singularity (at $z=0$) is $|1-0| = 1$.

So $R = 1$.

**Answer:** $\frac{1}{z} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} (-1)^n(z-1)^n$ for $|z-1| < 1$

### Example 4: Verifying with Geometric Series

Verify Example 3 using geometric series.

**Solution:**

$$\frac{1}{z} = \frac{1}{1 + (z-1)} = \frac{1}{1 - (-(z-1))}$$

This is geometric series with ratio $r = -(z-1)$:

$$\frac{1}{1-r} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} r^n$$

$$\frac{1}{z} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} [-(z-1)]^n = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} (-1)^n(z-1)^n$$ ✓

Converges when $|-(z-1)| < 1$, i.e., $|z-1| < 1$ ✓

**Answer:** Verified using geometric series

## Finding Taylor Series from Known Series

Often easier to manipulate known Taylor series than compute derivatives.

### Example 5: Series for $\cos z$

Find Taylor series of $\cos z$ from $\sin z$.

**Solution:**

Since $\cos z = \frac{d}{dz}\sin z$:

$$\cos z = \frac{d}{dz}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^k z^{2k+1}}{(2k+1)!}\right)$$
$$= \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^k (2k+1)z^{2k}}{(2k+1)!}$$
$$= \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^k z^{2k}}{(2k)!}$$

$$= 1 - \frac{z^2}{2!} + \frac{z^4}{4!} - \frac{z^6}{6!} + \cdots$$

**Answer:** $\cos z = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^k z^{2k}}{(2k)!}$

### Example 6: Series for $e^{2z}$

Find Taylor series of $e^{2z}$ using $e^z$.

**Solution:**

$$e^z = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{z^n}{n!}$$

Substitute $2z$ for $z$:

$$e^{2z} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(2z)^n}{n!} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{2^n z^n}{n!}$$

**Answer:** $e^{2z} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{2^n z^n}{n!}$

### Example 7: Series for $\frac{1}{1+z^2}$

Find Taylor series centered at $z = 0$.

**Solution:**

$$\frac{1}{1+z^2} = \frac{1}{1-(-z^2)}$$

Geometric series with ratio $r = -z^2$:

$$\frac{1}{1+z^2} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} (-z^2)^n = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} (-1)^n z^{2n}$$

$$= 1 - z^2 + z^4 - z^6 + \cdots$$

Converges when $|-z^2| < 1$, i.e., $|z| < 1$.

**Answer:** $\frac{1}{1+z^2} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} (-1)^n z^{2n}$ for $|z| < 1$

### Example 8: Product of Series

Find first few terms of Taylor series for $e^z \sin z$.

**Solution:**

$$e^z = 1 + z + \frac{z^2}{2} + \frac{z^3}{6} + \cdots$$

$$\sin z = z - \frac{z^3}{6} + \cdots$$

Multiply:
$$e^z \sin z = \left(1 + z + \frac{z^2}{2} + \frac{z^3}{6} + \cdots\right)\left(z - \frac{z^3}{6} + \cdots\right)$$

$$= z + z^2 + \frac{z^3}{2} - \frac{z^3}{6} + \cdots$$
$$= z + z^2 + \frac{z^3}{3} + \cdots$$

**Answer:** $e^z \sin z = z + z^2 + \frac{z^3}{3} + \cdots$

## Radius of Convergence and Singularities

**Geometric Principle**: Radius of convergence = distance to nearest singularity.

### Example 9: Finding Radius Geometrically

For $f(z) = \frac{1}{z^2 + 1}$ centered at $z_0 = 0$, find radius of convergence.

**Solution:**

Singularities: $z^2 + 1 = 0 \implies z = \pm i$

Distance from $z_0 = 0$ to singularities:
$$|i - 0| = |i| = 1$$
$$|-i - 0| = |-i| = 1$$

Nearest singularity is distance $1$ away.

**Answer:** $R = 1$

### Example 10: Radius at Different Center

For same function $f(z) = \frac{1}{z^2+1}$ centered at $z_0 = 2$, find radius.

**Solution:**

Singularities still at $z = \pm i$.

Distance from $z_0 = 2$:
$$|i - 2| = |2 - i| = \sqrt{4 + 1} = \sqrt{5}$$
$$|-i - 2| = |2 + i| = \sqrt{4 + 1} = \sqrt{5}$$

**Answer:** $R = \sqrt{5}$

### Example 11: Multiple Singularities

For $f(z) = \frac{1}{z(z-3)}$ centered at $z_0 = 1$, find radius.

**Solution:**

Singularities at $z = 0$ and $z = 3$.

Distance from $z_0 = 1$:
- To $z = 0$: $|0 - 1| = 1$
- To $z = 3$: $|3 - 1| = 2$

Nearest singularity determines radius: $R = \min(1, 2) = 1$

**Answer:** $R = 1$ (limited by singularity at $z=0$)

### Example 12: Entire Function

For $f(z) = e^{z^2}$ centered at any $z_0$, find radius.

**Solution:**

$e^{z^2}$ is entire (composition of entire functions).

No singularities anywhere in $\mathbb{C}$.

Distance to nearest singularity = $\infty$.

**Answer:** $R = \infty$ for any center

## Uniqueness of Taylor Series

**Uniqueness Theorem**: If $\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} a_n(z-z_0)^n = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} b_n(z-z_0)^n$ in a neighborhood of $z_0$, then $a_n = b_n$ for all $n$.

**Consequence**: If $f(z) = g(z)$ in a neighborhood, all derivatives match: $f^{(n)}(z_0) = g^{(n)}(z_0)$.

### Example 13: Identifying Coefficients

If $\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} a_n z^n = \frac{1}{1-z}$ for $|z| < 1$, find $a_n$.

**Solution:**

We know:
$$\frac{1}{1-z} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} z^n$$

By uniqueness: $a_n = 1$ for all $n$.

**Answer:** $a_n = 1$

### Example 14: Extracting Coefficients

If $f(z) = 3 + 2z + z^2 - 4z^3 + \cdots$, find $f''(0)$.

**Solution:**

Taylor series: $f(z) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{f^{(n)}(0)}{n!}z^n$

Coefficient of $z^2$ is $\frac{f''(0)}{2!} = 1$

$$f''(0) = 2! \cdot 1 = 2$$

**Answer:** $f''(0) = 2$

### Example 15: Verifying Identity

Verify $e^{iz} = \cos z + i\sin z$ using Taylor series.

**Solution:**

$$e^{iz} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(iz)^n}{n!} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{i^n z^n}{n!}$$

Separate even and odd terms:
$$= \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{i^{2k}z^{2k}}{(2k)!} + \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{i^{2k+1}z^{2k+1}}{(2k+1)!}$$

Since $i^{2k} = (i^2)^k = (-1)^k$ and $i^{2k+1} = i \cdot i^{2k} = i(-1)^k$:

$$= \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^k z^{2k}}{(2k)!} + i\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^k z^{2k+1}}{(2k+1)!}$$
$$= \cos z + i\sin z$$ ✓

**Answer:** Euler's formula verified via Taylor series

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: B**
Taylor series $\sum a_n(z-z_0)^n$ converges in disk $|z-z_0| < R$
Radius $R =$ distance to nearest singularity
Disk centered at $z_0$ (not line or annulus)
May be infinite (entire functions) or zero (only at center)
Circular symmetry in complex plane

**Problem 2: C**
Singularity at $z = 2$
Distance from center $z_0 = 0$ to $z = 2$ is $|2-0| = 2$
$R = 2$
Series: $\frac{1}{z-2} = -\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{1-z/2} = -\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(z/2)^n$ for $|z|<2$
Converges in disk $|z| < 2$

**Problem 3: D**
Entire function = analytic everywhere (no singularities)
Distance to nearest singularity = $\infty$
$R = \infty$ regardless of center $z_0$
Examples: $e^z$, $\sin z$, $\cos z$, polynomials
Taylor series converges for all $z$

**Problem 4: C**
Taylor series definition: $a_n = \frac{f^{(n)}(z_0)}{n!}$
Normalizes derivative by factorial
Ensures correct power in series: $f(z) = \sum \frac{f^{(n)}(z_0)}{n!}(z-z_0)^n$
Can extract from Cauchy's integral formula
Uniquely determined by function

**Problem 5: B**
$\cos z = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^k z^{2k}}{(2k)!} = 1 - \frac{z^2}{2!} + \frac{z^4}{4!} - \cdots$
Only even powers: $z^0, z^2, z^4, z^6, \ldots$
$\cos$ is even function: $\cos(-z) = \cos z$
Compare: $\sin z$ has only odd powers
Derivatives of $\cos$ at $z=0$: odd derivatives are zero
