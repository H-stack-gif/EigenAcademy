# Complex Analysis Topic 2.4: Physical Interpretations

Complex analysis isn't merely abstract mathematics—it's the natural language for describing two-dimensional physical phenomena. When fluid flows around an obstacle, the complex potential $F(z) = \phi + i\psi$ encodes both the velocity potential $\phi$ and stream function $\psi$. The curves $\phi = \text{const}$ are equipotential lines; the curves $\psi = \text{const}$ are streamlines showing particle paths. These families of curves are orthogonal, intersecting at right angles—a consequence of the Cauchy-Riemann equations. This geometric structure repeats across physics: in electrostatics ($\phi$ is electric potential), in magnetostatics ($\phi$ is magnetic potential), in heat flow ($\phi$ is temperature), and in quantum mechanics ($\phi$ and $\psi$ form wave functions).

The power of complex analysis in physics stems from conformal mapping. A conformal map preserves angles, so it transforms one flow pattern into another while maintaining the physical structure. Want to understand airflow over an airfoil? Map it conformally to flow over a cylinder, solve that simpler problem, then map back. Want to find the electric field between oddly-shaped conductors? Map to a configuration with known solution, then transform. The Riemann mapping theorem guarantees such maps exist, while explicit transformations (Joukowski, Schwarz-Christoffel) provide calculable solutions.

This article explores physical applications of complex analysis. We'll see how analytic functions model fluid flow, electrostatics, and heat conduction, understand why equipotential curves and field lines are orthogonal, use conformal mapping to solve boundary value problems, and compute physical quantities from complex potentials. You'll learn to translate physical problems into complex analysis, exploit analyticity to simplify calculations, and interpret complex functions as physical fields.

## Complex Potential for Fluid Flow

Consider **two-dimensional, incompressible, irrotational flow** of an ideal fluid.

**Velocity potential** $\phi$: satisfies $\vec{v} = \nabla\phi$ (irrotational means $\nabla \times \vec{v} = 0$)

**Stream function** $\psi$: satisfies $\vec{v} = (\psi_y, -\psi_x)$ (incompressible means $\nabla \cdot \vec{v} = 0$)

**Complex potential**: $F(z) = \phi(x,y) + i\psi(x,y)$

If $F$ is analytic, then:
- $\nabla^2\phi = 0$ and $\nabla^2\psi = 0$ (both harmonic)
- Cauchy-Riemann ensures $\vec{v} = \nabla\phi$ and streamlines $\psi = \text{const}$ are orthogonal

**Velocity**: $\vec{v} = \overline{F'(z)}$ (complex conjugate of derivative)

### Example 1: Uniform Flow

The complex potential $F(z) = Uz$ represents uniform flow with speed $U$ in the positive $x$-direction. Find the velocity field.

**Solution:**

$$F(z) = U(x + iy) = Ux + iUy$$

$$\phi = Ux, \quad \psi = Uy$$

$$F'(z) = U$$

$$\vec{v} = \overline{F'(z)} = \overline{U} = U$$

In component form: $\vec{v} = (U, 0)$ (constant velocity to the right)

**Streamlines**: $\psi = Uy = \text{const}$ → horizontal lines $y = \text{const}$

**Equipotentials**: $\phi = Ux = \text{const}$ → vertical lines $x = \text{const}$

**Answer:** Uniform flow with velocity $(U, 0)$; streamlines are horizontal

### Example 2: Source at Origin

The complex potential $F(z) = \frac{m}{2\pi}\log z$ represents a source of strength $m$ at the origin. Describe the flow.

**Solution:**

In polar form $z = re^{i\theta}$:
$$F(z) = \frac{m}{2\pi}(\ln r + i\theta)$$

$$\phi = \frac{m}{2\pi}\ln r, \quad \psi = \frac{m\theta}{2\pi}$$

$$F'(z) = \frac{m}{2\pi z}$$

$$\vec{v} = \overline{F'(z)} = \overline{\frac{m}{2\pi z}} = \frac{m}{2\pi\overline{z}} = \frac{m}{2\pi} \cdot \frac{z}{|z|^2} = \frac{m}{2\pi r}\hat{r}$$

**Velocity**: Radial outward flow with magnitude $\frac{m}{2\pi r}$

**Streamlines**: $\psi = \frac{m\theta}{2\pi} = \text{const}$ → rays from origin

**Equipotentials**: $\phi = \frac{m}{2\pi}\ln r = \text{const}$ → circles centered at origin

**Answer:** Radial outward flow; streamlines are rays, equipotentials are circles

### Example 3: Circulation

The complex potential $F(z) = -\frac{i\Gamma}{2\pi}\log z$ represents circulation of strength $\Gamma$ around the origin. Find the velocity.

**Solution:**

$$F(z) = -\frac{i\Gamma}{2\pi}(\ln r + i\theta) = \frac{\Gamma\theta}{2\pi} - i\frac{\Gamma\ln r}{2\pi}$$

$$\phi = \frac{\Gamma\theta}{2\pi}, \quad \psi = -\frac{\Gamma\ln r}{2\pi}$$

$$F'(z) = -\frac{i\Gamma}{2\pi z}$$

In polar: $F'(re^{i\theta}) = -\frac{i\Gamma}{2\pi re^{i\theta}} = -\frac{i\Gamma}{2\pi r}e^{-i\theta}$

$$\vec{v} = \overline{F'(z)} = \frac{i\Gamma}{2\pi r}e^{i\theta} = \frac{\Gamma}{2\pi r}\hat{\theta}$$

**Velocity**: Tangential flow (counterclockwise) with magnitude $\frac{\Gamma}{2\pi r}$

**Streamlines**: $\psi = -\frac{\Gamma\ln r}{2\pi} = \text{const}$ → circles centered at origin

**Answer:** Circular flow around origin; velocity decreases as $1/r$

### Example 4: Flow Around Cylinder

The complex potential $F(z) = U\left(z + \frac{a^2}{z}\right)$ represents uniform flow past a cylinder of radius $a$. Show the cylinder is a streamline.

**Solution:**

On $|z| = a$: write $z = ae^{i\theta}$

$$F(ae^{i\theta}) = U\left(ae^{i\theta} + \frac{a^2}{ae^{i\theta}}\right) = U(ae^{i\theta} + ae^{-i\theta})$$
$$= Ua(e^{i\theta} + e^{-i\theta}) = 2Ua\cos\theta$$

This is real, so $\text{Im}(F) = \psi = 0$ on $|z| = a$.

Since $\psi = 0$ on the circle, it's a **streamline** (fluid flows parallel to boundary, not through it).

**Physical interpretation**: Cylinder acts as obstacle; fluid flows around it.

**Answer:** Circle $|z| = a$ is streamline $\psi = 0$; models flow around cylinder

## Electrostatics

For **two-dimensional electrostatics** in a region with no charges:

**Electric potential** $\phi$: satisfies $\nabla^2\phi = 0$ (Laplace's equation)

**Electric field**: $\vec{E} = -\nabla\phi$

**Complex potential**: $F(z) = \phi + i\psi$ where $\psi$ is constructed to make $F$ analytic

**Field**: $\vec{E} = -\overline{F'(z)}$

**Equipotentials**: Curves $\phi = \text{const}$

**Field lines**: Curves $\psi = \text{const}$ (orthogonal to equipotentials)

### Example 5: Point Charge

The complex potential $F(z) = -\frac{Q}{2\pi\epsilon_0}\log z$ represents a line charge of density $Q$ per unit length. Find the electric field.

**Solution:**

$$F'(z) = -\frac{Q}{2\pi\epsilon_0 z}$$

$$\vec{E} = -\overline{F'(z)} = \frac{Q}{2\pi\epsilon_0\overline{z}} = \frac{Q}{2\pi\epsilon_0} \cdot \frac{z}{|z|^2}$$

In polar: $\vec{E} = \frac{Q}{2\pi\epsilon_0 r}\hat{r}$

**Magnitude**: $|\vec{E}| = \frac{Q}{2\pi\epsilon_0 r}$ (inverse with distance)

**Direction**: Radially outward

**Answer:** Radial electric field with magnitude $\frac{Q}{2\pi\epsilon_0 r}$

### Example 6: Parallel Plate Capacitor

For parallel plates at $x = 0$ (potential $V_0$) and $x = d$ (potential $0$), the potential is $\phi = V_0(1 - x/d)$. Find the complex potential.

**Solution:**

$$\phi(x,y) = V_0\left(1 - \frac{x}{d}\right)$$

This is harmonic: $\phi_{xx} = 0$ and $\phi_{yy} = 0$.

Find harmonic conjugate:
$$\psi_y = \phi_x = -\frac{V_0}{d}$$
$$\psi = -\frac{V_0 y}{d} + g(x)$$

$$\psi_x = g'(x) = -\phi_y = 0$$

So $g(x) = C$, taking $C = 0$:

$$F(z) = V_0\left(1 - \frac{x}{d}\right) - i\frac{V_0 y}{d} = V_0\left(1 - \frac{x + iy}{d}\right) = V_0\left(1 - \frac{z}{d}\right)$$

**Electric field**:
$$F'(z) = -\frac{V_0}{d}$$
$$\vec{E} = -\overline{F'(z)} = \frac{V_0}{d}\hat{x}$$

Uniform field between plates.

**Answer:** $F(z) = V_0(1 - z/d)$; uniform field $\vec{E} = (V_0/d, 0)$

### Example 7: Orthogonality of Field Lines and Equipotentials

Show that electric field lines and equipotential curves are orthogonal.

**Solution:**

Equipotential: $\phi(x,y) = c_1$ (constant)

Field line: $\psi(x,y) = c_2$ (constant)

Gradient of $\phi$: $\nabla\phi = (\phi_x, \phi_y)$ (perpendicular to level curves $\phi = c_1$)

Gradient of $\psi$: $\nabla\psi = (\psi_x, \psi_y)$ (perpendicular to level curves $\psi = c_2$)

From Cauchy-Riemann: $\phi_x = \psi_y$ and $\phi_y = -\psi_x$

$$\nabla\phi \cdot \nabla\psi = \phi_x\psi_x + \phi_y\psi_y = \psi_y\psi_x + (-\psi_x)\psi_y = 0$$

Gradients orthogonal means level curves orthogonal.

**Answer:** Cauchy-Riemann equations ensure orthogonality

## Heat Conduction

For **steady-state heat conduction**:

**Temperature** $T$: satisfies $\nabla^2 T = 0$ (Laplace's equation in steady state)

**Heat flux**: $\vec{q} = -k\nabla T$ (Fourier's law, $k$ = thermal conductivity)

**Complex temperature**: $F(z) = T + i\psi$ where $\psi$ makes $F$ analytic

**Isotherms**: Curves $T = \text{const}$ (lines of constant temperature)

**Heat flow lines**: Curves $\psi = \text{const}$ (orthogonal to isotherms)

### Example 8: Temperature Between Cylinders

Two concentric cylinders: inner radius $a$ at temperature $T_1$, outer radius $b$ at temperature $T_2$. Find temperature distribution.

**Solution:**

By symmetry, $T$ depends only on $r$: $T = T(r)$.

Laplace in polar: $\frac{1}{r}\frac{d}{dr}\left(r\frac{dT}{dr}\right) = 0$

$$\frac{d}{dr}\left(r\frac{dT}{dr}\right) = 0$$

Integrate: $r\frac{dT}{dr} = C_1$

$$\frac{dT}{dr} = \frac{C_1}{r}$$

Integrate: $T = C_1\ln r + C_2$

Boundary conditions:
- At $r = a$: $T = T_1 \implies C_1\ln a + C_2 = T_1$
- At $r = b$: $T = T_2 \implies C_1\ln b + C_2 = T_2$

Solving:
$$C_1 = \frac{T_2 - T_1}{\ln b - \ln a} = \frac{T_2 - T_1}{\ln(b/a)}$$

$$T(r) = T_1 + \frac{T_2 - T_1}{\ln(b/a)}\ln\frac{r}{a}$$

**Answer:** $T(r) = T_1 + \frac{T_2 - T_1}{\ln(b/a)}\ln(r/a)$ (logarithmic profile)

### Example 9: Heat Flow Through Corner

Using conformal mapping, the temperature in a wedge of angle $\alpha$ with sides at temperatures $T = 0$ and $T = T_0$ can be found. For $\alpha = \pi/2$ (right angle), the map $w = z^2$ transforms the wedge into a half-plane. Explain.

**Solution:**

**Original domain** (wedge): $0 < \arg(z) < \pi/2$

**Mapping** $w = z^2$: doubles angles, so $0 < \arg(w) < \pi$ (upper half-plane)

In half-plane, with $\text{Im}(w) = 0$ at temperature $0$ on negative real axis and $T_0$ on positive real axis, solution is:
$$T = \frac{T_0}{\pi}\arg(w)$$

Transform back: $w = z^2 = r^2e^{i2\theta}$, so $\arg(w) = 2\theta$

$$T = \frac{T_0}{\pi}(2\theta) = \frac{2T_0\theta}{\pi}$$

For wedge angle $\pi/2$: at $\theta = \pi/2$, $T = T_0$ ✓

**Answer:** Conformal mapping transforms corner problem to half-plane with known solution

### Example 10: Maximum Temperature Principle

In steady-state heat conduction with no heat sources, why must the maximum temperature occur on the boundary?

**Solution:**

Temperature satisfies $\nabla^2 T = 0$ (harmonic function).

By **maximum principle** for harmonic functions: maximum occurs on boundary.

**Physical interpretation**: Heat flows from hot to cold. If hottest point were interior, heat would flow away in all directions, violating steady state.

**Answer:** Maximum principle: harmonic function achieves maximum on boundary

## Computing Physical Quantities

### Example 11: Circulation and Flux

For fluid flow with complex potential $F(z)$, the **circulation** around a closed curve $C$ is:
$$\Gamma = \oint_C \vec{v} \cdot d\vec{r} = \text{Im}\left[\oint_C F'(z) \, dz\right]$$

The **flux** across $C$ is:
$$\Phi = \oint_C \vec{v} \cdot \hat{n} \, ds = \text{Re}\left[\oint_C F'(z) \, dz\right]$$

Compute both for $F(z) = \frac{m}{2\pi}\log z$ around $|z| = R$.

**Solution:**

$$F'(z) = \frac{m}{2\pi z}$$

Around $|z| = R$: $z = Re^{i\theta}$, $dz = iRe^{i\theta}d\theta$

$$\oint_{|z|=R} F'(z) \, dz = \oint_{|z|=R} \frac{m}{2\pi Re^{i\theta}} \cdot iRe^{i\theta} \, d\theta$$
$$= \oint_0^{2\pi} \frac{mi}{2\pi} \, d\theta = \frac{mi}{2\pi} \cdot 2\pi = mi$$

**Circulation**: $\Gamma = \text{Im}(mi) = 0$

**Flux**: $\Phi = \text{Re}(mi) = m$ (source strength)

**Answer:** Zero circulation, flux equals source strength $m$

### Example 12: Force on Cylinder

For flow around cylinder with complex potential $F(z) = U(z + a^2/z) + \frac{i\Gamma}{2\pi}\log z$ (uniform flow plus circulation), the force per unit length is given by the **Blasius theorem**:
$$\vec{F} = -\frac{i\rho}{2}\oint_C [F'(z)]^2 \, dz$$

where $\rho$ is fluid density. Compute for the cylinder.

**Solution:**

$$F'(z) = U\left(1 - \frac{a^2}{z^2}\right) - \frac{i\Gamma}{2\pi z}$$

On large circle $|z| = R \to \infty$, dominant terms:
$$F'(z) \approx U - \frac{i\Gamma}{2\pi z}$$

$$[F'(z)]^2 \approx U^2 - 2U \cdot \frac{i\Gamma}{2\pi z} = U^2 - \frac{iU\Gamma}{\pi z}$$

$$\oint [F'(z)]^2 \, dz \approx \oint \left(U^2 - \frac{iU\Gamma}{\pi z}\right) dz$$

First term integrates to zero (entire function). Second term:
$$-\frac{iU\Gamma}{\pi}\oint \frac{dz}{z} = -\frac{iU\Gamma}{\pi} \cdot 2\pi i = 2U\Gamma$$

$$\vec{F} = -\frac{i\rho}{2} \cdot 2U\Gamma = -i\rho U\Gamma$$

In components: $\vec{F} = (0, \rho U\Gamma)$ (lift force perpendicular to flow)

**Answer:** Lift force $\rho U\Gamma$ perpendicular to flow (**Kutta-Joukowski theorem**)

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: C**
$F(z) = Az = A(x + iy) = Ax + iAy$ ($A$ real)
$\phi = Ax$, $\psi = Ay$
Streamlines: $\psi = Ay = \text{const}$ → $y = \text{const}$ (horizontal lines)
Equipotentials: $\phi = Ax = \text{const}$ → $x = \text{const}$ (vertical lines)
Velocity: $F'(z) = A$, so $\vec{v} = (A, 0)$ (uniform horizontal flow)

**Problem 2: B**
$F(z) = \log z = \ln r + i\theta$
$F'(z) = 1/z$
$\vec{v} = \overline{F'(z)} = \overline{1/z} = z/|z|^2 = (1/r)\hat{r}$
Radial velocity, magnitude $1/r$
This is a **source** (but problem asks about velocity field)
Singularity at origin where potential undefined

**Problem 3: B**
Cauchy-Riemann equations ensure orthogonality
$\nabla\phi \perp \nabla\psi$ because $\phi_x = \psi_y$ and $\phi_y = -\psi_x$
Level curves of $\phi$ (equipotentials) ⊥ level curves of $\psi$ (field lines)
Physical: electric field $\vec{E} = -\nabla\phi$ points perpendicular to equipotentials
Field lines show direction of $\vec{E}$, hence perpendicular to equipotentials

**Problem 4: B**
Maximum principle for harmonic functions
Non-constant harmonic function: max/min on boundary
Physical: steady-state temperature can't be hottest in interior
Heat flows from hot to cold; interior points at equilibrium
Used to prove uniqueness of boundary value problems

**Problem 5: D**
$F(z) = z^2 = r^2e^{i2\theta}$
In Cartesian: $z^2 = (x+iy)^2 = x^2 - y^2 + 2ixy$
$\phi = x^2 - y^2$, $\psi = 2xy$
Streamlines $\psi = 2xy = c$: rectangular hyperbolas
Equipotentials $\phi = x^2 - y^2 = c$: orthogonal hyperbolas
Flow in $90°$ corner (symmetric about bisector)
Also describes stagnation point flow
