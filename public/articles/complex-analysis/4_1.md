# Complex Analysis Topic 4.1: Power Series

In calculus, power series like $\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{x^n}{n!} = e^x$ represent functions as infinite polynomials, converging in some interval around a center point. Complex power series $\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} a_n(z-z_0)^n$ extend this concept to the complex plane, but their behavior is remarkably different. While real power series converge in intervals $(x_0 - R, x_0 + R)$ on the number line, complex power series converge in **disks** $|z - z_0| < R$ in the plane. Every point on the circle $|z - z_0| = R$ behaves identically—either all points give convergence or all give divergence. This circular symmetry reflects the isotropy of the complex plane under rotations.

The radius of convergence $R$ can be computed from the coefficients using formulas like the ratio test or root test, but it also has deep geometric meaning: $R$ equals the distance from $z_0$ to the nearest singularity of the function. Within the disk of convergence, the power series not only converges but represents an **analytic function**. You can differentiate and integrate term-by-term, and the resulting series converge to the derivative and integral of the function. This makes power series the natural language for analytic functions—within their radius of convergence, functions are completely determined by their coefficients.

This article develops the theory of complex power series from foundations. We'll define convergence in the complex plane, compute radii of convergence using standard tests, explore the disk of convergence as a geometric object, differentiate and integrate power series term-by-term, and understand when series represent analytic functions. You'll master techniques for finding convergence regions, manipulating series algebraically, and recognizing power series as building blocks of complex analysis.

## Definition and Convergence

A **power series** centered at $z_0$ is an expression:

$$\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} a_n(z - z_0)^n = a_0 + a_1(z-z_0) + a_2(z-z_0)^2 + \cdots$$

where $a_n$ are complex coefficients.

The series **converges at $z$** if the sequence of partial sums $S_N = \sum_{n=0}^{N} a_n(z-z_0)^n$ has a limit as $N \to \infty$.

**Absolute convergence**: Series converges absolutely if $\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} |a_n(z-z_0)^n|$ converges.

### Example 1: Testing Convergence at Specific Points

Does $\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{z^n}{n!}$ converge at $z = 1$ and $z = i$?

**Solution:**

At $z = 1$:
$$\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{1^n}{n!} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n!} = e$$

This is the series for $e^1$, which converges.

At $z = i$:
$$\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{i^n}{n!}$$

Use ratio test: $\left|\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}\right| = \left|\frac{i^{n+1}/(n+1)!}{i^n/n!}\right| = \frac{|i|}{n+1} = \frac{1}{n+1} \to 0 < 1$

Converges absolutely.

**Answer:** Converges at both points (in fact, converges everywhere)

### Example 2: Geometric Series

Find where $\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} z^n$ converges.

**Solution:**

This is a geometric series with first term $1$ and ratio $z$.

Converges when $|z| < 1$, with sum:
$$\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} z^n = \frac{1}{1-z}$$

Diverges when $|z| \geq 1$.

**Boundary behavior**: On $|z| = 1$ (unit circle), series diverges. For example, at $z=1$: $\sum 1^n$ diverges.

**Answer:** Converges for $|z| < 1$ to $\frac{1}{1-z}$

### Example 3: Testing with Root Test

Determine convergence of $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{z^n}{2^n}$.

**Solution:**

Use root test:
$$\limsup_{n \to \infty} \sqrt[n]{|a_n z^n|} = \limsup \sqrt[n]{\frac{|z|^n}{2^n}} = \limsup \frac{|z|}{2} = \frac{|z|}{2}$$

Converges when $\frac{|z|}{2} < 1$, i.e., $|z| < 2$.

Diverges when $|z| > 2$.

**Answer:** Converges for $|z| < 2$

## Radius of Convergence

**Theorem**: For power series $\sum a_n(z-z_0)^n$, there exists $R \in [0, \infty]$ such that:
- Series converges absolutely for $|z - z_0| < R$
- Series diverges for $|z - z_0| > R$

$R$ is the **radius of convergence**.

**Formulas**:

**Ratio test**: $R = \lim_{n \to \infty} \left|\frac{a_n}{a_{n+1}}\right|$ (if limit exists)

**Root test**: $R = \frac{1}{\limsup_{n \to \infty} \sqrt[n]{|a_n|}}$

### Example 4: Computing Radius via Ratio Test

Find the radius of convergence of $\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{z^n}{n^2}$.

**Solution:**

$$\frac{a_n}{a_{n+1}} = \frac{1/n^2}{1/(n+1)^2} = \frac{(n+1)^2}{n^2} = \left(\frac{n+1}{n}\right)^2 = \left(1 + \frac{1}{n}\right)^2$$

$$R = \lim_{n \to \infty} \left(1 + \frac{1}{n}\right)^2 = 1^2 = 1$$

**Answer:** $R = 1$; converges for $|z| < 1$

### Example 5: Computing Radius via Root Test

Find the radius of convergence of $\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} 2^n z^n$.

**Solution:**

$$a_n = 2^n$$

$$\sqrt[n]{|a_n|} = \sqrt[n]{2^n} = 2$$

$$R = \frac{1}{\limsup 2} = \frac{1}{2}$$

**Answer:** $R = 1/2$; converges for $|z| < 1/2$

### Example 6: Series with Alternating Terms

Find the radius of convergence of $\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} (-1)^n n z^n$.

**Solution:**

$$a_n = (-1)^n n$$

$$\left|\frac{a_n}{a_{n+1}}\right| = \frac{|(-1)^n n|}{|(-1)^{n+1}(n+1)|} = \frac{n}{n+1} \to 1$$

$$R = 1$$

**Answer:** $R = 1$; converges for $|z| < 1$

### Example 7: Infinite Radius

Find the radius of convergence of $\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{z^n}{n!}$.

**Solution:**

$$\frac{a_n}{a_{n+1}} = \frac{1/n!}{1/(n+1)!} = \frac{(n+1)!}{n!} = n+1 \to \infty$$

$$R = \infty$$

Series converges for all $z$ (entire function).

**Answer:** $R = \infty$; converges everywhere

## Disk of Convergence

The set $\{z: |z - z_0| < R\}$ is called the **disk of convergence**.

- **Interior** ($|z-z_0| < R$): Series converges absolutely
- **Exterior** ($|z-z_0| > R$): Series diverges
- **Boundary** ($|z-z_0| = R$): Behavior varies; must test individually

### Example 8: Behavior on Boundary

For $\sum z^n$ with $R = 1$, test convergence at $z = 1$, $z = -1$, and $z = i$.

**Solution:**

All three points satisfy $|z| = 1$ (on boundary).

At $z = 1$: $\sum 1^n = \sum 1$ diverges

At $z = -1$: $\sum (-1)^n$ diverges (oscillates)

At $z = i$: $\sum i^n$ where $i^n$ cycles through $i, -1, -i, 1, \ldots$ (diverges)

**Answer:** Diverges at all three boundary points (typical for geometric series)

### Example 9: Convergence on Part of Boundary

For $\sum \frac{z^n}{n^2}$, test $z = 1$ and $z = -1$ (on boundary $|z| = 1$).

**Solution:**

At $z = 1$: $\sum \frac{1}{n^2}$ (p-series with $p = 2 > 1$) converges

At $z = -1$: $\sum \frac{(-1)^n}{n^2}$ (alternating series) converges

**Answer:** Converges at these boundary points (unusual; depends on coefficients)

### Example 10: Shifted Center

Find the disk of convergence for $\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} (z-i)^n$.

**Solution:**

Geometric series centered at $z_0 = i$ with ratio $(z-i)$.

Converges when $|z - i| < 1$.

**Disk**: Center at $i$, radius $1$.

**Geometric description**: Circle in complex plane centered at $(0, 1)$ with radius $1$.

**Answer:** $|z - i| < 1$ (disk centered at $i$ with radius $1$)

## Operations on Power Series

Within the disk of convergence, power series can be:
- **Added**: $\sum a_n z^n + \sum b_n z^n = \sum (a_n + b_n) z^n$
- **Multiplied**: $\left(\sum a_n z^n\right)\left(\sum b_n z^n\right) = \sum c_n z^n$ where $c_n = \sum_{k=0}^{n} a_k b_{n-k}$
- **Differentiated**: $\frac{d}{dz}\sum a_n z^n = \sum n a_n z^{n-1}$
- **Integrated**: $\int \sum a_n z^n \, dz = C + \sum \frac{a_n}{n+1} z^{n+1}$

### Example 11: Adding Series

If $f(z) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{z^n}{n!}$ and $g(z) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} (-z)^n/n! = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n z^n}{n!}$, find $f(z) + g(z)$.

**Solution:**

$$f(z) + g(z) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{z^n}{n!} + \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n z^n}{n!}$$
$$= \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{[1 + (-1)^n]z^n}{n!}$$

For even $n$: $1 + (-1)^n = 1 + 1 = 2$
For odd $n$: $1 + (-1)^n = 1 - 1 = 0$

$$= \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{2z^{2k}}{(2k)!} = 2\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{z^{2k}}{(2k)!}$$

This is $2\cosh z$.

**Answer:** $2\cosh z$

### Example 12: Differentiating Term-by-Term

Differentiate $f(z) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} z^n = \frac{1}{1-z}$ for $|z| < 1$.

**Solution:**

**Direct differentiation**:
$$f'(z) = \frac{d}{dz}\left(\frac{1}{1-z}\right) = \frac{1}{(1-z)^2}$$

**Term-by-term**:
$$\frac{d}{dz}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} z^n = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} nz^{n-1} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} (n+1)z^n$$

Both equal $\frac{1}{(1-z)^2}$ for $|z| < 1$ ✓

**Answer:** $f'(z) = \frac{1}{(1-z)^2}$; term-by-term differentiation valid

### Example 13: Integrating Series

Integrate $\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} z^n$ from $0$ to $z$ (where $|z| < 1$).

**Solution:**

$$\int_0^z \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \zeta^n \, d\zeta = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \int_0^z \zeta^n \, d\zeta$$
$$= \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \left[\frac{\zeta^{n+1}}{n+1}\right]_0^z = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{z^{n+1}}{n+1}$$
$$= \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{z^n}{n}$$

Also, integrating $\frac{1}{1-z}$:
$$\int_0^z \frac{1}{1-\zeta} \, d\zeta = [-\ln(1-\zeta)]_0^z = -\ln(1-z)$$

So $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{z^n}{n} = -\ln(1-z)$ ✓

**Answer:** $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{z^n}{n} = -\ln(1-z)$

## Analyticity of Power Series

**Theorem**: A power series $f(z) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} a_n(z-z_0)^n$ represents an **analytic function** in its disk of convergence $|z - z_0| < R$.

Moreover, $f$ can be differentiated term-by-term infinitely many times, and each derivative is also analytic.

### Example 14: Showing Analyticity

Show that $f(z) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{z^n}{2^n}$ is analytic for $|z| < 2$.

**Solution:**

The series has radius of convergence $R = 2$.

By the theorem, $f$ is analytic in $|z| < 2$.

Can also verify: $f(z) = \sum \left(\frac{z}{2}\right)^n = \frac{1}{1 - z/2} = \frac{2}{2-z}$

This is a rational function, analytic except at $z = 2$.

In $|z| < 2$, no singularities, so analytic ✓

**Answer:** Analytic for $|z| < 2$

### Example 15: Derivatives of Series

If $f(z) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} a_n z^n$ has radius $R$, what is the radius of convergence of $f'(z) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} na_n z^{n-1}$?

**Solution:**

The derivative series is:
$$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} na_n z^{n-1} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} (n+1)a_{n+1} z^n$$

Radius of convergence:
$$R' = \lim_{n \to \infty} \left|\frac{(n+1)a_{n+1}}{(n+2)a_{n+2}}\right| = \lim \frac{n+1}{n+2} \cdot \left|\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_{n+2}}\right|$$
$$= 1 \cdot R = R$$

**Answer:** Same radius $R$ (differentiation preserves radius of convergence)

## Practice Problems

**Problem 2:** The radius of convergence of $\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} n! z^n$ is:

A) $0$
B) $1$
C) $\infty$
D) $1/2$
E) $2$

**Problem 3:** Power series can be differentiated term-by-term:

A) Never
B) Outside radius of convergence
C) Inside radius of convergence
D) Only at $z = 0$
E) Only for polynomials

**Problem 4:** For $\sum a_n z^n$ with radius $R$, convergence on $|z| = R$:

A) Always converges
B) Always diverges
C) Depends on coefficients
D) Converges at exactly one point
E) Undefined

**Problem 5:** The function $f(z) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} z^n/n!$ equals:

A) $\sin z$
B) $\cos z$
C) $e^z$
D) $\log z$
E) $1/(1-z)$

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: B**
Geometric series $\sum z^n = \frac{1}{1-z}$
Converges when $|z| < 1$ (strictly less than)
Diverges when $|z| \geq 1$
Boundary $|z|=1$: diverges everywhere (e.g., at $z=1$: $\sum 1$ diverges)
Important example: radius of convergence $R=1$

**Problem 2: A**
$a_n = n!$ grows very fast
Ratio test: $\frac{a_n}{a_{n+1}} = \frac{n!}{(n+1)!} = \frac{1}{n+1} \to 0$
$R = 0$
Converges only at $z=0$ (trivially)
Factorial growth → zero radius

**Problem 3: C**
Inside disk of convergence $|z-z_0| < R$: can differentiate term-by-term
$\frac{d}{dz}\sum a_n z^n = \sum na_n z^{n-1}$
Derivative has same radius of convergence
Outside disk: series diverges (can't differentiate)
Fundamental property for analytic functions

**Problem 4: C**
Behavior on boundary $|z-z_0|=R$ varies with coefficients
Example 1: $\sum z^n$ diverges everywhere on $|z|=1$
Example 2: $\sum z^n/n^2$ converges everywhere on $|z|=1$
Must test each boundary point individually
General theory: convergence/divergence depends on $a_n$

**Problem 5: C**
$\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{z^n}{n!} = e^z$ (definition of complex exponential)
Has radius $R = \infty$ (entire function)
Verify: ratio test gives $R = \lim(n+1) = \infty$
Other series:
- $\sin z = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n z^{2n+1}}{(2n+1)!}$
- $\cos z = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n z^{2n}}{(2n)!}$
