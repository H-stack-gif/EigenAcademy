# Complex Analysis Topic 6.1: Conformal Mappings

A function $f: \mathbb{C} \to \mathbb{C}$ transforms the plane, sending points $z$ to points $f(z)$ and curves to curves. But most transformations distort geometry—stretching in some directions more than others, skewing angles, creating unrecognizable images. **Conformal mappings** are the rare transformations that preserve local geometry: they maintain angles between curves and preserve shapes of infinitesimal figures. Near any point where $f'(z_0) \neq 0$, an analytic function acts conformally, rotating by $\arg f'(z_0)$ and scaling by $|f'(z_0)|$, but crucially preserving angles. This angle preservation makes conformal maps invaluable for transferring problems between domains—solving difficult boundary value problems in irregular regions by mapping to circles or half-planes where solutions are tractable.

The condition for conformality is remarkably simple: $f$ must be analytic and $f'(z_0) \neq 0$. The analyticity requirement (satisfying Cauchy-Riemann equations) ensures that the transformation is orientation-preserving and locally looks like a similarity transformation—a rotation followed by uniform scaling. Points where $f'(z) = 0$ are **critical points** where conformality fails; here the function may have branch points or map multiple sheets together. Understanding where and how conformality holds is essential for applications ranging from fluid dynamics to electrostatics to cartography.

This article develops the theory of conformal mappings from foundations. We'll define conformality in terms of angle preservation, prove the fundamental connection between analyticity and conformality, identify critical points where conformality fails, compute magnification factors and rotation angles, and understand geometric properties preserved under conformal maps. You'll master recognizing conformal maps, analyzing their local behavior, and understanding why analytic functions with nonzero derivatives are the natural geometric transformations of the complex plane.

## Definition of Conformal Mapping

A function $f$ is **conformal** at $z_0$ if it preserves angles between curves passing through $z_0$.

More precisely: If $\gamma_1$ and $\gamma_2$ are smooth curves intersecting at $z_0$ with angle $\theta$ between them, then their images $f(\gamma_1)$ and $f(\gamma_2)$ intersect at $f(z_0)$ with the same angle $\theta$.

**Key theorem**: If $f$ is analytic at $z_0$ and $f'(z_0) \neq 0$, then $f$ is conformal at $z_0$.

**Local behavior**: Near $z_0$, 
$$f(z) \approx f(z_0) + f'(z_0)(z - z_0)$$

The derivative $f'(z_0) = |f'(z_0)|e^{i\arg f'(z_0)}$ acts as:
- **Rotation** by angle $\arg f'(z_0)$
- **Scaling** by factor $|f'(z_0)|$

### Example 1: Testing Conformality

Is $f(z) = z^2$ conformal at $z = 1$ and at $z = 0$?

**Solution:**

$f$ is analytic everywhere (polynomial).

$f'(z) = 2z$

**At $z = 1$**: $f'(1) = 2 \neq 0$, so $f$ is conformal at $z = 1$ ✓

**At $z = 0$**: $f'(0) = 0$, so $f$ is **not conformal** at $z = 0$ ✗

At origin: $f(z) = z^2$ doubles angles (maps $\theta$ to $2\theta$), not preserving them.

**Answer:** Conformal at $z=1$, not conformal at $z=0$

### Example 2: Angle Doubling at Critical Point

Show that $f(z) = z^2$ doubles angles at the origin.

**Solution:**

Consider ray from origin at angle $\theta$: $z = re^{i\theta}$

Image: $f(z) = r^2e^{2i\theta}$ (ray at angle $2\theta$)

Two rays at angles $\theta_1$ and $\theta_2$ with angle difference $\Delta = \theta_2 - \theta_1$ map to rays at angles $2\theta_1$ and $2\theta_2$ with angle difference $2\Delta$.

Angle is doubled, not preserved.

**Answer:** Angles doubled at $z=0$ (critical point)

### Example 3: Rotation and Scaling

For $f(z) = e^{i\pi/4}z$, find the rotation angle and magnification factor.

**Solution:**

$$f'(z) = e^{i\pi/4} = \cos(\pi/4) + i\sin(\pi/4) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} + \frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}$$

**Rotation**: $\arg f'(z) = \pi/4 = 45°$ (counterclockwise)

**Magnification**: $|f'(z)| = |e^{i\pi/4}| = 1$ (no scaling)

$f$ rotates by $45°$ without changing size.

**Answer:** Rotation $45°$, magnification $1$

### Example 4: Exponential Function Conformality

Where is $f(z) = e^z$ conformal?

**Solution:**

$f'(z) = e^z$

$f'(z) = 0$ never (exponential never zero)

Therefore $f$ is conformal everywhere in $\mathbb{C}$.

**At specific point**: At $z = 0$, $f'(0) = e^0 = 1$

Locally: rotation by $\arg(1) = 0$, scaling by $|1| = 1$ (identity near origin)

**Answer:** Conformal everywhere

### Example 5: Finding Critical Points

Find all points where $f(z) = z^3 - 3z$ is not conformal.

**Solution:**

$$f'(z) = 3z^2 - 3 = 3(z^2 - 1) = 3(z-1)(z+1)$$

$f'(z) = 0$ when $z = 1$ or $z = -1$

These are critical points (conformality fails).

Elsewhere $f'(z) \neq 0$, so $f$ is conformal.

**Answer:** Not conformal at $z = \pm 1$; conformal elsewhere

## Geometric Properties

### Example 6: Magnification Factor

For $f(z) = z^2$ at $z = 2$, find how much a small region is magnified.

**Solution:**

$$f'(2) = 2 \cdot 2 = 4$$

Magnification factor: $|f'(2)| = 4$

A small disk of radius $\epsilon$ around $z=2$ maps approximately to disk of radius $4\epsilon$ around $f(2) = 4$.

Area magnification: $|f'(2)|^2 = 16$

**Answer:** Linear magnification $4$, area magnification $16$

### Example 7: Orthogonal Curves

If two curves intersect orthogonally at $z_0$, do their images under conformal $f$ intersect orthogonally?

**Solution:**

Yes! Conformal maps preserve **all** angles, including right angles.

If $\gamma_1 \perp \gamma_2$ at $z_0$ (angle $90°$), then $f(\gamma_1) \perp f(\gamma_2)$ at $f(z_0)$.

This is why conformal maps preserve orthogonal grids (like latitude/longitude).

**Answer:** Yes, orthogonality preserved

### Example 8: Circles to Circles

Does $f(z) = z^2$ map the circle $|z| = 1$ to a circle?

**Solution:**

Let $z = e^{i\theta}$ (unit circle)

$$f(z) = (e^{i\theta})^2 = e^{2i\theta}$$

As $\theta$ ranges from $0$ to $2\pi$, $2\theta$ ranges from $0$ to $4\pi$.

This traverses unit circle **twice**.

Image: Still the unit circle $|w| = 1$, but covered twice.

**Answer:** Yes, maps to $|w|=1$ (traversed twice)

### Example 9: Lines Under Exponential

What is the image of the horizontal line $\text{Im}(z) = c$ under $f(z) = e^z$?

**Solution:**

Let $z = x + ic$

$$f(z) = e^{x+ic} = e^x e^{ic} = e^x(\cos c + i\sin c)$$

As $x$ varies: $e^x$ ranges from $0$ to $\infty$

$$w = e^x(\cos c + i\sin c)$$

This is a ray from origin at angle $c$.

All points $re^{ic}$ for $r > 0$.

**Answer:** Ray from origin at angle $c$

### Example 10: Vertical Lines Under Exponential

What is the image of the vertical line $\text{Re}(z) = a$ under $f(z) = e^z$?

**Solution:**

Let $z = a + iy$

$$f(z) = e^{a+iy} = e^a e^{iy} = e^a(\cos y + i\sin y)$$

As $y$ varies from $-\infty$ to $\infty$, $e^{iy}$ traces unit circle.

$$w = e^a \cdot (\text{unit circle})$$

This is a circle centered at origin with radius $e^a$.

**Answer:** Circle $|w| = e^a$

## Angle Preservation

### Example 11: Verifying Angle Preservation

Two curves meet at $z_0 = 1$ with angle $\pi/3$. Under $f(z) = z^2$, find the angle at $f(1) = 1$.

**Solution:**

Since $f'(1) = 2 \neq 0$, $f$ is conformal at $z_0 = 1$.

Conformal maps preserve angles.

Angle at image point: still $\pi/3$.

Note: The curves rotate by $\arg f'(1) = \arg(2) = 0$, but relative angle preserved.

**Answer:** Angle $\pi/3$ (preserved)

### Example 12: Non-Preservation at Critical Point

At $z_0 = 0$, two rays meet at angle $\pi/6$. What angle do their images under $f(z) = z^2$ make?

**Solution:**

At critical point $z = 0$ (where $f'(0) = 0$), angles are **not** preserved.

$f(z) = z^2$ doubles angles at origin.

Original angle: $\pi/6$

Image angle: $2 \cdot \pi/6 = \pi/3$

**Answer:** Angle $\pi/3$ (doubled, not preserved)

### Example 13: Conjugate Function

Is $f(z) = \bar{z}$ (complex conjugate) conformal?

**Solution:**

$$f(x + iy) = x - iy$$

$$u = x, \quad v = -y$$

Check Cauchy-Riemann:
$$u_x = 1, \quad v_y = -1$$ 
$$u_x \neq v_y$$ (C-R fails)

$f$ is not analytic, so not conformal (in the usual sense).

**Answer:** Not conformal (not analytic)

## Jacobian and Area

For conformal map $f$ with $f'(z_0) \neq 0$:

**Jacobian**: $J = |f'(z_0)|^2$

**Area magnification**: Infinitesimal area multiplied by $|f'(z_0)|^2$

### Example 14: Computing Jacobian

For $f(z) = e^z$ at $z = 1$, find the Jacobian.

**Solution:**

$$f'(z) = e^z$$
$$f'(1) = e$$

$$J = |f'(1)|^2 = e^2$$

Small region of area $A$ near $z=1$ maps to region of area approximately $e^2 A$.

**Answer:** $J = e^2$

### Example 15: Maximum Magnification

For $f(z) = z^2$ in region $1 \leq |z| \leq 2$, where is magnification greatest?

**Solution:**

Magnification factor: $|f'(z)| = |2z| = 2|z|$

Maximum when $|z|$ is maximum: $|z| = 2$

$$|f'(z)|_{\max} = 2 \cdot 2 = 4$$

Area magnification: $4^2 = 16$

**Answer:** Maximum at $|z| = 2$ with magnification $4$

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: B**
Conformal = angle-preserving
Definition: angles between curves at $z_0$ equal angles between image curves at $f(z_0)$
Does NOT preserve distances (except for isometries)
Does NOT preserve areas (except when $|f'| = 1$)
Fundamental geometric property

**Problem 2: C**
Theorem: $f$ analytic and $f'(z_0) \neq 0$ ⟹ $f$ conformal at $z_0$
Nonzero derivative ensures local invertibility
$f'(z_0) = 0$ creates critical point (angle preservation fails)
Example: $z^2$ at origin ($f'=0$) doubles angles
Example: $e^z$ conformal everywhere ($f' = e^z \neq 0$)

**Problem 3: C**
Critical point: $f'(z_0) = 0$
Angles typically multiplied by order of zero
$f(z) = z^n$ at origin: angles multiplied by $n$
Not conformal at critical points
Function still analytic (conformality ≠ analyticity)

**Problem 4: D**
Linear magnification: $|f'(z_0)|$
Derivative magnitude gives local scaling factor
$|f'| = 2$ means distances doubled locally
Area magnification: $|f'(z_0)|^2$ (Jacobian)
Rotation angle: $\arg f'(z_0)$

**Problem 5: C**
Conformal maps preserve angles (defining property)
Do NOT preserve distances (except isometries)
Do NOT preserve areas (except when $|f'| = 1$)
Do NOT preserve straight lines (e.g., $e^z$)
Do NOT preserve circles (e.g., $z^2$)
