# Complex Analysis Topic 5.1: Residues

Computing contour integrals in real calculus reduces to finding antiderivatives via the fundamental theorem. Complex contour integrals lack such simplicity—the integral $\oint_C f(z) \, dz$ around a closed contour depends not just on the function but on what singularities lie inside $C$. Yet there's a beautiful shortcut: if $f$ has isolated singularities inside $C$, the integral equals $2\pi i$ times the sum of **residues** at those singularities. The residue at $z_0$ is simply the coefficient $a_{-1}$ of $\frac{1}{z-z_0}$ in the Laurent series—a single number that completely determines the contribution of that singularity to any contour integral around it.

Computing residues is an art that combines algebraic manipulation with Laurent series expansion. For simple poles, residues can be found instantly using limit formulas. For higher-order poles, differentiation formulas apply. For essential singularities, you must extract the Laurent series coefficient, though such singularities rarely appear in applications. Once you know the residues, contour integration becomes trivial: sum the residues and multiply by $2\pi i$. This transforms difficult integral computations into algebraic calculations.

This article develops the theory and computation of residues. We'll define residues from Laurent series, derive formulas for computing residues at poles of various orders, handle multiple singularities, work with rational functions, and understand the geometric meaning of residues. You'll master the computational techniques that make the residue theorem practical, turning complex integration into a tool for evaluating even real integrals.

## Definition of Residue

For function $f$ with isolated singularity at $z_0$, the Laurent series is:

$$f(z) = \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} a_n(z-z_0)^n = \cdots + \frac{a_{-2}}{(z-z_0)^2} + \frac{a_{-1}}{z-z_0} + a_0 + a_1(z-z_0) + \cdots$$

The **residue** of $f$ at $z_0$ is:

$$\text{Res}(f, z_0) = a_{-1}$$

the coefficient of $\frac{1}{z-z_0}$.

**Integral formula**:
$$\text{Res}(f, z_0) = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_C f(z) \, dz$$

where $C$ is any simple closed contour around $z_0$ (and no other singularities).

### Example 1: Residue from Laurent Series

Find $\text{Res}\left(\frac{1}{z^2} + \frac{3}{z} + z, 0\right)$.

**Solution:**

The Laurent series is already given:
$$f(z) = \frac{1}{z^2} + \frac{3}{z} + z$$

Coefficient of $\frac{1}{z}$ is $a_{-1} = 3$.

**Answer:** $\text{Res}(f, 0) = 3$

### Example 2: Finding Residue from Series Expansion

Find $\text{Res}\left(\frac{e^z}{z^3}, 0\right)$.

**Solution:**

Expand $e^z$:
$$e^z = 1 + z + \frac{z^2}{2!} + \frac{z^3}{3!} + \frac{z^4}{4!} + \cdots$$

$$\frac{e^z}{z^3} = \frac{1}{z^3} + \frac{1}{z^2} + \frac{1}{2!z} + \frac{1}{3!} + \frac{z}{4!} + \cdots$$

Coefficient of $\frac{1}{z}$: $a_{-1} = \frac{1}{2!} = \frac{1}{2}$

**Answer:** $\text{Res}(f, 0) = \frac{1}{2}$

### Example 3: Zero Residue

Find $\text{Res}\left(\frac{\cos z}{z^2}, 0\right)$.

**Solution:**

$$\cos z = 1 - \frac{z^2}{2!} + \frac{z^4}{4!} - \cdots$$

$$\frac{\cos z}{z^2} = \frac{1}{z^2} - \frac{1}{2!} + \frac{z^2}{4!} - \cdots$$

No $\frac{1}{z}$ term appears (only even powers).

**Answer:** $\text{Res}(f, 0) = 0$

## Residue at Simple Poles

If $f$ has a **simple pole** at $z_0$ (pole of order 1), then:

$$\text{Res}(f, z_0) = \lim_{z \to z_0} (z-z_0)f(z)$$

**Proof sketch**: Near simple pole, $f(z) = \frac{a_{-1}}{z-z_0} + a_0 + a_1(z-z_0) + \cdots$

Multiply by $(z-z_0)$: $(z-z_0)f(z) = a_{-1} + a_0(z-z_0) + \cdots$

Taking limit: $\lim_{z \to z_0}(z-z_0)f(z) = a_{-1}$ ✓

### Example 4: Simple Pole Formula

Find $\text{Res}\left(\frac{1}{z-2}, 2\right)$.

**Solution:**

$$\lim_{z \to 2}(z-2) \cdot \frac{1}{z-2} = \lim_{z \to 2} 1 = 1$$

**Answer:** $\text{Res}(f, 2) = 1$

### Example 5: Rational Function

Find $\text{Res}\left(\frac{z+1}{z-3}, 3\right)$.

**Solution:**

Simple pole at $z = 3$.

$$\lim_{z \to 3}(z-3) \cdot \frac{z+1}{z-3} = \lim_{z \to 3}(z+1) = 4$$

**Answer:** $\text{Res}(f, 3) = 4$

### Example 6: Quotient of Analytic Functions

If $f(z) = \frac{g(z)}{h(z)}$ where $g(z_0) \neq 0$, $h(z_0) = 0$, and $h'(z_0) \neq 0$ (simple zero), find residue at $z_0$.

**Solution:**

$f$ has simple pole at $z_0$ since $h$ has simple zero.

$$\text{Res}(f, z_0) = \lim_{z \to z_0}(z-z_0)\frac{g(z)}{h(z)}$$

Near $z_0$: $h(z) \approx h'(z_0)(z-z_0)$ (linear approximation)

$$= \lim_{z \to z_0}\frac{(z-z_0)g(z)}{h'(z_0)(z-z_0)} = \frac{g(z_0)}{h'(z_0)}$$

**Formula**: $\text{Res}\left(\frac{g}{h}, z_0\right) = \frac{g(z_0)}{h'(z_0)}$

**Answer:** $\frac{g(z_0)}{h'(z_0)}$ when $h$ has simple zero at $z_0$

### Example 7: Using Quotient Formula

Find $\text{Res}\left(\frac{e^z}{z^2-1}, 1\right)$.

**Solution:**

$g(z) = e^z$, $h(z) = z^2 - 1$

At $z_0 = 1$: $g(1) = e$

$h'(z) = 2z$, so $h'(1) = 2$

$$\text{Res}(f, 1) = \frac{e}{2}$$

**Answer:** $\frac{e}{2}$

## Residues at Poles of Higher Order

If $f$ has pole of **order $m$** at $z_0$:

$$\text{Res}(f, z_0) = \frac{1}{(m-1)!}\lim_{z \to z_0}\frac{d^{m-1}}{dz^{m-1}}\left[(z-z_0)^m f(z)\right]$$

**Special cases**:
- $m = 1$ (simple pole): Previous formula
- $m = 2$ (double pole): $\text{Res}(f, z_0) = \lim_{z \to z_0}\frac{d}{dz}\left[(z-z_0)^2 f(z)\right]$

### Example 8: Residue at Double Pole

Find $\text{Res}\left(\frac{z}{(z-1)^2}, 1\right)$.

**Solution:**

Pole of order $m = 2$ at $z = 1$.

$$\text{Res}(f, 1) = \lim_{z \to 1}\frac{d}{dz}\left[(z-1)^2 \cdot \frac{z}{(z-1)^2}\right]$$
$$= \lim_{z \to 1}\frac{d}{dz}(z) = 1$$

**Answer:** $\text{Res}(f, 1) = 1$

### Example 9: Higher Order Pole

Find $\text{Res}\left(\frac{e^z}{z^4}, 0\right)$.

**Solution:**

Pole of order $4$ at $z = 0$.

$$\text{Res}(f, 0) = \frac{1}{3!}\lim_{z \to 0}\frac{d^3}{dz^3}\left[z^4 \cdot \frac{e^z}{z^4}\right]$$
$$= \frac{1}{6}\lim_{z \to 0}\frac{d^3}{dz^3}(e^z)$$
$$= \frac{1}{6}\lim_{z \to 0}e^z = \frac{1}{6}$$

**Answer:** $\text{Res}(f, 0) = \frac{1}{6}$

### Example 10: Verifying with Laurent Series

Verify Example 9 using Laurent series.

**Solution:**

$$e^z = 1 + z + \frac{z^2}{2!} + \frac{z^3}{3!} + \frac{z^4}{4!} + \cdots$$

$$\frac{e^z}{z^4} = \frac{1}{z^4} + \frac{1}{z^3} + \frac{1}{2!z^2} + \frac{1}{3!z} + \frac{1}{4!} + \cdots$$

Coefficient of $\frac{1}{z}$: $\frac{1}{3!} = \frac{1}{6}$ ✓

**Answer:** Confirmed $\text{Res}(f, 0) = \frac{1}{6}$

## Multiple Singularities

For function with several isolated singularities, compute residue at each separately.

### Example 11: Two Simple Poles

Find residues of $f(z) = \frac{1}{z(z-1)}$ at $z=0$ and $z=1$.

**Solution:**

Partial fractions: $\frac{1}{z(z-1)} = \frac{1}{z-1} - \frac{1}{z}$

**At $z = 0$**:
$$\text{Res}(f, 0) = \lim_{z \to 0} z \cdot \frac{1}{z(z-1)} = \lim_{z \to 0}\frac{1}{z-1} = -1$$

**At $z = 1$**:
$$\text{Res}(f, 1) = \lim_{z \to 1}(z-1) \cdot \frac{1}{z(z-1)} = \lim_{z \to 1}\frac{1}{z} = 1$$

**Answer:** $\text{Res}(f, 0) = -1$, $\text{Res}(f, 1) = 1$

### Example 12: Sum of Residues

For $f(z) = \frac{2z}{z^2+1}$, find residues at both poles.

**Solution:**

Poles at $z = i$ and $z = -i$ (where $z^2 + 1 = 0$).

$g(z) = 2z$, $h(z) = z^2 + 1$, $h'(z) = 2z$

**At $z = i$**:
$$\text{Res}(f, i) = \frac{g(i)}{h'(i)} = \frac{2i}{2i} = 1$$

**At $z = -i$**:
$$\text{Res}(f, -i) = \frac{g(-i)}{h'(-i)} = \frac{-2i}{-2i} = 1$$

**Sum**: $1 + 1 = 2$

**Answer:** $\text{Res}(f, i) = 1$, $\text{Res}(f, -i) = 1$, sum $= 2$

### Example 13: Mixed Order Poles

Find all residues of $f(z) = \frac{1}{z^2(z-1)}$.

**Solution:**

Singularities: double pole at $z=0$, simple pole at $z=1$.

**At $z = 0$** (order 2):
$$\text{Res}(f, 0) = \lim_{z \to 0}\frac{d}{dz}\left[z^2 \cdot \frac{1}{z^2(z-1)}\right]$$
$$= \lim_{z \to 0}\frac{d}{dz}\left(\frac{1}{z-1}\right) = \lim_{z \to 0}\frac{-1}{(z-1)^2} = -1$$

**At $z = 1$** (order 1):
$$\text{Res}(f, 1) = \lim_{z \to 1}(z-1)\frac{1}{z^2(z-1)} = \lim_{z \to 1}\frac{1}{z^2} = 1$$

**Answer:** $\text{Res}(f, 0) = -1$, $\text{Res}(f, 1) = 1$

## Residues and Integrals

The residue directly determines contour integrals via:

$$\oint_C f(z) \, dz = 2\pi i \sum \text{Res}(f, z_k)$$

where sum is over all singularities inside $C$.

### Example 14: Computing Integral via Residue

Evaluate $\oint_{|z|=2} \frac{dz}{z-1}$.

**Solution:**

Singularity at $z = 1$ (inside $|z| = 2$).

$$\text{Res}\left(\frac{1}{z-1}, 1\right) = 1$$

$$\oint_{|z|=2} \frac{dz}{z-1} = 2\pi i \cdot 1 = 2\pi i$$

**Answer:** $2\pi i$

### Example 15: Integral with Multiple Residues

Evaluate $\oint_{|z|=3} \frac{dz}{z(z-2)}$.

**Solution:**

Singularities at $z=0$ and $z=2$ (both inside $|z|=3$).

From partial fractions: $\frac{1}{z(z-2)} = \frac{1/2}{z-2} - \frac{1/2}{z}$

$$\text{Res}(f, 0) = -\frac{1}{2}, \quad \text{Res}(f, 2) = \frac{1}{2}$$

$$\oint_{|z|=3} \frac{dz}{z(z-2)} = 2\pi i\left(-\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{2}\right) = 0$$

**Answer:** $0$

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: B**
Residue = $a_{-1}$ = coefficient of $(z-z_0)^{-1} = \frac{1}{z-z_0}$
From Laurent series: $f = \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} a_n(z-z_0)^n$
Only $1/(z-z_0)$ term contributes to $\oint f dz$
Formula: $\text{Res}(f, z_0) = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_C f dz$
Critical coefficient for integration

**Problem 2: C**
Simple pole formula: $\text{Res}(f, z_0) = \lim_{z \to z_0}(z-z_0)f(z)$
Multiplying by $(z-z_0)$ cancels the pole
Limit extracts $a_{-1}$ coefficient
Example: $\frac{1}{z-1}$ at $z=1$: $\lim_{z \to 1}(z-1)\frac{1}{z-1} = 1$
Most commonly used residue formula

**Problem 3: A**
$\frac{1}{z^2}$ has pole of order $2$ (not simple pole)
Laurent series: $\frac{1}{z^2}$ (only one term)
No $\frac{1}{z}$ term, so $a_{-1} = 0$
Residue is zero despite having singularity
Only coefficient of $1/z$ matters for residue

**Problem 4: B**
Quotient formula for simple poles
$h(z_0) = 0$ with $h'(z_0) \neq 0$ means $h$ has simple zero
Therefore $f$ has simple pole at $z_0$
Formula: $\text{Res}(f, z_0) = \frac{g(z_0)}{h'(z_0)}$
Very useful for rational functions

**Problem 5: C**
Residue theorem: $\oint_C f = 2\pi i \sum \text{residues inside}$
Only one singularity $z_0$ inside, with residue $3$
$\oint_C f = 2\pi i \cdot 3 = 6\pi i$
Must multiply by $2\pi i$ (not just residue value)
Connection between residue and contour integral
