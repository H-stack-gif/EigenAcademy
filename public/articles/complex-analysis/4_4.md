# Complex Analysis Topic 4.4: Zeros and Singularities

Every analytic function has a local structure determined by its zeros (where $f(z) = 0$) and singularities (where $f$ fails to be analytic). Near a zero of order $m$, the function behaves like $f(z) \approx c(z-z_0)^m$, vanishing to order $m$. Near an isolated singularity, the Laurent series reveals whether the singularity is **removable** (can be "fixed" by redefining at one point), a **pole** (blows up like a finite negative power), or **essential** (wildly oscillating behavior described by the Casorati-Weierstrass theorem).

The classification of singularities is both algebraic and geometric. Removable singularities are gentle—the function has a removable discontinuity. Poles are controlled—near a pole of order $m$, $f(z) \approx \frac{c}{(z-z_0)^m}$. Essential singularities are chaotic—arbitrarily near an essential singularity, $f$ comes arbitrarily close to any complex value. This dramatic difference in behavior shows how Laurent series coefficients determine function structure.

This article develops the theory of zeros and singularities. We'll classify zeros by order and understand their multiplicity, define and identify isolated singularities (removable, poles, essential), use Laurent series to classify singularities algebraically, apply the Riemann removable singularity theorem, and understand the behavior near essential singularities. You'll learn to analyze local function behavior, classify singularities from Laurent series, and understand how singularities control global function properties.

## Zeros of Analytic Functions

**Zero**: $z_0$ is a zero of $f$ if $f(z_0) = 0$.

**Order (multiplicity)**: $z_0$ is a zero of **order $m$** if:
$$f(z_0) = f'(z_0) = \cdots = f^{(m-1)}(z_0) = 0$$
but $f^{(m)}(z_0) \neq 0$.

Near zero: $f(z) = (z-z_0)^m g(z)$ where $g(z_0) \neq 0$.

### Example 1: Finding Order of Zero

Find the order of zero of $f(z) = z^3 - 3z^2 + 3z - 1$ at $z = 1$.

**Solution:**

$$f(1) = 1 - 3 + 3 - 1 = 0$$ ✓ (zero at $z=1$)

$$f'(z) = 3z^2 - 6z + 3$$
$$f'(1) = 3 - 6 + 3 = 0$$ (order $\geq 2$)

$$f''(z) = 6z - 6$$
$$f''(1) = 6 - 6 = 0$$ (order $\geq 3$)

$$f'''(z) = 6$$
$$f'''(1) = 6 \neq 0$$ (order exactly $3$)

Alternatively: $f(z) = (z-1)^3$ (perfect cube)

**Answer:** Zero of order $3$ at $z=1$

### Example 2: Simple vs. Multiple Zeros

Classify zeros of $f(z) = z^2(z-1)$.

**Solution:**

Zeros at $z = 0$ and $z = 1$.

At $z = 0$: Factor $(z-0)^2$ appears, so order $2$ (double zero)

At $z = 1$: Factor $(z-1)^1$ appears, so order $1$ (simple zero)

**Answer:** Double zero at $z=0$, simple zero at $z=1$

### Example 3: Finding Zeros from Series

If $f(z) = 3z^4 + z^5 + \cdots$ (Taylor series at $z=0$), what is order of zero at origin?

**Solution:**

$$f(z) = 3z^4 + z^5 + \cdots = z^4(3 + z + \cdots)$$

First non-zero term has power $4$.

$$f(0) = 0, \quad f'(0) = 0, \quad f''(0) = 0, \quad f'''(0) = 0, \quad f^{(4)}(0) = 4! \cdot 3 \neq 0$$

**Answer:** Zero of order $4$

### Example 4: Sine Function Zeros

Find order of zeros of $\sin z$.

**Solution:**

$$\sin z = z - \frac{z^3}{3!} + \frac{z^5}{5!} - \cdots$$

At $z = 0$: First term is $z$ (power $1$), so order $1$ (simple zero)

At $z = n\pi$ ($n \neq 0$): Can show $\sin(n\pi) = 0$ and $\cos(n\pi) = \pm 1 \neq 0$

Since $(\sin z)' = \cos z$ and $\cos(n\pi) \neq 0$, these are simple zeros.

**Answer:** All zeros of $\sin z$ are simple (order $1$)

## Isolated Singularities

**Isolated singularity**: $f$ is analytic in punctured disk $0 < |z - z_0| < r$ but not at $z_0$.

**Three types**:
1. **Removable**: Can redefine $f(z_0)$ to make $f$ analytic
2. **Pole of order $m$**: Principal part has finitely many terms (highest power $m$)
3. **Essential**: Principal part has infinitely many terms

### Example 5: Identifying Removable Singularity

Classify the singularity of $f(z) = \frac{\sin z}{z}$ at $z = 0$.

**Solution:**

$$\sin z = z - \frac{z^3}{3!} + \frac{z^5}{5!} - \cdots$$

$$\frac{\sin z}{z} = 1 - \frac{z^2}{3!} + \frac{z^4}{5!} - \cdots$$

**Laurent series**: No negative powers! (All powers $\geq 0$)

Can define $f(0) = 1$ to make $f$ analytic at $z=0$.

**Answer:** Removable singularity at $z=0$

### Example 6: Identifying Simple Pole

Classify the singularity of $f(z) = \frac{1}{z-1}$ at $z = 1$.

**Solution:**

$$f(z) = \frac{1}{z-1}$$

Laurent series: Just $\frac{1}{z-1}$ (single term, power $-1$)

**Principal part**: $\frac{1}{z-1}$ (highest power is $1$)

**Answer:** Simple pole (pole of order $1$) at $z=1$

### Example 7: Pole of Higher Order

Classify the singularity of $f(z) = \frac{1}{z^3}$ at $z = 0$.

**Solution:**

$$f(z) = \frac{1}{z^3}$$

Laurent series: $\frac{1}{z^3}$ (single term, power $-3$)

**Principal part**: $\frac{1}{z^3}$ (highest negative power is $3$)

**Answer:** Pole of order $3$ at $z=0$

### Example 8: Essential Singularity

Classify the singularity of $f(z) = e^{1/z}$ at $z = 0$.

**Solution:**

From earlier:
$$e^{1/z} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n! z^n} = 1 + \frac{1}{z} + \frac{1}{2!z^2} + \frac{1}{3!z^3} + \cdots$$

**Principal part**: Infinitely many terms ($\frac{1}{z}, \frac{1}{z^2}, \frac{1}{z^3}, \ldots$)

**Answer:** Essential singularity at $z=0$

## Classification via Laurent Series

**Criterion**: At isolated singularity $z_0$, examine Laurent series $\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} a_n(z-z_0)^n$:

- **Removable**: $a_n = 0$ for all $n < 0$ (no principal part)
- **Pole of order $m$**: $a_{-m} \neq 0$ and $a_n = 0$ for $n < -m$ (finite principal part)
- **Essential**: $a_n \neq 0$ for infinitely many $n < 0$ (infinite principal part)

### Example 9: Classifying from Series

Classify singularity if $f(z) = \frac{2}{z} + 1 + z + z^2 + \cdots$ at $z=0$.

**Solution:**

Laurent series given.

**Principal part**: $\frac{2}{z}$ (only one term with power $-1$)

**Answer:** Simple pole (order $1$) at $z=0$

### Example 10: Multiple Classification

Classify all singularities of $f(z) = \frac{z+1}{z^2(z-3)}$.

**Solution:**

Singularities at $z = 0$ (appears squared in denominator) and $z = 3$ (appears linearly).

**At $z=0$**: Near $z=0$:
$$\frac{z+1}{z^2(z-3)} \approx \frac{1}{z^2(-3)} = -\frac{1}{3z^2}$$

Pole of order $2$.

**At $z=3$**: Near $z=3$:
$$\frac{z+1}{z^2(z-3)} \approx \frac{4}{9(z-3)}$$

Simple pole (order $1$).

**Answer:** Pole of order $2$ at $z=0$; simple pole at $z=3$

### Example 11: Removable via Cancellation

Classify singularity of $f(z) = \frac{z^2-1}{z-1}$ at $z=1$.

**Solution:**

$$f(z) = \frac{(z-1)(z+1)}{z-1} = z+1 \quad (z \neq 1)$$

Near $z=1$: $f(z) \to 2$

Can define $f(1) = 2$ to make $f$ analytic.

**Laurent series**: $f(z) = z + 1$ (polynomial, no negative powers)

**Answer:** Removable singularity at $z=1$

### Example 12: Essential at Infinity

Classify singularity of $f(z) = e^z$ at $z = \infty$.

**Solution:**

Consider $g(w) = f(1/w) = e^{1/w}$ at $w = 0$.

$$e^{1/w} = 1 + \frac{1}{w} + \frac{1}{2!w^2} + \cdots$$

Essential singularity at $w=0$.

Therefore $f$ has essential singularity at $\infty$.

**Answer:** Essential singularity at $\infty$

## Riemann's Removable Singularity Theorem

**Theorem**: If $f$ is analytic in $0 < |z-z_0| < r$ and **bounded** near $z_0$, then the singularity at $z_0$ is removable.

**Consequence**: If $\lim_{z \to z_0} f(z)$ exists (finite), singularity is removable.

### Example 13: Applying Riemann's Theorem

Show that $f(z) = \frac{1-\cos z}{z^2}$ has removable singularity at $z=0$.

**Solution:**

$$\cos z = 1 - \frac{z^2}{2!} + \frac{z^4}{4!} - \cdots$$

$$1 - \cos z = \frac{z^2}{2!} - \frac{z^4}{4!} + \cdots$$

$$\frac{1-\cos z}{z^2} = \frac{1}{2!} - \frac{z^2}{4!} + \cdots$$

This is analytic at $z=0$ (Taylor series).

**Limit**: $\lim_{z \to 0} \frac{1-\cos z}{z^2} = \frac{1}{2}$ (finite)

By Riemann's theorem: removable.

**Answer:** Removable singularity; define $f(0) = 1/2$

### Example 14: When Riemann Doesn't Apply

Does $f(z) = \frac{1}{z}$ have removable singularity at $z=0$?

**Solution:**

$$\lim_{z \to 0} \frac{1}{z}$$ does not exist (goes to $\infty$)

$f$ is unbounded near $z=0$.

Riemann's theorem doesn't apply.

**Classification**: Simple pole (not removable).

**Answer:** Not removable; simple pole at $z=0$

### Example 15: Behavior Near Essential Singularity

State the Casorati-Weierstrass theorem about essential singularities.

**Solution:**

**Casorati-Weierstrass Theorem**: If $f$ has essential singularity at $z_0$, then in any punctured neighborhood of $z_0$, $f$ takes values arbitrarily close to **every** complex number.

**Meaning**: Near essential singularity, $f$ is "wild"—its range is dense in $\mathbb{C}$.

**Example**: $e^{1/z}$ near $z=0$ comes arbitrarily close to any $w \in \mathbb{C}$.

**Answer:** Near essential singularity, function values dense in complex plane

## Practice Problems

**Problem 1:** A zero of order $2$ is called:

A) Simple zero
B) Double zero
C) Essential zero
D) Removable zero
E) Pole

**Problem 2:** A removable singularity has principal part:

A) Infinitely many terms
B) One term
C) No terms (empty)
D) Exactly two terms
E) All powers

**Problem 3:** The singularity of $\frac{e^z - 1}{z}$ at $z=0$ is:

A) Simple pole
B) Pole of order $2$
C) Essential
D) Removable
E) None

**Problem 4:** An essential singularity has:

A) Finite principal part
B) Infinite principal part
C) No principal part
D) Only one term
E) Bounded behavior

**Problem 5:** Near a pole of order $m$, $|f(z)|$:

A) Goes to zero
B) Goes to infinity
C) Is bounded
D) Oscillates
E) Is constant

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: B**
Zero of order $2$ = double zero = multiplicity $2$
$f(z_0) = 0$ and $f'(z_0) = 0$, but $f''(z_0) \neq 0$
Near $z_0$: $f(z) = (z-z_0)^2 g(z)$ where $g(z_0) \neq 0$
Example: $(z-1)^2$ has double zero at $z=1$
Simple zero = order $1$, triple zero = order $3$, etc.

**Problem 2: C**
Removable singularity: Laurent series has no negative powers
Principal part empty: $a_n = 0$ for all $n < 0$
Can redefine at one point to make analytic
Example: $\frac{\sin z}{z} = 1 - \frac{z^2}{6} + \cdots$ (no $1/z$ terms)
Contrast: pole has finite principal part, essential has infinite

**Problem 3: D**
$e^z = 1 + z + \frac{z^2}{2!} + \cdots$
$e^z - 1 = z + \frac{z^2}{2!} + \cdots$
$\frac{e^z-1}{z} = 1 + \frac{z}{2!} + \cdots$ (no negative powers)
Define $f(0) = 1$ makes it analytic
Removable singularity

**Problem 4: B**
Essential singularity: infinitely many nonzero $a_n$ with $n < 0$
Example: $e^{1/z} = 1 + \frac{1}{z} + \frac{1}{2!z^2} + \frac{1}{3!z^3} + \cdots$
Principal part never terminates
Casorati-Weierstrass: function values dense in $\mathbb{C}$
Wildly oscillating behavior near singularity

**Problem 5: B**
Pole of order $m$: $f(z) \sim \frac{c}{(z-z_0)^m}$ as $z \to z_0$
$|f(z)| \to \infty$ as $z \to z_0$
Function "blows up" at pole
Contrast: removable has finite limit
Essential has dense range (no limit)
