# Complex Analysis Topic 6.2: Linear Fractional Transformations

Among all conformal mappings, **linear fractional transformations** (also called **Möbius transformations**) form a special class with remarkable properties. These functions $f(z) = \frac{az+b}{cz+d}$ where $ad - bc \neq 0$ are the simplest non-trivial conformal maps, yet they're surprisingly powerful. They map circles and lines to circles and lines, preserve angles, and can be completely determined by specifying where three points map. Every Möbius transformation can be decomposed into a composition of translations, rotations, scalings, and inversions—the fundamental geometric operations of the extended complex plane $\mathbb{C} \cup \{\infty\}$.

The most important property of Möbius transformations is their preservation of **cross-ratios**: if four points $z_1, z_2, z_3, z_4$ have cross-ratio $(z_1, z_2; z_3, z_4) = \frac{(z_1-z_3)(z_2-z_4)}{(z_1-z_4)(z_2-z_3)}$, their images $w_1, w_2, w_3, w_4$ have the same cross-ratio. This invariance makes Möbius transformations act like "rigid motions" of the Riemann sphere. They form a group under composition, with inversion and the identity as special cases. Finding the Möbius transformation mapping three given points to three target points reduces to a simple algebraic calculation.

This article develops the theory of linear fractional transformations systematically. We'll define Möbius transformations and determine when they're well-defined, decompose them into elementary operations, prove they map circles and lines to circles and lines, derive the cross-ratio and its invariance, construct transformations mapping specific points to desired locations, and explore special cases like inversions and the Cayley transform. You'll master finding and applying Möbius transformations to solve geometric and analytic problems.

## Definition and Basic Properties

A **linear fractional transformation** (Möbius transformation) is:

$$w = f(z) = \frac{az + b}{cz + d}$$

where $a, b, c, d \in \mathbb{C}$ and $ad - bc \neq 0$.

**Condition** $ad - bc \neq 0$ ensures $f$ is not constant.

**Domain**: If $c \neq 0$, pole at $z = -d/c$; define $f(-d/c) = \infty$ and $f(\infty) = a/c$.

If $c = 0$, $f(z) = (a/d)z + (b/d)$ (linear function).

### Example 1: Verifying Non-Degeneracy

For what values of $k$ is $f(z) = \frac{z + k}{2z + 4}$ a Möbius transformation?

**Solution:**

$$a = 1, \quad b = k, \quad c = 2, \quad d = 4$$

$$ad - bc = 1 \cdot 4 - k \cdot 2 = 4 - 2k$$

Condition: $ad - bc \neq 0$

$$4 - 2k \neq 0 \implies k \neq 2$$

**Answer:** All $k \neq 2$

### Example 2: Finding Poles and Values at Infinity

For $f(z) = \frac{2z + 3}{z - 1}$, find $f(1)$, $f(\infty)$, and the pole.

**Solution:**

**Pole** at $z = 1$ (denominator zero):
$$f(1) = \infty$$

**At $z = \infty$**:
$$f(\infty) = \frac{a}{c} = \frac{2}{1} = 2$$

**At another point**, say $z = 0$:
$$f(0) = \frac{3}{-1} = -3$$

**Answer:** $f(1) = \infty$, $f(\infty) = 2$, pole at $z=1$

### Example 3: Composing Möbius Transformations

If $f(z) = \frac{z+1}{z-1}$ and $g(z) = \frac{2z}{z+1}$, find $(g \circ f)(z)$.

**Solution:**

$$g(f(z)) = g\left(\frac{z+1}{z-1}\right) = \frac{2 \cdot \frac{z+1}{z-1}}{\frac{z+1}{z-1} + 1}$$

$$= \frac{\frac{2(z+1)}{z-1}}{\frac{z+1+z-1}{z-1}} = \frac{\frac{2(z+1)}{z-1}}{\frac{2z}{z-1}} = \frac{2(z+1)}{2z} = \frac{z+1}{z}$$

This is also a Möbius transformation.

**Answer:** $g \circ f(z) = \frac{z+1}{z}$

### Example 4: Inverse of Möbius Transformation

Find the inverse of $f(z) = \frac{2z + 1}{z - 3}$.

**Solution:**

Set $w = \frac{2z + 1}{z - 3}$ and solve for $z$:

$$w(z - 3) = 2z + 1$$
$$wz - 3w = 2z + 1$$
$$wz - 2z = 3w + 1$$
$$z(w - 2) = 3w + 1$$
$$z = \frac{3w + 1}{w - 2}$$

Inverse: $f^{-1}(w) = \frac{3w + 1}{w - 2}$

Verify: $ad - bc = 3 \cdot 2 - 1 \cdot 1 = 5 \neq 0$ ✓

**Answer:** $f^{-1}(z) = \frac{3z + 1}{z - 2}$

## Decomposition into Elementary Transformations

Every Möbius transformation is a composition of:
1. **Translation**: $z \mapsto z + b$
2. **Rotation/scaling**: $z \mapsto az$ (where $a \neq 0$)
3. **Inversion**: $z \mapsto 1/z$

### Example 5: Decomposing Simple Case

Express $f(z) = \frac{1}{z}$ as elementary operations.

**Solution:**

$f(z) = 1/z$ is already elementary (inversion).

This is the fundamental operation; others are linear.

**Answer:** Single inversion

### Example 6: Decomposing General Form

Decompose $f(z) = \frac{3z + 2}{z + 1}$.

**Solution:**

Method: Rewrite by polynomial division.

$$f(z) = \frac{3z + 2}{z + 1} = \frac{3(z+1) - 1}{z + 1} = 3 - \frac{1}{z+1}$$

Steps:
1. $z \mapsto z + 1$ (translation)
2. $z \mapsto 1/z$ (inversion)
3. $z \mapsto -z$ (scaling by $-1$)
4. $z \mapsto z + 3$ (translation)

**Answer:** $f = T_3 \circ M_{-1} \circ I \circ T_1$ where $T_a(z) = z+a$, $M_a(z) = az$, $I(z) = 1/z$

### Example 7: Fixed Points

Find fixed points of $f(z) = \frac{2z + 1}{z + 2}$ (where $f(z) = z$).

**Solution:**

$$\frac{2z + 1}{z + 2} = z$$

$$2z + 1 = z(z + 2)$$
$$2z + 1 = z^2 + 2z$$
$$z^2 = 1$$
$$z = \pm 1$$

**Answer:** Fixed points at $z = 1$ and $z = -1$

## Circles and Lines

**Key Property**: Möbius transformations map the family of circles and lines to itself.

(A line is considered a "circle through infinity")

### Example 8: Inversion and the Real Axis

Under $f(z) = 1/z$, what is the image of the real axis?

**Solution:**

Real axis: $\text{Im}(z) = 0$, i.e., $z = x$ for $x \in \mathbb{R}$.

$$f(x) = \frac{1}{x}$$

This is still real for $x \neq 0$.

At $x = 0$: maps to $\infty$

At $\infty$: maps to $0$

Image: Real axis (including $\infty$)

**Answer:** Real axis (to itself)

### Example 9: Circle Under Inversion

Find the image of $|z - 1| = 1$ under $f(z) = 1/z$.

**Solution:**

Circle passes through origin ($z = 0$ satisfies $|0-1| = 1$).

Circles through origin map to **lines** under inversion.

The circle $|z-1| = 1$ passes through $0$ and $2$.

Under $f$: $0 \mapsto \infty$, $2 \mapsto 1/2$

The circle also passes through $1 + i$ (top of circle).
$$f(1+i) = \frac{1}{1+i} = \frac{1-i}{2}$$

Image is the line through $\infty$, $1/2$, and $(1-i)/2$.

This is the vertical line $\text{Re}(w) = 1/2$.

**Answer:** Line $\text{Re}(w) = 1/2$

### Example 10: Circle Not Through Origin

Find the image of $|z| = 2$ under $f(z) = 1/z$.

**Solution:**

Circle not passing through origin maps to circle.

Let $z = 2e^{i\theta}$ (circle $|z| = 2$)

$$f(z) = \frac{1}{2e^{i\theta}} = \frac{1}{2}e^{-i\theta}$$

This traces circle $|w| = 1/2$ (orientation reversed).

**Answer:** Circle $|w| = 1/2$

### Example 11: Line Under General Möbius

What is the image of the imaginary axis under $f(z) = \frac{z - i}{z + i}$?

**Solution:**

Imaginary axis: $z = iy$ for $y \in \mathbb{R}$.

$$f(iy) = \frac{iy - i}{iy + i} = \frac{i(y-1)}{i(y+1)} = \frac{y-1}{y+1}$$

Set $w = u + iv = f(iy)$. Then $v = 0$ and $u = \frac{y-1}{y+1} \in \mathbb{R}$.

As $y$ ranges over $\mathbb{R}$, $u$ covers $(-\infty,1) \cup (1,\infty)$ with a jump at $y=-1$ (pole).

Image: Entire real axis except $u=1$ corresponds to $y \to \infty$.

**Answer:** Real axis $(-\infty,\infty)$


## Cross-Ratio

The **cross-ratio** of four points $z_1, z_2, z_3, z_4$ is:

$$(z_1, z_2; z_3, z_4) = \frac{(z_1 - z_3)(z_2 - z_4)}{(z_1 - z_4)(z_2 - z_3)}$$

**Invariance**: If $f$ is a Möbius transformation and $w_k = f(z_k)$, then:

$$(w_1, w_2; w_3, w_4) = (z_1, z_2; z_3, z_4)$$

### Example 12: Computing Cross-Ratio

Find the cross-ratio $(1, i; -1, -i)$.

**Solution:**

$$\frac{(1 - (-1))(i - (-i))}{(1 - (-i))(i - (-1))} = \frac{2 \cdot 2i}{(1+i)(i+1)} = \frac{4i}{(1+i)^2}$$

$$(1+i)^2 = 1 + 2i - 1 = 2i$$

$$= \frac{4i}{2i} = 2$$

**Answer:** $(1, i; -1, -i) = 2$

### Example 13: Using Cross-Ratio to Find Transformation

Find Möbius transformation with $f(0) = 1$, $f(1) = i$, $f(\infty) = -1$.

**Solution:**

Use cross-ratio formula. For any fourth point $z$ mapping to $w = f(z)$:

$$(z, 0; 1, \infty) = (w, 1; i, -1)$$

$$(z, 0; 1, \infty) = \lim_{p \to \infty}\frac{(z-1)(0-p)}{(z-p)(0-1)} = \lim_{p \to \infty}\frac{-(z-1)p}{-(z-p)} = z-1$$

(Using L'Hôpital or direct limit)

$$(w, 1; i, -1) = \frac{(w-i)(1-(-1))}{(w-(-1))(1-i)} = \frac{2(w-i)}{(w+1)(1-i)}$$

$$z - 1 = \frac{2(w-i)}{(w+1)(1-i)}$$

$$(z-1)(w+1)(1-i) = 2(w-i)$$

Solving for $w$: (algebra omitted)

$$w = \frac{(1-i)z - (1+i)}{-(1-i)z + (3-i)}$$

Simplify to standard form.

**Answer:** $w = \frac{(1+i) + (1-i)z}{(1+i) + (i-1)z}$ (after simplification)

## Finding Möbius Transformations

**Theorem**: A Möbius transformation is uniquely determined by where it sends three distinct points.

**Method**: To find $f$ with $f(z_k) = w_k$ for $k = 1, 2, 3$, use:

$$\frac{w - w_1}{w - w_2} \cdot \frac{w_3 - w_2}{w_3 - w_1} = \frac{z - z_1}{z - z_2} \cdot \frac{z_3 - z_2}{z_3 - z_1}$$

### Example 14: Three Point Mapping

Find the Möbius transformation with $f(0) = 1$, $f(1) = 0$, $f(\infty) = 2$.

**Solution:**

For $\infty$, use limiting form. Start with general points, then take limits.

Cross-ratio approach: $(w, 1; 0, 2) = (z, 0; 1, \infty)$

$$(z, 0; 1, \infty) = z - 1$$ (from previous calculation)

$$(w, 1; 0, 2) = \frac{(w-0)(1-2)}{(w-2)(1-0)} = \frac{-w}{w-2}$$

$$z - 1 = \frac{-w}{w-2}$$

$$(z-1)(w-2) = -w$$
$$wz - 2z - w + 2 = -w$$
$$wz - 2z + 2 = 0$$
$$w(z-2) = 2z - 2$$
$$w = \frac{2(z-1)}{z-2}$$

Verify: $f(0) = \frac{-2}{-2} = 1$ ✓, $f(1) = 0/-1 = 0$ ✓, $f(\infty) = 2$ ✓

**Answer:** $f(z) = \frac{2z - 2}{z - 2}$

### Example 15: Cayley Transform

The **Cayley transform** $f(z) = \frac{z - i}{z + i}$ maps the upper half-plane to the unit disk. Verify $f(i) = 0$ and $f(-i) = \infty$.

**Solution:**

$$f(i) = \frac{i - i}{i + i} = \frac{0}{2i} = 0$$ ✓

$$f(-i) = \frac{-i - i}{-i + i} = \frac{-2i}{0} = \infty$$ ✓

For $\text{Im}(z) > 0$, can show $|f(z)| < 1$ (maps upper half-plane to disk).

**Answer:** Verified; maps $\mathbb{H} \to \mathbb{D}$

## Answer Key

**Problem 1: B**
Definition: $f(z) = \frac{az+b}{cz+d}$ with $ad - bc \neq 0$
Also called linear fractional transformation
Condition $ad - bc \neq 0$ ensures non-degeneracy
Includes translations ($c=0$), inversions ($a=d=0$)
Forms a group under composition

**Problem 2: C**
Fundamental property: circles and lines → circles and lines
Line = "circle through infinity"
Preserves the family of circles/lines
Example: $1/z$ maps circle $|z|=2$ to circle $|w|=1/2$
Example: $1/z$ maps line through origin to line

**Problem 3: B**
Cross-ratio $(z_1, z_2; z_3, z_4)$ invariant under Möbius
$(w_1, w_2; w_3, w_4) = (z_1, z_2; z_3, z_4)$ where $w_k = f(z_k)$
Fundamental invariant (like distance for isometries)
Used to construct transformations
Four points lie on circle/line iff cross-ratio is real

**Problem 4: C**
Key property of inversion
Circle through origin → line (not through origin in image)
Circle not through origin → circle not through origin
Line through origin → line through origin
Line not through origin → circle through origin

**Problem 5: C**
Three points determine unique Möbius transformation
Specify $f(z_1) = w_1$, $f(z_2) = w_2$, $f(z_3) = w_3$
Use cross-ratio formula to construct
Two points: infinitely many transformations
Four points: over-determined (generally no solution)
