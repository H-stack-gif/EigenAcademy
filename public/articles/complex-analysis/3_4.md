# Complex Analysis Topic 3.4: Consequences

Cauchy's integral formula isn't just a computational tool—it's a gateway to profound theorems about analytic functions. From this single formula flow remarkable results: Morera's theorem (the converse to Cauchy), Cauchy's inequality (bounding derivatives), and Liouville's theorem (proving every bounded entire function is constant). These consequences reveal the extraordinary rigidity of analytic functions. Unlike real differentiable functions, which can have wild local behavior, complex analytic functions are globally constrained by their local properties.

Liouville's theorem provides an elegant proof of the fundamental theorem of algebra: every non-constant polynomial has a root. The argument is indirect—assume a polynomial $p(z)$ has no roots, then $1/p(z)$ is entire and bounded, hence constant by Liouville, contradiction. This shows how complex analysis proves results about polynomials that seem purely algebraic. Cauchy's inequality bounds higher derivatives by the maximum modulus, revealing how function values on a circle control all derivatives at the center.

This article explores the major consequences of Cauchy's integral formula. We'll prove Morera's theorem and use it to establish analyticity, derive Cauchy's inequality and apply it to bound derivatives, prove Liouville's theorem and understand bounded entire functions, and use these results to prove the fundamental theorem of algebra. You'll see how Cauchy's formula generates a cascade of powerful theorems that illuminate the structure of analytic functions.

## Morera's Theorem

**Morera's Theorem**: If $f$ is continuous in domain $D$ and $\oint_C f(z) \, dz = 0$ for every closed contour $C$ in $D$, then $f$ is analytic in $D$.

**Significance**: This is a **converse** to Cauchy's theorem. It provides a way to prove functions are analytic without checking Cauchy-Riemann equations.

### Example 1: Applying Morera's Theorem

Show that if $f$ is continuous and $\int_a^b f(x) \, dx = 0$ for all paths in $D$, then $f$ is analytic.

**Solution:**

Given: $\oint_C f(z) \, dz = 0$ for all closed $C$ in $D$ (stated condition).

Also: $f$ is continuous.

By Morera's theorem: $f$ is analytic in $D$.

**Proof sketch of Morera**: If $\oint f = 0$ for all closed contours, then $\int_{z_0}^z f$ is path-independent, so we can define $F(z) = \int_{z_0}^z f(\zeta) \, d\zeta$. Then $F'(z) = f(z)$ (can be shown), so $f$ has an antiderivative. Since $f = F'$ and $F$ is analytic (integrals of continuous functions along paths yield analytic functions when path-independent), $f$ is analytic.

**Answer:** $f$ is analytic by Morera's theorem

### Example 2: Proving Analyticity of Series

If $\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} a_n z^n$ converges for $|z| < R$, show the sum is analytic.

**Solution:**

Let $f(z) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} a_n z^n$ for $|z| < R$.

Power series are continuous in their disk of convergence.

For any closed contour $C$ in $|z| < R$:
$$\oint_C f(z) \, dz = \oint_C \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} a_n z^n \, dz = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} a_n \oint_C z^n \, dz$$

Since $z^n$ is a polynomial (entire), by Cauchy's theorem:
$$\oint_C z^n \, dz = 0$$ for all $n$

Therefore:
$$\oint_C f(z) \, dz = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} a_n \cdot 0 = 0$$

By Morera's theorem: $f$ is analytic.

**Answer:** Power series are analytic in their disk of convergence

### Example 3: Checking Conditions

Does $f(z) = |z|^2$ satisfy Morera's theorem conditions?

**Solution:**

$f(z) = |z|^2$ is continuous everywhere ✓

Check: $\oint_C |z|^2 \, dz = 0$ for all closed $C$?

On $C = $ circle $|z| = 1$: $|z|^2 = 1$

$$\oint_{|z|=1} 1 \cdot dz = \oint_{|z|=1} dz$$

Parametrize $z = e^{it}$: $dz = ie^{it} dt$

$$\oint dz = \int_0^{2\pi} ie^{it} \, dt = i[e^{it}]_0^{2\pi} = i(1-1) = 0$$

This is $0$! But $|z|^2$ is not analytic (violates Cauchy-Riemann).

What's wrong? Need to check **all** closed contours, not just circles. In fact, $|z|^2$ fails for some contours.

**Answer:** Condition fails for some contours; $|z|^2$ not analytic

## Cauchy's Inequality

**Cauchy's Inequality**: If $f$ is analytic in $|z - z_0| \leq R$ and $|f(z)| \leq M$ on $|z - z_0| = R$, then:

$$|f^{(n)}(z_0)| \leq \frac{n! M}{R^n}$$

**Proof**: From Cauchy's formula for derivatives:
$$f^{(n)}(z_0) = \frac{n!}{2\pi i}\oint_{|z-z_0|=R} \frac{f(z)}{(z-z_0)^{n+1}} \, dz$$

Using ML inequality:
$$|f^{(n)}(z_0)| \leq \frac{n!}{2\pi} \cdot M \cdot 2\pi R \cdot \frac{1}{R^{n+1}} = \frac{n! M}{R^n}$$

### Example 4: Bounding First Derivative

If $|f(z)| \leq 10$ for $|z| \leq 2$ and $f$ is analytic, bound $|f'(0)|$.

**Solution:**

Use Cauchy's inequality with $z_0 = 0$, $R = 2$, $M = 10$, $n = 1$:

$$|f'(0)| \leq \frac{1! \cdot 10}{2^1} = \frac{10}{2} = 5$$

**Answer:** $|f'(0)| \leq 5$

### Example 5: Bounding Second Derivative

If entire function $f$ satisfies $|f(z)| \leq 100$ for $|z| = 10$, bound $|f''(0)|$.

**Solution:**

Use Cauchy's inequality with $z_0 = 0$, $R = 10$, $M = 100$, $n = 2$:

$$|f''(0)| \leq \frac{2! \cdot 100}{10^2} = \frac{200}{100} = 2$$

**Answer:** $|f''(0)| \leq 2$

### Example 6: Optimizing Radius

If $|f(z)| \leq e^{|z|}$ for all $z$ and $f$ is entire, find best bound on $|f'''(0)|$ using $R = 5$.

**Solution:**

On $|z| = 5$: $|f(z)| \leq e^5$

Cauchy's inequality with $z_0 = 0$, $R = 5$, $M = e^5$, $n = 3$:

$$|f'''(0)| \leq \frac{3! \cdot e^5}{5^3} = \frac{6e^5}{125}$$

**Answer:** $|f'''(0)| \leq \frac{6e^5}{125}$

### Example 7: Taylor Coefficients Bound

Show that if $f$ is analytic in $|z| < R$ with $|f(z)| \leq M$, then the Taylor coefficients $a_n = \frac{f^{(n)}(0)}{n!}$ satisfy $|a_n| \leq \frac{M}{r^n}$ for any $r < R$.

**Solution:**

From Cauchy's inequality at radius $r$:
$$|f^{(n)}(0)| \leq \frac{n! M}{r^n}$$

Taylor coefficient:
$$a_n = \frac{f^{(n)}(0)}{n!}$$

$$|a_n| = \frac{|f^{(n)}(0)|}{n!} \leq \frac{1}{n!} \cdot \frac{n! M}{r^n} = \frac{M}{r^n}$$

**Answer:** $|a_n| \leq \frac{M}{r^n}$

## Liouville's Theorem

**Liouville's Theorem**: Every **bounded entire function** is constant.

**Proof**: Suppose $f$ is entire and $|f(z)| \leq M$ for all $z$.

Apply Cauchy's inequality with $z_0 = 0$ and arbitrary $R$:
$$|f'(0)| \leq \frac{M}{R}$$

As $R \to \infty$: $|f'(0)| \leq \frac{M}{R} \to 0$

Therefore $f'(0) = 0$.

Since $z_0$ was arbitrary, $f'(z) = 0$ everywhere.

Function with zero derivative everywhere is constant.

### Example 8: Applying Liouville

Show that $e^z$ is not bounded.

**Solution:**

$e^z$ is entire (analytic everywhere).

Suppose $e^z$ were bounded: $|e^z| \leq M$ for all $z$.

By Liouville's theorem: $e^z$ would be constant.

But $e^0 = 1$ and $e^1 = e \neq 1$, contradiction.

Therefore $e^z$ is unbounded.

**Explicit unboundedness**: On real axis, $|e^x| = e^x \to \infty$ as $x \to \infty$.

**Answer:** $e^z$ unbounded (Liouville implies non-constant entire functions are unbounded)

### Example 9: Sine Function

Is $\sin z$ bounded?

**Solution:**

$\sin z = \frac{e^{iz} - e^{-iz}}{2i}$ is entire.

On imaginary axis $z = iy$:
$$\sin(iy) = \frac{e^{-y} - e^y}{2i} = i\sinh(y)$$

$$|\sin(iy)| = |\sinh(y)| = \left|\frac{e^y - e^{-y}}{2}\right| \to \infty$$ as $y \to \infty$

Therefore $\sin z$ is unbounded.

**Answer:** No; $|\sin z|$ unbounded on imaginary axis

### Example 10: Quotient of Entire Functions

If $f$ and $g$ are entire with $|f(z)| \leq |g(z)|$ for all $z$, and $g$ has no zeros, what can we say about $f/g$?

**Solution:**

$h(z) = \frac{f(z)}{g(z)}$ is entire (since $g$ never zero, no singularities).

$$|h(z)| = \frac{|f(z)|}{|g(z)|} \leq \frac{|g(z)|}{|g(z)|} = 1$$

$h$ is bounded entire function.

By Liouville's theorem: $h$ is constant.

Therefore $f(z) = C \cdot g(z)$ for some constant $C$.

**Answer:** $f$ is constant multiple of $g$

## Fundamental Theorem of Algebra

**Fundamental Theorem of Algebra**: Every non-constant polynomial $p(z)$ has at least one complex root.

**Proof using Liouville**:

Suppose $p(z)$ has no roots.

Then $f(z) = \frac{1}{p(z)}$ is entire (no singularities).

For large $|z|$: $|p(z)| \approx |a_n z^n|$ where $a_n$ is leading coefficient.

Therefore $|p(z)| \to \infty$ as $|z| \to \infty$.

This means $|f(z)| = \frac{1}{|p(z)|} \to 0$ as $|z| \to \infty$.

So $f$ is bounded (goes to zero at infinity, continuous elsewhere).

By Liouville: $f$ is constant.

Therefore $p$ is constant, contradicting assumption.

### Example 11: Applying FTA

Show that $z^5 + 3z^2 - 7$ has a root.

**Solution:**

This is a non-constant polynomial (degree 5).

By fundamental theorem of algebra: it has at least one complex root.

In fact, it has exactly 5 roots (counting multiplicity).

**Answer:** Has at least one root (by FTA)

### Example 12: Factorization

Use FTA to show every polynomial of degree $n$ has exactly $n$ roots (counting multiplicity).

**Solution:**

By FTA, $p(z)$ has a root $z_1$.

Factor: $p(z) = (z - z_1)q(z)$ where $\deg(q) = n-1$.

Apply FTA to $q(z)$: has root $z_2$.

Continue: $p(z) = (z-z_1)(z-z_2) \cdots (z-z_n) \cdot c$

where $c$ is constant (leading coefficient).

**Answer:** Factors completely into $n$ linear factors

### Example 13: No Transcendental Analog

Does every entire function have a root?

**Solution:**

No! Liouville only applies to **bounded** entire functions.

$e^z$ is entire but has no roots (never zero).

$\sin z$ has infinitely many roots ($z = n\pi$), but this is not required.

$e^z + 1$ is entire with roots at $(2k+1)\pi i$.

**Answer:** No; $e^z$ is counterexample (entire with no roots)
